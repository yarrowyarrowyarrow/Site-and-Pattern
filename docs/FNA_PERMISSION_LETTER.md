# Draft letter — asking the Flora of North America Association

Not sent. This is a draft for the project owner to adapt and send if and when
they want to fill the catalogue's flower measurements from published
descriptions. See [`DATA_SOURCES.md`](DATA_SOURCES.md) for why asking is the
better move than reasoning carefully about fair dealing.

**Where to send it:** the Flora of North America Association's contact address,
via [floranorthamerica.org](http://floranorthamerica.org/). eFloras.org is
operated by Missouri Botanical Garden and Harvard University Herbaria; if the
request concerns the *website* rather than the *content*, MBG is the right
recipient. Ask FNAA first — they hold the copyright in the descriptions.

---

**Subject:** Permission request — recording floral measurements for a
noncommercial native-plant app

Dear Flora of North America Association,

I am building **Site & Pattern**, a free desktop application that helps people
in Alberta and the Canadian prairies convert lawns to native habitat — pollinator
plantings, ecological restoration, that sort of work. It is released under a
noncommercial licence (PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0), is not sold, and is not
part of any commercial product or service.

The app draws each species in a 3D preview so that someone can see what a
planting will actually look like before they dig. To do that honestly it needs a
few quantitative characters per species, and I would like to ask about four of
them:

- number of ray florets or petals on one floret
- number of florets in the drawn unit
- head or flower diameter, in centimetres
- how far the inflorescence is held above the foliage

These are exactly the numbers your descriptions give, and I have not found them
in any machine-readable open dataset. My catalogue covers roughly 400 species of
the prairie provinces and boreal Canada, so volumes 19–21 in particular would
cover most of what I need.

**What I would like to do**, if you are willing:

- record only those numeric values — never the descriptive text itself;
- cite Flora of North America per species, visibly, in a `flower_data_citation`
  field that ships with the application and is shown to users;
- access pages at human pace, one species at a time, initiated by a person, with
  a descriptive User-Agent identifying the application, honouring your
  `robots.txt`.

I would not redistribute your descriptions, reproduce any prose, or present the
result as a substitute for consulting the Flora.

I recognise that facts as such are outside copyright, but I would rather ask than
rely on that — partly because a systematic extraction across 400 species is a
different act from looking up one number, and partly because I would like this
project to be something you would be comfortable being cited in.

If a blanket permission is not something you grant, I would welcome any of:

- guidance on what scale or method of access would be acceptable;
- a pointer to a licensed data product I should be using instead;
- simply a no, which I will respect.

Happy to answer questions about the project, and glad to send you a copy of the
application or its source.

With thanks for the Flora, which is a remarkable piece of work,
