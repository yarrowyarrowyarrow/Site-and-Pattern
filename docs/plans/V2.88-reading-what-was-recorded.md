# V2.88 — Reading what was recorded

*Batch A of the V2.87 model audit ([report](https://claude.ai/artifact/4DxfwtbLxQsLHWMdUkELXD),
summarised in `docs/SPRITE_AUDIT.md`, sixth pass): the fixes that need code and
no new models. Every one of them is the viewer, or the scene builder feeding
it, reading the wrong thing: a flower cluster's size instead of the flower's,
an animal's common name instead of its recorded kind, the thorax colour instead
of the band colours, a rounded number instead of the one the model was built
from, and the alphabet instead of the design.*

## How the audit measured it

Every plant was rendered through the real viewer (the sprite-gallery path,
baked models loaded, Balanced detail, July, mature size), and the viewer's
model-selection logic was reproduced in Python over the shipped catalogue, so
each of the 424 plants and 1,144 animals could be checked against the model it
actually gets. What users see was measured by running
`scene_wildlife.wildlife_for_scene` on 400 random designs of 8 to 30 catalogue
plants, day and night, in July.

## A1 — Flowers in clusters were the size of the cluster

**215 plants** (every raceme, spike, panicle, corymb, umbel, whorl and cyme)
drew each small flower **3.6 to 15 times too big, median 7.8x** (the
audit report rounded the low end to 4). A goldenrod's
6 mm florets rendered 9 cm across; Scarlet Globemallow's 2.5 cm flowers, 22 cm,
on a 25 cm plant.

**The cause is one expression.** `15-florets.js` builds each floret in a frame
"1.0 across (so the caller scales by real diameter)" and then scaled it by
`size * f.scale`, where `size` is `_headSize`'s answer: the reach of the WHOLE
inflorescence. Heads and solitary flowers were right only because for them
`_headSize` returns the flower's own diameter.

**The second half of the same mistake:** a cluster started at 70-92% of the
plant's height and grew upward from there by its (oversized) reach, so **64 of
the 215 topped out more than 25% above the plant's recorded height** (Butte
Primrose 3.4x, Water Arum 2.8x). A recorded height includes the flowers.

**Decision.**

- A cluster's reach is computed from the species' REAL floret count
  (`florets_per_head`), not from how many florets the viewer draws, and capped
  at 45% of the plant's drawn height: an inflorescence cannot be taller than the
  plant it is on.
- Each drawn floret stands in for `n / drawn` real ones, so it is drawn at
  `d * sqrt(n / drawn)`: the drawn florets cover the area the real ones would.
  A goldenrod panicle draws 20 florets at 1.7 cm over a 26 cm plume instead of
  20 at 9 cm. Capped at half the cluster's reach, never below the flower's own
  diameter.
- A cluster hangs from the top of the plant (between 86% and 100% of its drawn
  height) instead of starting there.
- Heads and solitary flowers are unchanged; the audit found them right.

**Left alone:** `flower_height_frac` is 1.0 on all 293 described species, so it
still scales the top but differentiates nothing yet; the Stylised billboard path
is untouched (Stylised is a deliberate diagram).

## A2 — Moths were drawn as butterflies

`scene_wildlife` decided butterfly vs moth from the common name (`"moth"`,
`"sphinx"` or `"clearwing"` in it). Since the V2.59-V2.65 fauna expansion, 207 of
the 343 lepidoptera have their scientific name as their common name, so **159
moths were drawn as butterflies**, mostly in the default orange-brown, and so
were 14 of the 23 skippers (the 9 with "skipper" in the name got the skipper
silhouette). At night, **76% of the animals in a scene had the
wrong body plan**, almost all of them this.

`lepidoptera_attributes.kind` has recorded `butterfly | moth | skipper` for
every one of the 343 since schema v40. `src/flight_model.py` already has a
separate skipper wingbeat, which nothing could reach because the caller only
ever passed "moth" or "butterfly".

**Decision.** The recorded kind wins (`db.fauna.lep_kinds()`); the name test
stays as the fallback for a row with no attributes. It drives the silhouette
(moth / skipper builds), the default colours for an undescribed species, and
the flight model. The viewer's `kind` stays `butterfly | moth`, because a
skipper flies by day like a butterfly and the viewer's animation tables key on
that; the skipper is the build.

## A3 — Wasps, beetles, bugs and ants were drawn as hoverflies

`_insect_appearance` matched `"lady beetle"`, `"beetle"`, `"lacewing"` and the
dragonfly words in the common name, and drew everything else as a
yellow-and-black hoverfly. 347 of the 408 `other_insect` rows have their
scientific name as their common name, so **208 were drawn with the wrong body
plan**: 92 wasps and sawflies, 63 beetles, 33 true bugs, aphids and hoppers,
6 ants, 7 grasshoppers and crickets, 1 thrips. By day this was about one animal
in nine in every design, and the Aerial Yellowjacket, which renders as a
hoverfly, is the sixth most common animal in the simulation.

Every row's description already names its group (*"Crabronid wasps,
native."*, *"Flower longhorns, boreal and montane."*); all 266 genera fall in
exactly one group.

**Decision.** `src/fauna_body_plan.py` (new, Qt-free) reads the description and
name into one of fourteen groups, first match wins, with a test that every
insect in the catalogue lands in a group and none falls through. The viewer then
gets the nearest model it HAS:

| group | drawn as | why |
|---|---|---|
| hoverflies, bee flies, other flies | the fly (hover build) | right |
| dragonflies, damselflies, lacewings | as before | right |
| beetles, lady beetles | the beetle model | right; 63 fixed |
| wasps, sawflies | the fly | interim: they fly to flowers, and C1 (F173) builds a wasp |
| true bugs and aphids, ants, grasshoppers, thrips | the beetle model | interim: they walk on the plant rather than hover over it, which the beetle's crawl does |

The interim rows are the least-wrong existing model, not a claim that an ant
looks like a beetle; batch C1 (F173) gives them their own bodies. Each group also gets
a group-level colour and typical body length (so the "shown N x life size"
label stops calling a grasshopper 8 mm), in the same spirit as the bee genus
table: a fallback for a species nobody has described.

## A4 — The recorded bee bands never reached the bees in the scene

Schema v58 records each described bee's tergite colours, T1 to T6
(`band_pattern`), and `scene_wildlife` resolves them to `band_colours`. Only the
fly-as-a-bee avatar (`06-fly.js`) reads them. The ambient bees use the baked
model first, and `glbCritter` only toggles its three generic `Band` shells,
which are the thorax (fuzz) colour. So **54 of the 69 described bees showed the
wrong pattern**, and the American Bumble Bee (thorax recorded black) rendered
solid black; it is the second most common animal in the simulation.

**Decision.** When `band_colours` is present, `glbCritter` hides the three
generic shells and rings the abdomen with one band per recorded tergite, front
to back, each following the abdomen's taper, as children of the `Abdomen` node
so the build's shape rescale carries them. The sting tip takes the last band's
colour, as a bumble bee's hair does. No model rebuild.

## A5 — Five wildflowers got an unrelated leaf model

Heart-leaved Alexanders (compound leaves) was drawn with small broad simple
leaves, and four others likewise. They sit exactly on a height/canopy class
boundary (ratio 1.0 or 1.5). The model generator computes canopy as
`1.5 * spacing` = `0.6000000000000001` and bakes the lower class; the viewer
received the canopy rounded to 3 decimals by `src/scene3d.py`, got exactly 1.0,
and asked for the upper class, which was never built. The lookup then fell back
to **unit 0 of the file**, not the neutral unit its comment promises, because
herb keys have carried aspect and branch segments since V2.34-V2.35 and
`broad_1` no longer exists in them.

**`test_model_assets` passed throughout** because it computes the key the
GENERATOR's way. It was guarding the generator against itself.

**Decision.**

- `scene3d.py` stops rounding `mature_canopy_m`. Both sides now divide the same
  two doubles, so they agree bit for bit. Rounding the generator instead would
  flip these five to the upper class on both sides but needs the herb models
  rebuilt; not worth a rebuild for a boundary tie.
- A missing key falls back to the NEAREST baked unit of that form (same blade,
  then same size class, nearest aspect, same branching), not unit 0.
- New guard: the key the viewer will ask for, computed from what `build_scene`
  actually sends, must be a baked key for every species in the catalogue.

## A6 — Height caps that the flowers ignored

The groundcover body is capped at 18 cm (the mat is a flat model), but flowers
and fruit were placed at the plant's real height, so **nine groundcovers had
flowers floating above them** (Canada Anemone's at 60 cm over an 18 cm mat).
Aquatics were floored at 0.5 m for the reed tuft, so **seven small or floating
aquatics were drawn 2.5 to 25 times too tall** (a 2 cm duckweed as a 0.5 m reed).

**Decision.** One function, `bodyHeightOf(p)` in `04-quality.js`, says how tall
a plant is drawn, and the body, florets, billboards, fruit and seed heads all
read it. The aquatic floor drops to 5 cm, the groundcover one's value. The
floating aquatics still have the wrong body (a reed tuft); batch D1 (F175)
builds the pond, but they are now the right size.

## A7 — Animals were chosen in alphabetical order

`wildlife_for_scene` sorted candidates by `(taxon, common_name)` and took the
first eight bees, seven butterflies and moths, six birds and five other insects.
**65% of the animals in a daytime design had names starting with "A"**, against
13% of the catalogue. "A Digger Bee", "A Leafcutter Bee", "Agapostemon", then
Andrena after Andrena.

**Decision.** Within each taxon, candidates are ordered by a stable hash of the
species and the design's set of plants: deterministic for one design (so a
re-push shows the same animals), different between designs, and neutral about
names, relationship types and how generalist a species is. The relationship
priority still decides which plant an animal stands on.

## What it measured, after

The same measurements the audit made, re-run on the finished code.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn floret ÷ recorded flower, 215 cluster species | 3.6–15x, median 7.8x | **median 1.36x**, at most 3.2x |
| Cluster species whose flowers top the plant by more than a quarter | 64 | **0** |
| Night: animals on the wrong body plan (400 designs, July) | 76% | **0** |
| Day: animals on the wrong body plan | about 1 in 5 | **0**, and 2% on a stand-in flagged `interim` |
| Day: animals whose name starts with "A" | 65% | **14%** (13% of the catalogue) |
| Day: distinct species seen across the 400 designs | 429 | **882** |
| Described bees drawn with the wrong band pattern | 54 of 69 | **0** |
| Species asking the viewer for a leaf model that was never baked | 5 | **0** |

The residual above 1x is the rule, not slack: a drawn floret stands in for
`n / drawn` real ones and covers their area, which is 2.6x for Canada Goldenrod's
160. The render probe measured four clusters topping out at 1.00–1.07x the plant
(Scarlet Globemallow 0.255 m on 0.25 m, Butte Primrose 0.213 on 0.20), Canada
Anemone's flowers at 0.174 m on the 0.18 m mat, and the duckweed at 0.05 m.

**Looked at, not only measured.** Before/after contact sheets of the cluster
species, the capped groundcovers, the small aquatics and every described bee.
Clusters read as the plant's flowers now: Fireweed and Scarlet Butterfly Plant as
spikes, Harebell as bells, Butte Primrose as a low rosette with its flowers on it,
Heart-leaved Alexanders with its compound leaves back. Everything else in the
full-catalogue diff (trees, grasses, shrubs) moved by the same amount as rendering
the unchanged code twice, which is wind sway, not a change.

**A trade-off, kept.** The tiny-flowered legumes (the prairie clovers, the
milk-vetches) now show small specks of colour, because that is how big their
flowers are, and a bed of them reads less saturated than the blobs did. The fix
for that, if one is wanted, is the flowering-stem count and heads per plant
(F180's tuning), not bigger flowers.

## The render probe, and the race it found in itself

`tests/test_accuracy_render.py` sends real catalogue plants through
`build_scene` to `html/accuracy_probe.html` in the URL fragment, so the page holds
no copy of the data, and reads back each floret's drawn diameter (a new per-part
instance scale in `permaMeasure`; florets are tagged `part: 'floret'`), where the
flowers top out, the plant's height, and the baked bee's rings. Against the
pre-fix viewer it fails fourteen ways, each naming the species and the numbers
(*"Butte Primrose: flowers reach 0.84 m on a plant drawn 0.20 m tall (4.2x)"*);
the two controls, a head flower and a bee with nothing recorded, pass on both.

**The first version flaked, about one run in five**, always the same way: the
first plant built no florets. The page waited for `permaSetScene` (chunk 05),
`permaMeasure` (10) and the models to load, but the chunks load one after another
and `09-models.js` starts fetching the models when it evaluates, six chunks before
`15-florets.js`. On a fast model load the first plant was pushed before florets
existed and silently got the billboard. Reproduced by serving `15-florets.js`
three seconds late: the old wait lost the florets three runs in three, the new
one, which waits for every function the page calls and the load event, none. It
also stops waiting after 30 s and reports which function never arrived, so a
missing chunk fails the test instead of skipping it, and a genuine no-WebGL boot
now skips by saying so rather than by timing out.

**The app is not exposed to the same race**, checked rather than assumed: the
desktop host queues every push until Qt's `loadFinished`, and a page whose
florets chunk was served three seconds late still had every chunk, the last one
included, evaluated when its load event fired.

## What could not be verified here

- **No real desktop or GPU.** Every render was headless Chromium on software GL
  (SwiftShader), which changes lighting and edges, not shapes or sizes. The Qt
  window itself, the sprite gallery in the app, and walk and fly modes were not
  driven.
- **Nothing was compared against photographs.** The audit's judgments of look are
  the auditor's; the six flower colours in F172 need a flora before the data
  changes.
- **`tests.test_undo_redo` runs on a desktop only**, as for every release since
  V2.40 (it segfaults in a GPU-less container's teardown); this change does not
  touch undo.
