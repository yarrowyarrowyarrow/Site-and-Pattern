## What the review found, and what the code said

Findings 6 and 7.

**Seasons were half drawn.** One function, `seasonalColor`, tinted a plant by the month's number,
and nothing else about a plant changed with the season:

- A perennial stood at its July height on 21 April and was green on 21 October and in January's
  snow. Nothing in the viewer changes a herb's height by month.
- Trees went bare from November to March. **Shrubs and the hedge-sized saskatoon in the review
  lot's front bed never did**: `buildShrubLayer` had no bare rule, so a shrub stood in the snow in
  full brown leaf. Vines and pond plants each had their own copy of the rule.
- The months were the viewer's own (leaves April to October, snow November to March), typed into
  five chunks, while the app has held the region's frost dates in its pack since V3.25 and says
  them on Site Info, the sun path and the planting plan.
- **The October ground.** The meadow was tinted by multiplying a green texture by a straw colour,
  which gives dark olive, not straw. Since V3.45 a yard's ground is pale, so this now shows on a
  walk through a wild community and on a drawn meadow.

**The preview quietly kills the plan.** The line at the top has counted the losses since V3.43
("136 lost to shade"). It does not say where, which plants, or what shades them, and a person
looking at a thin bed cannot tell a plan that failed from a preview that is wrong.

## What V3.46 builds

### The season, decided once (`src/scene_season.py`)

Qt-free, called by `build_scene` after every plant's body is settled, additive fields, no
`SCENE_VERSION` bump. The dates come from the pack's frost dates (`region_climate.frost_dates`),
so a second region's pack moves them with no code change:

| | Rule | Edmonton region |
|---|---|---|
| Herbs start | 30 days before the last spring frost (no earlier than 45 before) | 15 April |
| Herbs at full height | 35 days after it, or two weeks into the plant's first month in flower if that is sooner | 19 June |
| Leaves open | from 7 days before the last spring frost to 21 days after | 8 May to 5 June |
| Leaves and herbs turn | from 14 days before the first fall frost | 2 September |
| Full autumn colour | 7 days after the first fall frost | 23 September |
| Leaves down, herbs dry | 28 days after the first fall frost | 14 October |
| Snow on the ground | November to March, the viewer's rule since V2.24 (the pack records no snow dates) | |

**Every offset is an estimate nobody recorded**, stated in the module as V3.45's fence heights
are, and the block carries its basis ("typical for the Edmonton region: last spring frost May 15,
first fall frost Sep 16") for the window to say. It is a typical year, not this year's weather.

Each plant that is not evergreen carries `season`:

- `look`: `dry` (last year's stems, standing), `young`, `green`, `turning`, `bare` (a woody
  plant with its leaves down) or `held` (a semi-evergreen's bronzed winter leaves).
- `t`: how far through `young` or `turning` it is.
- `up`: the share of this year's height it stands at, for a herb coming up in spring.

Rules a plant's own records move: **a plant in flower is up and is not dry** (Prairie Crocus in
April stands at flowering height while its neighbours are a hand high), and an evergreen carries
no block at all and is drawn as it always was.

### The viewer draws it (`html/scene3d/01d-season.js`)

A new chunk after `01c-leaves.js`. One function colours every plant body (`bodyColor`), one says
whether a woody plant is leafless (`leafless`), and `bodyHeightOf` multiplies by `up`, so the
flowers, the fruit and the seed heads sit on the plant as it is drawn (V2.88's rule). The
animals perch by the same figure (`scene_wildlife._drawn_height`). The trees, **the shrubs**, the
vines and the pond plants ask the one `leafless`. A scene with no `season` (the probes, anything
older) is drawn by the month rules as before.

Snow and the ground's state come from the scene too (`scene.season.snow`, `.ground`): the ground,
the surfaces, the roofs and the falling snow each had their own copy of "November to March".

**The October ground:** a dormant meadow is its own tawny texture, not the green one multiplied.

### The losses, said where and which (`src/scene_timeline.py`)

`scene.timeline_notes`, decided in Python from the shade model's own results, and a chip in the
picture that opens them:

- One note per place: the drawn bed (or lawn, or other area) the plants are in, else "elsewhere in
  the yard". *"46 of the 56 plants in the garden bed at the back are shaded out by the fifth
  summer, most of them by the evergreen tree already here."* Then the kinds and how many of each.
- **What shades them** is asked of the shade model (`SuccessionEngine.main_shader`: the one caster
  that accounts for most of a plant's shade in the year it died), and named as the scene knows
  it: a tree already here, the house, a building next door, a tree the plan itself planted.
- Front and back are said only where the street's side is known (V3.45's rule for the street
  itself); otherwise the compass.
- Short-lived kinds that have lived out their years get a note of their own, which is not a
  fault and says so. So do the gaps that self-seeders have filled.
- The chip rings each lost plant's spot on the ground while its note is open.
- **It is the model's estimate and says so**, with what the model reads: the growing plants, the
  trees and buildings on the map, the plant's recorded need for sun.
