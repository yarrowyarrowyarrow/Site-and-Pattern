## What the review found, and what the code said

Findings 6 and 7.

**Seasons were half drawn.** One function, `seasonalColor`, tinted a plant by the month's number,
and nothing else about a plant changed with the season:

- A perennial stood at its July height on 21 April and was green on 21 October and in January's
  snow. Nothing in the viewer changed a herb's height by month.
- Trees went bare from November to March. **Shrubs never did**: `buildShrubLayer` had no bare
  rule, so the saskatoon in the review lot's front bed stood in the snow in full brown leaf. Vines
  and pond plants each had their own copy of the rule.
- The months were the viewer's own (leaves April to October, snow November to March), typed into
  five chunks, while the app has held the region's frost dates in its pack since V3.25 and says
  them on Site Info, the sun path and the planting plan.
- **The October ground.** The meadow was tinted by multiplying a green texture by a straw colour,
  which gives dark olive, not straw. Since V3.45 a yard's ground is pale, so this showed on a walk
  through a wild community and on a drawn meadow.

**The preview quietly kills the plan.** The line at the top has counted the losses since V3.43
("136 lost to shade"). It did not say where, which plants, or what shades them, and a person
looking at a thin bed could not tell a plan that failed from a preview that is wrong.

## What asking "what shades them?" found

The notes needed the shade model to say which caster shades each lost plant, so it was given that
answer (`SuccessionEngine.main_shader`) and asked it of the review lot's 136 plants lost by the
fifth summer. The review had read them as shade from the trees ("46 of 56 in the bed under the
spruce"). The model said otherwise:

| What shaded the plant most | Plants lost by the fifth summer |
|---|---|
| The garage, 3.4 m tall | 82 |
| A tall wildflower beside it (Fireweed, the two asters) | 54 |
| Either existing tree | 0 |

**A building was a circle.** The model read a building as its `canopy_radius_m`, which for a
scanned building is the circle out to its farthest corner (`osm_features.ring_radius_m`), and
counted any plant inside that circle as standing *under* it, in the 90% shade of a tree's crown,
all day. So a plant up to 2.8 m outside the garage's walls on any side, the sunny south side
included, was under the garage. V3.43 found the walker stopped by the same circle and gave him the
walls; the shade model kept the circle. The 2D shade map has always cast a building's real outline.

**A wildflower was a spruce.** A plant's shade is weighted by its foliage: a deciduous crown gets a
break for the months it is bare, and "everything else" is opaque all year, on the rule that unknown
is not transparent. *Herbaceous* is not unknown. A fireweed stalk, which is not above ground from
October to May, shaded the strawberries at its foot as an evergreen would, and the plan's own low
fill (which Plan My Yard plants under and between the tall plants on purpose) was the model's
commonest victim.

**The spruce shaded nothing** because Plan My Yard already keeps plants out from under an existing
tree's crown.

So both were put right in the model, where they were wrong:

- A building with an outline casts **that outline dragged down-sun by its shadow's length**
  (`in_swept_ring`), once per plant and building since neither grows. Nothing stands under a
  building. A building marked as a point has only a size and keeps its circle.
- A herbaceous plant gets the leaf-off break a deciduous crown gets (0.6 of an evergreen's
  shade). Evergreen, semi-evergreen and unknown stay opaque.

| Review lot, 365 plants | Lost to shade before | After |
|---|---|---|
| By the fifth summer | 136 (37%) | **14 (4%)** |
| By the tenth | 184 (50%) | **39 (11%)** |
| By the twenty-fifth | 212 (58%) | **67 (18%)** |

The 14 are all in the bed north of the house, in the house's real shadow: that is the loss 3D-5
is for. The later ones are tall wildflowers slowly crowding full-sun plants over ten to
twenty-five years, which is a thing that happens.

## What V3.46 built

### The season, decided once (`src/scene_season.py`)

Qt-free, called by `build_scene` after every plant's body is settled, additive fields, no
`SCENE_VERSION` bump. The dates come from the pack's frost dates (`region_climate.frost_dates`),
so a second region's pack moves them with no code change:

| | Rule | Edmonton region |
|---|---|---|
| Herbs start | 30 days before the last spring frost (never more than 45) | 15 April |
| Herbs at full height | 35 days after it, or two weeks into the plant's first month in flower if that is sooner | 19 June |
| Leaves open | from 7 days before the last spring frost to 21 days after | 8 May to 5 June |
| Leaves and herbs turn | from 14 days before the first fall frost | 2 September |
| Full autumn colour | 7 days after the first fall frost | 23 September |
| Leaves down, herbs dry | 28 days after the first fall frost | 14 October |
| Snow on the ground | November to March, the viewer's rule since V2.24 (the pack records no snow dates) | |

**Every offset is an estimate nobody recorded**, stated in the module as V3.45's fence heights
are. The scene carries the sentence that says so ("The seasons are drawn as typical for the
Edmonton region: last spring frost May 15, first fall frost Sep 16. A typical year, not this
year's weather."), and the window shows it on the Season list. Outside the provinces a pack covers
the default pack's dates still draw the picture and the sentence says no dates are known for the
place.

The window always asks for the 21st of the month, so what a person sees is:

| | Jan–Mar | Apr | May | Jun–Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov–Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Herbs | dry stems | a hand high | most of the way up | green | turning | dry stems | dry stems |
| Woody plants | bare | bare | young leaves | green | autumn colour | bare | bare |
| Ground | snow | waking | green | green | turning | dormant | snow |

Each plant that is not evergreen carries `season`: `look` (`dry`, `young`, `green`, `turning`,
`bare`, or `held` for a semi-evergreen's bronzed winter leaves), `t` (how far through `young` or
`turning`), and `up` (the share of this year's height a herb coming up stands at). Its
`height_m` is untouched: a dozen things read it, and the views must not move with the month.

Rules a plant's own records move: **a plant in flower is up and is not dry** (Prairie Crocus in
April stands at flowering height while its neighbours are a hand high; an aster still in flower in
October has not gone to straw). An evergreen carries no block and is drawn as it always was; so
does a cactus.

### The viewer draws it (`html/scene3d/01d-season.js`)

A new chunk after `01c-leaves.js`, and the one place the viewer reads the season:

- `bodyColor` colours every plant body; `leafless` says whether a woody plant's leaves are down,
  and **the trees, the shrubs, the vines and the hedges all ask it**; `diedBack` hides what floats
  on a pond.
- `bodyHeightOf` is now `fullBodyHeightOf` (V2.88's answer, in `04-quality.js`) times the
  season's share, so the flowers, the fruit and the seed heads sit on the plant as it is drawn.
  The animals perch by the same two numbers (`scene_wildlife._drawn_height`).
- `seasonSnow` and `seasonGround` answer for the ground, the surfaces, the roofs and the falling
  snow, each of which had its own "November to March".
- The old month rules live on **here only**, for a scene that does not say (the probes, anything
  older). A test fails on a month comparison in any other chunk.

**A dormant meadow and a just-thawed lawn are their own paintings.** A multiply can only darken:
tan cannot be made from green.

A grass keeps its seed head on its dry stems through the winter; the month gate had stopped it at
December.

### The losses, said where and which (`src/scene_timeline.py`, `html/scene3d/25b-timeline.js`)

`scene.timeline_notes`, decided in Python from the shade model's own results:

- One note per **place**: the drawn area the plants are in, else the rest of the yard. *"14 of the
  108 plants in the garden bed at the back (the south-east one) are shaded out by the fifth summer, by
  the house. 26 more are struggling in the shade."* Then the kinds and how many of each.
- **What shades them** is the model's answer, named as the scene knows the thing: a tree already
  here, the house, a building in the yard, the building next door, a tree the plan planted.
- Front and back only where the street's side is known (V3.45's rule for drawing the street);
  otherwise the compass. Two beds that would get the same words are told apart, and a bed keeps its
  name whichever beds lost plants.
- Short-lived kinds that have lived out their years get a note that says it is how they grow. So
  do the gaps the plan's own plants have since seeded into.
- It says it is the app's estimate and what it reads, and what to do: click one of the plants on
  the map and choose Swap.

In the picture, a button under the line at the top (*Show where, and which plants*) opens the list
and draws a ring on the ground at every spot a note is about: amber for shade, grey for
short-lived, green for a gap since filled. Pressing a note rings only its spots. The list and the
rings survive a slider move, which rebuilds the scene.

## Corrections made while building

- **The shade model was the larger half of finding 6.** The handover had this increment say what
  the preview shows and left what it shows to 3D-5. What it showed was wrong for two reasons that
  have nothing to do with the planner, found only because the notes had to name a cause.
- **"The bed by the spruce"** was the handover's example sentence. No plant on the review lot is
  lost to the spruce.
- **A lawn or a meadow cannot be made dormant by a tint.** The first build tinted, as V3.45 did;
  the April lawn stayed summer green beside bare shrubs.
- **A bed's name moved between years.** With two beds at the back and losses in one, it was "the
  garden bed at the back" in the fifth summer and "(the south-east one)" in the tenth. Every drawn area
  is named once.
- **The two new tiles ran the sheet's page out of time**: only the first sheet came back. The
  script's budget is doubled. And `--tiles` wrote a tile named `yard` over the sheet named `yard`.
- Three of the new shade-model tests were written expecting exactly 0.9 under an evergreen; a
  plant under a crown and in its shadow at every sampled hour reads 1.0, as it always has.

## What is left alone

- **Last year's stems are not drawn beside the new shoots in April.** The app's advice is to cut
  back in spring, and the picture is of a bed where that was done.
- **A herb coming up is its summer self at a share of its height**, not a spring form of its own.
- **One calendar for every plant**, moved only by its months in flower and its foliage. A
  warm-season grass comes up later than a cool-season one, an oak holds brown leaves into winter,
  a tamarack turns after the aspens: none of it is in the catalogue.
- **Snow is November to March** by the viewer's old rule, now said in one place.
- **A hedge** has no plants of its own to ask and follows the general rule for leaves.
- The line at the top still says "Fifth summer · October".
- **The shade model's other simplifications**: every growing plant casts as a tapering crown, a
  point building is a circle, nine sun moments stand for the season, and a plant's own height is
  not in the test. And **Plan My Yard still plans with no shade to read** (3D-5).
- The notes are in the 3D picture only: not in the Overview, the PDF or the 2D timeline, and Swap
  is said, not offered as a button (the picture has no bridge for it).
- Light (3D-6), the trees and herbs themselves (3D-7), the laptop (3D-8).

## Not verified

- **A real graphics card, Windows, macOS and frame rate.** The gates and the sheets are headless
  Chromium drawing in software. The season adds no per-frame work but one read by the falling
  snow; the rings are one small mesh per note, built per scene push.
- **The button and the list inside the app's own window.** They were pressed in headless Chromium
  and in a browser by script. Inside QtWebEngine, with a real mouse and with a screen reader, they
  were not: the window's test checks the Season list's tooltip and that the scene is pushed.
- **Whether a person reads the notes as meant.** Nobody but the author has read them.
- **Whether the shade model is now right, as against less wrong.** Two faults were found because a
  cause was asked for and the answer was absurd. The model was not compared with a measured yard,
  and its other simplifications are listed above.
- **A pin outside the provinces** draws by the default pack's dates and says no dates are known:
  unit-tested, not looked at.
- **The Flyover** was not run. Its one frame captioned "spring green" on 21 March, which is under
  snow, is now April; a test holds its captions to the season.
