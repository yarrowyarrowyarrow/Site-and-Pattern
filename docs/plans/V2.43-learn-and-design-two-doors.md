# V2.43 — Learn and Design become two doors

## Context

The app currently opens onto one thing: a map, six tabs, and the whole
professional toolset. The author's report:

> *"having someone start with all of the features of the site and then have
> a design it and then getting into the 3D view is just a little bit too
> complicated at the moment."*

and

> *"I'd love the design side to be sort of professional, and that's where you
> go once you understand the basics and you wanna design a landscape."*

So: **the boot screen becomes a game main menu that delineates Learn from
Design.** Learn is where a beginner lands — fun, gamified, and made of real
landscapes you can genuinely plant into. Design is today's app, unchanged,
behind its own door, for when you know what you're doing.

There is one prior decision to argue past. `docs/plans/V2.42-first-user-on-the-relationship-web.md`
explicitly says:

> **What I would not do:** ship a top-level Design/Learn toggle that hides half
> the app. … A mode split that makes a beginner choose "am I designing or
> learning?" before they have a yard drawn asks a question they cannot answer yet.

That objection is about a **mid-session toggle**. This is not one. It is a door
at boot, freely re-openable, and the question it asks is *"do you want to learn
about this, or design your yard?"* — which a beginner can answer on day one,
unlike *"am I designing or learning?"* mid-flow. The V2.42 warning stands
against the thing it named; this is a different shape. Recorded so the reversal
reads as a decision, not an oversight.

---

## The key architectural move

**Learn mode carries a real project: the reference landscape.**

`src/reference_ecosystem.build_reference_project()` already returns a genuine
GeoJSON FeatureCollection of `plant_feature()` records — the same shape the
user's own design uses. It is a real project that nothing currently lets you
edit.

Make it editable, and every design-coupled teaching tool in the repo binds to
it with **zero rewrites**, because all of them already take `placed_plants` as
a plain argument:

| Module | Takes | Works on the sandbox immediately |
|---|---|---|
| `src/lesson_track.py` | `placed_plants, structures` | ✅ |
| `src/plant_impact.py` (F46 pull-a-plant) | placed plants | ✅ |
| `src/habitat_score.py` | placed plants | ✅ |
| `src/docent.py` | `placed_plants` | ✅ |
| `src/chickadee_scenario.py` | placed plants | ✅ |
| `src/field_study.py` | an arbitrary plant list | ✅ |
| `src/scene_wildlife.py` | a built scene | ✅ |

This also answers V2.41's rejection of catalogue-wide lessons (*"a catalogue-wide
mode is real work and half of it would be worse than none"*) — the lessons are
not catalogue-wide. They are about the fen you just planted.

## Backlog — the further work, in the order I'd do it

### A. Creature realism (the flight problem)

The author's report: birds *"are just gliding as if along a zip line"*,
butterflies should flap *"fast, fast, slow"*, and it *"should be mathematically
correct"*.

**Why it looks wrong today.** `html/scene3d/07-wildlife.js:49` `flapWings` is
a good function fed by hardcoded per-taxon constants — `speed` is a raw
multiplier on the rAF millisecond clock, **not Hz, and not derived from any
data**. Worse, the *body path* and the *wings* are completely decoupled: the
bird's motion is `c.pos.lerp(tgt, dt * 3.0)` (`07-wildlife.js`, the `perch`
branch), an exponential ease toward a waypoint. That decoupling **is** the
zipline.

**The fix has three layers.**

1. **Beat frequency from morphology, not magic numbers.** Pennycuick (1996) for
   birds: `f = m^(3/8) · g^(1/2) · b^(-23/24) · S^(-1/3) · ρ^(-3/8)`. Greenewalt
   (1962) / Deakin (2010) allometry for insects. **Honesty note (P9):** for a
   11 g chickadee with a 203 mm span the formula gives **~13 Hz**, while
   published measurements are **25–30 Hz** — it is calibrated for cruising
   flight of larger birds and underestimates small passerines in bounding
   flight. So: seed measured values where they exist, fall back to the formula,
   and band both. Do not ship a number the formula cannot support.
2. **Bout structure — this is the author's "fast fast slow".** A second
   envelope on top of the beat: `burst_beats` + `pause_s`. Butterflies flap-glide;
   chickadees fly in **bounding flight** (a burst of ~5–8 beats, then wings
   fully folded and a ballistic dip); bees are continuous with no bouts.
3. **Couple the body to the wings.** The bounding dip and the glide sink must
   come from the flight state, not from a lerp. Any new movement branch **must**
   call `critterHeading()` — `tests/test_wildlife_heading.py` greps for
   hand-rolled headings and fails the build.

**Rendering honesty:** at 30–60 fps a 200 Hz bumblebee wingbeat is unrenderable
and aliases into nonsense. Above ~40 Hz, draw a swept blur arc instead of a
discrete wing — which is also what the eye actually sees. A P5 point, not a
compromise.

**Data needed.** Bees have `body_length_mm` (69 rows); Lepidoptera have
`wingspan_min/max_mm` and `flight_style` (31/31 rows, complete). **Birds have
no attributes table at all** — 24 species in `fauna_master.json`. So:

- New `bird_morphology` table + `data/bird_morphology_master.json`:
  `scientific_name, mass_g, wingspan_mm, wing_area_cm2, flight_style, source`.
  ~24 rows, sourceable from the CRC Handbook of Avian Body Masses and Cornell
  Birds of the World. **This is the one thing I'd ask you to verify rather than
  have me estimate.**
- A `flight_style` column for bees (they have none).
- Two fields already reach JS and are **read by nothing**: `resting_posture`
  (`critterInfo.rest`) and `wing_shape` (only used to pick the swallowtail GLB).
  Wing aspect ratio → beat rate, and posture → the settled pose, are both free.
- **Two flap tables must stay in sync**: `06-fly.js:174/239` (procedural) and
  `09-models.js:291` (`_GLB_CRITTER`). Drive both from one table or they drift.

New chunk `html/scene3d/16-flight.js` (`07-wildlife.js` is 698/800).

**Creature models.** The bee is spheres plus two flat `CircleGeometry` discs;
the bird is spheres, a cone beak and a box tail. Making these look real is
Blender work through `scripts/blender/assetlib/fauna.py`, and it is its own
increment. The morphology data to colour them per-species already exists.

## What changed while building it

Four things the plan got wrong, recorded because a plan nobody can learn from
is worse than none.

1. **`src/learn_flow.py` was not in the plan.** The Learn wiring took
   `onboarding_flow.py` to **646 of its 620-line ceiling**, and that guard says
   in its own comment that the fix is "extraction, never raising the number
   without a split plan". So the Learn-mode glue — the second screen, the
   lessons window, the sandbox and coverage readers — moved into its own flow
   module. The seam is clean: the cold-start path and the Learn path share only
   the start screen. The guard did exactly the job it was written for.
2. **The Learn menu's third door is the lessons, and they cost almost
   nothing.** `LearnPanel` turned out to be a self-contained 115-line
   `QWidget` fed by two provider callables, with no `MainWindow` dependency at
   all — so Learn mode hosts it directly (`LearnStudioWindow`) instead of
   opening the whole app to reach one side tab. This is the plan's central
   claim actually paying out.
3. **`pull_consequence` does not write its own sentence.** The first cut had
   one. `plant_impact.pull_plant_impact` already returns a `verdict` that is
   tested and honest about redundancy ("*Pulling one Balsam Poplar costs
   nothing here — 1 more still feed the same species. That redundancy is
   resilience.*"), so the module now returns that. The app must not grow two
   answers to "what does pulling this cost", and a test pins it.
4. **A QWebChannel signal must not share a slot's name.** `inspected` as both
   signal and slot shadowed the slot on the JS side with a connect/disconnect
   object that is not callable. The signal is `species_inspected`; the slot
   keeps the name the viewer calls.

Schema was at **61**, not the 58 the CLAUDE.md notes implied — bumped to **62**.
Roadmap IDs landed as **F96/F97/F98** (F95 was the next free one).

### What the run actually said

**2,549 tests, OK, 10 skipped.** PyQt6, PyQt6-WebEngine and the Qt runtime libs
were installed first, then numpy / shapely / rasterio / pyproj / Pillow — an
increment that is almost entirely widget code proves nothing behind a green
suite full of skips. The remaining 10 are `bpy` (Blender), a root-permissions
case, and one import-order guard. **Zero Qt or WebEngine skips**, so every
widget test ran.

`tests/test_undo_redo.py` still segfaults mid-run (139) and is excluded from
that count, per the CLAUDE.md runbook. Verified against a `git stash`ed tree:
**identical exit 139 without this diff**, so it is the GPU-less container, not
the change.

**What I could not verify here:** anything needing a real desktop or a GPU —
the walk-mode click-to-plant raycast, the QWebChannel round trip, whether the
ghost marker lands under the cursor, and how the palette bar looks. Those are
guarded by text-level contract tests (the chunk is loaded, the mapping is
written down, the viewer carries no placement logic, the slot name is not
shadowed) but the actual click has never been performed. It needs a hand-check
on a real machine, and the click path for it is in the hand-back.
