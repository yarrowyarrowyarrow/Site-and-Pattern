# V2.68 — twenty-four is too many for one list

V2.67 adopted the surveyed layer and left two decisions open, both of them the
kind that change what gets recommended, so neither was made unasked. The author
made both in one sentence:

> *"I want the drop down to have tabs subtabs and subsubtabs to account for the
> 3 levels. This way we don't have one overwhelming menu with 24 items. As for
> the heuristic tags, I want it to reflect reality as best as possible."*

Two asks that look independent and are not. The second is only survivable
because of the first, and that is the whole reasoning of this increment.

---

## The connection, stated up front

The old vocabulary had six regions. The new one has twenty-four. Every
`ab_ecoregion` tag in `data/plants_master.json` names one of the old six, of
which exactly one key survives, so 431 of 432 species were carrying a tag
pointing at a region that no longer exists.

There are only two ways to move a tag when the target has split, and before
this increment both were bad:

- **Fan it out.** `boreal_mixedwood` becomes all nine Boreal Plains ecoregions.
  This asserts nine records where the evidence supports one — P9 failing
  through the back door, and the exact thing V2.67's schema note refused.
- **Drop it.** Honest, and throws away every tag that was merely coarse rather
  than wrong.

The three-level tree adds the option that was missing: **keep the claim at the
level its evidence actually supported.** A tag that cannot name one ecoregion
can still name the ecozone, and the filter will match it. That is not a
consolation prize, it is the correct precision — and it did not exist as a
representable value until the author asked for the hierarchy.

---

## The tags: measured, not decided

`scripts/migrate_ecoregion_tags.py`. The obvious approach is to put the two
name lists side by side and write the mapping down. **The V2.67 plan did
exactly that, and got one of four right.** It is worth quoting, because it
reads as confident as any of these documents:

> *"Four of the six old keys are clean renames the survey made
> (`mixedgrass_prairie` is Mixed Grassland, `moist_mixedgrass` is Moist Mixed
> Grassland, `fescue_foothills` is Fescue Grassland, `aspen_parkland` is
> unchanged)."*

So the mapping comes from the polygons instead. The old file is still in git;
the script reads it out of `V2.66`, intersects each old region with the
surveyed layer in an equal-area projection, and asks how the old claim actually
distributes. Same discipline the pipeline used to join Alberta's subregions to
ELC — **spatial overlap, never name matching** — and for the same reason.

What it found:

| old key | best ecoregion | best ecozone | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mixedgrass_prairie` | **mixed_grassland 70%** | — | → `mixed_grassland` |
| `moist_mixedgrass` | moist_mixed_grassland 39% | **Prairies 92%** | → `zone_prairies` |
| `fescue_foothills` | fescue_grassland 42% | **Prairies 74%** | → `zone_prairies` |
| `aspen_parkland` | aspen_parkland 40% | Boreal Plains 54% | **cleared** |
| `boreal_mixedwood` | mid_boreal_uplands 22% | Boreal Plains 54% | **cleared** |
| `subalpine_montane` | western_alberta_upland 19% | Boreal Plains 22% | **cleared** |

The rule: take the ecoregion when one accounts for two thirds of the old area,
else the ecozone on the same test, else clear. Two thirds rather than a half,
because at a half a region splitting 51/49 gets recorded as one of them, which
is a coin toss wearing a measurement's clothes.

**Three of the six were misplaced, not coarse**, and that is the finding that
mattered. `aspen_parkland` survived as a key and looked like the one safe
rename in the whole set — its own polygon only overlaps the real Aspen Parkland
by 40%; the traced arc sits too far north, with a third of it in Boreal
Transition. `subalpine_montane` was drawn east of the mountains it is named
after and barely touches them.

For those three the ecozone does not rescue the tag, it **disguises the error**.
`subalpine_montane` would become Boreal Plains — not a coarse description of an
alpine plant but a wrong one — and `aspen_parkland` would become Boreal Plains
at 54% against 46% Prairies, a coin toss reported as a fact.

So they are cleared. 431 → **384 species keep a tag; 47 lose their last one**,
and 389 tag values were rewritten. That cost is small and was going to be paid
anyway: `scripts/seed_ecoregion_ranges.py` derives ranges for 427 of the 432
species straight from occurrence records against the correct polygons —
*evidence*, where these were inference — and the heuristic tag has always been
the weaker fallback those rows override.

## The tree: what it is and where the argument is

`src/ecoregion_tree.py`, Qt-free, ~280 lines, and it declares nothing. The
three levels and every parent/child link are **read from the shipped polygon
file**, which has carried all three since V2.67 adopted it:

```
ecozone      6   the physiographic system   Boreal Plains
ecoregion   24   the unit inside it         Mid-Boreal Uplands
subregion   21   Alberta's own finer split  Central Mixedwood
```

Built from the file rather than beside it for the reason `src/ecoregion.py`
already gives at length: the polygon file *is* the vocabulary, and a second
copy is a second thing to keep in step. The one hand-transcribed copy that did
exist was already wrong about Interlake Plain's ecozone.

Twenty-one Alberta natural subregions — Dry Mixedgrass, Northern Fescue, Upper
Foothills — become selectable for the first time. They were in the data from
the day of adoption and nothing had ever offered them.

**The one rule worth arguing about: matching runs along a lineage, both ways.**
Filtering for Mid-Boreal Uplands also returns plants tagged only "Boreal
Plains", and filtering for Boreal Plains returns anything tagged with a region
inside it.

Downward is definitional. Upward is the judgement call, and it is the direction
P9 points: a plant tagged only at the ecozone is one whose evidence was never
finer than that, so whether it grows in *this* ecoregion is **unknown, not no**.
Hiding it silently drops a probably-suitable native from a search whose entire
purpose is finding natives. Showing it risks one that turns out not to belong.
For a planting recommendation that is the better error.

It is also what makes the migration above worth anything: match only exactly,
and the 304 species now tagged `zone_prairies` would answer no query at all.

## Three bugs the work surfaced

**A key collision the brief had warned about, one namespace further along.**
"Athabasca Plain" is an ELC *ecoregion* on the Boreal Shield in Saskatchewan
**and** an Alberta natural *subregion*, and they are different ground. The
pipeline was careful to join the two frameworks spatially rather than by name
— and then the first cut of `ecoregion_tree` slugged both to `athabasca_plain`
and collided them anyway. Symptom: asking for the lineage of Mid-Boreal Uplands
returned a Boreal Shield region, because walking down to the subregion and back
up reached the ecoregion of the same name. Fixed with `zone_` / `sub_` prefixes
on the two derived levels; ecoregion keys keep their bare form because they are
already in the polygon file, in every species tag and in the website's URLs.

**44 of 115 polygon pieces were slivers** — Aspen Parkland × Subalpine at
17.6 km², an artefact of the spatial join's edges rather than a real place.
Blanking the *label* rather than the geometry drops 19 phantom subregions
without punching holes in the map.

**`where` was computed per piece**, so Aspen Parkland — the central arc — was
labelled "south AB / SK" off whichever fragment sorted first. Computed from the
dissolved region it reads "central AB / SK", which is where it is.

## Four tests that were green and should not have been

Retiring a vocabulary is a good way to find out which tests were only ever
agreeing with themselves. All four of these passed for the whole of V2.67.

**The caption on 432 public pages.** `ecoregion_map.CAVEAT` read *"Approximate
extents, not surveyed boundaries: the regions are hand-traced ... the outlines
are a diagram"*, and it kept reading that after V2.67 replaced the hand-traced
outlines with the National Ecological Framework. Its test asserted the literal
string `"Approximate extents"`, so it passed by construction. The About page
said the same thing in its own words, with its own passing test.

Understating a source is not the safe direction of error it looks like. A
reader told the outline is a sketch has no reason to trust it at the edge,
which is the only place an outline is load-bearing. Both captions now name the
survey and disclose the ~900 m simplification, and both tests were rewritten to
compare the caption against **the polygon file's own provenance line** rather
than against a remembered string — so the next source change fails loudly in
either direction instead of publishing a stale claim.

**Two filter tests that could not fail.** `test_every_result_claims_the_region
_it_was_found_under` and `test_a_multi_select_is_the_union_of_its_parts`
filtered on `boreal_mixedwood` and `mixedgrass_prairie`. A dead key matches
nothing, `for p in []` runs no assertions, and `set() | set() == set()`, so
both reported green over an empty result set. They now read the live keys off
the data instead of hard-coding them, with a floor test underneath.

**A fixture testing the same path twice.** `TestSeedingIntoTheCatalogue` fed
the seeder a `boreal_mixedwood` row, which the seeder had been correctly
rejecting since adoption — by exactly the rule the fixture's own `"atlantis"`
case exists to prove. So the fixture tested the rejection path twice and the
acceptance path never.

And two more in the publication render, found by running it rather than by a
test, since nothing tests a picture:

**`render --from-shipped` stamped PLACEHOLDER on the surveyed layer.** The flag
had been treated as a synonym for "placeholder geometry", which it was while
the shipped file held six traced shapes. It now asks the file's own provenance
line, the same source the caption test uses.

**The legend ran through the credit.** Three columns was tuned against six
regions; twenty-four is eight rows, and the last row of names printed on top of
the first line of the source attribution — losing a region name and a citation
together. Columns are now derived from the entry count against a six-row
budget, so the legend widens instead of growing down.

## The blast radius was wider than the filter

The first pass fixed the filter and the tags and ran the tests I thought were
involved. The full suite found nine more failures in five modules I had not
looked at, and the pattern in all of them is the same: **a hand-typed copy of
the vocabulary, written when there were six regions.**

| Where | What it did | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `site_facets._ecoregion_options` | 6 values; `zone_prairies` rendered as an *unlabelled* sidebar entry for 304 species | derived from the tree, ecozone then its ecoregions |
| `site_facets._ecoregions` | raw tags only, so ticking an ecozone missed everything tagged below it | expands **upward** into the row |
| `static_site_render` sidebar | drew every declared option; the hub pages had always pruned empty ones, so the two disagreed | prunes on the same rule |
| `pattern_language._ECOREGION_LABEL` | 7 keys; a plant in any other region contributed *nothing* to the generated prose | `ecoregion_display` |
| `llm_design._ECOREGION_WORDS` | 7 keys; a site outside them scored no community preference and fell back to catalogue order | derived, and now matches on the ecozone too |
| `ecoregion.ecoregion_display` | knew ecoregions only, so callers guarding on `name == key` treated every ecozone as unknown | covers all three levels |

None of these raised. Every one degraded into silence — a blank sidebar label,
a missing clause, a blander generated design — which is why the tests that
should have caught them were the vacuous ones described above.

The rule that would have prevented all of it is the one `src/ecoregion.py` has
stated since V2.38 and that adoption relied on: **the polygon file is the
vocabulary.** Six modules kept private copies anyway, and each was correct on
the day it was written.

### Left as a naming collision, deliberately

`reference_ecosystem.py` and `reference_ecosystem_window.py` key six curated
Learn-mode communities on the *old* strings — `mixedgrass_prairie`,
`boreal_mixedwood`, `subalpine_montane`. Checked and **not** changed:

They resolve their plants **by genus**, never by ecoregion tag, so the
reference landscapes work exactly as before, and the only other consumer
(`graduation.py`) copies the key as a provenance label rather than filtering on
it. What they actually are is *community archetypes* — "mixedgrass prairie" is
a vegetation type, real independently of which polygon it falls in — that
happen to reuse ecoregion key strings.

Renaming them to surveyed keys would be the name-matching this whole increment
exists to refuse, and it would break the `reference_ecoregion` recorded in
already-saved Learn projects. The honest fix is to give the archetypes their
own namespace, which is a scoped piece of work rather than a rider on this one.

## The state this leaves the app in, honestly

**Two of twenty-four regions currently match any plant** — `aspen_parkland`
(309, via the ecozone lineage) and `mixed_grassland` (29). Nothing in the
Boreal Plains, the Shield, the Taiga or the Cordillera answers a query at all.

That is the correct behaviour for the data as it stands and not a regression to
fix in code: the heuristic tags that used to answer those queries were the
misplaced ones, and the derived ranges that will answer them properly come from
`scripts/seed_ecoregion_ranges.py`, a multi-hour network job running on the
author's machine. Until it lands the filter says nothing rather than guessing,
which is the whole argument of the increment applied to itself.

**One test is deliberately red** — `test_every_derived_key_is_a_real_geographic
_ecoregion` — and it is the alarm for exactly this window. Everything
downstream of it *skips* with a message naming the command to run, because a
suite with a dozen expected reds is a suite nobody reads. The condition clears
itself when the derivation re-runs; no edit required.

### One thing in the window is wrong, not merely missing

Worth stating separately because it is the exception to "the filter says
nothing rather than guessing".

Five of the six old derived keys are **inert**: they match no filter, so the
species falls back to its migrated tag and nothing claims anything false.
`aspen_parkland` is the sixth, and it **survived by name while its polygon
moved** — the traced arc behind the existing derivation overlaps the surveyed
Aspen Parkland by only 40% and sits a third inside Boreal Transition.

So during the window, 309 species assert a parkland range computed against
ground that is substantially somewhere else, and they assert it at full
confidence. Scarlet Globemallow — a dry-prairie plant whose migrated tag reads
*Mixed Grassland, Prairies* — displays "Aspen Parkland, 65 records, high
confidence", because a derived row overrides the heuristic column.

Not fixable by translation, for the reason the whole increment argues. It is
fixed by the re-derivation, and `src.ecoregion_ranges.stale_keys()` is the
one-line way to ask whether you are still in the window:

```python
python -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'.'); \
from src.ecoregion_ranges import stale_keys; print(sorted(stale_keys()))"
```

Empty list means the derivation is current and everything above is over.

## What was deliberately left alone

**The 47 cleared species were not re-tagged by hand.** Sitting down with a
flora and assigning each one an ecoregion is a real piece of work and a
defensible one, but it is not this increment, and doing it inline would put
hand-authored judgements in the same field as measured ones with nothing
distinguishing them.

**Saskatchewan has no subregions in the tree.** Alberta publishes a subregion
layer and Saskatchewan does not. The asymmetry is honest; inventing one would
be inventing data.

**`plant_panel.py` was not given a bigger ceiling.** The tree builder took it
to 1605/1600 and the guard's own rule is to extract. `build_ecoregion_tree`
moved to `filter_widgets.py`, where the widget it drives already lives — so
every other multi-select filter gets hierarchy support for free instead of the
panel growing a private copy. `filter_widgets.py` and `ecoregion_tree.py` are
ceilinged on arrival, the V2.41 precedent.

## What could not be verified here

The suite runs headless in a container, so the dropdown was checked by
rendering it offscreen and reading the pixels back: 8 rows collapsed instead of
26, opening Prairies reveals 5 ecoregions, opening Mixed Grassland reveals
three Alberta subregions, disclosure markers present, subtitles aligned under
their labels. That is not the same as using it, and the feel of the thing — how
much indentation reads as "inside", whether the markers are big enough to hit —
needs a real desktop.

`scripts/seed_ecoregion_ranges.py` is a multi-hour network job and ran on the
author's machine, not here.
