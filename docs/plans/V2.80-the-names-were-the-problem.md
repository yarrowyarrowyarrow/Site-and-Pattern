# V2.80 — the names were the problem

*What the twenty unsourced species turned out to be, and what it cost to fix them.*

## Where this started

V2.80 landed VASCAN and 414 of 434 species gained
`native_provinces_source='flora'`. Twenty did not, and the site published *"Not
established"* on the sixteen of those with a page. The author's reading was that
those sixteen were probably non-native and should go:

> "They probably should not be included such as common yarrow (the local variety
> is boreal yarrow). Once this is resolved we can drop this line which simply
> introduces doubt."

That guess was right about the mechanism and wrong about the size. Sorting the
twenty produced **four different reasons**, and only five of them were the
introduced species the guess assumed.

| Reason | Count | What was done |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced to AB/SK per VASCAN | 4 | removed |
| Introduced, but the native race is already carried | 1 | merged |
| Filed under a name VASCAN does not accept | 8 | renamed |
| A duplicate of a row already in the catalogue | 1 | removed |
| Recorded by VASCAN, but not for these provinces | 2 | **open** |
| A fruit cultivar no flora will ever carry | 4 | **open** |

The eight renames are the headline. They were never a nativity problem at all:
*Urtica dioica*, *Viola canadensis*, *Mentha arvensis* and five others are
native here, and the lookup missed them because the catalogue filed them under a
name the flora does not accept. A rename clears
`native_provinces_source` deliberately, so re-running the archive under the
corrected name fills the provinces **and** the source from the archive itself,
rather than from a province list hand-copied out of a `--suggest` printout.

## What the removals actually cost

`remove_taxon.py` counts before it writes, which is the only reason the
following were caught rather than discovered later as missing data.

**Common Sunflower** (*Helianthus annuus*) took 113 edges and left **9 animals
with no plant relationship at all** — Red-winged Blackbird, Mourning Dove and
White-breasted Nuthatch among them. Those animals stay in the database and drop
off the website, which publishes only animals with a documented plant. It was
tempting to re-point the edges onto one of the three native *Helianthus* the
catalogue carries; that was refused. Yarrow is a naming problem, so re-pointing
is a correction. *H. annuus* is a real distinct species, so re-pointing would
assert a relationship no source made.

**Yarrow** (*Achillea millefolium*) was the opposite case and merged into
*Achillea borealis*: 332 edges kept, nobody orphaned, each edge keeping its
original source and gaining `renamed_from`. It cost 30 hand-edited lines across
`polycultures.py`, `calendar_data.py`, `onboarding.py`, `sprite_gallery.py` and
two scripts.

**Stiff Goldenrod** was the one nobody asked for. *Solidago rigida* and
*Oligoneuron rigidum* had shipped as two rows under one common name since V2.69,
making opposite nativity claims on two published pages, allowlisted in
`data_quality.KNOWN_NATIVITY_CONFLICTS` with the reason *"waits on F137
(VASCAN)"*. The archive settled it: VASCAN treats *Oligoneuron* as a synonym of
*Solidago*, so the duplicate row went. **The allowlist is now empty** — kept as
a mechanism, because an entry that outlives the thing it excused turns a
temporary silence into a permanent one.

## Two mistakes worth recording

**The duplicate rows had different common names.** *Monarda fistulosa* was in
both `plants_master.json` and `garden_plants.json` — as *Wild Bergamot* and as
*Bee Balm (Wild Bergamot)*. Deleting the garden row orphaned **145 edges**,
because edges key on common name, and `validate-data` went from clean to **148
errors**. The fix was to re-point them and drop 135 exact duplicates, leaving
158. The lesson is narrow and sharp: *the duplicate to delete is the row, but the
key that has to survive is the name*, and here the two rows did not share one.

**Recording that removal made the guard fire on the survivor.** The exclusion
entry for *Oligoneuron rigidum* listed `common_names: ["Stiff Goldenrod"]`, and
`validate_excluded_taxa` maps every listed common name to the exclusion — so it
reported that *Solidago rigida* was "back in plants_master.json". For a merge
where the common name deliberately continues on another row, the name is not
excluded and must not be listed. The entry now says so in a note.

## What was deliberately not done

**The four fruit cultivars are still here.** The instruction was to delete them,
and the measurement changed the picture enough to hand back: they anchor a
seeded polyculture *named* "Evans Cherry Community" (the cherry is its centre
plant at offset 0,0), an apple guild with a shade-tolerant variant, the Wildlife
Corridor Hedge, and 48 planting-calendar rows. Nothing crashes — `_get_plant_by_name`
returns `None` and the seeder skips silently — which is the problem: the
communities would quietly hollow out.

The alternative costs nothing and was recommended instead: **the website
publishes a species only if its nativity has a source.** That is the author's own
"only facts backed by data" applied one level up. It drops the cultivars from
grownativeplants.ca automatically, keeps the fruit trees in a landscape design
tool where an apple is legitimately useful, needs no per-species flag, and
catches anything unsourced in future without anyone remembering to.

**Two species are recorded by VASCAN but not for AB or SK** — *Solidago
nemoralis* (Manitoba only) and *Spiraea douglasii* (a BC coastal species). Both
have plausible native counterparts already in the catalogue (*S. missouriensis*;
*Spiraea splendens* var. *rosea*, which is pink and is what "Pink Spirea"
probably means). Both would be a substitution rather than a rename, which is the
sunflower argument again, so both wait on the author.

## Where it leaves the count

Twenty unsourced species became **fourteen**, and eight of those are renames
that clear the moment the archive is re-read. After that run: **six** — the four
cultivars and the two absentees, all four reasons known and none of them a
mystery. The catalogue is 428 rows, down from 435.

## Tooling this added

* `scripts/rename_taxon.py` — reports first, re-keys the three
  scientific-name-keyed data files, warns when a rename can move a public URL
  (only when two species share a common name, since slugs key on that), and
  refuses to write nativity from a transcription.
* `scripts/warm_photo_cache.py` — one command for the 377 hotlinked
  photographs, so `build-site` bundles them.
* `scripts/fetch_flower_colour.py --columns` learned to read past a preamble
  line and to say outright whether a file has the two columns it needs. A real
  USDA export began `Search Type: Characteristic` with the header on line 2, so
  the probe reported one column for a four-column file — the same class of
  mistake the probe exists to catch.
