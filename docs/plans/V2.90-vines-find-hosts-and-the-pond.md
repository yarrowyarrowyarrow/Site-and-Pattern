# V2.90 — Vines placed where they can climb, and the pond

*Two items, both the owner's choice: **F181**, the follow-up V2.89 opened, and
**F175**, the next family on the audit's smallest-first list. The owner's
decision for F181, verbatim, choosing between a vine with nothing to climb
lying flat, being left out, or being placed with a note:*

> *"I choose c."*

*That is: place it, and say in the design notes that there is nothing for it
to climb.*

## F181 — The generator seats vines where they can climb

**What was wrong.** Since V2.89 a vine climbs the tree or shrub whose footprint
touches its own and otherwise lies on the ground. The generator had no rule
connecting the two: a vine group took the best free 6 m anchor cell like any
other group and was laid out in a row (`layout.default_layout_for` gives vines
the edging pattern). So a generated vine touched a host only by chance, and in
a generated design the V2.89 rule mostly showed vines lying flat.

**The rule.** Trees and shrubs are placed before vines (`_LAYER_ORDER`), so
when a vine group comes up, its possible hosts are already in the ground. Each
vine is seated at the foot of one of them:

- **At the base, not at the drip line.** The seat is 0.25 to 0.6 m from the
  host's centre (half its mature crown radius, clamped), inside the crown. That
  is where a clematis is planted to climb a shrub. It is also the only distance
  that keeps the vine touching its host at every age the timeline draws: a
  vine seated at the mature crown edge would be a metre from a young shrub, and
  lie flat in every year but the last.
- **On the sunny side first.** Bearings are tried south, then west, east,
  south-west, south-east, north-west, north-east, north. In Alberta a vine on
  the north side of its host climbs into shade.
- **Not smothering the host.** One vine per shrub under 2 m across, two on a
  bigger shrub or a tree, three on a tree over 6 m across. A shrub buried in
  vines is a design mistake, not a relationship.
- **Spread across hosts.** The host carrying the fewest vines wins, then the
  earlier-placed one, so a design always seats the same way.
- **A seat has to be free.** Inside the boundary, clear of keep-out, and at
  least 0.3 m from every other placed plant but the host.

**Option (c), when nothing can hold a vine.** With no tree or shrub in the
design, or every host full, the vine is placed as it was before, and the design
notes say so, naming the species and how many. The check is the 3D preview's
own answer rather than a second copy of the rule: `build_scene` at year 0
(maturity) runs V2.89's `vine_habit`, and any vine it draws on the ground is
reported. So the note can never disagree with the picture. It counts existing
trees and the shrubs inside communities, which the seating does not use (see
below). It also runs last, after the critic's repairs and the goal and wildlife
top-ups, which place any plant they add, a vine included, at a single spot in
the boundary.

**Left alone, on purpose:**

- **Existing trees are not seated at.** Their keep-out circle keeps planting
  off the ground under them, so the nearest seat is the drip line, metres from
  the trunk. The viewer then draws the vine running along the ground to the
  trunk. The note still counts an existing tree that a vine happens to touch.
- **Communities keep their authored layouts.** A seeded community is placed as
  a unit at its stored offsets.
- **Plant mixes, and the plants the critic and top-ups add.** They place their
  own way. The note catches any vine they leave with nothing to climb.

## F175 — The pond

**What the audit found.** Seventeen aquatics shared one reed tuft. V2.88 made
the small ones the right height, not the right shape. A survey of every
wetland row found 23, with five kinds of body between them:

| Body | Species | Drawn today |
|---|---|---|
| Floating leaves | Yellow Pond-lily, Floating Marsh-marigold, Ivy-leaved Duckweed | reed tuft |
| Submerged | Canada Waterweed, Spiked Water-milfoil, Sago Pondweed, Common Bladderwort | reed tuft, 0.1 to 0.5 m up in the air |
| Broad leaves on stalks | Broad- and Arum-leaved Arrowhead, Broad-leaved Water-plantain, Buckbean, Water Arum (a `wildflower`) | reed tuft; Water Arum as a grassy herb |
| Whorled spike | Common Mare's-tail | reed tuft |
| Reed | Cattail, Great Bulrush, Giant Bur-reed, Rat Root, Small Bottle Sedge | reed tuft (right) |

Plus Water Parsnip, an upright leafy umbellifer drawn as a reed tuft, and the
graminoid bulrushes, drawn as grasses, which is right.

**The water.** There is water in a scene only where a design has a pond
structure. `struct_pond.glb` draws it as an opaque sheet **10 cm above the
ground**, an axis-aligned ellipse 0.41 by 0.30 of the pond's size. So a lily pad
drawn at ground level inside a pond is under the water and invisible.

**Decisions.**

- **The body follows the habit (as V2.89 did with horsetails).** `growth_form`
  says what the plant's body is, and `plant_type` stays its habitat and layer.
  Two corrections to the data:
  - Waterweed, milfoil, pondweed and bladderwort were recorded `floating`, with
    the pond-lily. They are submerged plants: they live under the water and only
    their flowers, and sometimes their shoot tips, reach the surface. A new
    value, `submerged`.
  - Water Parsnip was recorded `emergent`, which is where it grows, not what it
    looks like. It is an erect leafy herb (`erect`) and is drawn as one.

  Both are standard descriptions of these species, not a flora read here.
- **Decided once, in Python.** `src/pond_habit.py` gives every wetland plant a
  `drawn` block in `build_scene`: its body, the water level under it, and how
  high it is drawn. The viewer draws the block, and `scene_wildlife` perches
  animals on it through V2.89's `drawn_frame`. That is the V2.88 lesson for the
  third time: the body, the flowers and the animals each read one number.
- **What a floating or submerged plant's recorded height means.** It is the
  length of stem under the water, like a vine's length, not how tall the plant
  stands. A floating plant is drawn at the water surface, with its flowers at
  most 5 cm above it. A submerged plant shows only what reaches the surface: a
  few shoot tips at the surface, and flower stalks up to 10 cm above it.
- **Inside a pond, on the water; outside, on the ground.** A floating or
  submerged plant whose position is inside a pond's water ellipse is drawn at
  that pond's surface. Anywhere else it is drawn at ground level, where a
  pond-lily in a dry bed is still visibly a pond-lily.
- **Gone in winter.** Floating and submerged bodies are not drawn from November
  to March: the pond is frozen and their leaves have died back or sunk.
  Emergents brown with the season, like every other herb.
- **Drawn, not baked, for the reason the horsetails were.** These are simple
  procedural shapes, Stylised draws the procedural path anyway, and a baked copy
  would be a second definition to keep in step. The reed tuft keeps its GLB.
- **`04-quality.js` has no room.** It was at 892 of its 900 lines. The pond
  gets its own chunk, `html/scene3d/23-pond.js`, and the routing in
  `04-quality.js` is kept to a few lines.
- **Schema v90**, because the seed data changed.

**Left alone:**

- The generator does not put aquatics in ponds. That is a design rule, like
  F181 was for vines.
- Pond-lily flowers are drawn by the flower layer as they are recorded
  (`globe`). Bladderwort's bladders and the cattail's spike are left alone, the
  spike being the flower layer's already.
- A pond on a slope: its water is level at the height of the ground under its
  centre, and a plant at its edge stands on its own ground.

## What changed on the way

1. **Mare's-tail came out as a scouring-rush.** Its unit is the horsetail
   builder's fourth, and the layer was built with a fixed count of three
   variants, taking the unit number modulo three: unit 3 wrapped to unit 0,
   the banded scouring-rush. Every height and part check passed, because both
   bodies are one part at the recorded height. The count is now the real number
   of units, and the render probe compares the two bodies' vertex counts (6,372
   each with the bug).
2. **A small pond is ringed by spikes.** The pond model scales its width with
   `size_m` but keeps the authored height of its stones, so the 1.5 m ponds
   first given to small species in the gallery wore 38 cm stone spikes, and the
   duckweed's frame was mostly a rock. Gallery ponds are now never smaller than
   the authored 6 m.
3. **Blades faced the ground.** A broad leaf's upper face was oriented down and
   out, and most arrowhead blades rendered nearly black. A leaf leaning outward
   faces up and in, and now does.
4. **`04-quality.js` got room first.** The horsetail unit picker V2.89 added
   there moved to `14-layers.js`, next to the builder it indexes. The file ends
   at 893 of 900 lines, with the pond in its own chunk.
5. **The scene contract had never documented `drawn`**, not even for V2.89's
   vines. It does now, for both.

  - The sprite gallery gains a test that each floating or submerged species is

- **Renders** (headless Chromium, software GL):

## Found, not fixed

- **The critic's repairs are all planted on one spot (F183).** The repair and
  top-up steps place every plant they add at `_one_position_in_boundary`, the
  boundary's first grid cell, so in a test design the three plants they added
  (Basket Willow, Buckbean, Maximilian Sunflower) stood on exactly the same
  point. They also ignore moisture: Buckbean, a pond plant, was added to a dry
  yard as a larval host. Both predate this increment. The vine note catches a
  vine they add; nothing catches the rest.
- **Buckbean reads thin.** Its leaf is recorded at 8 cm, a genus estimate, and
  drawn as the whole three-leaflet leaf, which is small for a plant whose
  leaflets alone can reach about 10 cm. A flora question, for F172.
- **The generator does not put aquatics in ponds**, as planned. A pond-lily
  placed in a dry bed lies on the grass.
