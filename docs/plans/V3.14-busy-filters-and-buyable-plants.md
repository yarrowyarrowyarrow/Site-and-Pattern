## 1. A second click on a filter reopened its list (F223)

**The cause is Qt on Windows, read off its source, not guessed.** In Qt 6.11
(`qwidgetwindow.cpp`, `qapplication.cpp`), a press that closes a popup is
*replayed* to the widget under the pointer when the platform says so.
`QPlatformIntegration::styleHint(ReplayMousePressOutsidePopup)` is `true` by
default, Windows keeps the default, and xcb returns `false`. Qt sets
`WA_NoMouseReplay` on a combo's popup for a press on a non-editable combo, and
for an editable one only on its arrow (`QComboBoxPrivateContainer::mousePressEvent`).
The filters are editable combos with a read-only line edit, and the line edit's
release opens the list. So on Windows a click on the box with the list open
closed it on the press, then the replayed press and its release reopened it, in
one motion.

**I could not find a regression in our code.** The line edit's release has
opened the list since at least V2.61 (history before that was squashed). V3.04
answered the first report by making a click on a *name* close the list. Its plan
said "Qt's popup consumes the click that closes it, as dropdowns on every
platform do". That is true on Linux and macOS and false on Windows, so the box
itself never closed there.

**The fix** sets `WA_NoMouseReplay` on the popup when the closing press is inside
the box, which is exactly what Qt does for a non-editable combo, the kind this
box looks like. Qt clears the flag before every press (`qapplication.cpp`:
`w->setAttribute(Qt::WA_NoMouseReplay, false)` before `notify_helper`), so it
holds for that one press. A press elsewhere is still replayed, so on Windows one
click still moves from one open filter to the next.

**How it is checked.**

- Real clicks under X, with `xdotool` against Xvfb. Eleven sequences pass both
  before and after: open, close by the box, open and close by the arrow, a name
  ticks and closes, a name unticks, a box ticks and stays open, close by
  clicking outside. X does not replay, so this shows the fix changes nothing
  that worked. It cannot show the Windows bug.
- `tests/test_filter_widgets.py` `TestASecondClickOnTheBox` does what Qt on
  Windows does next: unless the popup carries the flag, it sends the press and
  release again to the line edit. On the V3.13 widget the list reopens and the
  test fails. With the fix it passes. A second test checks that a press
  elsewhere is not spared. `test_a_ticked_name_clicked_again_is_unticked` covers
  the other way to read "unselect".
- One order-dependent failure of my own: an earlier module's events closed the
  first popup the next test opened. The test now drains events first, as its
  neighbours do.

## 2. Communities by where every plant is recorded (F224)

**Measured.** The habitat facet read `plants.ecoregion`, the column the
occurrence derivation superseded at schema v59. It names an ecoregion on 125 of
424 plants (Mixed Grassland on 123) and Aspen Parkland on none, while
`plant_ecoregions` records 336 species there. Top-level communities by habitat.
The middle column is the 68 communities V3.13 shipped under the new rule. The
last adds the five "Easy" ones of section 6, which are all Aspen Parkland.

**The rule** (`src/community_regions.py`): a community belongs in an ecoregion
when *every* member with a known range has been recorded there. A member whose
range is unknown counts neither way. Regions are ordered by the members'
records. I chose every member because the Edmonton switch (F220) asks the same
of a community, and a community is planted whole. I measured the softer rules
too: at two-thirds, 59 of 68 communities filed under Aspen Parkland, which is
no longer a filter. A member's range is what `search_plants` reads (the derived
rows, else the column's ecoregion tags), so the plant filter and the community
filter cannot disagree about one plant.

Seventeen communities have no region holding all their plants, nearly always
because of one plant (Spring Beauty in *Moist Parkland Hollow*, Maximilian
Sunflower in *Parkland Sunny Meadow*). They read **Mixed regions**, and a
community with no recorded member reads **Not recorded**. Both read
"Generalist" before, which claims the opposite. Riparian and wet meadow are
conditions, not places, and still come from any member's tags.

The habitat dropdown is now the plant filter's ecoregion tree, matched along the
lineage. It opens on 10 rows (six ecozones, two niches, two fallbacks) instead
of 27 flat labels. Ticking the Prairies ecozone shows 52 communities.

**Found on the way:** the Site tab's *Browse N reference communities for this
ecoregion* had never shown since V2.37. Its count compared the habitat list with
a set, which raised `TypeError: unhashable type: 'list'` into an
`except: return 0`. It now uses the library's own filter and reads 46 for Aspen
Parkland (45 communities filed there, and one listed as the heading of a
variation that is), the number the link opens on.

## 3. The plant filters in two tiers (F225)

Unfolded in the side panel, the picker showed nine dropdowns in five rows and
ten toggles in three. Now four dropdowns (Type, Sun, Water, Where to buy) and
four toggles (Native, Edmonton native, Easy to find, Pet safe) show first. The
other ten wait behind **More filters**, which reads "More filters ▸ 2 on" when
some of them are on. Any filter that is on is still a removable chip on the
line above. Rendered at the side panel's 437 px: four rows of filters instead
of eight, and the list starts about 110 px higher. The Plant Directory, a window
with room, opens with both tiers showing.

`src/filter_area.py` holds the tiers. `EVERYDAY` is one tuple, so moving a
filter between tiers is a one-word change. The choice is a judgment made for the
owner's aim of useful, available plants in real yards. *Restoring toward* is
past the fold because a dropped pin ticks it, and then it shows as a chip and in
the count.

The community panel kept its layout. Its habitat list got the tree (section 2),
which was the busiest of its five lists.

## 5. Where a plant can be bought (F226)

**What was wrong.** `availability_class` came from name keywords in
`scripts/apply_sourcing_data.py`. Any name containing "aster", "goldenrod",
"cinquefoil", "currant" or "columbine" read "usually stocked by garden centres".
That put Alaska Harebell, Alpine Aster, Skunk Currant, Marsh Cinquefoil and
Silverweed among 66 such species, and only orchids, gentians, the wood lily and
the sundew read rare. The script did not even reproduce the data: it never
assigns seed-or-plug, which 82 species carry, so re-running it would have undone
them. Two more mismatches:

- *Easy to find*'s tip said "Stocked somewhere other than a specialist grower",
  while its query keeps the native nurseries' plants. The tip now says what the
  query does.
- The desktop species page printed the raw tier ("· rare", "· big box"), where
  the website says "hard to find in the trade". It now uses the website's
  sentence (`sourcing.describe`).

**The re-rank** is `scripts/rank_availability.py`, which owns the field and is
its provenance record (the `seed_flower_colour.py` pattern). It has explicit
lists for big box (9) and garden centre (35), then rules, each with the reason
written into the plant's note:

- seed or plug for the grasses, sedges and rushes;
- rare for orchids, gentians and other plants slow from seed, hemiparasites,
  bog plants, a wintergreen that depends on its soil fungi, and floating or
  submerged water plants;
- rare for a plant with under 10% of its records in the prairie, parkland and
  boreal-transition ecoregions, where nurseries sell, or under 25 records in
  the two provinces. An exception list keeps the foothills plants that
  Calgary-area nurseries grow.

| | V3.13 | V3.14 |
|---|---|---|
| big box | 15 | 9 |
| garden centre | 66 | 35 |
| native nursery | 255 | 213 |
| seed or plug | 82 | 83 |
| rare | 6 | 84 |

**It is a judgment, labelled one.** The trade lists are my reading of what
Alberta garden centres and big-box stores carried in 2024-26. The session could
not reach any nursery's site (the proxy returns 403), and I did not route around
it. Where unsure I chose the lower tier, which is the owner's direction.
`OWNER` in the script holds the owner's corrections and wins over everything.
`tests/test_availability_rank.py` fails if the catalogue and the script
disagree, so a tier edited by hand goes in `OWNER`, with whose call it was. The
big-box and garden-centre notes say the plant is usually or often sold as a
cultivar, which matters for a native planting. `apply_sourcing_data.py` no
longer touches availability.

## 6. Designs and the library favour plants people can buy (F226)

- **The offline generator**: a rare plant waits with the wetland specialists,
  after every bucket's good candidates. Among equals the easier to buy ranks
  higher: `sourcing.ease` is added to the ecological value, 2 for a garden
  centre or big box and 1 for a native nursery, so a keystone species still
  beats a plain one.
- **The design review's repairs** reach for a rare plant last.
- **The offline community pick**: among communities that fit the goals or the
  region, one people can buy whole gets +1, one with any rare member −1, and
  one with more than a third rare −2. Availability alone never picks one.
- **A design note** names any rare plant that arrives anyway, which happens
  through a community the site's region matched: "Rarely sold, even by native
  nurseries: Sitka Valerian. Ask a nursery before you count on it."
- **Five "Easy" communities**: Pollinator Border, Berry Hedge, Shady Corner, Dry
  Slope and Rain Garden, 36 members. None is rare in the trade, all are on the
  Edmonton list, and all are recorded in Aspen Parkland. The *Budget-friendly /
  easy to find* goal hints at them.
- **The library**: a row says "2 hard to find" when it holds plants rarely sold,
  and *Sort: Easiest to buy* orders by that share.
- **The coverage guard** (`TestCommunityCoverage`) required every species a
  native nursery sells to sit in some community, which is the "include all
  plants" pressure the owner named. It now asks it of the garden-centre and
  big-box natives: 38, all covered.

## Tests that had to change, and why

- **`test_open_ground.test_offline_additions_stand_apart_too`** needs the design
  review to add at least two plants, to check that they stand apart. It builds a
  deliberately thin offline design. Its meadow mix now takes Smooth Aster (a
  garden-centre plant) over Lindley's Aster, and Smooth Aster flowers into
  October, so the review had one gap left to fill instead of two. Measured: one
  addition, against two under the V3.13 ranking. The test now removes the mix
  too (`_offline_plant_mix` returns `None`, which is how the function says the
  pool cannot support one), and the review adds two under the new ranking and
  three under the old.
- **`test_plant_directory.test_one_vocabulary_for_every_picker`** keeps any
  panel from constructing a `CheckableComboBox(` itself. My first habitat
  dropdown did. It now comes from `make_multi_combo`, as the library's other
  four dropdowns do, and is connected only once its tree is built, because
  drawing a branch's marker changes an item and would have refreshed the list
  once per branch.
- **`test_edmonton_communities`** and **`TestCommunityCoverage`**, above.
- **`test_filter_widgets`**: my own new test opened the first popup after
  `test_plant_panel_smoke`, and that module's leftover events closed it. It now
  drains them first, as its neighbours do.

  carries them.
