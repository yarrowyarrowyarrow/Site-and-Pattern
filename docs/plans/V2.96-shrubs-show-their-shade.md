# V2.96 — Shrubs show their shade

*One item: **F188**, found by V2.94: no shrub has ever drawn the shade baked into
its model. The owner asked what was left after V2.95, was offered F188 then F174,
and said "Continue".*

## What was wrong

The shrub layer asks for its leaf material without vertex colours
(`04-quality.js`, `buildShrubLayer`):

```js
surfaceMaterial(MAT_PRESETS.shrubLeaf, surf)          // V2.95
```

`surfaceMaterial(preset, cls, vc)` takes `vc` to mean "this geometry carries a
colour attribute", and reads a missing argument as no. The preset itself says
`vertexColors: true`; the argument overrides it. Every other foliage call passes
`true`: tree crowns, needles, herb leaves, the succulents. Of the viewer's 17
calls, the shrub layer's is the only one that passes nothing. So:

- **the baked shrubs** (all 53 shrub species in Balanced and Lifelike) never drew
  the shade baked into their eight models, and
- **the procedural shrubs** (Stylised) never drew the warm-top, cool-base
  gradient every other Stylised plant has.

Nothing failed. Both geometry paths carry the colour attribute (`_glbBakedGeo`
guarantees one, `applyFoliageGradient` writes one); the material ignored it. Two
materials in the shared table, `MATS.shrub` and `MATS.shrubFoliage`, have vertex
colours on and are drawn by nothing. By their comments they are what the shrub
layer drew with before it asked for a material per species (F63, V2.33). This
clone is shallow, so the commit that lost the argument cannot be named.

## What turning it on does

Every shrub in the catalogue, alone, mature, in July, under the scene's own
default sun, through the real viewer with the baked models (Balanced): four
views at a standing person's eye (1.6 m) and one from above, each with the
two-sided mask V2.94 settled on. Soapweed Yucca is left out: the succulent layer
draws it (V2.93). 53 shrubs.

| | Eye level | From above | Near-black at eye level |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2.95 (no shade drawn) | 0.345 | 0.448 | 19% |
| Shade on, the shipped bake | 0.224 | 0.264 | 35% |

Turned on alone, the shade is the trees' problem from V2.94 over again. The shrub
foliage is baked with the settings the trees had before V2.94: full occlusion
0.85, rays reaching 60% of the shrub, the ground at the lowest leaf. The eight
models' leaves average 0.28, with 67% of their vertices under 0.3.

## The change

1. **The shrub layer passes `true`**, like every other foliage call.
2. **The shrubs are baked with `CROWN_AO`**, the trees' crown bake since V2.94
   (strength 0.65, rays reaching 15% of the plant, the ground at 0, the vertical
   gradient from 0.8 at the bottom). The leaves now average 0.64, with 2% under
   0.3; the trees' are 0.58 and 4%.
3. **Every `surfaceMaterial` call must say** whether its geometry carries
   colours. A test fails on a call with two arguments, which is the bug, not
   just this instance of it.

| | Eye level | From above | Near-black at eye level | Shade between clusters, from above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2.95 | 0.345 | 0.448 | 19% | 0.170 |
| **V2.96**: shade on, rebaked with `CROWN_AO` | **0.295** | **0.373** | **25%** | **0.202** |
| A lighter bake, tried: 0.5, 10%, gradient 0.85 | 0.312 | 0.399 | 23% | 0.187 |
| The trees after V2.94, for comparison | 0.237 | 0.315 | 38% | |

"Shade between clusters" is how much the brightness varies across the plant at
the scale of a leaf cluster (a 15-pixel window at 360 pixels), relative to the
plant's mean, so a uniformly darker plant does not count as deeper. From above,
where a flat-lit shrub reads as a cut-out, it rises 19%; from a path, 3%. At leaf
scale nothing measurable changes: leaf edges, stems and the shadow map dominate.

**Why the trees' bake and not a lighter one.** The lighter bake won back a third
of the brightness the shade costs and gave back half the depth it adds. The
darkest shrubs stayed dark under both, because what makes them dark is mostly
their colour:

- **40 of the 53 shrubs draw one colour**, the shrub default `#4f7a3a`
  (`scene_contract._FOLIAGE_BY_TYPE`, chosen in V1.90). Nothing records a shrub's
  leaf colour; only a few genera have their own (willow, dogwood, birch, the
  silver buffaloberries and sagebrushes).
- Seven of the eight most darkened wear the default. By near-black share from a
  path: bracted honeysuckle 11% → 51%, highbush cranberry 26% → 52%, low-bush
  cranberry 24% → 46%, beaked hazelnut 28% → 47%, alder-leaved buckthorn 11% →
  30%. The eighth, red-osier dogwood (24% → 45%), has its genus's own green,
  `#5a8246`, only a little lighter. Under the lighter bake those six are 24-48%.

One bake rule for every crown is simpler to keep honest than a second set of
numbers that buys 0.017 of brightness.

**This darkens shrubs, and says so.** From a path they lose 0.05 of luma, and the
share of near-black pixels goes from 19% to 25%. They stay brighter than the
trees on every measure. The before-and-after sheet is the argument for it: from
above, the shrubs stop being flat green cut-outs.

### The models

Only the eight shrub models change. Each rebuilt file was compared with the
shipped one accessor by accessor, as V2.94 did for the trees: **only the
foliage's `COLOR_0` differs**. Positions, normals, indices and the stems' shade
are identical, and two rebuilds are byte-identical. The exporter's version string
moves from `v4.2.57` to `v4.2.83`, as the trees' did.

One file loses a unit. `shrub_mound.glb` carried a seventh variant, `narrow_2`,
that no species uses since V2.93's catalogue changes (V2.94 counted it among six
unused variants in four models). It was last in the file, so dropping it
renumbers nothing: units 0 to 5 have the same geometry and the same half-widths.
Its manifest entry loses the key and the half-width; the other seven entries are
identical to the ones a full build writes. The manifest is written by
`manifest_dict`, as `write_manifest` does, not edited by hand.

### Stylised

The procedural shrubs now take the gradient every other Stylised plant has: the
lower masses darker, the tops warmer. The facets still read as facets.

- 12 shrubs: eye level 0.301 → 0.283, near-black 2% → 17%, from above 0.417 →
  0.413.
- The Stylised trees are at 29% near-black from a path (V2.94).

## Left alone, on purpose

- **The shrub default green.** Recording a leaf colour per species needs a source
  per species, like the flower colours in F172. Noted under F180.
- **Common juniper** draws as a star of bare canes with needle fringes, in every
  month: the prostrate form's smallest narrow leaf, one of the species F180
  already counts as bare stems. Unchanged here.
- **The ground bounce (F187)** is still the owner's call.
- **The other dead entries in the shared material table.** `MATS.shrub` is
  deleted, because it is the shrub material this replaces and would mislead the
  next reader. `MATS.foliage` and `MATS.shrubFoliage` are also drawn by nothing,
  but `test_scene3d_render.FlatLeafMaterialsTest` reads them to find the presets
  it checks, so they stay.
- **Three models still carry unused variants**: `herb.clump`, `herb.erect` and
  `layer.groundcover`. Rebuilding them would renumber their units for nothing
  visible.

## How it is checked

- `tests/test_leaf_light.py`: the trees' and the shrubs' foliage are baked with
  `CROWN_AO` (it said the shrubs must not be, "see F188 first"); **every shipped
  shrub's leaves average at least 0.45, with no more than a fifth under 0.3**, as
  for the trees.
- `tests/test_scene3d_render.py`: every `surfaceMaterial` call in the viewer
  passes its third argument.
- `tests/test_accuracy_render.py`: two shrubs photographed from above as drawn,
  and again with their foliage's colour attribute whitened. If the shade is drawn
  the two differ. Drawn over whitened must be at most 0.95 (the shade is drawn)
  and at least 0.6 (it is not the old dark bake).

## Results

- **The render probe, V2.96**: from above, Western Snowberry draws at 0.743 of
  its whitened brightness and Red Osier Dogwood at 0.756. The probe's light
  routine was generalised to take a list of shots so the two checks share it;
  V2.94's readings did not move (aspen 2% near-black and 0.307 from its shaded
  side, paper birch 8% and 0.303).
- **Against V2.95**, run from a scratch worktree with this branch's tests and
  probe page: 13 failures, each for its own reason.
  - The shrub bake is not `CROWN_AO`.
  - All eight shipped shrub models fail the crown shade test (averages 0.22 to
    0.40).
  - Both call-site tests fail at `04-quality.js:688`.
  - Both shrubs draw at exactly 1.0 of their whitened brightness.

  The tree light test passes there, as a control.
- **V2.96's viewer over V2.95's shrub models** (the shade drawn, the old bake):
  the probe reads 0.50 and 0.51 and fails the lower bound. So the test catches
  both ways this can go wrong.
- **The committed code draws what was measured.** The survey switched the
  argument on at runtime. Six shrubs rendered from the committed viewer (60
  frames with their masks) match those renders in all but 2 of about 7.8
  million pixels, by at most 8 levels: software-renderer noise at an edge.
- **Neighbouring modules** (the guard, the philosophy weave, model assets, tree
  shapes, leaf light, the render gates and probe): 114 tests, 1 skip.
- **Full suite** (`scripts/run_tests.py`, as CI runs it): 3,976 tests, 0 failed,
  0 errors, 7 skipped (pypdf not installed three times; four environmental),
  `RESULT: OK`. `validate-data` is clean.
- **CI on the pushed commit** (`641de754`): Tests, the macOS DMG and the Windows
  installer all green.
- The before-and-after sheet (six shrubs from a path and from above, and the
  worked example from the street and the air) is in the session, not the repo.

## What could not be verified here

- **Against photographs.** The judgments of look are the author's, from renders.
- **On a real desktop GPU.** Every render is headless Chromium on the SwiftShader
  software renderer, running the same viewer files the app loads.
- **Other light.** July at midday was measured. October and January were looked
  at for eight shrubs, not measured closely: the autumn colours still read, with
  shade inside the crowns from above; the evergreens are a little deeper. Night
  and a low sun were not looked at.
