# V3.11 — Species numbers you can see at every zoom, and a species found from the legend (F218)

*The owner, 7 October 2026, after the first Species legend (F217): "when we go
into the species they are the same colour if they are the same type and can't be
told apart. Is it possible to keep the base for the base design but when flipping
to per species legend to have the species be different colours on the map and
matching this on the legend?"* With two screenshots: the legend's seven species,
five of them the same purple, and a satellite map whose plants were dots.

*Then, interrupting the colour work: "I didn't realize there were numbers as I did
not zoom in enough. I feel if this number system could be improved that would be
great, with this colour work you are currently doing being either too complicated
or possibly better as a secondary (third) option."*

This is the second legend increment on the V3.11 branch: a refinement of F217,
which shipped there the day before, so it rides the same branch. A copy of
V3.11 already installed is not offered it by Check for Updates, which compares
versions only; a fresh download of the V3.11 installer carries it.

## What was wrong

**A number was drawn only on a plant at least 7 px across on screen, and nothing
said so.** F217's `_fitSpeciesNumbers` set every other number's opacity to 0
(`_NUMBER_MIN_RADIUS_PX = 7`, a radius). At the zoom a whole yard is read at, every
flower is 1 to 4 px, so the owner saw the legend's numbers and none on the map;
only the two willows, at 2 to 2.5 m spacing, were big enough to carry theirs. The
owner's layout re-created (seven species, two of each, 18 × 14 m, at 9 px a metre)
is the left half of
[`V3.11-species-numbers-before-after.jpg`](../img/ui/V3.11-species-numbers-before-after.jpg):
the two willows numbered, the five flowers not.

The same colour within a type is not a fault: plants have one colour scheme, by
type (V3.03), and Species keeps it, which is what the owner meant by keeping "the
base for the base design".

## The colour route, measured and set aside

The first answer to the owner's question was a colour per species. It was
measured before any of it was built, and the measurement agrees with the owner's
second thought.

- **A map is an all-pairs form**: any two species can stand side by side, so every
  pair has to be told apart, not only neighbours in a list. A max-min search over
  sRGB (OKLab ΔE × 100, Machado 2009 protan and deutan at severity 1.0, through
  `src/colour_distance.py`, the model the dataviz validator uses) finds 10 colours
  at normal ΔE ≥ 23 and colour-blind ΔE ≥ 10.8, and 12 at 20.2 and 8.1, among
  lightness 0.43 to 0.85 and chroma ≥ 0.10. As swatches, then, colour stretches to
  about a dozen species.
- **Drawn as markers it does not.** At a 3 px radius the 2 px outline is three
  quarters of what shows. With today's marker (outline 60% darker, fill 35%), the
  best ten's closest pair, as the eye averages it over lawn, canopy, soil, roof,
  the street map and a park, is ΔE 3.7 (colour-blind 2.0): one colour. A marker
  redesigned for it (outline 20% darker, fill 75%) reaches 14.2 (7.9), barely the
  floor. Okabe-Ito does worse (11.0 / 5.5 at best), Tableau 10 and Kelly's far
  worse (2.3 and 1.2 in today's style).

So at the zoom where the numbers went missing, colour would not have told the
species apart either. Colour per species is left open as **F219**, a third
legend mode if the numbers turn out not to be enough, with these numbers.

## What was decided

### Numbers at every zoom (`numberGroups`, `html/map/13-species-numbers.js`)

- **A plant big enough to hold its number carries it**, on it, as before. Big
  enough is now a 9 px radius, because the number sits in a 16 px tag.
- **Smaller plants of one species close together share one number**: a plant joins
  the nearest group of its species within 40 px of the plant that began it (taken
  top to bottom, left to right), and the group's number sits on the plant nearest
  its middle. It is a printed planting plan's tag for a drift. Two species never
  share one; one species far apart gets one per group. As the map zooms in the
  groups split until every plant has its own.
- **No number covers another.** One that would tries the eight places one tag
  around its plant; with none free it waits, and the legend says how many wait:
  *Zoom in for 3 more numbers*, the only words added, and a fact about this map
  rather than an instruction.
- **A plant's own number is placed first**, so a group's cannot take a shrub's
  middle and read as the shrub's.
- **The map's own labels are kept clear of when there is room and covered when
  there is not.** The first build treated them as walls, and on the owner's layout
  at a whole-yard zoom it hid five of seven numbers behind the boundary's lengths
  and area, which there are bigger than the yard: the fault being fixed, again.
  They are measured less their padding, because a willow's own number pushed out
  of the willow by the 1 px border of the "14 m" label read worse than touching it.
- **The tag** is white with the printed planting map's dark green (`#1b5e20`),
  12 px bold, so it reads on a satellite photo and keeps the app's 12 px floor. It
  replaces the white-on-halo digits, which only read on a plant.
- **It follows the map**: worked out again on every zoom, every layer change,
  150 ms after a dragged plant stops (the number moves with its plant meanwhile),
  and when the timeline resizes the plants (`04-tools.js`).

### A species found from the legend

Each species line is a button. **Pointing at it, or tabbing to it, rings that
species' plants** on the map, a dark ring under a white one, at least 7 px
whatever the zoom, so a 1 px dot can be found. **A click keeps them ringed**
(`aria-pressed`), through the legend's rebuilds, until a second click, Type, or
closing the legend. The rings sit in their own pane with `pointer-events: none`:
the plants' canvas covering a lower pane is what hid the boundary from clicks
(F213), and a layer above the plants must not take theirs.

The lines grew to the 24 px every control keeps, so the Species list is about a
third taller than it was; the legend has scrolled rather than run off a short map
since V3.03, and still does.

### Code

`html/map/13-species-numbers.js` is new, loaded after `12-legend.js`: the legend
would have gone past its ceiling, so the numbers moved out on arrival, with the
rings. `numberGroups` is pure (screen pixels in, tags out), so node runs it.
`12-legend.js` keeps the model and the HTML; a species line carries `data-find`.
No Python changed: no new bridge call, nothing saved.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **A colour per species**: F219, above.
- **Leader lines** from a moved number to its plant. A number moves at most one
  tag away, and the rings answer "which plants" exactly.
- **The count in the tag** ("7 ×5"): the legend carries the totals, and the tag
  stays the size of a digit.
- **The PDF's planting map** numbers every plant at a fixed size on the page, as
  F41 designed; its scale is the page's, not the screen's.
- **The legend panel** can sit over numbers in the map's lower left; those are not
  counted as waiting. The panel closes with a click or L.
- **A plant's hover tooltip** still names it without its number.

## How it is checked

- **Node** (`tests/test_legend.py` › `TestNumbersAtEveryZoom`, nine tests): a big
  plant holds its own number; a drift of five shares one, on its middle plant; the
  same species far apart is numbered twice; two species never share; in a crowd of
  twelve no number covers another, none strays more than one tag, and placed plus
  waiting is twelve; a shrub keeps its middle against a drift round it; a label is
  avoided when there is room and covered when not; the same map lays out the same;
  the old 7 px rule is gone. Plus the species line's button and its spoken name.
- **The real page in headless Chromium** (`html/legend_probe.html`): at 20 px a
  metre all five plants carry their numbers, 0 px off their plants; at 2.5 the two
  Wild Bergamots 2.6 m apart share one, and nothing overlaps (until F218 there was
  no number at this zoom); at 0.1, with eight more species, 8 of 12 are placed,
  none overlapping, and the legend says *Zoom in for 4 more numbers*, gone again
  after; pointing at Wild Bergamot rings its two plants and leaving lets them go;
  a click keeps them through a rebuild; tabbing to Harebell rings its one and
  tabbing away brings the kept ones back; a far-out ring is 7 px; Type and closing
  the legend let them go.
- **Looked at**: screenshots of the owner's layout, the worked example and a
  fourteen-species meadow at 4, 9, 12, 20 and 30 px a metre, on a generated aerial
  texture and on the street map's colour. They are what found the labels covering
  numbers, and then the first fix hiding them.
- The touched modules and the architecture guard: 237 tests, OK, one skip (the
  commit-history test, which needs more history than this shallow clone).

## What could not be verified here

- **Real satellite imagery.** The tile servers cannot be reached from the
  container; the aerial background was a generated texture of lawn, canopy, soil
  and light patches. The tag is white with a shadow for a busy photo, but it has
  not been seen on one.
- **The desktop app.** The page ran in headless Chromium, not in the app's
  QtWebEngine on Windows; nothing on the Qt side changed.
- **How it feels** at the owner's own zoom, yard and plant count, and with thirty
  or more species.
