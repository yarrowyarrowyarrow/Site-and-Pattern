# V2.78 — a third of the dots were somewhere else

Four asks in one increment: stop the maps drawing British Columbia, add the
phenology bar, do what can be done about VASCAN nativity, and make the maps
worth looking at.

---

## Context

V2.77 shipped the specimens-only rendering and the author looked at it. Their
reply named one fault and three pieces of work:

> *I noticed some spillover into BC... ensure only AB and SK for right now.*

The spillover was real, larger than it looked, and the fix turned out to
implicate the caption as well as the picture.

## The spillover was 31.7% of the harvest

`scripts/seed_ecoregion_ranges.py` asks GBIF for records inside the polygon
layer's **bounding box** plus half a degree. That box runs to -120.5 and 48.5,
so it takes in a wedge of British Columbia, a strip of Montana and North
Dakota, the Manitoba edge and a slice of the Northwest Territories. The
derivation ignores every one of them correctly: `_containment_lookup` returns
`[]` and no record is credited to a region.

The **drawing** did not, because `map_svg` emits its `overlay` seam *outside*
the group it clips to the subject provinces. Everything thematic was clipped
and the records were not.

| | records |
|---|---|
| cached | 555,477 |
| outside Alberta and Saskatchewan, and drawn anyway | **175,876 (31.7%)** |

On one species (*Elymus trachycaulus*) it was 551 of 1,251. On a map whose
entire argument is about what a shaded region claims, a third of the ink was
about ground the layer has no authority over.

**The province outline alone is not the test.** `data/basemap_prairie.geojson`
is Natural Earth 1:10m and Alberta plus Saskatchewan come to **193 vertices**
between them. That is fine for drawing and much too coarse for deciding a
record's fate along the continental divide, where the border is a wiggle and
the mountains are full of collections. Dropping a real Banff specimen because
our basemap cut a corner would be the same class of error as the 5 km buffer:
a display simplification reaching into a claim. So
`ecoregion_basemap.in_subject_provinces` keeps a point that is inside the
coarse outline **or** inside any surveyed ecoregion polygon, cheap test first.
Probed against Golden, Revelstoke, Cranbrook, Fort St John, Great Falls,
Brandon and Hay River (all out) and Banff, Lake Louise, Waterton, Calgary,
Grande Prairie and Saskatoon (all in).

The guard caught the first attempt at this: `ecoregion_basemap.py` went to
271 lines against a ceiling of 260, and the house rule is a split rather than a
bigger number. `src/subject_area.py` is that split and the boundary is worth
having on its own terms -- `ecoregion_basemap` draws the ground, `subject_area`
decides whether a piece of evidence is on it. A shim keeps the old import
working, in the same shape as that module's existing `_point_in_ring` one.

`map_svg` also grew a **`chrome` seam**, emitted last and unclipped, because
the legend added below sits in the corner of the *frame* -- which on this
projection is over British Columbia. Routed through `overlay` the new clip
deleted it.

## The caption was wrong twice, and the second time is the interesting one

The V2.77 caption said *"the shading is derived from all 1,251"*. Once the
subject filter existed that was plainly false: 551 of those are in BC and the
derivation never counted them.

Fixing it to *"422 of the 691 the shading counts"* opened a smaller hole that
took a third pass to see. 691 counted, minus 268 not specimens, minus 10 for
licence, leaves **nine records unaccounted for**. They are real: records inside
a region that never reached the three-record floor, so they are held, they are
ours, and they shade nothing.

Nine records out of 1,251 is a rounding error, and losing them quietly is the
precise habit this catalogue exists to correct. The caption now runs four
tiers that close:

> 422 of the 700 records this species has in Alberta and Saskatchewan (268 not
> a specimen; 10 licence does not permit redrawing). The shading is derived
> from 691 of them, not from the dots; the other 9 fall in regions with too few
> records to shade. A further 551 records fall outside the two provinces and
> are neither counted nor drawn.

## The dots were one fixed size (F142)

`_MIN_R`/`_MAX_R` were absolute SVG units, and **360 of 422 dots on a real
species sit at the minimum**. So every dot was effectively one fixed size while
the map around it scaled: a 360px contact-sheet thumbnail drew its dots two and
a half times larger relative to the ground than the 900px version of the same
map, and the two disagreed about how crowded the species is. They are fractions
of the width now.

## There was no key at all

Two versions of a map running **three independent encodings at once** -- region
fill for confidence, dot colour for the kind of record, dot size for how
precisely that record knows where it is -- with no statement of any of them.
The one that misleads hardest unexplained is the hollow dot: it means *this
record stated no location accuracy* and looks like a circle drawn for emphasis.

`legend_svg` draws the three, in SVG rather than HTML so a standalone `.svg`
handed to somebody is self-describing, and it omits the observation colour on a
specimens-only map rather than keying something the picture never draws.

## The phenology bar (F143)

`src/phenology_bar.py`, and it is on **424 of 430 species pages**.

The catalogue has carried `bloom_period` since its first seed file and shown it
three ways, all text: "Jun–Sep" in a cell, twelve month chips, a sentence in
the directory. None of them answer the question a person reading a plant list
actually has -- *what is flowering in July, and does anything carry August* --
because comparing two species means holding a calendar in your head.

**Fruit sits on the same axis on purpose.** 285 species carry `fruit_period`,
and the gap between the two is the information: a shrub that flowers in May and
fruits in September is doing eleven months of work, and no arrangement of two
separate strings shows that.

Three refusals are the substance:

- **Nothing recorded draws nothing.** Twelve empty cells is not the absence of
  a claim, it is the claim that we checked and this plant never flowers, which
  for the six species with no window would be false (P9).
- **One parser.** `parse_period` delegates to `habitat_score.parse_month_range`,
  which has read these since V1.x and handles all 37 spellings in the shipped
  data. A second parser here would be free to disagree with the first.
- **The caption says what the data is not.** These are stated ranges from
  horticultural references, not phenology series, so the bar cannot show a peak
  inside the window or that bloom runs later in the north. A bar looks like a
  measurement.

The bar also goes under each map on the contact sheet, which is the atlas page
the author was pointing at: where it grows, and when it does the thing you are
planting it for.

## Nativity: the fetch cannot run here, so the mark went on instead (F144)

VASCAN is unreachable from this environment (`data.canadensys.net` gets 403 at
the proxy, and so does `api.gbif.org`). `scripts/fetch_flora_nativity.py` and
`scripts/ingest_flora_nativity.py` were written and tested in V2.75 and still
have never run.

What could be done without the network is the thing the outside review actually
complained about. The retired generator's own docstring states it:

> *The heuristic was published as a fact. An outside botanical review read the
> site and said, correctly, that many species listed as native to AB and SK are
> native to only one. They are reading the output of this file.*

`native_provinces` has **no source column** -- unlike `flower_colour_source`,
unlike `safety_source`. 354 of 430 species publish "AB, SK" from an inference
about ecoregion continuity across the 110th meridian, on a reference site named
GrowNativePlants, with no mark, while flower colour has carried one since
V2.48. That is exactly backwards.

`src/nativity.py` puts the mark back on, and names the actual heuristic rather
than saying a vague "unverified" -- **429 species pages now carry it**. Two
provenances wear one string and get different notes: Saskatchewan is the
ecoregion inference, Alberta is an editorial flag from the first seed file, and
one note for both would be false for one of them. The one hand-set row
("SK,MB") is not credited to the heuristic that preserved rather than produced
it.

**No schema bump.** The note is derived, not stored, because today there is no
per-species variation to store and 430 copies of one sentence would be
inventing a data shape ahead of the data. `SOURCE_FIELD` is the seam VASCAN
writes into, `provenance` already prefers it, and a test fails the moment the
column appears so that the derivation gets removed rather than left to argue
with real data.

## Postscript: the hand-off command was the broken one

Pushing this branch and telling the author to fetch it produced:

```
$ git fetch origin V2.78
 * tag               V2.78      -> FETCH_HEAD
$ git switch V2.78
fatal: invalid reference: V2.78
```

Origin carries both `refs/heads/V2.78` and `refs/tags/V2.78` at the same
commit, because the release workflow tags every `V*` push. A bare ref name is
ambiguous when a tag shares it; git resolved the tag, and a tag fetched that
way lands in `FETCH_HEAD` and nowhere else, so no remote-tracking branch was
created for `git switch` to find.

`CLAUDE.md` has warned about this trap since V2.72 and gives the cleanup for a
clone that already has stray tags. It gave no recipe for the case that actually
comes first -- **getting a branch that is not in the clone yet** -- so the
command a person reaches for is the failing one, and the hand-off here repeated
it from three paragraphs below the warning.

The block now carries the working refspec, why *both* halves of it are
load-bearing, and a table matching the two failure messages to their causes.
The "never delete the remote tags" warning is untouched: each anchors a GitHub
Release and `github_releases.parse_release_version` reads `tag_name` off those
releases to drive the in-app updater, so the tags are the updater's input
rather than an accident to tidy away.

## Postscript 2: seven errors that only exist on Windows

The author ran the full suite and got **seven errors this container cannot
produce**:

```
records = json.loads((self.tmp / "plants_master.json").read_text())
UnicodeDecodeError: 'charmap' codec can't decode byte 0x8f in position 899031
```

`Path.read_text()` and the builtin `open()` decode with the **locale** codec.
On Linux that is UTF-8 and everything works. On Windows it is cp1252, and
`data/plants_master.json` carries **966 non-ASCII bytes** that cp1252 cannot
decode at all.

**The project had already learned this.** V1.95 hit it in
`src/sprite_gallery.py`, fixed it, left a comment beside the fix, and wrote
`test_seed_reads_pin_utf8_encoding` -- a guard that reads **exactly one file**.
So when V2.75's data-quality tests read the same seed JSON the same bare way,
nothing objected, and two releases shipped a suite that was green everywhere
except on the machine belonging to the person who reads the output.

That is the lesson worth keeping: **a guard scoped to the file where the bug
was found does not stop the bug, it stops that instance of it.**

**Three shapes, two of them found by the instrument rather than by the crash.**
Verifying the fix with `PYTHONWARNDEFAULTENCODING=1 -W error::EncodingWarning`
turned up two more families the tests were not yet failing on:

| shape | sites | live or latent |
|---|---|---|
| `read_text` / `write_text` / builtin `open` | 37 | **live** -- the seven errors |
| `subprocess(text=True)` | 17 | latent, and one is `retag_releases.py` reading `git log --format=%s` over this repo's em-dash-laden commit subjects |
| `tempfile.NamedTemporaryFile(mode="w")` | 2 | latent |

**No shipped code was crashing.** Every `src/` hit in the first sweep was a
false positive (`rasterio.open`, `Image.open`, `fiona.open`,
`webbrowser.open`, `QIODevice.open`); the one real shipped site,
`onboarding_flow`'s `git rev-parse`, reads an ASCII branch name and degrades
inside a bare `except`. The app was fine. The tests were not.

`TestEveryTextFileReadPinsItsEncoding` now covers all three shapes across
`src/`, `scripts/`, `tests/` and `tools/`, and was checked against eight
synthetic cases before being trusted -- it flags the three real shapes and
none of the five attribute-call `open`s. The strict run is clean: **3,396
tests, OK, no EncodingWarning**.

Recorded in `legacy-lessons` (entry 19) and in the `testing` skill, because a
comment beside one fix is exactly what failed to carry the lesson last time.
