# V2.79 — the range stops being made of ecoregions

The outside botanical review's original criticism, arriving a second time and
landing a level deeper than any of the fixes it had produced so far.

**Status: in flight.** See [Handing this over](#handing-this-over) at the end.

---

## Context

Everything from V2.75 to V2.78 improved *how* the ecoregion shading was derived
— containment instead of a 5 km proximity buffer, the BC sliver that was
absorbing the mountains, the harvest cap that was hiding the north. Each was a
real fix. Every one of them left the assumption underneath untouched.

The author named it after seeing the corrected maps:

> *"Going by ecoregion seems flawed (according to the feedback this whole review
> is based on). Usually you have the dots and a shaded area that shows the
> range. This range does not neatly conform to ecoregions."*

It does not, because it is not made of them. A range is where the plant has been
found; ecoregions are a classification laid *over* that. Shading a 100,000 km²
region because three records fall inside it is the same overstatement the review
objected to, one level above the bug it named.

## F145 — the range as occupied grid cells

`src/species_range.py` bins each species' usable records into a **0.25 degree
grid** and ships the occupied cells. `scripts/seed_species_ranges.py` derives it
offline from the V2.75 point cache.

```
426 species with a range, 56,513 cells, 0.86 MB
365,095 records used
 20,369 refused as too coarsely georeferenced (over the 10 km limit)
174,038 outside Alberta and Saskatchewan
```

The whole catalogue's range data is smaller than one of today's species pages.

**A grid rather than an alpha hull, deliberately.** A hull interpolates: it
draws boundary through ground where nothing was recorded, and the interpolation
is invisible once filled in — the review's complaint in a smoother shape. An
occupied cell asserts one thing: *at least one usable record falls inside this
square*. It also needs no new dependency; every `src/` use of `shapely` is
guarded because the app is expected to run without it, and putting the website
build behind a hull library would be a poor trade for a smoother edge.

**What a cell refuses to claim**, and the tests pin the refusals rather than the
arithmetic: not that the plant grows throughout the square (0.25° is ~28 km
north-south), not that an unshaded square is absent rather than unrecorded, and
nothing at all for the 8 species with no usable in-province record — they draw
nothing, the same rule `phenology_bar` follows.

**The ecoregion layer is demoted, not deleted.** Its counts, bands, filters and
region hub pages all still work and answer a different question.

## The map renderer

`src/range_map.py` draws the range, the records, and enough geography to locate
them. **91 KB against the ecoregion map's 846 KB.**

Specimens are **filled** and observations **hollow** — shape, not only hue — so
the distinction survives greyscale, print, and red-green colour deficiency. That
matters because the site is about to publish both layers with a toggle between
them.

Grid squares are drawn as four projected corners rather than rectangles: the
projection is a conic, so a square in degrees is not a rectangle on the page and
drawing it as one leaves hairline gaps between neighbours toward the top of the
map. Province borders are drawn twice, under and over the range wash, so an edge
stays legible where the wash sits against it.

## The three palettes read identical, and the reason is arithmetic

The author's verdict on the variants: *"all look identical."* Correct, and it is
not a matter of taste:

| species | range cells | marks drawn | marks per cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Gaillardia aristata* | 361 | 4,046 | **11.2** |
| *Opuntia polyacantha* | 183 | 5,401 | **29.5** |
| *Cornus canadensis* | 455 | 4,707 | **10.3** |

The palettes differ almost entirely in the **range wash**, and the wash is
completely buried under ink. At 460 px the map draws tens of marks per grid
square, so none of the colour is visible and all three look the same.

**The fix is not a fourth palette.** It is deciding what the marks are *for*
when there are thousands of them — see the handover below.

## VASCAN, in two halves

### F148 — a missing field was being published as an absence

The nativity fetch ran clean (434 species, zero failures) and reported **182
species not recorded from Alberta or Saskatchewan** — 42% of a native plant
catalogue, *Amelanchier alnifolia* among them, the defining parkland shrub this
entire range pipeline was started over in V2.38.

Two probes found the cause, and it is not about the flora:

| query | rank | distribution |
|---|---|---|
| `Amelanchier alnifolia` | species | **absent** |
| `Amelanchier alnifolia var. alnifolia` | variety | AB, SK, MB native |
| `Alnus incana` | species | **absent** |
| `Alnus incana subsp. tenuifolia` | subspecies | AB, SK native; MB excluded |

**VASCAN attaches distribution to the lowest accepted taxon.** The parser was
reading the right key all along; `assess()` let a *missing* block fall through
to `origin: absent, verdict: not_here`, with a `why` reading *"VASCAN records no
Alberta or Saskatchewan distribution."* False, about 173 species.

Third instance in this repo of **a failure is not an absence** — V2.75's rate
limit logged 208 throttled species as growing nowhere, V2.78's harvest cap made
a truncated fetch indistinguishable from a complete one. Recorded as entry 20 in
`.claude/skills/legacy-lessons/`, with the rule: *every external field is three
states, not two, and code that models two publishes the third as whichever of
the other two it falls through to.*

`--reassess` re-runs verdicts over an already-fetched file with **no network**,
so the fix cost a re-read rather than 434 requests. `not_here` went **182 → 9**.

### F149 — read the checklist, because the API cannot be asked

Two more probes settled how the 173 can be reached:

```
Amelanchier alnifolia var      numMatches: 0
Amelanchier alnifolia subsp    numMatches: 0
```

`search.json` is **exact-name only**, and `parentNameUsageID` points the wrong
way, so a taxon's children cannot be enumerated from it.

**The autonym shortcut is a trap and is deliberately not implemented.** Guessing
`<species> var. <epithet>` resolves *Amelanchier alnifolia var. alnifolia* and
misses *Alnus incana* completely, whose Alberta taxon is `subsp. tenuifolia`
rather than the autonym `subsp. incana`. A rule that answers some species and
silently skips others produces a result that looks complete and is not. The
*Alnus* case is a test so nobody can reintroduce it quietly.

`scripts/vascan_archive.py` reads the published Darwin Core Archive and rolls
each species up over its **accepted** descendants: native in one child beats
excluded in a sibling, because one variety being absent says nothing about the
species. Synonyms do not vote — a synonym's distribution is the accepted taxon's
counted twice.

**No download URL is in the code.** This project's sessions cannot reach
`data.canadensys.net` to verify one, and a URL asserted from memory that quietly
fetches the wrong dataset *and parses successfully* is the failure hardest to
notice. `--from-archive` with no path prints what to fetch and exits non-zero —
the rule `tools/ecoregions/fetch.py` already sets for unreachable sources:
*never substitute a different dataset, never write a placeholder.*

Verified end to end against a synthetic archive built over the real 434-species
catalogue: both canaries, the two species carrying no distribution of their own,
resolve through their children.

## What the VASCAN fetch actually found

Unaffected by the bug, and **none of it applied**:

| | count |
|---|---|
| VASCAN agrees the species is native in AB/SK | 243 |
| recorded introduced or excluded here | 9 |
| name not matched at all | 9 |

The nine `not_here`: *Achillea millefolium*, *Helianthus annuus*, *Prunus
tomentosa*, *Maianthemum racemosum*, *Solidago nemoralis*, *Stachys palustris*,
*Festuca ovina*, *Solidago simplex*, *Agoseris parviflora*.

*Achillea millefolium* is **not a simple removal**: the catalogue also carries
*Achillea borealis*, and whether North America's native yarrow races belong to
the same species is a live taxonomic dispute. A second source has to settle it.

The nine unmatched include three cultivars (expected — cultivars are not in a
wild flora) plus *Andropogon gerardii*, *Deschampsia caespitosa*, *Hedysarum
alpinum*, *Matteuccia struthiopteris*, *Oenothera caespitosa*, *Spiraea
betulifolia*, which are real taxa and probably superseded binomials.

## Two smaller things, both real

A test asserting the ingest refuses a missing fetch file had been **asserting a
fact about the working tree**: it passed for two releases only because nobody
had run the fetch, and started failing the moment the real file landed.
Redirecting the constant to prove the guard then crashed the error path itself,
which formatted the path with `relative_to()` — and that raises for anything
outside the repo, in the one code path whose entire job is to fail politely.

## Could not be verified here

- **How any of it looks.** Renders were sent to the author instead.
- **Anything needing the network.** `api.gbif.org` and `data.canadensys.net`
  both answer 403 at this environment's proxy, so the VASCAN archive path is
  tested against a synthetic archive and has never met the real one.

## Handing this over

Branch **V2.79**. Suite green: 3,444 tests, no `EncodingWarning`.

### Decisions already made by the author — do not re-litigate

- **Both record layers published**, with a toggle for specimens / observations /
  both.
- **`CC_BY_NC` publishable for occurrence coordinates only.** The photograph
  rule in `fetch_inaturalist_images.py` is untouched; a photograph is
  redistributed as a work, a coordinate is a fact about a place.
- **The five collectible taxa published as-is** — Yellow Lady's Slipper, two
  prickly pears, Ball Cactus, Hooded Ladies' Tresses. Raised explicitly that
  iNaturalist is *not* obscuring most of them (median coordinate uncertainty on
  Lady's Slipper is 31 m); the decision is that these records are already public
  at that precision on iNaturalist and GBIF. Reversible later by a withhold list
  shaped like `site_facets.WITHHELD_ROLES`.

### Open, in the order they matter

1. **The map visual.** Not a palette problem — see the marks-per-cell table
   above. The strongest option is to **shade each cell by how many records it
   holds and draw few or no individual marks**, which turns 4,000 overlapping
   dots into 361 legible squares and is what most modern atlases do. That wants
   a count per cell: a one-line change in `species_range.occupied_cells` plus an
   offline re-run of `seed_species_ranges.py`. Alternatives are subsampling the
   marks (honest only if the caption says so) or much smaller, lighter marks.
2. **F146 — shared geometry and client-side rendering.** A species page is
   **888 KB of which 846 KB is the ecoregion map**: 2,291 polygons repeated
   verbatim on all 430 pages, and the built site is **421 MB**. Moving the
   geometry to one cached file takes pages to roughly 40 KB and makes the toggle
   natural rather than bolted on.
3. **F147 — publish the points, wire the toggle.** 539,129 publishable points,
   ~7.5 MB, ~14 KB per species. Needs a shipped point file (the cache is a dev
   artefact), a `/method/` disclosure, and a `DATA_SOURCES.md` row saying why the
   occurrence rule differs from the photograph rule.
4. **VASCAN** — waiting on the author to download the archive and run
   `--from-archive`, then send the ingest report. The canary is *Amelanchier
   alnifolia*: it must land in `confirm`, and the `undetermined` bucket must
   collapse from 173 toward zero. Acting on the report is its own increment,
   because a superseded name moves plant ids, `plant_fauna_master.json` keys and
   public URLs.
