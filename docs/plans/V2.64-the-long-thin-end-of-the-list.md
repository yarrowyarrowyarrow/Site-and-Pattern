## The tail is not more of the same, and that is the whole plan

V2.63 read the top 200 by edge count and held the rest. The obvious reading of
"do the tail" is *another afternoon of the same work*. Measuring it first said
otherwise:

```
median edges per tail species     2
species with exactly one edge   457
genera with no rule at all      572 of 609
```

There is **no shortlist to take**. The concentration argument that bounded
V2.63 — top 200 covers 57% of the edges — has nothing left to bound. The tail
is read whole or not at all.

And the *kind* of relationship changes underneath it:

| | top 200 | the tail |
|---|---|---|
| `nectar` | 1,286 | 547 |
| `pollen` | 314 | 159 |
| `larval_host` | 131 | **217** |

Host records go from a tenth of the edges to nearly a quarter, and the animals
behind them are leaf miners, gall formers, seedhead flies, aphids, adelgids
and sap feeders rather than pollinators. That is the food web *underneath* the
flowers and it belongs in a habitat app — a leaf mine or a spindle gall is
recorded by finding it on the plant, which makes it better-evidenced than most
flower visits, not worse.

But it makes the per-animal question a second one. Not only **is it native**,
but **is this a relationship where the plant gives something**.

## A visit is not a reward, in four more shapes

V2.60 refused three `flowersVisitedBy` records for a White-crowned Sparrow —
true about where the bird was, false about what the flower gave it. The tail is
full of the same shape, and each one is held rather than admitted with a
`nectar` edge that misdescribes it:

- **Ambush bugs, crab spiders, assassin bugs, robber flies, mantidflies.**
  They are on the flower to eat the pollinators.
- **Dragonflies and damselflies** — six genera. An odonate takes nothing from a
  plant at any life stage. Every record here is an insect that landed.
- **Arachnids**, for a second and independent reason: the taxon vocabulary has
  no word for them, so the fetch files a jumping spider as `other_insect` —
  a false statement in a field the app prints next to the animal's name.
  Same for the eriophyid gall mites, whose galls *are* real relationships.

Green lacewings go the other way and are included: the larva is a predator, but
the **adult** feeds on pollen and nectar, so the flower record is a reward.

## Where the introduced species were hiding

Five automated nativity signals have now failed. This is where the sixth
would have: the tail's rejects are not exotic bumblebees, they are the
**everyday European insects of a settled landscape**, none of them flagged by
GBIF, every one of them holding real Alberta records.

- Carpet beetles (three *Anthrenus*), cluster flies, the Stable Fly,
  a Palaearctic muscid, an Oystershell Scale, the Bird Cherry-Oat Aphid
- The **Meadow Spittlebug** — now the commonest spittlebug in North America
- Five genera of European weevils, arriving with clover, alfalfa and nursery
  stock
- A **Chinese Mantis**, sold in garden centres as a beneficial, which eats
  pollinators
- Two deliberate **biocontrol releases** — *Larinus* and *Urophora*, put here
  on purpose to eat knapweed. Established, effective, and not prairie wildlife.

**39 rejects, 103 holds, 838 includes.** The rejects take 95 candidate edges.

## Birds are decided in one place

All 30 birds in the tail were **already** in `curate_birds.py` — 13 include, 6
hold, 11 reject — and the included ones have no row only because every one of
their records failed the diet routing. So no bird is in this file's pin, and a
test asserts it. Two tables that can disagree about the same animal is worse
than one.

## The bug the suite caught, again immediately

The first apply wrote **20 animals connected to nothing**. They were admitted
honestly — native, in range, real. Their edges then died downstream at a gate
this review does not run, almost all of them at the larval-host gate: adult
nectaring records GloBI files as `eatenBy`, which the fetcher's life-stage
default promotes into host claims, and which V2.59's Monarch bug taught this
repo to refuse.

`tests/test_derived_edges.test_cuckoo_bees_are_no_longer_orphans` caught it as
orphan fauna in the seeded DB — the right place to *notice* and the wrong place
to fix. `curate_birds.py` had solved this in V2.60 and reported it; this file
had not. `writable()` now asks the real `ingest_fauna_edges.review` which
animals survive, and 18 moths get no row. Orphans in the seeded DB: **20 → 2**.

The subtle half is that an animal which **already** has a seeded edge must
count as connected. On a re-run its candidates are all dropped as "already
seeded", which looks identical to never having had any — and without that
clause, re-applying the review would work exactly once.

## Genus defaults, species overrides — at 580 rules

**580 genus rules and 98 species overrides decide 1,180 animals.** That ratio
is the honest shape of the knowledge: I can say *Phyllonorycter* is a speciose
native leaf-mining genus and cannot place `Phyllonorycter nipigon`
specifically. A genus rule that is wrong is wrong visibly, in one line, for
everything under it.

Where a genus splits, it gets read species by species and the split is written
down — *Adelphocoris rapidus* (native) against *A. lineolatus* (European),
*Agrilus anxius* against *A. cyanescens*, *Choreutis diana* against
*C. pariana*. **One override is for a species that is not in this tail at all:**
*Coccinella septempunctata*. A lady-beetle rule that only holds because the
wrong species happens to be absent is not a rule.

## 143 more lepidoptera needed a kind

`lepidoptera_attributes.kind` is what the 3D scene and the habitat panel read
to tell a butterfly from a moth, and `lep_attributes()` falls back to
butterfly/day for an unknown genus — a safe default and a **silent** one that
would have filed 138 moths as butterflies without failing anything. A test now
asserts the table is complete rather than merely defaulted.

`activity` follows the **family norm** and departs from it only for documented
day-fliers: the clearwings, the buckmoths, *Orgyia* males, the Spear-marked
Black Moth, the day-flying noctuids. Stating the rule matters more than any
single call. Flight season stays the literal `"unknown"`, as in V2.63.

## What could not be verified here

- **Every one of the 838 tail calls**, and the confidence is genuinely lower
  than V2.63's. That review was mostly bumblebees and butterflies; this one is
  *Phyllonorycter*, *Lasioglossum* and acalyptrate flies. It is exactly why 580
  of the decisions are genus rules and 98 are species-level.
- **Whether the admitted edges are individually true.** The citation work makes
  each one checkable; none was checked against a flora.
- **The 18 orphans are not gone, only unwritten.** Their records are adult
  nectaring misfiled as host claims, and F128's 41% life-stage data is the
  route to recovering them — still not taken.
- **The remaining hold queue**: 103 species from the tail plus V2.63's 17, and
  the 42 trinomials. Held on purpose, and none of it is waiting on a decision
  anybody has to make.
