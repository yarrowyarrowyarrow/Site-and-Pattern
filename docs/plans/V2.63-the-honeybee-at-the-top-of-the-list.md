## Why a fifth review and not a fifth filter

The AB/SK occurrence gate (V2.61) and the introduced review (V2.62) took the
held queue from 2,898 animals to 1,252. Before writing 1,252 fauna rows, the
top of that list was worth a look:

| species | AB | SK | edges | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***Apis mellifera*** | 3,671 | 451 | **133** | the honeybee, straight through |
| *Bombus impatiens* | 55 | 5 | 89 | eastern; shipped for greenhouses |
| *Bombus vosnesenskii* | 11 | 0 | 73 | Pacific coast |
| *Bombus prshewalskyi* | 4 | **1,125** | 57 | **Central Asian** |
| *Bombus bimaculatus* | 0 | 406 | 54 | eastern North America |

**The honeybee is the instructive one.** V2.62's review only saw the 24 species
GBIF *flagged*, and after the strict Canada filter *Apis mellifera* came back
`unstated` — so it was never a candidate for review at all. It would have
entered the catalogue as a native Alberta bee with 133 relationships, in an app
whose entire premise is native plants for native pollinators.

And a Central Asian bumblebee with 1,125 Saskatchewan records is not a range.
It is a data error, and no threshold distinguishes it from a real one.

That is **four automated nativity signals that have failed** — the occurrence
facet, the checklist distributions, strict country filtering, and now the
occurrence floor — against **three hand reviews that worked**. The lesson has
stopped being about GBIF.

## Bounded by concentration

The edges are concentrated enough to make reading tractable:

```
top  100 species → 2,693 of 6,664 edges (40%)
top  200 species → 3,864 (57%)
all 1,252        → 6,664
```

200 covers well over half the value for an afternoon. **The 1,052-species tail
is held, not admitted.** Its animals are real and its edges are probably fine —
and "probably fine" is not what this catalogue claims about a `documented` edge.

Result: **159 include, 17 hold, 24 reject.** The rejects take 747 candidate
edges with them, 133 of them the honeybee's.

## Genus defaults, species overrides

**Because that is the actual shape of the knowledge.** For *Syrphus* or
*Hylaeus* I can say the genus is well represented across the prairies and
cannot place every species; for *Bombus* I can place most species one at a
time. Writing 200 flat, independent calls would have implied a uniform
confidence that does not exist.

A genus rule that is wrong is wrong *visibly*, in one line, for everything
under it. 47 genus rules and 84 species overrides cover all 200.

## The bug the guard test caught, immediately

`_shortlist()` computed "top 200 by edge count **among animals not yet in the
catalogue**". So the instant 159 of them were written, the window slid down and
pulled in **42 species nobody had read** — including *Megachile rotundata* and
*Vespula germanica*, both introduced.

Nothing bad reached the seed data (the apply predated the shift), but a review
that reviews a different 200 every time it runs is not a review. The list is
now **pinned**: 200 names, written down, and a test asserts the count.

Pinning then exposed a second gap in the same breath — *Polistes fuscatus* had
no genus rule and no override, so it fell silently to "not reviewed" inside a
list whose whole claim is that it was read. One line, and a test that fails on
any species in `REVIEWED` without a verdict.

## Naming: the binomial is the name

The author's call, and the honest one. Of the 159 written, **56 have an
accepted English name** and **103 keep their binomial**.

For a solitary bee that is not a compromise — *Andrena milwaukeensis* is what
that animal is called, and entomologists use it as the name. Generating
"Milwaukee Mining Bee" would have been writing a name nobody wrote, which is
the failure this codebase has spent five increments removing.

## What is refused, and why it is refused

- **Introduced**, whatever their records: honeybee, European Wool Carder Bee,
  European Paper Wasp, Thick-legged Hoverfly, Museum Beetle.
- **Out of range**: seven bumblebees (eastern, Pacific, Central Asian),
  Eastern Tiger Swallowtail (Alberta's is *P. canadensis*, already in the
  catalogue), Eastern Comma, Delaware Skipper.
- **Trinomials** — seven of them. The catalogue keys fauna on binomials, so a
  subspecies row resolves against nothing. This is the `Picoides pubescens`
  lesson from V2.60 arriving five more times.
- **Held** where I could not call it: *Eristalis arbustorum* (the literature
  disagrees on whether North American populations are native), *Coenonympha
  california* (ringlet taxonomy has been rearranged and I cannot tell which
  animal this key covers), *Helicoverpa zea* (native to the Americas but a
  migratory crop pest that does not overwinter here).

## Four more bugs the new rows exposed

None was introduced by this increment. All four were latent, waiting for an
animal nobody had described.

**A bee flying in January.** `scene_wildlife._season_months` read a bee's
flight season from `bee_attributes` and treated an empty result as *no
seasonal gate* — so a bee with no recorded phenology was active year-round, in
Alberta, in winter. Only 69 bees and 31 leps have an attributes row, so this
was always the common case; F127 simply made it visible. An unrecorded season
now falls back to the warm months, which is the same P9 move as everywhere
else: absence of a measurement is not licence.

**A potter wasp mistaken for a bat.** `test_no_bats` asserted `"bat" not in
name.lower()` and passed for years, then failed on *Ancistrocerus
adia**bat**us*. Now matched on the creature's form. That is the argument
against testing a taxonomic claim with `in`.

**46 leps with no `kind`.** The 3D scene and the habitat panel read
`lepidoptera_attributes.kind` to tell a butterfly from a moth, and a new lep
without one arrives as `None` and sorts nowhere. Assigned by family, with
`activity` kept separate from `kind` because a Police Car Moth is out in full
sun and a Dingy Cutworm is not. **Flight season is written as the literal
`"unknown"`** — the word the file already accepts for `overwintering_stage` —
rather than an invented range or a silently blank field.

**Three morphology guards pinned to a roster.** They asserted exactly 69 bees
and 31 leps, the set the bench was written against. But morphology is
*optional* and has a documented fallback — `scene_wildlife` says so at the top
— so a row with a `kind` and no wingspan is the intended shape. The guards now
apply to what has been described, and assert that the described set never
**shrinks**: losing a description is rot, not having one yet is a backlog.

## What could not be verified here

- **Every one of the 200 calls.** Unsourced, like the 142 rows already shipped
  and the two reviews before this. My confidence is genuinely lower on
  *Andrena* and *Lasioglossum* species ranges than on birds, which is exactly
  why those are genus rules rather than 29 individual assertions.
- **The 1,052-species tail**, entirely. Held, and worth another afternoon:
  2,800 more edges sit behind it.
- **Whether the reviewed species' edges are individually true.** The citation
  work (V2.62) makes each one checkable; none was checked against a flora.
