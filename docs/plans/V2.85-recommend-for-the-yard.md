# V2.85 — recommend for the yard, not the region

*F154 and F155 from the V2.83 review: the design generator suggested plants
that fit an ecoregion rather than a yard, and on a yard it barely suggested any.
The bridge between seventeen increments of catalogue work and what the app
actually recommends.*

## What was wrong, measured

The V2.83 review ran the offline generator (the path anyone without a local AI
model gets) on a 12 × 18 m front yard, 216 m², in Edmonton. Traced stage by
stage before changing anything:

**Too few plants, for two reasons, both in the placement engine.**

1. **Capacity was counted in 6 m anchor cells.** A 216 m² yard is six of them,
   and the density pass targeted 60% of six, so it never expanded anything.
   The AI path calls the same `_apply_density`, so its designs on a yard were
   just as thin.
2. **A large tree took the yard.** Placement anchors groups in layer order,
   trees first, and reserves the cells around each plant. On the pollinator run
   the first tree was Bur Oak, canopy 15 m, which reserved nearly every anchor:
   **two plants placed** before the critic topped the design up to five.

**The wrong plants, for three reasons.**

1. **Nativity was the Alberta flag wherever the pin was**, and any goal with a
   filter of its own dropped even that (`filters_for_goals(goals) or
   {"native_only": True}`). Every row in the catalogue is native to Alberta,
   Saskatchewan or both, so the real bug is province: Bur Oak, which VASCAN
   records for Saskatchewan only, reached Edmonton; a Regina yard got
   Alberta-only fescues and could never get a Saskatchewan-only native.
2. **"Fits the site" meant at least three records anywhere in the ecoregion.**
   Aspen Parkland runs from Calgary to Winnipeg; Broomweed (dry south) and
   Alpine Aster (foothills edge) both qualified for Edmonton.
3. **Communities and repairs bypassed the selection.** The fourth community
   picked for the Edmonton yard was the seeded **"Bur Oak Community"**, and the
   critic's gap repairs searched with their own `native_only=True`, which is
   how Moss Phlox, Alberta-only, reached Regina.

## What changed

**`src/site_fit.py` (new, Qt-free, P9)** is the one place the design side reads
the 0.25° occupancy grid the website has drawn since V2.79:

- `province_at(lat, lng)`: AB or SK by the 110th meridian inside the subject
  area, `""` outside it (Winnipeg, Vancouver), where callers keep their old
  behaviour.
- `locality(sci, lat, lng)`: `here` (the yard's grid square or the eight around
  it, about 80 × 50 km at Edmonton), `near` (the next ring), `elsewhere`, or
  `unrecorded`. **A ranking signal, never a filter**, and `unrecorded` ranks
  level with `elsewhere`, because an empty square is unsurveyed as often as
  unoccupied.

**In `src/llm_design.py`, for both the offline and the AI path:**

| Rule | Where |
|---|---|
| Native to the yard's province, whether or not a goal was ticked; the province supersedes the Alberta flag | `_site_filters`, `_native_default`, `_without_superseded_native`, in plant resolution, the palette and the offline pool |
| Nothing whose mature footprint (canopy squared) exceeds 25% of the ground | `_too_big_for` / `_fits_the_area`, in the offline pool and the palette |
| Plants recorded near the yard first, ahead of ecological value within each habit bucket | `_rank_offline_plants(site=)`, and the palette's order, which is what the AI model reads (it sees the first eight per type) |
| Communities pass the same tests: every member native to the province, a centre plant that fits | `_communities_for_site`, both paths |
| The critic's repairs and the goal and wildlife follow-ups search under the same three rules | `_site_scoped_query` wraps `query_plants` for them |
| A yard gets a finer anchor grid: `sqrt(area / 36)`, between 2.5 and 6 m, so ~1,300 m² and up keep 6 m exactly | `_anchor_spacing_m`, in the flat pool, the terrain-scored cell map and `_boundary_capacity` |
| The density target is plants, at 1 per m² of plantable ground × the density fraction, under a ceiling that scales with the density too: 100 sparse, 200 balanced, 300 full (300 was the old flat cap) | `_planting_capacity`, `_density_ceiling`, `_apply_density`, and the AI path's "aim for N plantings" hint |
| The density pass counts the meadow and community plants already committed | `_apply_density(already=)`, `_planned_elsewhere` |
| Trees, shrubs and vines share 35% of the ground by footprint; with four or more species, none exceeds a quarter of the fill | `_apply_density` |
| The offline path's individual species are the seven the meadow did not take, not seven minus the meadow's | `generate_design_offline` |

The last three came out of the first attempt, which is worth recording:
switching capacity to plants filled a Calgary yard with ten chokecherries,
eleven saskatoons and seventeen honeysuckle vines, whose drifts claimed every
anchor, so **the meadow and all three communities were dropped** and species
fell from 16 to 5. Then, once woody plants were budgeted, **one violet took 49
of 110 places**, because only one herbaceous species was left among the
individual picks. And the full suite then caught a fourth:
`test_full_denser_than_sparse` found sparse and full producing the *same*
78-plant design on a 3,600 m² lot, because at one plant per m² even "sparse"
reached the flat 300 ceiling on anything over about 1,000 m², and the
one-species cap held a one-species request at a quarter. The ceiling now scales
with the density and the species cap needs four species to share the fill.
Each was traced, not guessed at.

and no plant outside its province. Generation time is unchanged (about 4.5 s).

## Decided, and worth the author knowing

- **The large-lot change is deliberate.** Counted in plants, a lot of about
  1,000 m² or more reaches its density's ceiling (100 / 200 / 300); a 3,600 m²
  lot at "balanced" went 97 → 196. The anchor grid there is unchanged, and the
  density setting still orders the result on every size of lot.
- **Communities are withheld whole**, not trimmed, when one member is not native
  to the yard's province: dropping a member would change a curated planting's
  geometry. Measured across the 58 seeded communities: **10 withheld on an
  Alberta pin**, six of them only because they include Canada Goldenrod, which
  VASCAN records for Saskatchewan and not Alberta (Alberta's is likely
  *Solidago lepida*); **32 withheld on a Saskatchewan pin**, because most
  seeded communities were written for Alberta, many of them montane. Recorded
  as F160: fixing the community data is cheaper and better than loosening the
  rule.
- **Locality cannot stand in for nativity, and does not try to.** Bur Oak has
  **115 records around Edmonton**, almost certainly planted street and park
  trees that GBIF did not filter as cultivated. The province rule is what keeps
  it out; locality only orders what the rules allow.
- **The 25% footprint share** is a judgement, not a measurement: a 216 m² yard
  can take a Chokecherry but not a Trembling Aspen (canopy about 8 m) or a Bur
  Oak. On acreage the rule does not bind.

## Deliberately not done

- **The composition terms** (`placement_score.aesthetic_score`'s cohesion and
  rhythm bands) still measure at the 6 m scale on a small lot. They carry 15%
  of an anchor's score and apply to every candidate cell alike on a yard, so
  they were left rather than re-tuned blind.
- **The establishment band** (`src/establishment.py`) still reads ecoregion
  counts. It answers *has anyone recorded this growing here* for a design
  already made and would benefit from the same grid; a separate change.
- **The AI path was not run against a model.** Its palette, plant resolution,
  communities and repairs go through the same new rules and are tested with the
  fake client the suite already uses; no Ollama is available here.

## What could not be verified here

- **The GUI.** The generator was exercised headlessly and by the test suite;
  a design was not generated by clicking *Generate Design* on a desktop, and
  with terrain data (which needs the network) the terrain-scored cell map is
  used instead of the flat pool. Both now take the same anchor spacing, but only
  the flat pool ran here.
