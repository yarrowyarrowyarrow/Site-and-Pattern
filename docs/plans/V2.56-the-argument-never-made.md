# V2.56 — the argument the app has never made

**F76 (before / after / in five years) + F75 (cues to care).**
*Design principle P13 — a native planting has to be loved to survive.*
*Design principle P4 — time is the most undervalued design variable.*

## What this set out to do

The app argues superbly that a native yard is **ecologically valuable**. Nobody
converts a lawn on ecological grounds alone, and no spouse, neighbour or HOA
does. Principle 13 exists to name that — beauty is the mechanism the ecology
survives contact with people by — and Group B is the two features that act on it.

It was picked for three converging reasons: it is the backlog's own #1, the only
self-named weakness with nothing blocked on data or a pending decision; **the
design side had not had an increment since V2.42** (V2.53 was cross-cutting,
V2.54 was 3D, V2.55 was Learn); and every ingredient was already built.

## What was measured before deciding

Every F76/F75 ingredient was verified present, not assumed:
`presentation_still.render_request` and the `sidewalk` preset (F69/F77),
`snapshot_timeline.snapshot_years` (F2), `site_photo.feature_from_project`
(F24), `design_critic.critique_lines`, `placement_score`'s `_height_gradient`,
and `lawn_zones`. Four things the survey changed:

**There is no road data anywhere in the app.** `osm_features` fetches trees and
buildings only; `frontage`/`street` appear solely in `onboarding.py` and
`property_data.py`. So F75's "public frontage" cannot be derived.

**`critique_lines` is not displayed anywhere.** It takes a habitat-score dict and
is consumed only by `llm_design.py:1157`, the generate loop. So F75 needed a
project-shaped sibling and a surface of its own, not a new branch inside it.

**`snapshot_years`' floor is 20 years**, so years 1 and 5 can never be capped
away. The plan had assumed a fast-perennial design might cap year 5 down; it
cannot. The fallback stayed in as three defensive lines rather than machinery
built for a case that cannot occur, and says so.

**`scene3d_window` had no room.** At 914 of 950 lines, the ~90-line capture chain
was never going to fit.

## What was decided, and why

### F76

**A Qt-free spec builder plus a `_flow` for the viewer half**, mirroring
`presentation_still` exactly. `before_after.py` decides which panels, which
years, which camera and what to caption them; `before_after_flow.py` sets each
moment and captures it.

**One camera across every panel.** A viewpoint change between two images reads as
a change in the *design*. The only thing allowed to vary across this page is
time. `sidewalk` by default, because the neighbour's-eye view is the argument.

**Years come from `snapshot_timeline`, not from the number 5**, so the timeline
and this page cannot disagree about which years are worth showing.

**The two "before" cases are captioned differently, deliberately.** With the
user's photograph, panel one is *your yard as it is today* — the comparison that
converts somebody. Without it, the honest fallback is the design at year 0, and
its caption says outright that no photograph was added. Letting one stand in
silently for the other is exactly the small dishonesty this codebase keeps
finding in itself.

**The capture chain copies `sprite_gallery_window`**, which solved this first: a
**run token** so a second export cancels the first instead of interleaving into
one page, and **bounded waiting for the baked models**, because capturing early
yields the procedural fallback — which looks like a finished answer and is not
what the app draws.

**The PDF page follows the existing contract.** `export_pdf` already takes a
pixmap rendered elsewhere "because export is synchronous and the viewer's
capture is a callback"; `before_after=` is the same shape. The page geometry was
split into `before_after_layout` so it can be checked without rendering — the
failure it prevents (panels off the page, a caption running under the next
panel) is invisible until someone prints it.

### F75

**Six cues, and one of them is honestly a prompt.** Nothing in the app represents
a sign, so `SIGN` cannot be measured, and a geometric proxy for it would be a
measurement of nothing wearing the costume of evidence. It is offered as a
suggestion and reports `present=None`.

**The frontage reuses the assumption the app already makes.** The `sidewalk`
camera picks the south edge, "because that is the street side of a north-facing
prairie lot far more often than not." F75 makes the *same* assumption so the two
cannot disagree, and **names it in the line it affects**. Only one of the six
cues depends on it — a test asserts the other five never mention it, which is
what makes a wrong guess cost one line rather than the feature.

**Absence is not a finding.** A design with two plants is not badly graded, it is
unjudgeable; unknown heights vote on nothing; the tally excludes anything it
could not judge, so the ratio never implies a verdict that was not reached.

**Grasses are excluded from the showy repeat.** `flower_colour` buckets them as
wind-pollinated and their swatch is the *absence* of a showy flower; counting
them would credit the design for a repeat nobody can see.

**Surfaced in `on_this_design_panel`** beside "Where to grow next", computed in
`app.py` where the project lives and handed over as finished lines — the panel
stays presentation-only, like the lawn tally and the cost breakdown.

## What was found along the way

**F123 — the presentation still has never reached the PDF.** `export_pdf` takes
`still_pixmap`, `_draw_presentation_still` draws a full page from it, and
`app.py`'s `_on_export_pdf` calls `export_pdf(path, project, enriched, structs,
notes, pixmap)` and never passes it. F69's page has been unreachable from the
app's own Export PDF since it shipped — the same shape as `zone_description()`
having no caller, found in V2.55. **Reported, not fixed**: the 3D window saves a
still to a file and does not retain the pixmap, so the fix is a small design
decision rather than a wiring change. Backlog row F123.

**A corrupt site photo used to drop the before-panel silently**, leaving a
two-panel page with no *before* — the whole argument gone. Found by the test
suite. It now falls back to the year-0 render in place, carrying the page's
camera so it still compares like with like.

**A test that segfaulted the suite.** The obvious way to test the capture chain
is to pump a Qt event loop until the callback lands. Alone it passed; with the
full suite's modules imported it **aborted inside `processEvents`** — located
with `python -X faulthandler`, which pointed straight at the test's own pump.
The chain's single scheduling hop is now indirected through `_later`, and the
test replaces it with a queue it controls. That is strictly better than a real
loop: immediate execution would finish the chain before `capture_panels`
returned, making the run-token guard untestable, whereas a held queue catches a
run genuinely mid-flight. What it gives up is proof that `QTimer.singleShot` is
wired — one line, where sequencing bugs do not live.

## What could not be verified here

- **F76's images.** No GPU, and this container segfaults tearing down
  `QWebEngineView`. The specs, year selection, captions, camera agreement, page
  geometry and the whole capture *sequence* are tested; what a panel actually
  looks like is not.
- **The PDF page has never been printed.** The layout arithmetic is asserted to
  fit Letter at 300 dpi; whether three panels at that size are legible is a
  judgement needing paper.
- **F75 is fully verifiable headless** and is.

Full suite: **2927 tests, OK, 98 skipped**, with `libegl1` and `PyQt6-WebEngine`
installed first.
