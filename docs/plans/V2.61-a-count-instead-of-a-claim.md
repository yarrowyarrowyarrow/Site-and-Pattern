# V2.61 — a count instead of a claim

**F128** (a named study per relationship) **+ the AB/SK nativity gate.**
Branch `V2.61`, cut from `V2.60`. No schema change — no data moved yet.
Next free ID stays **F130**.

## The ask

> *"I do want proper citations for each relationship. I also want only flora
> and fauna native to AB and SK."*

Both are answerable, neither is answerable **here**: the container denies
GloBI and answers 403 to CONNECT for `api.gbif.org`. So this increment is the
machinery and the gates, and [`V2.61-runbook`](V2.61-runbook.md) is the two
fetches to run on your side. Same split as F125, which worked.

## Where the two asks actually stood

Measured before designing anything, because the flora half turned out to be
nearly done and the fauna half turned out not to be the job it looked like.

**Flora — already AB/SK.** All 432 rows in `plants_master.json` carry
`native_provinces`: 356 `AB,SK`, 75 `AB`, one `SK,MB`. Nothing to remove. The
five rows in `garden_plants.json` are cultivated on purpose — four fruit trees
and a bee-balm cultivar for food-forest designs — and are **left alone**, as a
product decision rather than a data one. Say the word and they go.

**Fauna — cleaner than expected, and unsourced.** No honeybee, no Cabbage
White, no House Sparrow; the catalogue was authored carefully. *Eristalis
tenax* is present and correctly flagged `ab_native = 0`. The actual problem is
narrower and worse:

> **142 of 167 fauna rows assert nativity as a boolean nobody sourced**, with
> no province data at all. Only the 25 birds added in V2.60 carry
> `native_provinces`.

And 2,898 held animals need that same call made 2,898 times, which no amount
of care makes tractable by hand.

## F128 — a named study per relationship

All 2,439 GloBI edges cite `globi`, because GloBI only fills `study_title`
when asked for observations rather than distinct interactions. That was the
V2.59 discovery and it is the one thing an observation-mode re-fetch fixes.

`--observations` adds `includeObservations=true`, which changes what a row
*is*: one per observed record rather than one per distinct interaction.
Several times the rows, and it buys three fields:

| field | what it unblocks |
|---|---|
| **citation** | the entire ask — a paper instead of an aggregator |
| **life stage** | the 700 `larval_host` records refused for want of it |
| **body part** | the sapsucker and browse records V2.60 had to drop |

### Reading columns through aliases, because nobody here can check

V2.59 hard-coded `study_citation`, which does not exist — GloBI returns
`study_title` — and **the test fixture repeated the same guess**, so the suite
passed while both were wrong and every edge arrived uncited.

The lesson was not "use `study_title`". It was that a container which cannot
reach the API must not encode a guess as if it were knowledge. So each field
is now a list of plausible spellings, the run reads whichever is populated,
and `--probe` reports **every** spelling with the percentage of rows that fill
it. A wrong guess degrades to a missing field instead of a silent wholesale
rejection.

The probe also refuses to be encouraging: if no spelling of `citation` is
populated, it says the run is not worth making.

### What "proper citation" is allowed to mean

GloBI hands over one free-text string; `sources_master.json` holds structured
records. `study_record()` parses what it can — a four-digit year, the authors
before it, a stable slug for the key — and **records that it parsed**:
`record_confidence: unverified`, with a note saying the author and year are a
split on the first year found and may be wrong even where the title is right.

That is still a real upgrade. A reader gets a named study they can look up
instead of "an aggregator has a record somewhere". It is not a transcription
from the work, and the bibliography has a field for saying so.

Edges that match no observation keep `globi`. Hand-authored citations are
never touched — those are somebody's reading of a book.

## The nativity gate

`scripts/fetch_fauna_nativity.py` asks GBIF, per animal, how many
georeferenced records sit inside Alberta and inside Saskatchewan, plus the
`establishmentMeans` facet.

**One request per species per province.** `limit=0` returns the match count
and no records, and the facet rides along free — so 3,065 animals cost ~6,100
requests rather than a paged harvest. The ecoregion seeder's throttle, its 429
backoff and its *a failure is not an absence* rule are **imported, not
retyped**: one rate-limit policy for the repo, and a rate-limited species is
left out of the file rather than recorded as "found nowhere".

### Occurrence is not nativity, and the output never pretends otherwise

A European Starling has tens of thousands of Alberta records. A Drone Fly is
abundant here and came from Europe. So the assessment keeps three things
apart:

- **`provinces`** — where records exist. Measured.
- **`origin`** — native / introduced / **unstated**, and unstated is the
  common case, because `establishmentMeans` is empty far more often than not.
  An empty field is the absence of a claim, not a claim of nativeness (P9 —
  absent is not estimated).
- **`verdict`** — what the gate should do.

`introduced` is refused however many records back it. That is the case that
makes the distinction load-bearing rather than pedantic.

### The floor is imported, not chosen

The first draft picked 5 and claimed it matched `ecoregion_ranges`. It did
not — `MIN_RECORDS` is 3. Rather than fix the number, the gate now imports it.
The repo's rule is that a module does not own a threshold another module
already owns; two numbers here would mean a species is "present enough" for
one feature and not the other, and nobody finds that for a year.

### What the gate deliberately does not do

**It does not re-litigate animals already in the catalogue.** Their nativity
was decided when the rows were authored, by a person. Overruling that on
occurrence counts — which cannot tell native from merely present — would be
the gate exceeding what it can measure. A test pins this with deliberately
damning data for a species the catalogue holds.

**With no fetch file it stands down** rather than rejecting everything, back
to V2.60 behaviour. A missing measurement is not evidence of absence.

### The Alberta box overshoots, and says so

Both provinces are surveyed rectangles — 49°N to 60°N, meridians at 110°W and
101.36°W — which is why a bounding box works at all. The exception:
**Alberta's western border follows the continental divide south of 54°N**, not
120°W, so the box takes in a sliver of British Columbia up to ~1.5° wide at
the southern end. Those records are in the Rockies within kilometres of the
border. Accepted rather than corrected, and written down rather than
discovered later.

## The test that matters

Everything else exercises `nativity_verdict` in isolation.
`test_the_gate_actually_fires_when_the_fetch_has_run` writes a real nativity
file to disk and drives the real `review()` through it, asserting that a
well-recorded species passes while an absent one, an introduced one and an
unmeasured one are each refused **for their own stated reason**.

A gate that is written but never wired is precisely how V2.59 shipped
`fruit_food` on conifers. Testing the helper would not have caught that.

## What could not be verified here

- **Both fetches.** Egress denied; the runbook is the handover. What is tested
  is every mapping, every gate and every parse, against fixtures.
- **How many of the 2,898 held animals survive.** Unknowable until the fetch
  runs. It could be most of them or very few.

## Postscript — the probes ran, and two of three assumptions were wrong

Written the same day, because a plan that records a prediction and not its
outcome is worth nothing.

**`study_title`: 100% populated.** F128 is go — this is the whole ask and it
is there. But the sampled value was
`https://scan-bugs.org/portal/collections/individual/index…`: a museum
specimen record, not a paper. Much of GloBI is collection aggregation. Those
are still real citations — one openable record, strictly more than "an
aggregator has it" — so they are filed as `kind: specimen_record` with the
portal as the title and **one key per portal**, or the bibliography would grow
an entry per specimen.

**`life_stage`: 6%.** Of the 700 `larval_host` records refused for want of a
stage, a few dozen become resolvable. Real, and much smaller than hoped.

**Body part: absent under every spelling.** `/interaction` does not return it
in any mode. Recovering the sapsucker and mammal-browse records this way is
**dead, not deferred** — a fact worth more than the feature was, because it
stops the next person spending an evening on it. The claim is struck from the
fetcher's docstring rather than left as an aspiration.

**And the starling test failed.** `Sturnus vulgaris` — 267,995 Alberta records,
introduced by any account — came back `review / unstated`, with **no
`establishmentMeans` facet at all** on any of the three probe species. GBIF
occurrence records do not carry the field in practice, so faceting them can
never separate introduced from native, and the gate as designed would have
waved every introduced species straight through on its record count.

The occurrence half is excellent: monarch 370/596, desert quail 0/0. Presence
is solved. So the fix splits the question rather than abandoning it —
**occurrence for *present*, checklists for *introduced*.**
`/species/match` → `/species/{key}/distributions` returns per-country rows
from checklist datasets including **GRIIS**, the register built for exactly
this question. Two more requests, spent only on species that already have
AB/SK records, since one with none is refused on the count alone.

That is the probe earning its keep for the third increment running. It cost
seconds; the run it prevented would have cost hours and produced a gate that
quietly approved starlings.
