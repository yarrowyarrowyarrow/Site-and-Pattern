# V3.02 — Keyboard and screen reader

*The first half of **F195**, step 5 of the V2.98 review of perusing, selecting and
placing plants and communities: "An accessibility baseline". The owner asked for
the next step after V3.01: "Continue". The step is rated L, and its findings fall
in two halves: what a keyboard and a screen reader can do, and what can be seen
(text size, contrast, target size, the map's colours and legends). This release
is the first half; V3.03 is meant to be the second.*

## What was wrong

Measured on V3.01 (119fcc1), the example design open at 1366 × 768, with the
accessibility tree read through AT-SPI (what Orca reads on Linux) and the keyboard
driven with xdotool.

Some of the review's findings had already gone. Its four steps so far named every
filter (V3.00, V3.01), gave a plant row its facts (V3.00), stopped Tab from arming
the map (V2.99) and replaced the painted card with a page (V3.00). The Browse panel
now has one unnamed control, its scroll bar. What was left:

**What a screen reader hears.**

| Where | Unnamed, and labelled by nothing |
|---|---|
| Start screen | all five main choices (Recover, Continue, Learn, Design, Open): "button". The words are in child labels |
| Plants → Plant Communities | 12: five filter dropdowns and their text fields, the search box, the community tree |
| Structures | 3: the search box, the list, a size box |
| Planning | 1: a number box |
| Site, Analysis, Learn, Browse | none. Their fields are labelled through their form rows, which AT-SPI reports as *labelled by* |

**What a keyboard can do.**

- **No focus is visible outside Browse.** The panels style their own borders, and a
  widget's own stylesheet beats its window's whatever the specificity, so Qt's own
  focus drawing is gone and a window-wide `:focus` rule would lose too. V3.00 gave
  Browse its own focus borders; nothing else has any.
- **The map cannot be used.** Leaflet pans with the arrow keys once the map has focus
  (it has `tabindex="0"`), but every tool acts on a click: placing a plant or a
  structure, a boundary's corners, a measurement, a note.
- **Single letters act from anywhere.** With keyboard focus on Plant Communities →
  New Community, pressing B started drawing a boundary and A switched to the
  Analysis tab (measured). The review's "typing *aster* switched me to Planning" is
  this. The window's `keyPressEvent` takes B, P, G, S, A, T, M, N and L unmodified
  whenever no focused control eats the key, and **Delete and Backspace** delete
  every selected map item the same way. None of it is listed anywhere.
- **On This Design's lists never take focus** (`NoFocus`), so a keyboard cannot
  reach the list of what is in the design.
- **The community builder's grid is mouse-only**: a member is added by a click,
  removed by a right-click and moved by a drag. A keyboard cannot add one plant.
- **The side panel's collapse strip** is `NoFocus`. Ctrl+\ does the same and is in
  the View menu.
- The review's "section toggles are unfocusable by design" were
  `collapsible_panel.CollapsiblePanel`'s. Nothing has used that class since V2.98
  moved Placement into the bar.

## The change

### Focus you can see, everywhere

One ring follows keyboard focus in every window (`src/focus_ring.py`). It shows
while the keyboard is in use and hides on a mouse press, the way a browser's
`:focus-visible` does: a ring on every clicked button would be noise to a mouse
user, and its absence is the bug for a keyboard user.

**The first build drew it wrong, and the keyboard walk is what showed it.** It
used `QFocusFrame` as Qt draws it under Fusion: *under* the control, visible only
in a two-pixel margin around it. Wherever a layout packs a control against its
parent's edge, that margin is clipped. A Tab walk measured which sides survived:
the search field showed only its bottom edge, the plant list only its top, the
side tab bars only their bottoms, and the panel's scroll area and the map nothing
at all. The ring is now drawn **over** the control, just inside its edge, by
giving the frame a style that says so (`_AboveStyle`): it sits in the nearest
window, toolbar or scroll viewport, so it is clipped exactly as the control is
and no more. It is **two-toned**, two pixels of #ffe082 and one of #1a2a1a inside,
because the yellow alone is about 1.3:1 on a white field and the dark line alone
would vanish on the dark panels. A test reads the pixels of all four edges of a
field packed edge to edge in a scroll area; it fails on the first build.

**The map draws its own.** Focus inside the map moves between the page's own
controls (the map, the area label, zoom, the attribution links, the legend), which
Qt sees as one widget throughout, so the window's ring leaves it alone and the page
draws one (`html/map/09-keyboard.js`). The browser's own outline could not do it:
Leaflet sets it one pixel *outside* the map, and the map fills the page, so the most
used control in the app was the one Tab stop with no visible focus. The page's ring
is the same two tones, inside the edge, above the panes and the controls.

### Names

Every control in the table above gets a name, and the start screen's rows get
their note as a description ("Continue", "Example — Front Yard Lawn Conversion").
A guard test walks the real window, every side tab and sub-tab, and fails on any
focusable control that has no name, no text and no label naming it, so the next
panel cannot ship without them.

Three kinds of control no form label could name were left, found by that guard
and by the AT-SPI scan rather than by the review: each panel's scroll area (a Tab
stop, so text no control leads to can be paged through); each tab widget, which
hands its focus to its tab bar but stays in the tree as the bar's parent, two
unnamed "fillers" on every side tab; and the map's render widget, which is what
Tab actually lands on. The first two are named after the tab they sit in once the
window is built (`src/accessible_names.py`), so a new panel is covered without
anyone remembering. `MapWidget` names the third "Map", and names its replacement
when QtWebEngine makes a new one after a renderer restart.

### The map by keyboard

Every map tool acts on one function, `onMapClick`, so the keyboard needs one
key, not one per tool: **Enter on the focused map acts at its centre**. It
places the plant or structure, adds the boundary's next corner, measures, drops
the note. **Shift+Enter finishes** what a double-click finishes (a boundary, a
hedgerow, a shape, a fill area, a contour). Arrow keys pan and + and − zoom,
which Leaflet already did and nothing said. While a tool is active and the
keyboard is in use, a mark shows the map's centre, and the cursor footprint V2.99
drew under the mouse follows it. Enter on a control inside the map (zoom, the
legend) still presses that control. The map tells a screen reader all of this
when it arrives, including how to leave.

Leaflet makes every marker a Tab stop and a "button", interactive or not, and this
map draws its labels as markers: a boundary's edge lengths took six presses of Tab
before the keyboard left the map. Markers are no longer Tab stops unless they do
something (the area label, which cycles its units), which took the map from 13 Tab
stops to 7.

### F6: between the map and the panel

The walk then measured the trip a keyboard user makes for every plant: **18 presses
of Tab from the plant list to the map** while placing (the placement bar, both
toolbars, then the map). **F6** and Shift+F6 now move between the map and the side
panel, the key that moves between the panes of a window on Windows and in
browsers (`keyboard_help.PaneSwitch`). It returns to the control you left in the
panel, so list, map, list is two presses, and from anywhere else it goes to the
map. The placing prompt says so: "Click the map, or press Enter on it (F6 takes
you there)".

### Shortcuts that wait for the map

The single letters and Delete/Backspace act only while the map has focus, as the
review asked. That is WCAG 2.1.4's "active only on focus". **Help → Keyboard
shortcuts** lists every key, read from one table (`src/keyboard_help.py`) that the
key handler also reads, so the list cannot drift from what the keys do; the menus'
keys are read from the window's own actions. P and G had done nothing at all:
they called `setCurrentWidget` on the side tabs with a panel that sits inside the
Plants tab, which Qt ignores. They open every tab level now.

### Ctrl+Shift+Z, which never redid anything

Listing the menus' keys showed Redo's twice. The window bound Ctrl+Shift+Z and
Ctrl+Y and then the platform's Redo keys, with a comment saying "Qt drops
duplicates". It does not: two bindings of one key are *ambiguous*, and Qt fires
neither. Pressing Ctrl+Shift+Z logged "Ambiguous shortcut overload" and redid
nothing, three times out of three. On Linux, Ctrl+Y was bound once, so it should
have worked; it was not tried before the fix. By Qt's documented
bindings, Windows' Redo keys are Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z, so both were doubled
there, and the offscreen test platform, which reports the same pair, shows exactly
that. Each key is bound once now (`keyboard_help.distinct_keys`), and a test fails
on any key the window binds twice.

### Lists and the builder

On This Design's two lists take focus: Enter or Space does what a click does
(select and frame on the map), and the Menu key opens the row's menu. In the
community builder, Enter in its plant list (the gesture that places a plant in
Browse) adds the plant to the community at the next free spot, and Delete in
Members removes one; the builder's hint says both. The toolbar's buttons, which a
`QToolBar` makes unfocusable, take Tab. The side panel's collapse strip takes
focus and says which way it goes. `CollapsiblePanel`, unused since V2.98, is
deleted.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **What can be seen**: text at 10–11 px (92% of Plant Communities' text), contrast
  under 4.5:1 (chips 4.06:1, counts 4.07–4.17:1, the mix hint 4.01:1, the AB badge
  4.18:1), targets under 24 px (13 of Communities' 19 controls), community plants
  at 1.09:1 against the yard fill, and no legend for either map colour scheme.
  V3.03, the second half of F195.
- **Moving a member in the builder by keyboard.** "Auto-arrange by layer" lays them
  out from the keyboard, and a placed member can be removed and added again. The
  layout itself is not a Tab stop: everything it does by mouse, the two lists do
  by keyboard.
- **Turning the single-letter shortcuts off.** Scoping them to the map meets the
  WCAG criterion; a switch would be a second answer to the same question. It
  leaves the review's own example as it was: typing "blue" *with the map focused*
  still starts a boundary and opens the legend, because the map having focus is
  exactly when the letters are meant to act. What changed is how focus gets there:
  only by clicking the map, Tab or F6, never by typing in a panel. If a mouse user
  who clicked the map and then typed a plant's name keeps doing this, a switch is
  the answer, and it would be a small one.
- **F6 with the side panel collapsed** stays on the map; Ctrl+\ opens the panel.
- **The pattern buttons** (Single, Row, Grid, Circle, Fill area) are named by their
  words, with their tooltip as the description and "Placement pattern" on their
  container. Only the chosen one is a Tab stop and the arrows move between them,
  which is Qt's rule for an exclusive group and the right one.

## How it is checked

- `tests/test_keyboard_baseline.py` (new, 25): the ring follows the keyboard and
  not the mouse, shows all four edges of a control packed against its parent (read
  in pixels; fails on the first build), and leaves the web view to its page; the
  shortcut table (letters unmodified only, distinct, all on the help page, the map
  has focus only when it or a child does, `show_panel` opens every level, a key
  bound twice listed once, the page scrolls); F6 there and back to the control left;
  containers named by their nearest tab; the builder (the next free spot, Enter
  adds, Delete removes, names); On This Design's lists; the start screen's names;
  the collapse strip; `CollapsiblePanel` gone.
- `tests/test_map_keyboard.py` (new, 14): `09-keyboard.js` run in node against a
  stubbed map. Enter acts at the centre for each of the eleven click tools and for
  no other mode, Shift+Enter finishes, Enter on a control inside the map is that
  control's, the centre mark and the previews follow the keyboard and give way to
  the mouse, the ring's class follows the keyboard and its rule sits inside the edge
  and over everything, F6 lands on the map itself, and what a screen reader hears.
- `tests/test_app_smoke.py`, on the real window: every control, shown or hidden,
  has a name, text or a label; the letters wait for the map and act on it; every
  tool and view toggle is reached by Tab; every scroll area and tab widget is named;
  the map and its render widget are named; no key is bound twice (fails on the old
  Redo with `{'Ctrl+Shift+Z': ['Redo', 'Redo'], 'Ctrl+Y': ['Redo', 'Redo']}`); F6
  moves between the panel and the map; the help page lists the keys.
- **Live**, under Xvfb at 1366 × 768 on the example design, with real keys
  (xdotool) and the accessibility tree read over AT-SPI, which is what Orca reads:
  the walks in *Results*.

## What could not be verified here

- **What a screen reader says.** The AT-SPI tree was read directly, which is what
  Orca reads, but no screen reader ran: the order, the wording and how much of each
  description Orca speaks are not checked. NVDA and Narrator on Windows and
  VoiceOver on macOS read through other bridges (UI Automation, NSAccessibility)
  and none of them ran.
- **Redo on Windows and macOS.** That both keys were doubled on Windows comes from
  Qt's documented bindings and from the offscreen platform reporting the same pair,
  not from a Windows machine. On macOS, Cmd+Shift+Z was doubled the same way.
- **F6 on a Mac keyboard**, where the function keys need Fn unless the system says
  otherwise. Shift+F6 does the same, and Tab still gets there.
- **The ring on the satellite basemap**, and with the operating system's high
  contrast settings. It was looked at over the drawn map and on the dark panels.
- **`tests/test_undo_redo.py`**, which segfaults in its own teardown wherever
  there is no GPU, this container and CI alike (see CLAUDE.md), so neither runs it.
  The Redo fix changes only which keys are bound; the redo itself was measured live
  above. It needs a real desktop.
