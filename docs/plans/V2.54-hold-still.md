# V2.54 — hold still, I'm looking at you

**F121 · pause and slow the ambient wildlife in the 3D preview.**
*Design principle P5 — perception is constructed, not received.*

## What this set out to do

The 3D preview has ambient wildlife: the animals the design's plants actually
support, placed on the plants that support them. V2.45b fixed *how* they move,
against two author reports the source still quotes verbatim
(`html/scene3d/07-wildlife.js`):

> *"the birds move too fast for me to see what they are doing"*
> *"their flight still does not resemble a natural flight for a bird"*

The second was answered properly — bird travel became constant-speed instead of
an exponential lerp that launched the bird at enormous speed and decelerated it
into the perch. The first was answered only by **halving every bird's speed
permanently**, and the code is openly uncomfortable about that, labelling it
"a deliberate legibility choice, not a claim about airspeed — unlike the
wingbeat, which stays exact."

That compromise exists because there was no other instrument. You could not stop
a creature to look at it. A chickadee that crosses the yard before you can focus
on it teaches nothing however correct its wingbeat is, and making the ecology
legible is the whole job (P5).

So: a speed control. **Paused · ¼× · ½× · 1×.**

## What was measured before deciding

**`animateWildlife` runs on two clocks, and only one of them is the obvious one.**
This is the entire reason the change is a design rather than a multiplier:

| Clock | What it drives |
|---|---|
| `dt`, from `_wildDt` | travel, the dwell countdown — position integration |
| **absolute `t`** | the **wingbeat** (`flapWings`), the wander wobble, the bob, the look-around, the hop, `boundOffset`, `beatGain` |

Scaling `dt` — the implementation anyone reaches for first — produces **a bird
hovering motionless in mid-air with its wings beating at full speed.** Not a
subtle defect: it is the kind of thing that reads as a physics bug and would
have been reported as one.

`19-roster.js`'s "Show its plants" spotlight creature has the same split, with
its own private `_spotPrevT` clock mirroring `_wildDt`.

## What was decided, and why

**One creature-clock, advanced inside `_wildDt`.** `animateWildlife` binds
`const t = _wildClock` and its whole body is unchanged — every existing `t`
expression keeps working, and one multiplier now moves travel, wings, bob and
hop together. The alternative (thread a speed argument through every motion
call) touches far more code and leaves the two clocks independently forgettable.

**No separate `paused` flag.** Pause is `_wildSpeed = 0`. Two pieces of state
that must agree are two pieces of state that can disagree.

**The early return moved.** It used to sit above `_wildDt`, so the clock stalled
whenever the wildlife group was hidden. The spotlight creature reads the same
clock and can be on screen in that state, so leaving the order alone would have
frozen its wings in a mode where nothing was paused.

**Resume was free.** The pre-existing `Math.min(0.05, …)` clamp already meant a
frame gap can advance at most 50 ms, so a pause of any length cannot teleport a
creature on release. Worth noting the clamp also *fixed a latent bug in
passing*: before this, a 200 ms stall clamped `dt` but not `t`, so the wingbeat
skipped ahead of the travel on every frame hitch. Now they cannot drift.

**The control is not a toolbar button.** `src/scene3d_window.py` is already the
literal F89 complaint — eight buttons, three sliders and two combos over two
rows — and sits at 914 lines against a 950 guard ceiling. An eleventh button
worsens the app's own worst-named UX problem and spends most of the remaining
headroom doing it. Instead the control is a HUD chip inside the viewer, beside
the creatures, visible only when there are creatures. Precedent:
`10-inspect.js` already builds an interactive HUD button.

**Therefore no bridge hook.** Nothing in Python needs to set the speed, so the
chip registers no `window.perma*` and drives a plain function. That keeps
`test_bridge_contract.py`'s registered-but-undriven guard out of it entirely —
no `_JS_DRIVEN_HOOKS` exemption to keep honest — and costs the Qt side nothing.

**The paused state is loud, not implicit.** The selected step is filled, not
merely outlined. A silently frozen scene reads as "the app has hung", which is
the same misdiagnosis `08-modes.js` records for the blank-window bug, where a
perfectly correct HUD sat over a dead scene and looked like a failed build.

### The spacebar was already taken

Space is the universal pause key and `_BEE_MOVE_CODES` (`06-fly.js`) binds it to
**ascend** in bee mode. The shortcut therefore stands down in bee and walk mode
— the same mode discipline that file already sets. Found before writing the
handler; it would otherwise have shipped as "pausing broke flying".

## What was deliberately left alone

- **The shipped speeds.** `_WILD_MOVE.perch` still runs at half the real
  airspeed. This increment does not relitigate that — but it removes the reason
  the compromise was forced, so it can now be revisited on its own merits with
  ¼× available as the instrument for judging it.
- **`06-fly.js`'s `flapWings(beeAvatar, t)`.** That is the bee's wings while
  *you* are flying it in first person — your own body, not ambient life.
- **Everything else in the render loop.** Sun, wind, camera orbit, precipitation
  and the cinematic dolly keep running while the animals are frozen. Pausing the
  world would be a different feature and a worse one: you want to move around a
  held creature and look at it.

## How it was verified

`tests/test_wildlife_clock.py` follows `test_wildlife_heading.py`'s pattern —
lift the function out of the viewer chunk and execute it under Node, so the
assertions are about numbers the shipped code produces rather than about the
shape of its source. Four behavioural claims: pause is actually still; slow
motion is proportional; **the two clocks stay in lockstep at every speed**; and
resume after a ten-second gap advances at most 50 ms.

The lockstep test was checked against a deliberately mutated clock that advances
from raw time while scaling `dt` — the hovering-bird bug — and it fails on it
(`dt: 0` with the clock still advancing 16.7 ms/frame), so the guard is not
vacuous.

Five source-shape guards run without Node, because the coupling is easy to undo
by accident and the symptom appears only on screen: `animateWildlife`'s
parameter must not be named `t` (that alone would silently reconnect the
wingbeat to real time), the clock must advance before the visibility check, the
spotlight must scale by the same speed, the Space handler must stand down in
first-person modes, and no bridge hook may appear.

Full suite: **2848 tests, OK, 98 skipped** — with `libegl1` and
`PyQt6-WebEngine` installed first, since without them ~200 widget tests skip
silently and prove nothing.

## What could not be verified here

- **Nothing in this increment is Python-visible**, so the Node tests and the
  shape guards are the whole automated story. The chip's appearance, its
  placement against the roster, and whether ¼× actually *feels* slow enough all
  need a real desktop with a GPU.
- **`tests/test_undo_redo.py` still segfaults mid-run in this container** (139,
  no summary line) — the documented environmental failure. Confirmed not to be
  this change: that module never loads the 3D viewer, and its only mention of
  `QWebEngineView` is a comment noting the crash pre-exists.
- **Whether ¼× is the right slowest step is a judgement that needs eyes.** If it
  still reads as too fast, that is evidence for revisiting the permanent halving
  in `_WILD_MOVE.perch` — which is precisely the argument this control now makes
  available.
