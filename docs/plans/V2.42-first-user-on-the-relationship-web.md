# V2.42 — First user on the relationship web, and the seam it exposed

## The report

Three specific things, from the first person to open the V2.31 relationship
overlay and the F46 pull-a-plant simulator:

1. *"I'm not sure what the inner and outer ring differentiate."*
2. *"I don't know what each icon represents."*
3. *"There is no option to click an icon or a plant to get the greater details."*
4. *"The 'Pull a Plant' feature does not relate to the plants actually on the
   design but rather seems like a somewhat arbitrary list."*

Plus a standing request: **say how to test a feature when the work is done.**

All four turned out to be real, and three of the four are the same underlying
mistake — a thing the code knew and never said.

## What was actually wrong

**The rings mean nothing, and looked like they meant something.** `_assign_lanes`
spills animals onto concentric "lanes" purely so labels stop colliding
(`_MIN_ANGULAR_GAP_DEG`, round-robin by bearing). Lane 1 versus lane 2 carries no
ecological content whatsoever. But two concentric rings on a diagram read as a
hierarchy — inner/outer, primary/secondary — and a reader is *right* to look for
one. The legend said "Animals sit on a ring", singular, and never mentioned that
there might be several or what they were for. Fixed by saying so outright, and
only when more than one lane is actually drawn.

**The icons were never keyed.** The ring draws 🐝 🦋 🐦 🪲 🐿 and — by deliberate
design — names only the specialists, because sixty labelled chips is a wall of
text rather than a picture. That density decision is right and stays. What was
missing is the two lines of legend that make it legible: the existing legend
explained the *line colours* and left the reader to guess whether 🪲 meant "beetle"
or "other". `_taxon_key` now returns mark → plain-English label → count, for the
taxa actually on screen (same rule as the edge legend: a key naming mammals when
no mammal is drawn sends the reader hunting for one).

**Hover gave counts; nobody could ask "which".** Hovering a chip said *"3 plants
here support it"* and faded unrelated lines. The obvious next question — *which
three?* — could only be answered by tracing lines by eye across a ring of up to
sixty chips. That is not an interaction. Nodes now carry `supported_by` (for an
animal) and `supports` (for a plant: name, *how*, specialist flag, evidence), and
clicking opens a popup listing them. The renderer stays dumb — all of it is
computed in `relationship_graph.py` and drawn, never looked up JS-side.

**Pull-a-plant was stale, not arbitrary.** `src/plant_impact.py` was always
design-aware: it takes the placed plants and always did. The list was populated
by `_populate_pull_combo`, which ran *only* from `_calc_habitat_score` — and that
is wired to a button. So the list was a snapshot of whatever was planted the last
time somebody pressed **Calculate**: it kept species since removed and never
gained the ones added after. To a user that is indistinguishable from an
arbitrary list. It is now refreshed from `set_placed_plants`, preserving the
user's selection across unrelated edits and dropping it only when that species
actually leaves the design.

Seven of the eight new tests in `tests/test_pull_a_plant.py` fail against the old
code, which is the point: a green suite had nothing to say about this, because
every unit involved was correct on its own.

## The convention this produced

`CLAUDE.md` gains **"Hand back a test plan"**. A green suite tells the author the
code works and tells the user nothing, because they cannot see it — and "I added
a relationship web" is not something a person can go and check. Every increment
now ends with a click path from a cold start, the preconditions, what success
looks like concretely enough to disagree with, the failure mode that is easy to
mistake for "working but empty", and a headless one-liner where one exists. Plus
an explicit statement of what could not be tested and why.

Three of the four reports above were features that *worked* and could not be
read. That is the failure mode this convention is aimed at.

## Explored, not built: splitting design from education

Raised by the author while reporting the above — *"I'm heavily considering having
a landscape design aspect to the app and a separate education aspect. Mixing them
is leading to some suboptimal results."* No edits made. This is the evidence.

### The surfaces today

Six side tabs — **Site · Plants · Structures · Analysis · Planning · Learn** —
plus a start screen with three doors and a standalone plant directory (F90,
V2.41). Sorted by what they are actually *for*:

| Clearly design | Clearly education | **Both, uneasily** |
|---|---|---|
| Site, Structures, Planning | Learn (Field Study / Lessons / Present) | Analysis → Habitat |
| Plants → Browse, Communities | Plant directory (F90) | Analysis → Bees |
| The map, the 3D preview | The docent / presentation stills | Pull-a-plant (F46) |
| | | The relationship web (F5) |
| | | Feed-a-chickadee (F47) |

### The evidence that the mixing costs something

**This increment is itself the evidence.** Both bugs live in the third column.

- **Pull-a-plant broke *because* of the mixing.** It is an education feature —
  learn what a plant is worth by removing it — that lives in a design panel and
  was wired to a design panel's refresh trigger (the habitat-score button).
  Nobody wired it to the design changing, because in the Analysis tab's own
  idiom, "the user pressed Calculate" *is* the refresh event. An education
  surface would have refreshed on the design changing, because that is the only
  thing it reacts to.
- **The relationship web's three complaints are all "this is a diagram pretending
  to be a map".** Animals get invented positions on a ring; the rings are a
  layout artefact; the icons are a taxonomy key. None of that is map. It is drawn
  on the map because the map is where the plants are — a real and good reason —
  but every one of the reported problems came from diagram conventions
  (legend, key, click-for-detail) that a map surface does not habitually provide.
- **F90 already split, and it went well.** The plant directory became its own
  surface in V2.41 explicitly because "a reference work's worth of controls
  cannot land here" — and the finding was that sixteen filters had worked the
  whole time with nothing to press. That is the same shape: capability present,
  unreachable because it was housed in something with a different job.

### The argument against splitting, which is not weak

The philosophy makes the mixing deliberate, not accidental. **P5** (make the
invisible ecology *visible*) and **P13** (a native planting has to be loved to
survive) both argue that the teaching has to happen *where the design is*, not in
a separate classroom the user visits once. A "Learn" section nobody opens teaches
nobody. The relationship web on the map is the philosophy working: you place a
shrub and watch the web grow.

So the honest framing is not *design vs education*. It is: **the app has a third
mode that is neither, and it is currently homeless.**

### The seam I would actually cut

Not two apps. Three *modes* over one project:

1. **Design** — place, draw, generate, phase, export. The map is the subject.
2. **Read** — the catalogue and the relationship web as a *reference and diagram*
   surface: full labels, keys, click-through, no pretence of being a map. This is
   where F90 already went, and where the relationship web probably belongs in
   its fully-labelled form.
3. **Understand-this-design** — the education layer that must stay welded to the
   design: pull-a-plant, feed-a-chickadee, the habitat breakdown, the docent.
   These are worthless divorced from the user's own yard, which is exactly why
   they should refresh on *the design changing* and nothing else.

The bug fixed in this increment is precisely a mode-3 feature that had been
plumbed as though it were mode 1. That is a cheap diagnostic for the rest: **for
every feature in the "both" column, ask what event should refresh it.** If the
answer is "the design changed", it is mode 3 and its wiring should say so. If the
answer is "the user asked a question", it is mode 2 and it wants a reference
surface with legends and detail panes.

**What I would not do:** ship a top-level Design/Learn toggle that hides half the
app. The V2.40 start-menu increment already learned this shape — the first cut
put the right content at the wrong moment, and the fix was about *sequence*, not
about walling things off. A mode split that makes a beginner choose "am I
designing or learning?" before they have a yard drawn asks a question they cannot
answer yet.

**Cheapest next probe, if this is worth pursuing:** take the relationship web's
fully-labelled form — every chip named, keys and detail panes present, no map
underneath — and put it in the plant directory as a second view. It costs little
(the payload already carries everything as of this increment), it tests the
"reference surface" hypothesis directly, and if it turns out the map version was
always the better one, nothing has been lost.

## What could not be verified

Both fixes are GUI-visible and this container has no display. They are tested
headlessly — the Qt widget tests run offscreen and the payload is asserted
directly — but **nobody has looked at the popup or the legend on a real screen.**
Specifically unverified: whether the popup is legible at the ring's density,
whether clicking a plant *halo* is reachable given the real plant marker sits
above it in `markerPane` (the fauna chips are unambiguous; plant nodes may need
the halo's outer edge), and whether the taxon key fits the legend box without
wrapping badly. Those need a desktop run.
