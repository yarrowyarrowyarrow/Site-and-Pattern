# V2.81 — a border read closer than it is drawn

*Why a montane penstemon was published as an Aspen Parkland plant, why the
overlap everybody blamed was not the reason, and the one rule that fixes both.*

## Where this started

Two species pages, side by side, with every occurrence dot in the Rocky
Mountains and a region list that read otherwise:

> "These are 2 examples of why the 900 m overlap is not good enough. I'd prefer
> no overlap. Aspen parkland cannot be absorbing these mountain native species,
> such as mountain forgetmenot and alberta penstemmon pictured."

*Penstemon albertinus*: **284** Northern Continental Divide records, **17**
Aspen Parkland. *Myosotis asiatica*: 306 montane, 4 parkland. The dots are a
thin line along the mountain front. The parkland is 100 km east.

## The overlap was not the cause

The obvious suspect was already documented. V2.78 measured it: each region's
share of a shared border is simplified separately, neighbours therefore overlap
by a sliver, and 0.81% of points inside the layer match two regions. The Method
page disclosed it as *"about eight records in a thousand"*.

Six per cent is not eight in a thousand, so the number was checked before the
fix was designed. Of *Penstemon albertinus*'s 547 cached records, **one** is in
two regions. The seventeen are not overlapping. They are inside Aspen Parkland
and inside nothing else.

Three hypotheses died in order:

1. **A leftover proximity buffer.** No — V2.75 pinned derivation to
   containment and the lookup was running at `near_m=0.0`.
2. **A mislabelled sliver.** A 5-vertex, 6.5 km² Aspen Parkland fragment does
   sit out there, and it looked like the answer for about ten minutes. A
   transect through it showed the parkland running unbroken from -114.545
   eastward: the fragment is a rounding artefact on the edge of a real lobe,
   not a stray.
3. **The boundary is in the wrong place.** Also no. The ELC Aspen Parkland
   genuinely reaches the foothills there; the Alberta subregion inside it is
   called Foothills Parkland.

## What it actually was

Two facts, and neither is a bug in the geometry.

**The seventeen records are one population.** They span **663 m north to south
and 291 m east to west**, thirteen distinct spots inside a single patch of
hillside. Seventeen collectors visiting one roadside over some decades, counted
as seventeen records, reads on the page as breadth.

**That patch is 25 to 202 metres inside the boundary.** And the boundary is
accurate to about 900 m — the site says so itself, under every map it draws:

> "simplified to about 900 m for display: an outline is accurate to roughly a
> kilometre, not to the metre."

So the pipeline was printing a caveat that the outline is good to a kilometre
and then asking it which side of itself a point 100 m away fell on. Both
halves are in the repo; only one of them was believed.

That is the exact shape P9 exists to catch, and it had already been caught once
in this same file. V2.75's own docstring says a buffered lookup "manufactures
range … in the direction that looks most like a real finding — a montane
species acquiring parkland records at the mountain front." Removing the buffer
fixed the 5 km version of that sentence and left the 100 m version standing.

## The rule

**A record counts for a region only where it lies more than the outline's own
positional accuracy inside that region.**

`ecoregion.confident_ecoregion`, with the margin taken from
`ecoregion.SIMPLIFICATION_M = 900.0` — the same number `ecoregion_map.CAVEAT`
prints in words, kept as a number so the sentence and the arithmetic cannot
drift. It is not a tuned threshold. Nothing was tried at 500 m or 1,500 m to
see which looked better; the layer states its error and the rule uses it.

Three details that are load-bearing:

**Distance is measured to a different region, never to a ring.** The layer
splits every ecoregion by Alberta natural subregion, so Aspen Parkland alone is
nine features and 65 polygon parts with internal seams running through the
middle of it. Measuring to the nearest ring would have deleted records from the
centre of the parkland.

**A record too near any border counts for nothing**, rather than for both. That
is what the author asked for — *"I'd prefer no overlap"* — and it is also the
honest answer: we do not know which side it is on, and saying so is cheaper
than picking.

**The overlap fix falls out of it for free.** Two polygons overlap *because*
their shared border was simplified twice, so a doubly contained point is by
construction within the margin of a boundary. The double count is now
structurally impossible rather than measured and apologised for. The Method
page's "eight records in a thousand" bullet is gone, replaced by the discard it
now makes instead.

## What it cost

Two measurements, because they have different denominators and both are worth
having. The pipeline's own, over the 531,272 records that clear the
coordinate-uncertainty filter, as the run printed it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Records counted | **340,293** |
| Set aside as too near a border | **19,045 (3.6%)** |
| Outside the layer altogether | 171,934 (F142's known 32%) |
| Region rows across the catalogue | 3,740 → 3,632 (**−108**) |
| Species whose region list changed | **94** |
| Region rows *added* | **0** |

And a like-for-like probe over the full 559,502-record raw cache, run first so
the margin's effect could be separated from the shipped file being a week stale:
358,091 counted against 379,715 under the old rule — **5.7% fewer** — with
1,697 records (0.30%) that had been counted twice now counted once or not at
all.

The row that matters is *rows added: 0*. The new rule is a strict subset of the
old, so nothing gained a region it could not previously support; every change
is a region shedding records it could not carry. Two species left the file
entirely and neither is the margin's doing: *Mimulus ringens* sat at exactly
three records and fell under the floor, and *Prunus tomentosa* is a fruit
cultivar V2.80 removed from the catalogue.

Both reported species come out right. *Penstemon albertinus* publishes
Northern Continental Divide alone. *Myosotis asiatica* keeps its three montane
regions and loses Aspen Parkland.

## What was deliberately left alone

**`lookup_ecoregions` keeps its 5 km buffer.** *Where is this yard* and *what
is this record evidence for* are different questions about one geometry, and
V2.75 separated them for a reason. A garden near the parkland edge should still
be told about the parkland.

**`subject_area` and the occurrence plotter keep plain containment.** *Is this
coordinate on ground the layer covers* is a third question again. A point 100 m
from a border is on our ground; it just cannot say which side. Renaming
`_containment_lookup` to `_record_lookup` wholesale would have quietly changed
what those two do, so both functions exist, each named for its question.

**Seventeen collections of one plant are still seventeen records.** The margin
removes this case because the population happens to sit near a line, but the
underlying inflation — record count read as spatial breadth — is untouched and
would still show for a heavily collected population in the middle of a region.
The occupancy grid on the species page already answers that question properly,
in 0.25° squares. Making the ecoregion counts count *places* rather than
records is a real increment and is not this one.

**The polygon layer is not rebuilt.** Nothing found here is a fault in it. The
6.5 km² fragment is a simplification remnant on a real lobe, and rebuilding
needs sources this project's sessions cannot reach.

## The re-derivation found a second bug, in the rename tool

The first run wrote **407 species where the file had 415**, and seven of the
eight missing names were still in the catalogue.

`scripts/rename_taxon.py` re-keys the three shipped files keyed by scientific
name. It does not re-key `data/fetched/plant_occurrences.json`, which is what
two of those three are *derived from*. So V2.80's eight renames left a cache
holding `Viola canadensis` while every derived file said `Viola rugulosa` —
consistent, correct, and one re-derivation away from losing the species. The
cache is a dev artefact and unshipped, which is exactly why it was easy to
forget and expensive to forget: the only other way back is re-fetching half a
million GBIF records.

**The seeder warned, and the warning was scrolled past.** It prints
*"8 of them are NOT in the cache and will derive to nothing"* — on line 2 of a
900-line log. That is a guard doing its job into a void, so the invariant is a
test now: every name in the shipped file must be a name the cache can produce
again, and every catalogue name must be answerable from the cache at all.

The eight pairs were not guessed. Each is a row in commit `34268d57` whose
scientific name changed while its common name did not; the six departures with
no partner are exactly the removals and merges that commit's message describes
(*Achillea millefolium* merged, *Helianthus annuus* and three others removed,
*Oligoneuron rigidum* deduplicated). Cache re-keyed, tool fixed, derivation
re-run from scratch.

## One thing fixed in passing

`lookup_ecoregions` computed its bounding-box pad as `near_m / max(per_lat,
per_lng)`. A degree of longitude is 70 km at 51°N against 111 km of latitude,
so a 5 km buffer padded the box by 3.1 km east-west: a region whose edge ran
4 km away was rejected by the pre-filter before its distance was ever measured.
Now `min`. A box test is allowed to be generous and is not allowed to be tight.

## How it is guarded

`tests/test_ecoregion_ranges.py`,
`TestABorderCannotBeReadCloserThanItIsDrawn` — the reported cluster's real
coordinate, the assertion that plain containment *would* still get it wrong
(so this cannot be mistaken for V2.75 arriving late), the three real cached
coordinates that used to be double-counted, the margin agreeing with the
printed caveat, and an interior point unchanged.

The shipped `data/plant_ecoregions.json` envelope is version 2 and now carries
`boundary_margin_m` and a `tally` of what was counted, set aside and out of
area. The Method page and the species-page caption both read those rather than
restating them, which is the rule every other figure on the site follows: a
page about how the numbers are made must not carry a number nobody remade.
