## The numbers were not misaligned, the labels were

`.figures` is a grid of `<dt>`/`<dd>` pairs. Grid cells stretch to the tallest
in the row, so "Species" (a one-line label) put its number one line above "With
a credited photograph" (two lines) and the row read as four unrelated numbers
rather than one bar of statistics.

Each figure is now a flex column with `margin-top: auto` on the `<dd>`. The
numbers sit on one line however many lines their labels take, and the fix costs
nothing when every label happens to be short.

## Copy

Landing page: the hero repeated 7,574 in the headline and again in the figure
row, and the word "prairie-hardy" was the author's to cut. "Where things grow"
lost its explanation of what an ecoregion is and gained an instruction. "By
flower colour" explained the grasses-and-sedges bucket, which is a good
paragraph in the wrong place — the bucket explains itself on its own page. "By
bloom month" lost its paragraph entirely; a row of month chips does not need
one.

The ecoregion map page led with *"Alberta and Saskatchewan in the 23 ecoregions
this catalogue records ranges against, grouped by the 6 ecozones"* — the larger
number first, under a map showing the smaller one. Reversed, because the page's
first choice is six-way.

Both numbers stay computed rather than written down. That sentence has been
wrong once already: it said "the six regions" for a whole increment after the
survey made it twenty-four, with the full list directly underneath
contradicting it.

### The About page

Longest and most convoluted, and the three specific cuts were all correct.

Two are worth recording because they touch P12, not style:

**The "What it does not contain" section is gone**, and nothing about the
catalogue's actual behaviour changed with it. The one-line statement — *"This
catalogue records horticultural and ecological information only. It contains no
Indigenous ecological knowledge, plant-use tradition or land-management
practice, and none should be inferred from it"* — lives in the **site footer**
and therefore appears on all 2,064 pages, with
`test_the_p12_statement_is_on_every_page` failing the build if one is missing
it. The withholding itself was never a promise made in prose: the `notes`
column is off unless `--include-notes` is passed, and `site_facets` owns
`WITHHELD_ROLES`.

**One paragraph in that section was false and had been for twenty increments.**
It said the generic `medicinal` use tag was *"not published or searchable
here"*. That was true in V2.48 and stopped being true in V2.50, when the author
ruled that a generic horticultural category is not sourced traditional
knowledge and can be published. `WITHHELD_ROLES` has been `()` ever since, and
the landing page has a "Medicinal 108" chip on it. Deleting the section removed
the false claim; it is recorded here so nobody re-adds the sentence from
memory.

Also cut: the grasses-and-sedges bullet (same reasoning as the landing page),
and the note that the region outlines "used to be hand-traced and this page
said so", which is history rather than disclosure.

`citations.disclaimer()` was rewritten at the source. It ended with an em dash,
the site normalises an em dash to a comma, and the result was a comma splice on
a public page. Fixing it in the sentence beats making the house style an
exception.

## The wildlife index

1,138 animals in five fixed taxon blocks of chips. That is a list, not a way to
find anything, and there was nothing to sort it by because the model carried
nothing sortable.

It is now the same filtered search the plant index is, over the same
`browse.js`, with five facets: kind of animal, how it uses the plant, how many
plants are recorded, whether it is a specialist anywhere, and whether we have a
photograph.

**The photographs are the reason the ask was the right one.** 58 rows in
`fauna` have carried a credited, openly-licensed iNaturalist photograph since
that fetch and **not one had ever been published** — the wildlife model simply
had no photo field. Adding it took one line, because
`static_site.photo_credit()` already takes any row with
`image_url`/`image_attribution`/`image_license` and already refuses to return
anything when the attribution is missing. 56 of the 58 reach a page; the other
two (American Kestrel, Big Brown Bat) have no documented plant and so have no
page, which is the P9 rule the index has always followed.

**And a second thing nobody had ever seen.** The animal page had a prose block
keyed on `animal["notes"]` — and `fauna` **has no `notes` column**. That key had
been reading `None` on every row since the model was written, so the block had
never rendered once. What the table does have is `description`, populated for
all 1,156 rows with real natural history: tongue length, nesting habit, the
genera it forages on. Every wildlife page now leads with it. Roughly a third of
the rows open by restating the common name — *"Cryptic Bumble Bee. A
short-tongued social bumble bee…"* — which is fine in a tooltip and a stutter
under an `<h1>` that already says it, so that prefix is trimmed.

Three decisions worth stating:

- **Facets declared in `static_site_wildlife.py`, not `site_facets.py`.** That
  table describes what a *plant* can be searched by and is baked into every row
  of the browse index; an animal is not a row in it. What the two share is the
  runtime — `browse.js` reads whatever `data-f` attributes the page rendered,
  so one script filters both pages without knowing either vocabulary. Two
  hardcoded assumptions had to go: the card lookup matched `plants/<slug>/`
  (now the last path segment of the href, which works on both), and the running
  count said "plants" (now read off `data-noun`).

- **Plant counts as bands, not a number.** How many plants are recorded for an
  animal is a fact about the catalogue's coverage as much as about the animal.
  A slider invites reading it as the latter.

- **`kinds` holds the raw relationship key**, not the display phrase. `groups`
  holds "sips nectar at", which is right as a heading over a list of plants and
  wrong on a checkbox — and a facet keyed on a display string drifts the first
  time somebody rewords a heading.

Animals staged into `assets/photos/wildlife/` rather than the same folder as
the species: slugs are unique within each set and nothing makes them unique
across both, so one flat folder is a filename collision waiting for the first
plant and insect that share a common name.

## Analytics

The renderer's docstring opened with *"no framework, no build step, no CDN, no
external request of any kind"*, and that is a real property, not a boast — it
is why the site works off a disk with `file://`. Analytics is a genuine change
to it, so it is built as an exception that has to be asked for:

- `write_site(analytics_token=...)`, `--analytics-token` on the CLI, **off by
  default**. With no token, nothing about the output changes.
- **Cloudflare Web Analytics**, because it sets no cookie and keeps no
  per-visitor identifier, so it needs no consent banner on a catalogue that
  currently has none — and the domain is already on Cloudflare. A script that
  did keep an identifier would not have been worth the exception.
- **Disclosed in the footer of every page it appears on.** A build that phones
  home and does not say so is the thing the docstring was protecting against.
- **A malformed token raises rather than publishing.** The value lands inside a
  quoted JSON attribute on 2,000 pages; the failure mode of a loose check is
  markup injection across the whole site. `^[A-Za-z0-9]{16,64}$`, and anything
  else is refused.
- The build prints which kind of build it just made, either way. "Did that one
  have analytics in it?" should not be answered by grepping the output.

The token is module state for the length of one `write_site` call rather than a
parameter threaded through `_page`'s fifteen call sites in five modules to say
one thing. `test_a_failed_build_does_not_leave_analytics_on` holds the seam
that makes that safe.

## What could not be tested here

- **The beacon never runs.** The proxy blocks it, and Cloudflare has no site
  registered yet in any case. What is tested is that the snippet and its
  disclosure reach every page, that neither appears without a token, and that a
  bad token is refused. Whether Cloudflare *counts* anything is verifiable only
  after the author creates the site and publishes — `docs/PUBLISHING_THE_SITE.md`
  Step 4 has the click path and says what a wrong-but-plausible outcome looks
  like (a well-formed token from a different site records nothing and does not
  error).
- **The live domain**, for the same proxy reason as V2.70.
- **The image cache is wiped by a container restart**, which is worth writing
  down because it looks like a code fault: the first rebuild this session
  reported *"56 copied from cache, 321 left as links"* and would have published
  321 hotlinks to iNaturalist. The species bytes are recoverable with no
  network at all — every past publish copied them to `assets/photos/` on the
  `gh-pages` branch, so `git show origin/gh-pages:<path>` refills the cache.
  The 58 animal photographs had never been published and had to come from the
  source.
