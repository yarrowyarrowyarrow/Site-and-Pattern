## 1. The map

### The colours, and the thing they were about to cost

The ask is one sentence and the design problem underneath it is not: **the fill
was already carrying something**. Every region was drawn in one green, lightened
by GBIF confidence band, so a range seen three times did not look like one seen
three hundred (P9). Spending hue on region identity meant deciding what happened
to that.

Dropping it was never on. The alternative that works: **hue takes identity,
lightness takes confidence.** Six hues after the convention every published
natural-regions map of the prairies uses (mountains violet, foothills olive,
boreal green-teal, parkland yellow-green, grassland gold warming to amber), each
mixed toward white by band. Both claims keep a channel, and neither has to be
read off a key to be understood.

Two constraints fell out of that and are pinned by tests:

- **The palest band must still be recognisably its own colour.** `low` stops at
  55% toward white rather than fading out, and the "not recorded here" grey was
  moved to `#cfd0ca` — almost no chroma, so it cannot be mistaken for a faint
  region. The test asserts the low band keeps at least 30 points of chroma.
- **A key map must not claim confidence it does not have.** The home and map
  pages used to pass a fabricated `"medium"` for every region, which produced a
  tooltip reading "medium confidence" about nothing. `map_svg(reference=True)`
  is now the honest mode for a navigation map, and a test asserts the word
  "confidence" does not appear in one.

### Three bugs the colour exposed

None of these were the ask. All three were invisible while the map was one flat
green, and all three are the reason the drawing looked wrong rather than merely
plain.

**The frame was letterboxed.** Alberta and Saskatchewan are very nearly square
once longitude is compressed by cos(latitude); every caller asked for a
landscape box (760x470). The projector letterboxes rather than distort, so a
third of the figure was blank on each side and the map rendered at roughly half
the size of its own container. `frame_height(width)` returns the height that
leaves no bars, and every call site now uses it.

**"Montane" was printed in British Columbia.** The label anchors were placed by
hand and one had drifted a degree east of its own strip. The two western strips
are also about 25px wide against a 50px word, so they are now set *along* the
strip at 55 degrees, which is the bearing of the continental divide through this
window. Every anchor is asserted to fall inside its own polygon.

**British Columbia was drawn as a hole.** Alberta's border leaves the divide at
54 degrees north and runs up the 120th meridian; the province ring was missing
that corner, so it closed diagonally and the strip beside northern Alberta was
not land. Fixed in `scripts/draw_ecoregions.py` and redrawn.

### And a fourth, which was not cosmetic

Drawing the regions in colour showed a thin white sliver near Rocky Mountain
House. It was a real coverage hole: about 0.1 degrees wide and 0.6 tall,
belonging to no region, so `lookup_ecoregions` returned nothing and a site there
would have been told the catalogue does not reach it.

The cause is the same class as the V2.49 hole east of Calgary. The parkland
closed its western end on a meridian while the foothills' eastern edge runs
diagonally north-west, and above about 52.4 degrees the two part company.
`_park_west()` closes the parkland against that edge instead, which is also the
more honest line: the parkland/foothills transition really is diagonal.

**The sweep that was supposed to catch it did not**, because it sampled at 0.2
degrees and the hole is 0.1 wide. A gap narrower than the sample step is
invisible to the sample. `_coverage_gaps` now runs at 0.05 (about 82,000 points,
two seconds). Verified independently at 0.03: 227,540 points, zero uncovered.

### What was NOT done, and why

**The Peace River Parkland is still missing.** It is a real and recognisable
feature of the Alberta natural-regions map: a lobe of parkland in the Peace
country, surrounded by boreal. Adding it would move the map closer to the
reference and would change `lookup_ecoregions` for every site in that area,
which changes which plants those people are recommended. The suite pins Grande
Prairie as boreal, and which side of that line the city falls on is a real
editorial call about a real place. Raised rather than taken.

**The outlines are still hand-traced.** `scripts/prepare_ecoregions.py` has been
waiting since V2.38 for a session with egress to fetch the actual CEC Level III
polygons. Every drawing carries `CAVEAT` saying so, and the wording was updated:
they stopped being "hand-drawn boxes" in V2.49 and the caption had not caught up.

### The split

`src/ecoregion_map.py` hit 401 lines against a 340 ceiling. The guard asks for
extraction rather than a bigger number and the seam was real: that module
projects geometry into SVG, and `src/ecoregion_palette.py` now holds what a
colour *asserts*. The species page imports the palette and never touches the
projector.

## 2. The flower-colour backlog

`docs/DATA_GAPS.md` said 357 species still carry a genus-level guess and stopped
at "what this needs is a flora and a network". True, and not a next step. A
backlog you cannot start is the same as a backlog nobody started.

The honest finding: **most of it does not need a flora.** It needs a photograph
and thirty seconds, and 199 of them already have the photograph.

`scripts/colour_worklist.py` adds the two things the number was missing.

**An order.** 81 of the 357 are grasses, sedges and rushes, which have no showy
flower and no colour to get wrong; they are excluded outright rather than listed
as work. Of the 276 that remain, 110 sit in one of 25 genus groups where three
or more species carry one identical hex. That is precisely the shape of the bug
the author found in the columbines: one decision applied many times. A species
holding a hex alone was at least looked at once.

**A way to check without being a botanist.** `--sheet` writes a contact sheet:
photograph, claimed colour as a swatch beneath it, grouped worst-first. Wrong
ones are visible at a glance in a way that reading 276 hex codes is not.

What it deliberately does not do is guess harder. `--paste` emits the seeder
lines with the colour left **blank**. A script that pre-filled its own answer
would reproduce the original failure one abstraction higher.

## 4. Where to buy

V2.49 read the first half of the report ("any natives should also be listed in
native nursery") and added `native_specialist` to every native. Every plant on
the site is a native, so that made the filter match 432 of 432. **A filter that
matches everything is not a filter**, and the author's follow-up is the missing
half: the nursery should have the common plants of the big-box store and the
greenhouse, *not* the seed-and-plug-only ones.

`_ALSO_SOLD_BY` replaces the blanket rule with the actual retail implication,
which runs one way only: big-box implies greenhouse implies specialist, and
nothing implies big-box.

| Tier | Also appears under | Now matches |
|---|---|---|
| `big_box` | garden centre, native nursery | 17 |
| `garden_centre` | native nursery | 84 |
| `native_specialist` | itself | 343 (was 432) |
| `seed_or_plug` | itself | 83 |
| `rare` | itself | 6 |

`rare` stays out of the nursery too, which the author did not ask for and which
follows from the same reasoning: the lady's slipper and the gentians are where a
specialist is the likeliest place to find one *if anyone has one*, and that is
not the same as stocking it. Promising those on the nursery shelf would be the
original bug pointed the other way.

This deliberately does **not** reuse `db/nurseries.py:_AVAILABILITY_TO_SELLS`,
which answers a different question. That table decides which shops to list for a
plant, where an extra shop costs the reader one line to skim; this one decides
which plants match, where an extra plant is a wrong answer. The two are allowed
to differ and the reason is written down instead of the disagreement being tidied
away.

## 5. Publishing

`docs/PUBLISHING_THE_SITE.md`. The short version is that it is free and takes
about twenty minutes once: the site is a folder of ordinary HTML files, so
GitHub Pages, Netlify and Cloudflare Pages all host it at no cost. GitHub Pages
is the recommendation because the repository is already there.

The doc also names the two decisions worth making before going live rather than
after: 321 photographs are hotlinked by default rather than bundled, and 276
flower colours are published marked *not verified*. Both are honest as they
stand; both are the author's call, not a default to be discovered later.
