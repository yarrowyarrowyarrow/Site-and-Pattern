# V2.42 — Biological data review: gaps, citations, and what has to be enforced

## The ask

Review the biological data: where the gaps are, and where the citations and
sources are for what we do have. Flora↔fauna relationships in particular. The
stated stakes: *"I need them to know that the data is legitimate but also not
theft."*

That is two different questions with two different answers, and separating them
was the most useful thing this review did.

## What was measured

Every count in `docs/DATA_AUDIT.md` was computed from `data/*.json` directly
rather than carried over from `DATA_GAPS.md` or `DATA_SOURCES.md` — which turned
out to matter, since two of those documents' claims are wrong (below).

The suspicion in the ask was that the flora↔fauna relationships are thin on
sourcing. **That is not what the data says.** All 361 edges carry a real
bibliographic citation naming one of six real works; none is a `"literature"`
placeholder; all 361 plant and fauna names resolve against the catalogues. On
the "is it legitimate" axis, the relationships are in better shape than the
morphology attributes, where all 434 flower and 100 fauna records are the
seeder's estimate with zero citations.

The real finding is elsewhere: **the sourcing is invisible and unenforced.**

- `plant_fauna.source` reaches the view model and is dropped by every widget.
  The user sees photo credits and weather/soil sources; the ecology itself is
  uncited on screen. The data supports the conclusion the user wants their users
  to reach, and gives them no way to reach it.
- `validate_all()` has never opened `plant_fauna_master.json`. 361/361 sourced
  is an accident of how it was authored, not an invariant. Nothing would catch
  the next bulk add arriving bare.
- `src/db/relationships.py:180` hardcodes `evidence="documented"`, so a cited
  Monarch↔milkweed record and an **uncited companion-planting folk pairing read
  identically**. The companion tables have no sourcing columns at all. The most
  folklore-prone data in the app carries the app's strongest provenance claim.
- None of the six cited works appears in `docs/REFERENCES.md`, which
  `DATA_SOURCES.md` points at for exactly this purpose. REFERENCES.md is 129
  lines of design-philosophy books with no floras and no entomology.

## Corrections to existing docs found while measuring

Recorded here because both would otherwise be inherited as true:

- **`DATA_SOURCES.md:49-52`** says the gate enforces credits for
  `plants.image_url` via `validate_fauna_images`. That function filters
  `taxon != "bee"` over `fauna_master.json` and never touches plants. The plant
  photos are in fact clean — 323/323 attributed and licensed — but by authorship,
  not by enforcement.
- **`DATA_GAPS.md:90`** says every one of the 434 leaf records reads `estimated`.
  `leaf_data_source` is **absent from all 434**, so a reseed writes the schema
  default `''` and the validator's error branch can never fire.

A third, smaller one: the `_sources` header inside
`plant_fauna_master.json` declares four works; six are used. Packer, Pattie &
Fisher, and Acorn's *Bugs of Alberta* are cited on 47 edges and declared nowhere.

## What is genuinely strong, and should not be re-litigated

The photo pipeline already answers "not theft" properly: a CC whitelist, a
stricter CC0/CC-BY bar for bees, one shared `credit_line` formatter, a 3D
inspector that asserts the credit is not optional, and a build error on an
uncredited non-CC0 photo. 0 unattributed and 0 unlicensed across 381 images.
`plant_ecoregions` is the schema model the rest should copy — `occurrences` +
`confidence` + retrieval-dated `source`, all `NOT NULL`. And `DATA_SOURCES.md`
carries a worked copyright position (*CCH Canadian*, *Feist*) on reading facts
out of a flora, which is more diligence than most projects of this size manage.

**The "not theft" question is already answered well. It is answered in a file no
user will ever open.**

## Decision: ship the review, then build it

**Amended.** The first cut of this plan committed the audit and stopped, holding
the app-changing parts for the author. The author said go ahead, and asked for
the result to be *scientifically rigorous enough to survive horticulturalist
scrutiny*. **Parts B, C, D, E and F are all built.**

What that emphasis changed, concretely — these are the decisions a reviewer with
the books in front of them would go looking for:

1. **No invented bibliographic detail.** `data/sources_master.json` records what
   the seed citations actually claim and marks every entry `unverified`, with a
   note saying precisely what that means: the work is real and the citation is
   genuine, but the publisher/year/author-list here was transcribed, not checked.
   No ISBNs were guessed. `record_confidence` describes the *bibliographic
   entry*, never the ecological claim.
2. **The one non-citation was not laundered.** 16 edges cited *"Prairie-composite
   pollination literature; Acorn & Sheldon 2006"*. Mapping them to the Acorn &
   Sheldon key would have asserted a coverage the original author never claimed.
   They get an explicit `NOT_A_WORK` placeholder key instead, which the gate
   counts and warns about every run.
3. **Taxonomic synonymy is handled, and costs a confidence band.** See the
   correction below — this one nearly shipped as a silent data loss.
4. **Derived ≠ documented, structurally.** Separate table, separate `evidence`
   state, discounted strength, and a guard test keeping derived edges out of the
   scores.

The four parts as originally scoped:

**Part B · Make the citations checkable** — `data/sources_master.json` with the
six works as canonical keys; normalize the 11 edge strings (and 1 bee / 4 lep
strings) onto them; give `docs/REFERENCES.md` its first biological-sources
section; fix the two inaccurate lines above. The one string that cannot be
normalized honestly is `"Prairie-composite pollination literature; Acorn &
Sheldon 2006"` — its 16 edges keep the Acorn & Sheldon key and lose the
unattributable half.

**Part C · Enforce it** — `validate_plant_fauna()` (every edge sourced, source
resolves to a known work, relationship in the enum, names resolve — turning the
seeder's silent `continue` at `src/db/plants.py:1137` into a build error);
`validate_plant_images()`, the check `DATA_SOURCES.md` already claims exists;
`validate_safety_provenance()` for the 49/49 that currently comply by habit;
and move `_provenance_gap` into `scripts/_bench_common.py` so `tune_fauna.py`
gets the guard `tune_morphology.py` has.

**Part D · Stop the `evidence` claim being a lie** — three states rather than
two: `documented` (in a table *and* cited), `recorded` (in a table, uncited),
`derived` (computed). One line in `_edge_from_row`. Add `source`/`notes` to both
companion tables and stop the view hardcoding `''`. Leave them unpopulated —
an empty source reading `recorded` is honest; a blank one reading `documented`
is not. Requires a schema bump (60 → 61), as does the seed normalization in B.

**Part E · Let the user see it** — the smallest slice of F12. **Shipped.**
The audit's central finding was that the citations existed and no screen showed
one; `source` reached the view model and was dropped at the render call. Three
pieces:

- **`src/citations.py`** — key → citation, with two rules enforced at the render
  layer rather than trusted to callers: a `NOT_A_WORK` placeholder never renders
  as a reference, and `disclaimer()` states *both* limitations (the details are
  transcribed, and nobody has confirmed a work supports the claim citing it).
- **The species page** — *"Where these relationships came from"* under the
  wildlife block, plus range provenance under "Found in", which had carried
  `source` since schema v59 unused. Smooth Aster now shows 6 works behind its
  21 relationships.
- **Help → "Where This Data Came From…"** (`src/data_sources_flow.py`) — reads
  live counts from the database and the bibliography from JSON, so it cannot go
  stale. Answers the "not theft" question in the app instead of in a file only a
  developer would open.

While wiring it, `edges_for_plant` and `edges_among` turned out not to select
`evidence` at all — with the new fallback that made every derived edge read
`documented`, the exact lie this increment set out to remove. Caught by a test
asserting a monarch↔milkweed edge carries a source.

## Part F · Coverage — added after review

The author pushed back on the framing above, correctly: *"many fauna have no
relationships and 22.6% edge is actually very small."* Well-cited data you do
not have is still data you do not have, and the first draft of this plan let
citation quality stand in for coverage. Measured properly (`DATA_AUDIT.md` §6),
**coverage can be roughly doubled without asserting anything new**, because the
claims are already sitting in the shipped attribute files unread by the graph.

Four levers, in descending order of defensibility:

- **A · Promote genus-level host records.** `floral_host_genera` (69 bees) and
  `nectar_flower_genera` (31 leps) expanded against the catalogue's 235 genera:
  **+1072 edges, 22.6% → 45.6% plant coverage, orphan fauna 56 → 31.** No new
  claim — `src/bee_habitat.py` already makes exactly this inference at runtime
  for one panel; the graph just never sees it. This is the lever to take first.
- **B · Congeneric transfer, forage only.** Defensible for
  nectar/pollen/fruit/seed/cover, where the literature's own records are often
  genus-level. **Not defensible for larval hosts** — specialist herbivory is
  frequently species-specific and transferring it across a genus manufactures
  the false precision P9 forbids. Restricted lever: 633 of 1171 available
  transfers kept, **excluding 350 `larval_host`, 25 `nesting` and 99
  specialist-derived**. Adds 424 beyond A → **51.3%**.
- **C · A fauna↔fauna edge type.** 24 of the 31 remaining orphans are cuckoo
  bees whose `host_genus` is *another bee*, not a plant — Nomada→Andrena,
  Triepeolus→Melissodes, Epeolus→Colletes, Melecta→Anthophora, cuckoo
  Bombus→Bombus. They are structurally unreachable under a plant↔fauna-only
  model. Orphans **31 → 7**, and it is the most P5-aligned edge in the file: a
  cuckoo bee is a top-of-food-web indicator, so its presence *means* something
  a user cannot currently be shown.
- **D · Treat the fauna file as a coverage test for the flora.** Four cited host
  genera are absent from the plant catalogue and are not introduced species —
  ***Epilobium*/*Chamerion*** (fireweed, 7 refs, a major native bee plant),
  *Senecio*, *Salvia*, *Polygonum*. The animals are naming plants we do not
  ship. Cheap to check, and permanent once wired into the gate.

**Outcome: A, C and D shipped; B measured and declined.**

| Lever | Result |
|---|---|
| **A** genus-host promotion | **1131 edges**, plant coverage 22.6% → **46.5%** |
| **C** fauna↔fauna parasite edges | **140 edges**, orphan fauna 56 → **3** |
| **D** host-genus coverage check | `validate_host_genus_coverage`, warns every run |
| B congeneric forage | **not implemented** — breaks the anti-taxonomic-inference rule |

**The conditions, and how each resolved:**

1. **Three-state `evidence` had to land first.** It did, in the SQL view rather
   than in Python, so a caller cannot forget it. `html/map/07-network.js`
   already dashes derived edges; that encoding extends rather than gets
   reinvented.
2. **The Habitat Value Score needed a decision — and turned out to be safe for
   free.** Every scoring consumer (`habitat_score.py`, `placement_score.py`,
   `scene_dossier.py`, `db/fauna.py`) queries `plant_fauna` directly; only
   `relationships.py` reads the view. No headline number moved, and no project's
   score changed. That was luck, so it is now a guard test
   (`TestDerivedEdgesStayOutOfTheScores`) rather than a happy accident.
3. **The "hosts N caterpillars" labels** read `db/fauna.py`, covered by the same
   guard. They keep counting documented records only.

Derived edges are additionally discounted by confidence band in
`_derived_strength`, multiplicatively — so the edge vocabulary's own ordering
survives (a derived larval-host edge still outweighs derived cover) while the
whole derived population sits below the documented one and can never out-rank
it.

## Deliberately not doing, with reasons

- **Page-level citation for the 361 edges.** The strongest possible answer to
  "is this legitimate", and it means re-opening six books — not work that can be
  done from this repository. If it is wanted, the 31 **specialist** edges are
  where to start: they carry the most scoring weight and drive the specialist
  spotlight.
- **The two duplicate scientific names** (`Geum triflorum`,
  `Valeriana sitchensis`) — known, already flagged in `docs/review.md:131-137`,
  orthogonal to sourcing.

## Corrections made during implementation

Recorded in place, per this directory's house style.

**The fireweed error — my own, and the most instructive thing in this
increment.** §6 Lever D of the audit originally reported *Epilobium*/*Chamerion*,
*Senecio* and *Polygonum* as absent from the plant catalogue, and named fireweed
as a significant missing native bee plant. All three are present. The catalogue
carries them under **current** names — *Chamaenerion angustifolium* (a third
spelling of the fireweed genus), *Packera cana*, *Persicaria*/*Bistorta* — and
the audit had matched on superseded ones.

This was a matching failure, not a catalogue gap, and it would have shipped as
silent data loss: the derivation was dropping all 7 fireweed references. Fixed by
`GENUS_SYNONYMS` in `src/db/derived_edges.py`, which models these as *segregate*
relationships rather than equivalences — a synonym match is real but weaker than
a literal one, so it costs a confidence band. The general lesson is worth more
than the fix: **a host record written against a 20th-century flora uses the genus
name of its day, and matching it literally against a modern catalogue loses real
edges silently.** Any audit of biological data that ignores synonymy will
over-report gaps — as this one did.

**Cuckoo bees were being emitted as their own hosts.** The first run of
`cleptoparasite_edges` produced 112 social-parasite edges from 4 cuckoo
bumblebees, because their `host_genus` is their own genus (*Bombus*) and the
expansion did not exclude other parasites. A cuckoo bumblebee does not
parasitise another cuckoo bumblebee. Fixed by filtering candidate hosts to
non-parasitic genus members; 140 edges now, 24 parasites and 34 hosts with no
overlap, pinned by a test.

**`pollen_specialist` is a flag, not a genus.** Read initially as naming the
specialised host genus; it is 0/1/NULL. The three flagged bees each name exactly
one host genus, which is what oligolecty means — so the flag qualifies every
genus on the record, and those edges band `high`.

**Estimates came in low.** 1131 derived edges against a predicted 1072, and 3
remaining orphans against a predicted 7 — the synonym fix recovered edges the
estimate had written off, and parasite edges connect hosts as well as parasites.

## What could not be verified

The **contents of the six works**. This review read citation strings and their
internal consistency, not the books. If an edge's claim is factually wrong,
neither this audit nor the enforcement proposed above would catch it. The goal
throughout is to make each claim *checkable by someone holding the book* — not
to certify it. That distinction should survive into any UI copy: the app can
honestly say where a claim came from, and should not imply it has been
independently confirmed.
