# V2.66 — the map under the map

The ask was "the ecoregions, specifically the map — currently it looks a bit
sloppy". It arrived with a brief (now at
[`tools/ecoregions/CONTEXT.md`](../../tools/ecoregions/CONTEXT.md)) diagnosing
two prior attempts and a rendered target to match.

The brief's central claim is right and worth restating: **the fix is not to draw
better, it is to stop drawing and load real data.** What that turned out to
require was splitting the problem in two, because only half of it was blocked.

---

## What was actually wrong, measured

The map has three layers of problem and they are not equally hard.

**The polygons.** `data/ecoregions_canada.geojson` is six hand-traced shapes.
This is not news — `scripts/draw_ecoregions.py` says so in its own docstring and
`ecoregion_map.CAVEAT` prints it under every drawing. What is news:
`moist_mixedgrass` and `mixedgrass_prairie` **self-intersect** at
(-108.546, 52.184). Shapely refuses to intersect them without `make_valid`. The
brief's own "no self-intersection" structural check fails against the layer in
production.

**The basemap.** Worse than the polygons and nobody had looked. Alberta was 15
vertices, **Saskatchewan and Manitoba were five-vertex rectangles**, and the
Alberta/BC border was a straight line where the real one follows the continental
divide. There was no water layer at all — the missing Peace and Athabasca the
brief flags are not a styling omission, there were no rivers and no lakes.

**The palette.** Six colours varying in hue alone. Run through the dataviz
skill's validator, it fails every check:

```
[FAIL] Lightness band     outside band: #b3cc6b 0.805, #e3c96a 0.839
[FAIL] Chroma floor       reads gray: #8b79b8, #3f8f75
[FAIL] CVD separation     worst #e3c96a↔#b3cc6b ΔE 1.1 (protan)
[FAIL] Normal-vision      worst #e3c96a↔#b3cc6b ΔE 6.6 — below 15
```

ΔE 6.6 for a reader with **full colour vision**. The boreal teal and the
foothills olive are 7.7 apart.

---

## The split, and why

The polygons need the National Ecological Framework and the Alberta subregions.
Both hosts refuse this session's egress proxy (403 to CONNECT; only GitHub and
PyPI are allowed). Per the brief's first hard rule, nothing was substituted.

The basemap and the palette need neither. Natural Earth is on GitHub, and colour
is arithmetic. So those shipped, and the classification became a pipeline the
author runs on a machine with open egress.

That ordering matters: **screenshot A in the brief is not a discarded draft, it
is what the site serves today**, at 700px on `/map/` and at 420px on every one of
432 species pages. Waiting for the blocked download would have left it there.

## What shipped

`data/basemap_prairie.geojson`, 127 KB, from Natural Earth 1:10m via
`tools/ecoregions/basemap.py`. Alberta 15 vertices → **187**. Twelve provinces
and states, 21 lakes over 900 km², 32 rivers including all four the brief named.

Three things in it are less obvious than they look:

- **Lake area is measured on the equal-area conic, not in square degrees.** A
  square-degree threshold is stricter in the north, which would have dropped
  boreal lakes larger than southern ones it kept.
- **A union-of-everything "land" polygon is drawn first.** Provinces simplified
  independently disagree along shared borders, and the first render had white
  hairlines across the 60th parallel and the 49th. Painting the union underneath
  makes a sliver fall through to land instead of to the page.
- **Coordinates are rounded to 3 dp.** 110 m, a fortieth of a pixel, and half the
  file size — full float repr spends seventeen characters saying nothing.

`src/ecoregion_map.py` now projects through an **Albers equal-area conic**
instead of one cosine factor. The cosine is wrong at both ends of twelve degrees
of latitude, which on a map whose job is comparing how much ground each region
covers is the one defect that cannot be styled around.

The brief specifies `ESRI:102001`. Used verbatim it came out **tilted about
twelve degrees clockwise**, because Canada Albers centres on 96°W to keep the
whole country upright and this window is 14° west of that. Re-centred on the
window (parallels 50/58, meridian 110.5°W). Still Albers, still equal-area. The
exported GeoPackage keeps `ESRI:102001` because that is the number other tools
expect.

The thematic layer is **clipped in SVG to Alberta and Saskatchewan**. The
placeholder polygons run past the 49th into Montana and past the SK/MB border;
clipping says the true thing — this is the area the layer speaks for — without
a polygon-clipping library in a module that has always been dependency-free.

## The palette, and the rule that came out of it

Re-stepped so lightness carries **woody cover**: dark is closed forest, pale is
open grassland, spanning OKLCH 0.47 to 0.90. A reader who cannot separate the
hues still reads the north-south gradient.

That fixed five pairs of six. It cannot fix **Aspen Parkland against Moist Mixed
Grassland**: yellow-green against amber is the red-green confusion axis, and
they collapse to ΔE 4.3 under deuteranopia however the lightness is arranged. A
260,000-iteration search over OKLCH with the conventional hues held found no
solution. Releasing the convention found several, all of them a cyan boreal and
a magenta Rockies — separation is not the only thing the map owes its reader.

So the brief's one-off hatch for Cypress Upland became a general rule:

> **Two ecoregions that share a border must reach CVD ΔE 8, or one of them is
> hatched.**

`tests/test_ecoregion.py` computes the adjacency graph from whatever polygons
are shipped and enforces it in **both** directions — an unearned hatch fails
too, because a hatch is visual noise that has to be earned. When the ELC regions
land the test names the pairs needing attention rather than leaving it to
somebody's eye.

**Adjacency rather than all pairs** is the right constraint for a map. Boreal
Mixedwood and Mixedgrass Prairie are never seen edge to edge; separation budget
spent on them is wasted. Of 15 possible pairs, 11 touch.

One thing the new tests caught that nobody predicted: with the dry grassland
moved to a pale gold, it came within ΔE 7.4 of the "not recorded here" grey, so
a region with three occurrence records looked like a region with none. The grey
was re-stepped cooler and darker, and both the distance and a chroma ratio are
now pinned.

## The pipeline

`tools/ecoregions/`, six stages, resumable, each writing an intermediate.
Stages 1, 2 and 5 are exercised here; 3, 4 and 6 cannot be until the data lands.
`render --from-shipped` runs the whole cartographic path against the placeholder
polygons — a render pipeline that has never once run is not a render pipeline —
and stamps `[PLACEHOLDER GEOMETRY]` into its own title and caption.

The three rules that are structural rather than aspirational:

- **Stage 2 refuses to guess a field name.** The brief guessed `NSRNAME` and
  `ECOREGION_NAME`; this repo's `prepare_ecoregions.py` guessed `NA_L3NAME` and
  `L3_KEY` and shipped a key map built on them that nobody ever ran.
- **Stage 3 joins by area, never by name.** "Athabasca Plain" is a unit in both
  frameworks and refers to different polygons; a name join corrupts the
  northeast into a file that still looks valid.
- **Stage 4's failures are blocking and expectations are never auto-edited.**
  Every probe traces to a named defect. When one fails it reports which polygon
  the point actually landed in and stops.

`render.py` imports `src.ecoregion_palette`, so the printed map and the website
cannot drift and the legend swatches come from the dict that fills the polygons.
The brief's attempt-B colour swap is only reachable when those are two lists.

## Deliberately not done

`data/ecoregions_canada.geojson` is **untouched**. It feeds
`lookup_ecoregions`, which decides what gets recommended for somebody's actual
garden. Swapping it needs the region vocabulary, the plant filter, the
GBIF-derived ranges in `data/plant_ecoregions.json` (keyed by region names that
do not survive the change), about twenty pinned city lookups and a schema bump
considered together. The brief scopes it out of the first run.
`export --adopt` prints that checklist and still does not do it.

No schema bump this increment: no shipped seed data that reaches the database
changed. `data/basemap_prairie.geojson` is read at draw time, not seeded.

`data/provinces_prairie.geojson` is now unread but still written by
`draw_ecoregions.py`. Left alone rather than deleted in the same commit that
replaced its consumer.

## What could not be verified

- **Stages 3, 4 and 6 have never run against real data.** They are written
  against the AAFC v2.2 field names, which are a documented guess flagged as
  such in `FIELDS`. Stage 2 exists to correct them and stage 3 stops rather than
  proceeds if they are wrong.
- **The probe expectations are unverified against real attribute tables.** The
  brief says so itself. Stage 2 must be read before stage 4 is trusted.
- The **12 suite errors are pre-existing** `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named
  'PyQt6'` in this container, unchanged by this work. 3195 tests, no failures.
