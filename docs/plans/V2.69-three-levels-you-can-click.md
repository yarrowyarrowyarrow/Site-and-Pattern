# V2.69 — three levels you can click, and URLs that stop moving

Two asks after the V2.68 publish:

> *"fix the reseed breaks. Also I'd like all 3 layers of the ecoregions to be
> selectable on the map. Like choose from the most broad then more specific
> within that and again more specific within that. Or have 3 maps, whichever
> you think best represents this data succinctly and effectively."*

---

## Part one: the URLs

`_unique_slugs` broke a common-name collision by suffixing the **row id**, and
its docstring claimed that "keeps the URL stable across rebuilds ... a slug that
renumbered when a row was added would break every inbound link".

The reasoning was right and the field was wrong. **Row ids are not stable across
a reseed.** CLAUDE.md says so in three places, `src/db/photos.py` keys
photographs on `scientific_name` precisely to avoid it, and
`_remap_user_polyculture_plants` exists to repair the same damage *inside* the
database. The website was the one consumer nobody had checked.

It hid because the id only appears in a **collided** slug — a handful of pages
out of 1,570. The V2.68 publish is what exposed it: four live URLs changed
address between one publish and the next, silently, with no redirect.

Collisions now break on the scientific name:

```
stiff-goldenrod-solidago-rigida        aphrodite-fritillary-speyeria-aphrodite
stiff-goldenrod-oligoneuron-rigidum    aphrodite-fritillary-argynnis-aphrodite
```

Zero slugs in the catalogue now end in a bare number. **This renames those four
pages one last time**, and that is the end of it.

**The test that should have caught it was named
`test_colliding_names_keep_separate_stable_urls` and asserted `wild-rose-3` and
`wild-rose-7`** — it encoded the bug as the expected result. It now renumbers
every row, the way a reseed does, and asserts no URL moves.

Both collision pairs turn out to be **synonym duplicates of one taxon**
(*Solidago rigida* = *Oligoneuron rigidum*; *Speyeria* = *Argynnis*). Merging
them is a data decision, not a URL one, so they stay as two pages and are
flagged here.

## Part two: drill-down, not three maps

The author offered three side-by-side maps as the alternative and asked for a
recommendation. **Drill-down**, on three grounds:

1. **The reader's question is sequential.** "Where am I" then "what grows
   here". Three maps at once asks them to cross-reference; one map at a time
   answers.
2. **Twenty-four regions do not fit on one 700px map.** That crowding is what
   produced the abbreviation table that shipped *"Parkland"* as the name of
   Aspen Parkland. Six ecozones fit comfortably.
3. **The nesting is the point.** A drill-down *shows* containment. Three maps
   side by side hide the one relationship the reader needs.

```
/map/                        6 ecozones, coloured, clickable
/plants/ecoregion/zone-*/    the ecoregions inside one ecozone
/plants/ecoregion/<region>/  the Alberta subregions that overlap it
/plants/subregion/<sub>/     one subregion, and what it belongs to
```

The geometry was already there. Every feature in the polygon file has carried
`key`, `ecozone` and `ab_subregion` since adoption; nothing had ever grouped by
anything but the ecoregion, so **two thirds of the classification were
undrawable**. `region_geometry(level, within)` is the whole unlock.

## The finding that changed the design

The first cut treated subregions as a third tier and filed each one under its
parent ecoregion. That produced **77 pages for 21 subregions**, and put
**Montane inside Aspen Parkland** — because `sorted(parents)[0]` is
alphabetical, and the same shortcut this project has now been burned by three
times in two increments.

So it was measured instead. Share of each subregion's area by ecoregion:

| | dominant ecoregion | share | ecoregions it touches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry Mixedgrass | Mixed Grassland | 98% | 2 |
| Central Parkland | Aspen Parkland | 96% | 4 |
| Northern Fescue | Moist Mixed Grassland | 94% | 4 |
| Subalpine | Eastern Continental Ranges | 76% | 4 |
| Upper Boreal Highlands | Mid-Boreal Uplands | 65% | 2 |
| Northern Mixedwood | Hay River Lowland | 59% | 3 |
| Athabasca Plain | Athabasca Plain | 46% | 4 |
| **Montane** | Northern Continental Divide | **42%** | **6** |
| **Central Mixedwood** | Mid-Boreal Uplands | **31%** | **9** |

**Alberta's natural subregions are not a subdivision of the ELC ecoregions.**
They are a parallel classification of the same ground, agreeing in most places
and crossing in the mountains and the mid-boreal. Twelve of twenty-one sit ≥90%
inside one ecoregion; four genuinely cross-cut.

That killed the tidy tree and produced a better answer:

- **21 pages, one per subregion.**
- When one ecoregion accounts for **two thirds** of it (the same threshold
  `scripts/migrate_ecoregion_tags.py` already uses, for the same reason: at a
  half a 51/49 split gets reported as a fact), the page borrows that list **and
  says it is borrowed**.
- When none does, the page lists the overlaps **with their measured shares** and
  lends nothing. Montane reads *"spans 6 ecoregions, none of which accounts for
  two thirds of it ... follow the ecoregion your own site falls in."*

The shares are computed in `tools/ecoregions/adopt.py`, where an equal-area
projection and geopandas are available, and shipped as `sub_share` on every
feature. Neither dependency ships with the app, and the alternative — the
website guessing — is what this whole increment is about not doing.

### Why a subregion page is not a filter result

No species is tagged at subregion level and none should be. The derivation
resolves an occurrence to the ecoregion its coordinate falls in; pushing those
records one level finer would invent precision the records do not have.

A subregion page is a **locator**. *"I am in Dry Mixedgrass"* is a sentence
somebody in southern Alberta can say about their own yard, and the page answers
it: that is 98% inside Mixed Grassland, and here is what is recorded there.

## The same bug, three times, in one increment

`sorted(parents)[0]` — take the alphabetically first parent — appeared
independently in three modules within an hour:

| Where | What it produced |
|---|---|
| `static_site._subregion_pages` | Montane filed under Aspen Parkland (6% of it) |
| `ecoregion_palette._elc_colour_for_key` | Montane painted in Aspen Parkland's gold |
| `ecoregion.ecoregion_display` | a public legend reading "Dry Mixedgrass (Cypress Upland)" — 2% |

All three now call `ecoregion_tree.subregion_parents`, which is what it exists
for. Worth recording because none of them was a typo: each was written by
reaching for "a parent" when the data says there are several and they are not
equal.

## Three more caught by rendering the pages

Nothing tests a picture, so all three were found by building the site and
looking at it.

**The legend named colours that were not on the map.** The overview drew six
ecozones and printed a key of twenty-four ecoregions underneath. `legend_html`
now takes the same `level`/`within` as `map_svg` and calls the same geometry
function, so the two cannot disagree.

**A subregion focus map drew thirteen subregions.** The branch test checked both
the grouping key *and* the ecoregion, which looked like belt and braces: a piece
of Alpine belongs to Eastern Continental Ranges, which is on Montane's lineage,
so asking for Montane drew every subregion sharing any parent with it.
`lineage_keys` already walks both directions; the key test alone is correct at
every level.

**Labels collided on the per-region maps.** At a third of a column wide, "Moist
Mixed Grassland" and "Aspen Parkland" landed on top of each other. Focus maps
now draw no labels and carry a legend, which wraps and links.

## What was deliberately left alone

**Within an ecozone the five gold Prairies fills are nearly identical.** That is
`_STEP_SPREAD = 0.06`, measured in V2.66 as the ceiling before Taiga Plains
collides with Boreal Shield under simulated colour-vision deficiency. Widening
it only on focus maps would mean two palettes for one region depending on which
page you are on. The legend and the labels carry identity; the shared hue
correctly says *these are all Prairies*.

**Saskatchewan gets no third level.** Alberta publishes a subregion layer and
Saskatchewan does not, so an ecoregion with no subregions falls back to "here is
where this region is". Inventing one would be inventing data.

**The two synonym duplicate pairs are still two pages each.** Merging taxa is a
data decision.

## Ceilings

Four modules tripped the guard. One got the extraction it asks for:
`static_site_regions.py` now holds every page that answers **"where?"** — the
map page, the per-region maps, the subregion pages — and the renderer keeps the
ones that answer "what?". Third split off that file, after the species page and
the About page.

The other three were raised with the reason recorded: the drill-down's geometry
belongs in the module that owns "polygons to SVG", its colour in the one that
owns "what a colour asserts", and its page model in the model layer. Splitting
those by "drill-down vs not" would put two halves of one question in two files.

---

## What could not be verified here

The three levels were checked by rendering each page headless and reading the
pixels, which is how the legend mismatch, the thirteen-subregion map and the
label collisions were found. That is not the same as clicking through it on a
real browser at phone width.

The published site was not re-fetched after publishing: this sandbox's proxy
blocks `github.io`.
