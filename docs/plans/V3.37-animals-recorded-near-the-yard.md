## The rule (`src/fauna_fit.py`)

An animal's **expected** count near the place is its province records times its
group's share. Four answers:

| answer | when | Edmonton | drawn in 3D |
|---|---|---:|---|
| here | recorded near the place | 590 | yes |
| stray | recorded near it, under 1% of expected (`STRAY_SHARE`) | 5 | no |
| elsewhere | none near it, and either at least 10 expected (`ABSENT_EXPECTED`) or a group recorded at least 1,000 times an animal near the place (`SATURATED`) | 85 | no |
| unrecorded | none near it, fewer than 10 expected; or not counted | 464 | yes, as before |

Decided and why:

- **The share is the group's own, computed from the file.** Records measure
  where people look as much as where animals live. Comparing an animal with
  its own group cancels most of that: eBird volunteers near Edmonton, bee
  surveys in the south. Nothing is written down, so a re-fetch or a Calgary
  pack recomputes it.
- **Ten expected, not three.** Under even chance, three expected leaves a 5%
  chance of no record; ten leaves 0.005%. Records come in clumps (one survey,
  one keen observer), so the wider margin is the honest one. It costs
  nothing that matters: the 86 animals with 3–10 expected and none recorded
  are bees and flies whose absence V3.36 said not to trust.
- **`SATURATED` exists for one bird, and it is measured, not a list of
  groups.** The Rock Ptarmigan has 3 Alberta records, so it predicts 0.4 near
  Edmonton, which the expected-count rule calls unknown. But no regular yard
  bird can hide among 778,488 bird records. Birds have 15,888 records an
  animal near Edmonton; the next group has 70. The threshold of 1,000 sits in
  a gap two hundred times wide, so it reads the data rather than a judgement
  that "birds are well recorded".
- **Stray is a separate answer from elsewhere,** because "recorded here, now
  and then" is true of the Calliope Hummingbird and "never" is not. The 3D
  preview treats them alike. A page that says which is which can come later.
- **Outside every pack nothing changes.** A Calgary pin has no counts, every
  animal reads unrecorded, and the scene draws what it drew. This is why the
  bird tests at Calgary's coordinates did not move. No pin uses the default
  pack, as `design_native` does.
- **The counts ship in the pack** (`data/regions/edmonton/animal_records.json`,
  100 KB), derived by `scripts/derive_animals_here.py` from the two fetched
  files, which stay out of the installer. `--check` is a test. A pack whose
  circle has moved since its count is not written. Read from file, so no
  schema bump.

## Found on the way

- **Fauna rows GBIF cannot tell apart.** Identical counts near Edmonton *and*
  across Alberta mark rows that GBIF resolves to one taxon:
  - Pearl, Northern and Field Crescent (*Phyciodes tharos*, *cocyta*,
    *pulchella*): 676 near, 8,849 Alberta, each.
  - *Argynnis aphrodite* / *Speyeria aphrodite*: one species under two names
    in the catalogue.
  - *Argynnis mormonia* / *Speyeria mormonia*: the same case.
  - *Epistrophe emarginata* / *Epistrophella emarginata*: the same case.

  Two more pairs are one species in GBIF's two entries:
  - *Icaricia saepiolus* and *Plebejus saepiolus*: 75 and 62 near Edmonton.
  - *Lycaena heteronea* and *Tharsalea heteronea*: 1 and 0.

  The duplicate rows double-count those animals in "recorded as food for N
  kinds". The crescents' shared count makes the Pearl Crescent, an eastern
  butterfly, read as an Edmonton one. Not fixed here: merging fauna rows moves
  their edges, which is its own job (`scripts/remove_taxon.py` is for plants).
- **The chickadee eats nothing on the Edmonton list.** It is the most recorded
  animal near Edmonton (96,491), but its one food record is Canada Goldenrod's
  seed, and Canada Goldenrod is native to Alberta, not to Edmonton. Its other
  six edges are cover. That is a gap in the edge records, not in the rule.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **"Recorded as food for N kinds."** The count still includes animals not
  recorded near the yard. It is one number on the Overview, the Food page, the
  PDF and the API (V3.20's one-number rule), and its caveat already says the
  records are from across Alberta and Saskatchewan. Counting only animals
  recorded near the yard would change a headline, so it is the owner's call.
- **The Food page and the website** could say "recorded near Edmonton N
  times" beside an animal. Nothing reads the counts there yet.
- **The generator's wildlife top-ups** still add plants for any animal a goal
  names. A person who asks for an animal gets it.
