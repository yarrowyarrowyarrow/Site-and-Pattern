## V3.33: what is wrong, measured

Of 424 plants, 80 are grasses, sedges and rushes, which have no bloom colour to
get wrong. Of the other 344:

| Source | Plants | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `estimated` | 123 | a genus default: one hex for every species in the genus |
| `name`, `epithet` | 34 | read from "Yellow Columbine" or *flavescens*; usually right, never looked at |
| blank | 49 | no colour recorded: mostly trees and shrubs with no showy flower |
| `flora` | 137 | read from Budd's flora in V2.80 |
| `checked` | 1 | Oval-leaf Milkweed, from published descriptions |

**The flora reads are not all right either.** Prairie Coneflower is `flora` and
purple; its rays are yellow (the flora's "purple" is most likely the brown
centre). Common Paintbrush is `flora` green and red: the corolla is green, which
is what a flora describes, and the bracts are red, which is what anybody sees.
So the question this increment asks is the homeowner's question: **what colour
does it look in flower?** That is answered by looking at it, and it is recorded
as `photo` (`src/confidence.py`: "read off a photograph"), a mark that says
exactly what it is.

## How it works

No session can reach the photographs, so the work splits three ways, as the
stock review and the Edmonton-native review already do:

1. **The owner's PC fetches** (`scripts/fetch_colour_evidence.py`). For each
   plant on the list, iNaturalist's taxon, then up to six research-grade
   observations annotated *flowering*, inside Alberta and Saskatchewan first
   (anywhere when fewer than three). Two to four requests a plant, one a second,
   under the app's own User-Agent: about fifteen minutes. It caches to
   `data/fetched/colour_evidence.json` (never shipped, never published) and
   resumes where it stopped. `--probe "Ratibida columnifera"` prints one raw
   answer first, because this script was written without ever seeing one: the
   V2.79 lesson (a fixture that shares the reader's assumption passes against
   code that cannot open the real thing) applies to an API as much as to an
   archive.
2. **The owner looks** (`data/fetched/colour_review.html`, written by the same
   script, opened in any browser). One card per plant: what the app says now and
   why, six photographs of it in flower (each a link to its observation), and
   the colour buckets as buttons. The first colour pressed is the main one; a
   second is "and sometimes". *Looks right* keeps what is there; *No showy
   flower* and *Can't tell* are answers too. Answers stay in the browser between
   visits. *Save my answers* writes `colour_answers.json`.
3. **A session folds it in** (`scripts/fold_colour_answers.py`), after checking
   every answer: a plant on the list, colours from the eleven buckets. Each
   answer becomes the row's main hex (the bucket's own, so `classify_hex` reads
   it back to the same bucket), its colour list, and `photo`; the record of it,
   with the observations shown, goes in `data/flower_colour_checks.json`, which
   a test holds the catalogue to. A schema bump then reaches installed copies.

**Order of the list**: F172's six first; then the guesses, the genera where the
most species share one hex first (the shape of every colour bug found so far);
then the name and epithet reads; then the blanks; then the flora reads, last and
optional.

**What wins.** A photo check is the latest word on a plant, and
`seed_flower_colour.py`, which re-applies the name and epithet corrections,
leaves a checked plant alone, so running it later cannot undo an answer.

## Left alone

- **Sampling colour from pixels.** `fetch_flower_colour.py` already says why:
  one photograph in one light says *this photograph is pink*, not *this species
  is pink*. A person looking at six is the check.
- **The photographs themselves.** They are fetched for looking only. Which ones
  may ship, with what licence and credit, is V3.35.
- **Roseroot's shade**: the buckets cannot say dark red-purple from lavender;
  the swatch hex is the bucket's.

## Could not verify

- **The iNaturalist API's answers**, from here. The script names the fields it
  reads, checks them, and stops with what it found when they are not there;
  `--probe` is the first thing to run.
- The *flowering* annotation filter (term 12, value 13) is iNaturalist's
  Plant Phenology field as documented; if it returns nothing for a species the
  script tries again without it and says so on the card.
