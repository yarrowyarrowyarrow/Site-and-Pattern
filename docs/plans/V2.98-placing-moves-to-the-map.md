# V2.98 — Placing moves to the map

*One item: **F193**, step 3 of the V2.98 review of perusing, selecting and placing
plants and communities: "A panel that scrolls, and a placement bar that appears when
needed. Pattern, mix and fill options move to a compact bar over the map that shows
only while placing, freeing the column for browsing. Clears 1, 2 and 4." The owner
chose it first: "Let's do #3 (plant placement) first".*

The review ran the real app on a virtual display (1440 × 900 and 1366 × 768,
Arial-metric fonts, the example design, commit 93b96d8) and read the accessibility
tree through AT-SPI. Its six steps are numbered **F191–F196** here, in its order, so
"step 3" and "F193" are the same thing; the start-screen race it found is **F197**.
See the ID ledger in `ROADMAP_NEXT.md`.

## What was wrong

Both side-panel tabs ended in a collapsible **Placement** section holding the pattern
selector (Single / Row / Grid / Circle / Fill Area) and its settings, a mix builder,
and a Place button. The panel is one fixed-height column that does not scroll, so
opening Placement took its height from the list above it:

| Measured (review) | 1440 × 900 | 1366 × 768 |
|---|---|---|
| Communities visible, one selected, Placement open | 6 of 61 (92 px) | fewer |
| Height the five pattern buttons got (they need 23 px) | 11 px, labels illegible | **0 px**, gone; "Place on Map" cut in half |
| Plant rows left with Placement open and a mix built | about 7 | fewer |

And inside it, numbers that disagreed or did nothing (finding 4):

- **Qty** stayed visible and editable in Row, Grid and Circle, where its own tooltip
  says it is ignored. The number that counts there is Count.
- **Overlap** and **Base on mature canopy** showed in Single and Fill Area too, where
  `current_pattern()` never reads them.
- **Fill Area spacing defaulted to 1.5 m** (`placement_controls.py:277`) while the mix
  line above it said "~0.3 m spacing", so a forb fill came out about 25 times sparser
  than the plants' own guidance.

Reading the code for this found more places where what the panel said and what the
map did came apart:

1. **Esc on the map never reached Python.** `html/map/01-core.js:647` calls
   `setMode('none')` and tells nobody, so the chip kept "● Placing: …" and the status
   bar kept the gesture text (finding 9). A bar that "shows only while placing" would
   have made this worse: a banner over the map still saying *Placing* after Esc.
2. **A pending re-arm fired after a stand-down.** The 150 ms debounce timer calls
   `_auto_arm` directly; `request_rearm` checks `_armed` when it *starts* the timer,
   not when it fires. Change a Count, press Esc within 150 ms, and the map re-armed.
3. **A community was armed with the previous community's spacing.**
   `_on_polyculture_selected` arms (`_auto_arm`) *before* it sets Cell spacing to the
   new community's natural diameter, and Cell spacing never announced a change, so
   Row / Grid / Circle / Fill used the old number until something else re-armed.
4. **Double-clicking a community placed it Single** whatever the pattern said, while
   the chip, armed by the first click, said Row.
5. **"Place Mix on Map" (communities) never set `_armed`**, so the chip never showed
   it and changing the pattern did not re-arm it. With the pattern on Single it
   opened a modal dialog telling you to change the pattern.
6. **The plant chip said "the mix" in Single**, where the map places the selected
   plant: `_current_pattern()` drops the mix for Single.
7. **A plant mix with nothing selected could not be placed** in Row, Grid or Circle:
   the button was enabled and `_on_place_clicked` returned on `not _selected_plant`.
8. **Arming one panel left the other's chip armed.** Select a plant, then a
   community: both said "● Placing".
9. **Choosing a tool (Measure, Boundary, …) while placing left the chips armed**:
   only `_cancel_draw` stood them down, and the tools do not go through it.
10. **Esc did not stop a single community at all.** `_on_polyculture_click` places
    on any map click while Python's `_current_mode` is `'polyculture'`, and Esc on
    the map never changed it. Measured in the running app: arm Aromatic Herb Circle
    (Single), press Esc in the map (the map's mode reads `none`, Python's still
    `polyculture`, the chip still "● Placing"), click once on empty map outside the
    yard: **19 → 25 plants**, the whole community, drawn outside the boundary. The
    review had this as "the panel still says Placing" (finding 9); it is worse than
    that for communities, because the Python side is the one that places them.
11. **Community spacing did nothing for Row, Grid and Circle.** The map lays out a
    pattern at `currentPlant.spacing_m`, and for a community that was its preview
    member's own spacing: Aromatic Herb Circle (2.0 m across) previews on Wild
    Bergamot, 0.3 m. Measured: a 4.65 m row placed **16 communities, 96 plants**,
    one solid band, with Cell spacing reading 2.0 m. Only Fill Area read the field.
    Found by putting the field in the bar, where it reads as the thing that decides
    the row.

## The change

### A bar over the map, not a section in the column

While the map is armed to place, a bar floats over the top edge of the map:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ● Placing Wild Bergamot in a row. Click the start point, then the end point.  Colour ◉  [Done] │
│ [Single][Row][Grid][Circle][Fill area]  Count [auto]  ☐ Drift  Overlap [0 %]  ☐ Canopy width │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The first line says what the map will do on the next click, in words, and how to
stop. The second holds the settings for *this* pattern only, and wraps rather than
clips on a narrow map. When placing ends, by Done, Esc, a finished fill or another
tool, the bar goes.

**Over the map, not above it, and why that was tested rather than assumed.** A strip
in the column above the map (where `first_step_bar.py` lives) would push the map
down each time placing starts and back up when it stops, and Leaflet re-centres on a
resize, so the whole drawing jumps by half the bar's height at the moment you are
aiming a click. Floating costs a strip of map instead: the top of the map is covered
while you place, and you pan if your spot is under it. It is inset past the zoom
buttons, which stay reachable. Tried in the running app before choosing (Qt 6.11,
software GL): a plain QFrame over the QWebEngineView draws on top and stays on top
through map pans and redraws, and AT-SPI reads its label and button by name.

**Not a child of the map widget.** That would have been the easy way to follow the
map around, and it is invisible to screen readers: Qt's accessible interface for
`QWebEngineView` reports the web page as its only child. Measured, not assumed: a
label and a button parented to the map view were absent from the AT-SPI dump (0 of
440 lines), and the same label parented to the map's column appeared by name. The
bar is a child of the map's column and
tracks the map widget's geometry, which also keeps it over the map in split view
(the map is re-parented into a splitter inside the same column).

### What moves into the bar, and what stays

**Moves:** the pattern selector and its settings, from both tabs; Qty, now only in
Single; the plant's marker colour, only when placing one species; the communities'
spacing, now "Community spacing"; the status line; stopping.

*Amended during the work:* the colour sits on the **first** line, beside the name it
applies to. On the second line it pushed Row's settings onto a third at 1366 × 768
(a 121 px bar); moved, Row fits one line at both sizes (85 px).

**Stays in the panel: the two mixes.** This is a deviation from the review's
sentence ("pattern, mix and fill options move"), on purpose. A mix is built while
browsing, by dragging rows from the list onto it, so it has to be where the list is,
and a drop target in a bar that only exists while placing could not be reached. What
moves is the mix's *placement*: each mix strip gets a **Place mix** button, the one
explicit way to arm a mix, and the bar then says "Placing a row of your 3-plant mix".
The rows cap at four visible and scroll, as the community mix already did, so a full
mix no longer eats the list. *Found on the way:* both mixes' rows showed only one row,
because a scroll area asks for its minimum, not its content (34 px for rows needing
70 and 104); each is now sized to its rows up to the cap. The cap assumes 26 px rows
and the rows are about 34, so about three show before scrolling; left as it is.
The action buttons hide while a mix is empty, where they were four disabled buttons
under a one-line hint.

**The panel's Place button goes.** It had three jobs: status chip (now the bar's
first line), stand-down toggle (now Done and Esc), and the only way into Fill Area.
The last is the one that needed a decision.

### A panel that scrolls

The review's step had two halves, and moving the settings out was not enough on its
own. With a full community mix at 1366 × 768 the column needed **806 px of minimums
in 670**, so the layout squeezed the mix strip to 96 of its 181 px, its rows drawn
over its own Place mix button and the pattern card's text over its title: finding
1's failure, on a different section. Both panels are now `src/scroll_column.py`
columns, which scroll when the minimums cannot fit and change nothing when they can.

The first version scrolled when nothing needed to: at 1366 × 768 with a community
selected and no mix, a scroll bar and nothing squeezed. **One word-wrapping label
makes a layout report height-for-width, and QScrollArea then takes the *preferred*
height as the minimum** (779 px, against 671 px of real minimums and 656 of
viewport). The column's layout now reports no height-for-width, so it is sized by
minimums; the labels still wrap. The pattern card's floor went from 120 to 96 px (it
scrolls itself), which was the last 15 px between that state and no scroll bar.

### Fill Area arms like the other patterns

V2.37 excluded Fill Area from arming on selection, because entering fill mode
restarts the polygon: arrow-keying through the list would have thrown away the
corners you had drawn. With the Place button gone, that exclusion would leave Fill
with no way in at all. So the cause is removed instead: `setMode('fill')` now keeps
its points when the map is already drawing a fill, and a re-arm swaps *what* the
polygon will be planted with. Selecting another plant mid-drawing changes the plants,
keeps the corners, and the bar's first line says so. A finished fill still ends
placing (the map returns to idle, as before).

### The map reports its mode

`setMode` now tells Python every mode it enters (`bridge.onModeChanged`). Python acts
only when the map *leaves* placing: the bar goes, both panels stand down, the
in-flight placement stashes (not the pending fill, which the completed polygon still
needs) are dropped, and if the map went idle the status bar says Ready. It never
calls `_cancel_draw`, which would send the map a `cancelDraw` of its own.

The echo carries a sequence number, because a JS-side change and a Python-side one
can cross. Every Python-initiated mode change stamps `_pyModeSeq`; an echo stamped
older than Python's last change is stale and ignored. Without it, an Esc arriving a
moment after you picked a new plant would stand the fresh placement down.

Only the placing modes are handled. The same echo could reset the label after a
boundary or hedgerow finishes, and does not, because those completion handlers set
their own status and this increment is about placing.

### The smaller fixes

- The re-arm timer is stopped by `set_armed(False)`, and each panel's timer target
  checks `_armed` when it fires (2). *Correction:* a first draft put that check in a
  closure inside `rearm_timer`. The closure held the panel alive through its own
  timer, so in the tests a finished test's panel fired into the next one and
  segfaulted the run. A bound method, whose connection PyQt ties to the object's
  life, does not.
- A row, grid or circle of communities steps at Community spacing (11):
  `_enter_plant_mode` sends the community's spacing to the map, as it already sent a
  plant mix's.
- A community's spacing is set before it is armed, and editing it re-arms (3).
- Double-click, Enter and a click on the current row all arm with the pattern the
  bar shows; the click matters because with the button gone it is the only way back
  to the same plant after Done (4, and finding 9's "clicking the same plant again does
  nothing").
- Place mix arms with the current pattern, and from Single it switches to Row rather
  than opening a dialog (5).
- The first line names the plant in Single and the mix in Row, Grid, Circle and Fill
  (6); a mix with no list selection places, previewed by its first species (7).
- Arming one panel stands the other down (8); entering any other tool does too, via
  the echo (9).
- "Place ×5 on Map" sets Single and Qty 5 in the bar, so the bar agrees with the map.
- Fill spacing defaults to the plant's own spacing, or the mix's (the "~x m" number
  its line shows), and follows the selection, as a community's spacing already did.
- What the bar says is captured when the map is armed, not re-read from the current
  selection, so it always describes what the map holds.
- The status bar's mode line names the mix ("Row of your 3-plant mix"), where it
  named the species that previews it.
- Changing a mix row's ratio or colour re-arms (the armed recipe carries both); a
  mix cleared while armed, with nothing selected, stands the map down; choosing
  Single while a community mix is armed places the selected community rather than
  flipping the bar back to Row.

### Accessibility of the new bar

The review's baseline (step 5) is its own increment, but a new surface should not be
built below it. The bar's text is 13 px, contrast at least 4.5:1, every control has
an accessible name ("Row count", "Overlap", "Marker colour for …", "Done placing"),
every control shows keyboard focus, and targets are at least 24 px high. The Overlap
slider becomes a spin box: the slider's only readout was a separate label, and the
accessibility tree announced the slider as "Placement Mode".

- **Selecting still arms the map** (step 1, F191). The bar makes it visible, which
  was half of the complaint, but looking at a plant still readies a click. Changing
  that is the owner's call on a V2.37 decision made from tester feedback.
- **The painted card, the three pickers, the filters and the community list** (steps
  2, 4 and 6).
- **The accessibility baseline outside the bar** (step 5).
- **Finding 4's third bullet**, the habitat score being live in Stats and behind
  Calculate in Analysis. It is not about placing; it goes with step 5 or on its own.
- **Stickiness.** Single and pattern placement still stay armed after each click,
  and a fill still ends after one polygon.
- **Status-bar "Mode:" text.** It keeps its long gesture sentences, apart from
  naming the mix; the bar is the prominent answer and the status bar the quiet one.
- **The keyboard route into the bar.** It is in the tab order after the map:
  reachable, which the collapsed Placement section was not, but far from the list.
  A shortcut into it belongs with step 5.
- **A community's ghost preview** still draws its preview member's canopy, now at
  community spacing; a footprint for the whole community is step 6's.
- **The start-screen race** (F197), recorded in the backlog.

## CI: the first push segfaulted, and the cause was older than this increment

The first push (26a5032) went red on the Tests workflow (run 36753580548): exit 139,
`Fatal Python error: Segmentation fault`, with `processEvents()` in
`TestPlacementBarFlow._main` (`tests/test_placement_bar.py:329`) as the top Python
frame. The installer workflows passed, and the full suite had passed in this
container (4058 OK), so the first job was a reproduction.

**Reproduced.** `test_app_smoke`, `test_placement_arming` and `test_placement_bar`
in one process, with WebEngine imported before the `QApplication` (or the smoke
tests skip) and `MALLOC_PERTURB_=165`: 3 runs of 3 segfault. Under gdb the native
frame is `QMetaObjectPublisher::classInfoForObject`, called from `initializeClient`
on a message from a web page's channel. So the crash was not in the bar at all: a
page had just sent its first message to its `QWebChannel`, and an object registered
on that channel had been freed.

**What freed it.** The smoke tests' main window. Its `tearDownClass` calls `close()`
and `deleteLater()`. PyQt's `deleteLater()` hands the window to C++, and outside an
event loop Qt never runs a deferred delete (measured: `processEvents()` leaves it
pending; only a nested event loop or `sendPostedEvents(DeferredDelete)` runs it), so
the window and its still-loading map page lived on in C++. A later collection pass
found the window's Python wrappers unreachable and cleared them (traced: the
window's and the map's wrappers are collected inside `init_db` in
`test_placement_arming`'s setup, and the window's C++ object is not destroyed). The
C++ tree survived that; the map's bridge did not, because `MapWidget` built it with
no parent (`MapBridge()`), so the wrapper's attribute was its only owner: its C++
object was destroyed in the same pass. The channel still held it, since the page
had not connected yet, and the page's first message read the freed bridge.

**Older than V2.98.** V2.97 frees the bridge under a live page in the same sequence
(traced, with `test_placement_score` in place of the new module); nothing afterwards
pumps events long enough for the page to connect before the window is gone. The
flow tests in `test_placement_bar` are the first code that does, which is why the
crash landed in them, in a different test from run to run (anywhere from the 2nd
flow test to the 11th, across seven crashing runs). This increment's three new
smoke tests are not the trigger: with them removed it still crashed, 2 of 2.

**The fix** is one argument: `MapBridge(self)`, as `Map3DWidget` already built its
`Scene3DBridge(self)`. The bridge now lives exactly as long as the view, and the
view's destruction order, measured, is view, page, channel, bridge, so the channel
is always gone first. After it, the reproduction is clean 3 runs of 3.
`test_architecture_guard` gains `test_every_object_on_a_web_channel_has_a_qt_parent`
(every `registerObject` in `src/` is given an object built with a parent; it fails
on the old line, naming `src/map_widget.py:457`), and the smoke test asserts the
real bridge's parent and that C++ owns it. CLAUDE.md's table of whole-run aborts
gains the row, and how to get the native frame when the Python one is only
`processEvents()`.

In the app itself this could not happen during a session: the only `MapWidget` is
the main window's, and `main.py` holds the window until exit. It was a hazard for
anything that tears a map down with `deleteLater()` while its page is loading,
which today is the tests.

CI after the fix: pending.

## What could not be verified here

- **Windows and macOS.** The bar over the web view was tested on Linux (Xvfb,
  software GL, Qt 6.11). Qt composites a widget over `QWebEngineView` on every
  platform, but a GPU compositor was not in the loop here. If it failed somewhere,
  the failure would be visible: no bar while placing, and the panel no longer has a
  Place button to fall back on.
- **Screen readers, heard.** Names were read from the AT-SPI tree, not listened to
  in NVDA, VoiceOver or Orca. The bar appearing is not announced: PyQt6 does not
  expose Qt's accessibility events, so there is no alert to send.
- **HiDPI and OS text scaling.** (Split view was checked: with the 3D preview
  under the map, the bar stays over the map's top edge, above the splitter.)
- **`tests/test_undo_redo.py`**, which segfaults in a container (CLAUDE.md); it
  runs on a real desktop only, since CI leaves it out for the same reason.
