# V3.11 — The boundary can be pressed again (F213)

*The owner, 3 October 2026: "The boundary work seems to have regressed? I want to
be able to press the boundary to change it."*

## What was wrong (measured)

A headless-Chromium probe loaded the real `html/map.html`, drew a boundary,
and clicked inside it at a spot away from the area label:

| State | What the click landed on | Edit handles? |
|---|---|---|
| boundary only | the boundary's `path` | yes |
| one plant placed elsewhere | a `CANVAS` in `leaflet-overlay-pane` | **no** |
| one plant, click on the edge line | the same `CANVAS` | **no** |

Plants are drawn by one shared canvas renderer (`04-tools.js`
`canvasRenderer`, chosen because SVG plants starved the satellite tiles). Leaflet
gives a canvas renderer one `<canvas>` the size of the whole map, in the
overlay pane (z 400), and keeps it there for the rest of the session once
anything has been drawn on it: a plant, the grid, canopy rings. V2.37 moved the
boundary into its own pane *below* the overlay pane (`boundaryPane`, z 350), so
that it is the ground everything else is drawn on. Both decisions were right,
and together they meant the canvas covers the boundary everywhere: every click,
right-click and drag on it reaches the canvas, which finds no plant there and
hands the event to the map. So from the first plant on, the boundary could not
be pressed, could not be right-clicked (colour, labels, remove), and in edit
mode could not be dragged by its middle. A design with no plants still worked,
which is why it read as a regression. **It dates from V2.37**, checked on the
branches: V2.36 already drew plants on the canvas and kept the boundary in the
overlay pane, where an SVG is drawn over a canvas and so was reachable (and,
filling the yard, over the plants: "the topmost click target across the whole
yard", as V2.37's note in `01-core.js` puts what it fixed).

**Found while testing the fix, and older than it:** with the boundary in edit
mode, dragging one corner ended the edit. Letting go of a dragged handle clicks
on the handle, the handle has no click handler, so the click reaches the map,
and the map's "a click on the background ends the edit" took it. A boundary was
reshaped one corner per press. Measured with real mouse input on the V3.10 code
(no plants): pressed, dragged a corner, `boundaryEditId` was `null`.

Shapes (buildings, beds) were checked the same way and still work: an SVG in
the same pane as a canvas is always drawn over it (Leaflet's CSS gives the
canvas z-index 100 and SVG 200 inside a pane), and Leaflet's SVG root takes no
pointer events, so only what is drawn catches a click.

## What was decided

**The boundary stops being a click target, and the map decides when a click is
on it** (`boundaryAt` / `boundaryClicked` in `02-boundary.js`). A click that
reaches the map in idle mode, inside a shown boundary or within 6 px of its
line, and that landed on nothing else that takes clicks, is a press on the
boundary. That is one path whether or not a canvas exists, so the two cases
cannot drift apart again, and it is what V2.37 meant: the boundary is the
bottom layer, pressed where nothing on top of it is.

- **What counts as "nothing else"**: plants and shapes take their own clicks
  and stop them, so the map never sees those. Structures, hedgerows, the sun
  path, notes and the relationship web are interactive but stop only
  right-clicks, so the map does see their left clicks; the rule reads the
  click's own target and leaves anything with Leaflet's `leaflet-interactive`
  class alone. Labels take no pointer events and count as ground.
- **Right-click** shows the boundary's menu by the same test, after the modes
  that already use a right-click (cancelling a terrain box, a sun anchor, a
  half-placed pattern) have had it.
- **Drag the middle in edit mode** starts from the map's mousedown by the same
  test, idle mode only. A press with no movement no longer tells Python the
  geometry changed (it marked the design modified for nothing).
- **The handles are the boundary.** The corner and scale handles carry
  `sp-boundary-handle`, and a click on one is a press on the boundary, so the
  edit carries on from corner to corner. Pressing the boundary already being
  edited keeps its handles rather than rebuilding them, and where two
  boundaries overlap a press goes on with the one being edited.
- **The pin drop is not a boundary press.** Site's "drop the pin by hand" keeps
  the map's mode at `none` and only changes the cursor; `setSitePinDropMode`
  now also records that it is armed, and a click then drops the pin only (and a
  drag pans, rather than moving a boundary in edit mode).
- **A pointer over the boundary** in idle mode says it can be pressed, the cue
  an interactive polygon gave on its own.

**Not chosen:** passing the canvas's unused clicks to whatever is beneath it
(re-dispatching events under Leaflet), which would also have worked but edits
the renderer's internals and doubles every event's path; and moving the
boundary back above the plants, which is the V2.37 bug the low pane fixed.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **The relationship web is under the same canvas (F214, opened).** Its pane
  (`relationshipPane`, z 385) is below the overlay pane too, so with plants on
  the map, the element under an animal's chip and under an edge is the canvas:
  the web's tooltips, highlight and popups cannot be reached. Fixing it is a
  choice between raising its pane (its lines would then lie over plants, which
  V2.31 kept them from) and routing through the map as here, so it is the
  owner's, not folded into this fix.
- **Shapes and the pin drop.** A shape still enters its own edit mode when
  pressed during a pin drop, as it did before; nothing here changed it, and it
  was not asked about.
- **Starting a tool leaves boundary edit mode on.** `setMode` ends a shape's
  outline edit but not a boundary's; that is older than this and harmless now
  that the middle-drag only runs in idle mode.
- **The phone-scan import dialog** listens to map clicks with the map's mode at
  `none`; a click inside the boundary there now also opens the boundary's
  handles beside picking the point. It used to pick nothing at all with no
  plants on the map, and pick without handles with plants.

## How it is checked

- `tests/test_boundary_press.py` boots the real map page in headless Chromium
  (`html/boundary_probe.html`, which drives `map.html` in a frame) and presses
  it with mouse events sent to whatever the browser's own hit test finds under
  each pixel (`elementFromPoint`), so a layer over another catches them as it
  would a hand: with a plant on the map, a click
  inside the boundary opens its handles, a click on its line does too, a click
  outside closes them; a click on a plant, a shape or a structure inside the
  boundary does not open them; right-click opens its menu; in edit mode a drag
  in the middle moves it and a drag on a plant does not; a press without
  movement sends nothing; a dragged corner leaves the edit on; while placing, a
  click inside places once; a pin drop click drops only the pin; a hidden
  boundary cannot be pressed; the pointer changes over it. **Run against the
  V3.10 map scripts, eight of its fifteen browser checks fail** (the press, the
  press on and beside the line, shift to select, the right-click menu, the
  middle drag, a still press keeping the edit, the corner, the pointer) and all
  three source guards; with the fix, all eighteen pass.
- The same cases were pressed with real mouse input (Playwright driving
  Chromium, not committed): a press inside and on the line with a plant on the
  map, a corner dragged with edit mode kept, the right-click menu, and a 50 px
  middle drag that sent Python one geometry change.
- The map, bridge, keyboard, marquee, placing and architecture guard modules:
  206 tests, 0 failed (14 skipped, all for PyQt6, which this container lacks).
  The whole suite, with Qt, is left to CI on the push.

## What could not be verified here

- The packaged app on Windows: the probe drives the same page in Chromium,
  which is what QtWebEngine is, but not inside Qt's web view with its
  QWebChannel bridge (the probe records the bridge calls instead).
