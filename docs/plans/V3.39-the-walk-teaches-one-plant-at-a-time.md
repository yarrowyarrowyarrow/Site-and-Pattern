# V3.39 · The walk teaches one plant at a time (F255)

*Part of [the V3.38 plan](V3.38-the-learn-side-and-the-example.md), which
quotes the owner in full. The part this increment answers:*

> I'd like to be less overwhelmed by the number of creatures that shows up and
> have it be more targeted learning. This is this plant, here is what it does,
> here are creatures it supports (with photos for both the plant and the
> animal). Then if I click on an animal (from the list as they are hard to
> capture/select when they are moving) it tells a little bit about that
> animal, especially the plants it relies on, both on the design and not yet
> on the design. Relationality is key. The stand alone "who lives here" …
> simply overwhelms and does not encourage learning.

V3.38 made a click open the plant's card at all. This increment makes the
card worth opening, takes the roster down to what a person can use, and
fixes what the walks plant.

## What changes

### The plant card leads with what the plant is recorded as food for

1. Photograph and credit, name, scientific name.
2. **What it is recorded as food for**, one sentence per part of the plant
   (`scene_dossier._does`): *"Its flowers are recorded as food for 9 kinds:
   4 bees, 2 butterflies and moths, 3 other insects. Its leaves are recorded
   as food for the caterpillars of 13 butterflies and moths."* **Corrected
   from this plan's draft**, which said "Its flowers feed 18 bees": since
   V3.20 (OV-5) every page says *recorded as food for*, because the records
   say an animal was seen at the plant somewhere in two provinces, not here.
3. **Who would lose the only plant here they are recorded with**, four by
   their English names and the rest counted. The first build listed them all:
   fifteen names for an aspen, ten of them Latin, ahead of everything else.
4. **The animals, eight at most, one row each** (until now a row per record,
   so a bee at the nectar and the pollen was two rows), ordered to put first
   the ones a person can get to know: an English name (the Overview's rule,
   `yard_overview_items.english_name`), a photograph, one that depends on the
   plant, one out in the scene now. Each row says every way it uses the plant
   in the Food page's words (*nectar and pollen*, *caterpillar host*) and opens
   the animal's card. A photograph on a row carries its credit on the row.
5. **And the rest, folded**: *"and 41 more: 22 bees, 12 butterflies and moths,
   7 other insects, most known only by a Latin name"*, each still a link.
6. The rest of what the card had (badges, form, season bar, size over time,
   safety, where to buy, where the records come from) under *More about this
   plant*, closed.

### The animal card: the plants it relies on, here and not here yet

1. *← back to* the card you came from (a trail, so plant → animal → plant
   leads back step by step); Esc closes.
2. Photograph where the catalogue has one, else its group's sign.
3. Name, scientific name, group; its sentence from the catalogue; the
   months it is out; the bee and butterfly tables' facts.
4. **Here**: the plants in this landscape it uses, each saying every use
   (*"lays eggs on it and sips nectar at it"*) and opening that plant's card.
5. **Not here yet**: up to six catalogue plants it uses, native to Alberta in
   the walk; food first, **a butterfly's or moth's caterpillar hosts first
   among them** (the plant it relies on, where a nectar plant is one of
   many; the first build put the willows' nectar ahead of the birches), the
   walk's own plants next, documented records before derived ones, then the
   plants recorded as food for the most animals. **Plant one** beside the
   walk's own plants: it puts that plant on the trowel (`Scene3DBridge.
   choosePlant` → `walk_cards.on_plant_chosen`) and the next click on the
   ground plants it. Only the walk's own plants, because the planting list is
   the community's species on purpose (`reference_edit.palette`).

`what_it_feeds.animal_plans` answers *not here yet* for every animal at once
(four queries, where `animal_plan` per animal would have been about 1,200 per
scene push, and the walk pushes on every edit); `animal_plan` is now that
function for one animal, so the Food page and the cards cannot disagree. The
Food page's list changes with it: caterpillar hosts first for a butterfly or
moth, then the most-recorded plants, instead of alphabetical.

### Who is out, without the wall of names

*Identify* names only the creatures tied to the open card (a plant's users,
or that animal), and with no card open only those within 6 m, where it was
every creature within 13 m. The roster is one line, *"This landscape is
recorded as food for 447 kinds of animals."*, over a closed fold, *"26 are
out now"*, which opens onto the creatures out now by group, each a link to
its card, the ones known only by a Latin name folded again. Its count is the
dossier's `reach`, the V3.20 rule (food records, each animal once, shelter
alone not counted); it had been the habitat score's tally, which counts
shelter too.

spruce and aspen:

**Not checked against the source in this session.** The lists are the
dominant and characteristic species of each community as the Natural
Regions Committee's *Natural Regions and Subregions of Alberta* (2006)
describes them, from what I know of it; no session can reach the report to
check line by line, so the owner reviews the table (D7). Two gaps are the
catalogue's, not the table's: it has no Engelmann spruce or subalpine fir
(its *Balsam Fir* is *Abies balsamea*), so the subalpine walk leads with
lodgepole pine, white spruce and Douglas fir and says it is montane-leaning.

**Planted from rules, not coordinates (P1)**, in `src/reference_layout.py`:
trees in clumps (an aspen clone's stems about 2.4 m apart, spruce and pine in
a group about 3.4 m apart), the first clump north of the centre so its shade
falls away from the open ground and the rest joining it east and west;
shrubs in thickets of two or three at the grove's edge; wildflowers in drifts
of three to five; grasses in patches of four to six, spread over what is
left. **Light decides who stands where**, which is better than the draft's
"never under the crowns": a plant that needs full sun is never under a crown,
a plant of the shade goes under one (bunchberry, twinflower and wild lily
under the spruce; heart-leaved arnica under the pine), and a plant that takes
either stays at the edge or in the open. The square grows from 20 m to 26 m.

**What the measurements changed.**

| Found | So |
|---|---|
| The walker starts at the plants' centroid (`scene_contract._scene_origin`, `walkSpawn`), and a grove in the north pulls it north: the first layout put a balsam poplar 0.63 m from the riparian walk's start | The layout is centred on its own centroid and the clearing (3 m, no tree or shrub) follows it until the two agree; nearest woody plant to the start is now 2.9 m or more in every walk |
| Even shares let a layer run out before its last leads: the riparian walk had no fireweed, the prairie no crocus or golden bean | The leads' 70% is divided among them first, so every lead is planted (a test) |
| A matrix that took each grass's species from the nearest patch gave bluejoint one plant in the boreal walk | Grasses go in patches with quotas, like the drifts |
| The genera's other species included wet-ground plants in the dry fescue grassland (marsh aster, water avens) and prairie smoke by the river | A species fills a walk out only if it takes the community's typical ground: no wetter-only plant where the leads' middle is dry, no dry-only plant where it is wet |
| At 30 grasses the mixedgrass prairie read as a lawn with tufts in it (screenshot) | Grassland walks are denser: 97 to 130 plants, the forest walks 63 to 66; every one a real plant with a card, never filler (the V2.29 lesson in `02-plants.js`). A walk builds in under 0.1 s |
| Listing the leads in the community's scored species would raise the presence bar in eight layers, and move the habitat score of designs that compare with them | `resolve_reference_community` keeps `layers` exactly as it was (checked against the committed version for every walk) and returns the leads beside it |

**A saved walk keeps what the person planted** (`src/reference_upgrade.py`).
A walk you have planted in is a saved file, which was never rebuilt; it would
have kept its alphabetical trees for good. The file now records the version
of the community it was built from and its centre; an older one is built
afresh where it stood (the middle of its shipped plants' extent: 0.19 m off
on a real old walk) and the person's plants come back with their spot, their
planting year and their identity. The tag that says whose a plant is lives on
the feature, not the placed-plant record, which is why the first build of
this kept nothing.

**The planting list** offers every lead species first, then the other
species that fill the walk out, layer by layer, in the glossary's words
(tree, shrub, wildflower, grass, not "canopy" and "forb").

## Not changed here

- The score and features (V3.40), the quiz (V3.41), the lessons (V3.42).
- Animal photographs: 58 of 1,144 have one; the rest show their group's
  sign until F257 brings more. Photographs are shown only once the app has
  cached them, so a session's screenshots show the signs.
- The cards in the design's 3D preview get the same layout and data
  (`walk_cards.build` for both windows), but *Plant one* appears only in the
  walk: the design's trowel takes its plant from the plant list, and the
  preview does not limit *not here yet* to a province.
- The card and the roster share the top right corner, as before: an open
  card covers the roster until it is closed.
