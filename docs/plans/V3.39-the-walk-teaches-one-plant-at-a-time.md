# V3.39 · The walk teaches one plant at a time (F255)

*Part of [the V3.38 plan](V3.38-the-learn-side-and-the-example.md), which
quotes the owner in full. The part this increment answers:*

> I'd like to be less overwhelmed by the number of creatures that shows up and
> have it be more targeted learning. This is this plant, here is what it does,
> here are creatures it supports (with photos for both the plant and the
> animal). Then if I click on an animal (from the list as they are hard to
> capture/select when they are moving) it tells a little bit about that
> animal, especially the plants it relies on, both on the design and not yet
> on the design. Relationality is key. The stand alone "who lives here" …
> simply overwhelms and does not encourage learning.

V3.38 made a click open the plant's card at all. This increment makes the
card worth opening, takes the roster down to what a person can use, and
fixes what the walks plant.

## What changes

### The plant card leads with what the plant does

1. Photograph and credit, name, scientific name.
2. **What it does here**, one line per way it feeds or shelters, in the
   words `what_it_feeds` already uses everywhere else: *"Its flowers feed 18
   bees and 4 butterflies and moths. Its leaves feed the caterpillars of 31
   butterflies and moths. Its fruit feeds 9 birds."*
3. **Who would lose their only food here** if it were pulled (as before).
4. **The creatures it supports, eight at most**, ordered so the first are
   the ones a person can get to know: an English name, then a photograph,
   then a specialist (it has nowhere else to go), then out in the scene now.
   Each row says how the animal uses the plant and opens its card.
5. **And the rest, folded**: *"and 41 more: 22 bees, 12 moths, 7 other
   insects, most known only by a Latin name"*, one click to list them all,
   each still a link.
6. The rest of what the card had (badges, form, season bar, size over time,
   sources, safety, where to buy) under *More about this plant ▸*, closed.

### The animal card: the plants it relies on, here and not here yet

From a row in a plant card, a click on a creature, or the list of who is out:

1. *← back to the plant you came from*; Esc closes.
2. Photograph where the catalogue has one, else its group's sign.
3. Name, scientific name, group; its sentence from the catalogue; the
   months it is out; what the bee and butterfly tables say (nesting,
   tongue, how it overwinters).
4. **Here**: the plants in this landscape it uses and how, each a link to
   that plant's card.
5. **Not here yet**: up to six catalogue plants it uses that are native to
   the province, the ones feeding the most animals first, each with how it
   uses them, and **Plant one** beside any the walk offers. *Plant one* puts
   that plant on the trowel; the next click on the ground plants it.

### Who is out, without the wall of names

*Identify* labels only the creatures tied to the plant whose card is open
(none open: the few nearest you), and shows one line, *"This landscape feeds
470 kinds of animals. 26 are out now ▸"*. The arrow opens the ones out now
as a list of links, grouped, the Latin-only ones folded. The full roster
with every name is gone.

**Not checked against the source in this session.** The lists are the
dominant and characteristic species of each community as the Natural
Regions Committee's *Natural Regions and Subregions of Alberta* (2006)
describes them, from what I know of it; no session can reach the report to
check line by line, so the owner reviews the table (D7). Two gaps are the
catalogue's, not the table's: it has no Engelmann spruce or subalpine fir
(its *Balsam Fir* is *Abies balsamea*), so the subalpine walk leads with
lodgepole pine, white spruce and Douglas fir and says it is montane-leaning.

**Planted from rules, not coordinates (P1)**, in `src/reference_layout.py`:
trees in clumps (an aspen clone's stems about 2.4 m apart, spruce and pine in
a group about 3.4 m apart), the first clump north of the centre so its shade
falls away from the open ground and the rest joining it east and west;
shrubs in thickets of two or three at the grove's edge; wildflowers in drifts
of three to five; grasses in patches of four to six, spread over what is
left. **Light decides who stands where**, which is better than the draft's
"never under the crowns": a plant that needs full sun is never under a crown,
a plant of the shade goes under one (bunchberry, twinflower and wild lily
under the spruce; heart-leaved arnica under the pine), and a plant that takes
either stays at the edge or in the open. The square grows from 20 m to 26 m.

**A saved walk keeps what the person planted** (`src/reference_upgrade.py`).
A walk you have planted in is a saved file, which was never rebuilt; it would
have kept its alphabetical trees for good. The file now records the version
of the community it was built from and its centre; an older one is built
afresh where it stood (the middle of its shipped plants' extent: 0.19 m off
on a real old walk) and the person's plants come back with their spot, their
planting year and their identity. The tag that says whose a plant is lives on
the feature, not the placed-plant record, which is why the first build of
this kept nothing.

**The planting list** offers every lead species first, then the other
species that fill the walk out, layer by layer, in the glossary's words
(tree, shrub, wildflower, grass, not "canopy" and "forb").
