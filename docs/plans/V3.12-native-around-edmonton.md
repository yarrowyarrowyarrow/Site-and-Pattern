# V3.12 — native around Edmonton

The owner's ask, in their words:

> *"Next I'd like to review the nativity of plants. Specifically how the app
> compares to the website. I'd like to have the option to choose Edmonton
> specific native plants which is a more narrow list that is not yet available
> in either the website or the app. I'd like for this option to extend into
> plant communities too with the ones containing non native (to Edmonton)
> plants not showing up. Perhaps we would need to generate some more local to
> edmonton plant communities as well. [I want] to enable people to plant things
> specific to their region and as it is things are unclear. For example there
> is an eastern red columbine that is native to southern AB but which would be
> inappropriate for the edmonton region."*

Three IDs from the ledger: **F220** (native around Edmonton: the list, its rule,
the option in the app and on the website), **F221** (communities of the Edmonton
region), **F222** (the desktop species page says where a plant is native, and
the seed notes that contradicted VASCAN are corrected).

### The columbine is a correction, not an example

VASCAN (in `data/fetched/flora_nativity.json`) records *Aquilegia canadensis*
native in **Saskatchewan and Manitoba, with no Alberta entry at all**. It is not
native to southern Alberta either. The catalogue already says so
(`native_provinces: "SK"`, `native_to_alberta: 0`, no AB badge in the list, "Native
to: SK" on the website). The red columbine that *is* native to Alberta is
*Aquilegia formosa*, recorded in the southwest mountains (69 records, Eastern
Continental Ranges, high confidence), with no record within 50 km of Edmonton.

So where did "native to southern AB" come from? Three things on the desktop say
it without saying it:

1. The species page has **no "Native to" line**. It does have **"Found in: Moist
   Mixed Grassland (6 records, low confidence)"**, an ecoregion that straddles
   the Alberta-Saskatchewan border. All six records are in Saskatchewan; the line
   cannot say so.
2. The row's **notes** read *"plants sold in Alberta are often of Saskatchewan
   provenance, which may differ ecologically from true Alberta-native
   populations"*. There are no Alberta-native populations to differ from.
3. It is a member of the seeded **Caterpillar Host Garden**, with nothing on the
   community saying one member is not native to the province.

Four more rows' notes contradict VASCAN the same way, and all five are rewritten
(F222; schema 93 carries them to existing installs):

| species | the note said | VASCAN in Alberta |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern Red Columbine | "true Alberta-native populations" | no entry |
| Purple Coneflower | "ecologically appropriate for Alberta grassland restoration" | **introduced** |
| Bur Oak | "keystone oak of the aspen parkland" | **introduced** |
| Prairie Dropseed | "Alberta's mixed-grass prairie ecosystems" | no entry |
| Big Bluestem | "recommended for Alberta Zone 3-4 plantings" | no entry |

Each now says where VASCAN records it native and what it is in an Alberta
planting. The columbine's `native_region` ("SK provenance used in Alberta")
is corrected too. `tests/test_edmonton_communities.py` holds a pattern that
would have caught all five, run over every row not native to Alberta.

## Why "native around Edmonton" cannot be read off the records

The catalogue already knows where each species has been recorded: a 0.25° grid
(`data/plant_ranges.json`) and the published points (`plant_occurrence_points.json`),
both from the GBIF cache `data/fetched/plant_occurrences.json`. `site_fit.py`
says the problem in one line: *"It cannot stand in for nativity: Bur Oak has 115
records around Edmonton, planted trees."* Measured within 50 km of downtown:

| species | VASCAN | observations | herbarium collections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bur Oak | **introduced** in AB | 119 | **3** (1935–1952) |
| Manitoba Maple | native in AB | 546 | 14 |
| American Elm | not in AB | 13 | 1 |
| Balsam Fir | native in AB | 7 | 0 |

**Even herbarium specimens include planted trees**: bur oak has three. So
observations are context only, and VASCAN's Alberta list is a gate in front of
everything.

## The rule (F220)

A species is **native around Edmonton** when:

1. VASCAN records it native in Alberta, **and**
2. at least **three** distinct herbarium collections were made within **50 km of
   downtown Edmonton**, or the owner confirmed it on review.

Each part has a reason:

- **50 km from (53.5461, −113.4938)**, the point the basemap's Edmonton dot and
  the worked example already use. The owner chose the radius (the alternatives
  were 30–40 km and 80 km). It covers the metro region: St. Albert, Sherwood Park,
  Spruce Grove, Stony Plain, Leduc, Devon, Fort Saskatchewan, Beaumont and the
  west edge of Elk Island. The ground is mostly Central Parkland, with the Beaver
  Hills' Dry Mixedwood.
- **A record counts only if its whole uncertainty circle lies inside the 50 km.**
  A 10 km cut on uncertainty (the range map's rule) threw out pin cherry's only
  Edmonton specimen, a sheet georeferenced to the city with a coarse radius. For
  a 50 km question, "somewhere in Edmonton" is a perfectly good answer.
- **Specimens only.** A pressed sheet can be re-examined. A photograph's "wild"
  is a checkbox, and bur oak shows what that checkbox is worth in a city.
- **Three**, reusing `ecoregion_ranges.MIN_RECORDS` rather than inventing a
  number. Its comment is the argument: *"Two is a coincidence — a misidentified
  herbarium sheet plus a garden escape will do it."*
- **Distinct collections, not sheets.** One gathering is often split between
  herbaria (ALTA and CAN both holding it). Sheets with the same 0.01° spot and
  year count once.

Below the floor, a species is **not settled**, not "not native" (P9). The tiers
and what each claims:

| tier | count at V3.12 | the claim |
|---|---|---|
| documented | 193 | 3+ collections within 50 km |
| thin | 73 | 1–2 collections; not enough to say either way |
| observed | 30 | photographed within 50 km, never collected there; may be planted |
| unrecorded | 116 | nothing within 50 km, though recorded elsewhere |
| no data | 3 | nothing anywhere in the cache (Canada Waterweed, Butte Primrose, Lyall's Penstemon) |
| not in Alberta | 9 | VASCAN does not record it native in Alberta |

**The unrecorded list is the reason this works.** It is the mountains and the
dry south: Glacier Lily, Moss Campion, Yellow and Red Columbine, Thimbleberry,
Ball Cactus, the sagebrushes, Yucca, Winterfat.

### Your review is part of the rule

The owner chose this over shipping the computed list alone or waiting for a
published list. **`data/local_flora_rulings.json`** holds the owner's rulings, one
per species, each a yes or a no (see *The review page* for why there are no reasons). A yes overrides a thin or observed tier,
and "not native here" overrides documented. The derivation merges them, so a
re-run keeps them.

Rulings come from a review page published with this increment (see *The review
page*). 108 species are on it: the 73 thin and 30 observed, the 3 with no data,
and 2 documented species the evidence may be wrong about. Until the owner rules,
a borderline species is **out** of the Edmonton list and its page says why.

What the review has to settle, by kind:

- **Real natives with thin specimen records.** Pin cherry (1 collection, 189
  observations), highbush cranberry (2 / 436), wild bergamot, three-flowered avens,
  wild licorice, golden bean.
- **Naming, not botany.** 32 collections of "Canada Goldenrod" (*Solidago
  canadensis*) were made within 50 km, and VASCAN says *S. canadensis* is not in
  Alberta: the older determinations are of what VASCAN now calls *S. lepida*,
  which has 2. Yarrow likewise: 9 collections under *Achillea millefolium*,
  which the catalogue carries as *A. borealis* (1). Renaming catalogue rows is
  the author's open decision (`data_quality.KNOWN_NOMENCLATURE`) and is not made
  here.
- **Isolated records.** These look like plantings: Creeping Oregon Grape (one
  specimen 4 km from downtown, the next 299 km away), Western Mountain Ash,
  Alpine Bluegrass, Foothills Rough Fescue.
- **Documented, but probably not native here.** Manitoba Maple (14 collections;
  its Alberta range is the southeast, as I understand it, and Edmonton's are
  planted and escaped). Sandberg Bluegrass (8 collections, all 1991–2012, no
  observations, the nearest record outside the circle 119 km away; reads like
  research plots).

## Where it reaches

One Qt-free read side (`src/native_here.py`) answers for every surface, so the
surfaces cannot disagree. That disagreement has cost this project four times
(V2.38 Saskatoon, V2.80's `species_entry`, V2.85 the generator's flag, V3.00 the
picker's flag).

- **App, plant picker**: an *Edmonton native* quality beside *Native*
  (`search_plants(native_near="edmonton")`).
- **App, species page**: a *Native to* line (VASCAN, the gap above) and an
  *Around Edmonton* line with its evidence.
- **App, communities**: an *Edmonton natives only* switch in the list. A community
  passes when every member passes. The community's page names the members that
  do not.
- **App, Generate Design**: an *Edmonton natives only* goal, honoured by the plant
  pool, the community pick and the critic's repairs, the same three places V2.85
  had to fix for the province.
- **Website**: a *Native area* facet with one value, *Edmonton region*, in the
  sidebar's Site group, and its page `/plants/native-area/edmonton/` ("Native
  plants of the Edmonton region"). That page also counts what waits for review
  and lists it in a fold. Each species page gets an *Around Edmonton* row, and
  `/method/#around` states the rule with every number computed at build time.
  The first draft put it under *Native to* as a third province. A
  50 km circle is not a province, and the breadcrumb read "Plants by native
  around".

What had to move to make room: `plant_filters.py` and `site_facets.py` were at
their line ceilings, so the province helpers went to `native_here.py` (re-exported)
and the facet derivations to `src/site_facet_values.py`. `static_site_method.py`
sits at 260 of 260 with a shortened docstring.

## Communities of the Edmonton region (F221)

Only **one** of the 71 seeded communities survives the Edmonton filter on the
documented tier: Parkland Goldenrod & Aster Bank. The blockers are mostly
species the review is likely to confirm (wild bergamot in 10 communities, boreal
yarrow in 9, highbush cranberry in 6) and some that will not survive it (silvery
lupine, dotted blazingstar, Maximilian sunflower, showy milkweed). New
communities are built only from **documented** species, so they hold whatever
the review decides.

Seven were added (`EXAMPLE_POLYCULTURES`, eight members each, every one
documented, with the problem, context, forces and solution every seeded
community carries):

| community | the ground it is | leads with |
|---|---|---|
| Edmonton Aspen Grove | the aspen woods of the uplands | Trembling Aspen |
| Edmonton River Valley Spruce | the cool north-facing woods of the North Saskatchewan valley | White Spruce |
| Edmonton Fescue Slope | rough fescue grassland on dry south-facing slopes | Rough Fescue |
| Edmonton Slough Margin | the wet meadow around a prairie slough | Basket Willow |
| Edmonton Berry Thicket | the shrub thickets of the parkland's edges and ravines | Saskatoon Berry |
| Edmonton Pollinator Meadow | a lawn-conversion meadow, prairie crocus to smooth aster | Meadow Blazingstar |

The goal *Edmonton natives only* names them as its community hint, so the

the moment to swap it.

## The review page

**<https://claude.ai/artifact/VPeWXtdA1FFRD1n67MyNSg>**, private to the owner,
built by `tools/local_flora_review/` (its README says how to rebuild it and how a
session brings the rulings back).

One card per species, 108 of them. Each card shows:

- the evidence: every collection's year and distance from downtown, the
  observations, the nearest record outside the circle, and the counts for the two
  ecoregions the circle overlaps;
- a read from `tools/local_flora_review/reads.py`, labelled **opinion, from
  memory**: 32 likely native, 49 unsure, 27 likely not;
- links to iNaturalist (within 50 km) and GBIF;
- one question, *Native around Edmonton?*, with a **Yes** and a **No**.

Rulings are kept in the page's own database (`rulings`, one document per
species), not in the page's HTML. The owner can then rule from a phone in the
field, and a session can read the rulings without the owner copying anything.

**Corrected in place, on the owner's word, after the first publish.** The first
version asked for one of three rulings plus a reason, required the reason, and
printed it on the app's and the website's species pages ("Native, on review:
<reason>"). The owner: *"I don't want my reasons to be required ... and I
certainly don't want them listed in the app. Simply let me answer 'yes' to the
ones I am confident about and 'no' to the ones I am confident about and leave
out the rest."* So now:

- a ruling is a yes or a no;
- `parse_rulings` drops a reason rather than requiring one;
- a page prints "Native, confirmed on review." or "Not native here, on review."
  and then the evidence;
- the review page has no text box and no third button.

An unanswered species is left as the records have it. A tap saves, and a tap on
the same answer takes it back. A taken-back answer is stored as `unsettled`, so
the next fold-in removes what it replaced. Tests guard both halves: no reason
survives parsing, merging or the words, even one written into a file by hand.
The database was empty when the page changed shape, so no answer was lost.

This went on V3.12 rather than a new branch. No V3.12 build could have shown a
reason, because the rulings file shipped empty. The first fold-in of answers will
be a release of its own and carries this code with it.

Coming back: `ArtifactData list` with `out_dir`, then
`python scripts/derive_local_flora.py --merge <dir>`. That checks every ruling
through `parse_rulings` before writing anything, so one bad row writes nothing.
It then re-derives `data/local_flora.json` in the same run, because the data gate
fails on a rulings file that is newer than the list. The page writes the format
`--merge` reads: one row was written to the real database, read back with
`out_dir` (plain fields, no wrapper) and deleted.

Three things the reads turned up that the tiers alone hide:

- **Sticky Goldenrod** has no collection under its own name, but 12 within 50 km,
  1917-1944, under *Solidago simplex*, which V2.80 retired as introduced. Nothing
  since. Worth seeing the sheets before ruling either way.
- **Canada Waterweed** has no records in the cache at all. That is a gap in the
  harvest, not in the plant.
- **Creeping Oregon Grape**, **Western Mountain Ash**, **Alpine Bluegrass** and
  **Foothills Rough Fescue** each have one or two recent sheets near the city and
  nothing nearby. That is the signature of a planting. The rough fescue sheet may
  be plains rough fescue (*Festuca hallii*), which is documented here.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **Other places.** Calgary, Saskatoon and Regina would each need their own
  review. The data model is keyed by place so another can be added, but none
  is.
- **Members of the 13 communities.** Swapping Canada Goldenrod out of Aspen
  Parkland Edge is a naming decision (above), and the generator already keeps
  them off an Alberta pin.
- **The legacy region tags** (`zone_prairies` on 296 species, Yellow Columbine
  included). They are read only for the 8 species with no derived rows.
- **`native_region`**, a free-text field nothing displays. "Western Canada" is
  wrong for Canada Goldenrod, Switchgrass and Jerusalem Artichoke and is
  harmless.

## Could not be verified here

- **Anything on the network.** GBIF, VASCAN, iNaturalist and the City of
  Edmonton all answer 403 at this session's proxy. Published Edmonton lists
  (Edmonton Native Plant Society, the City) could not be read to cross-check.
- **Whether a specimen near Edmonton was wild.** The cache keeps coordinates,
  year, basis and dataset, but not the label's locality or habitat text, which
  is where "cultivated" or "Devonian Botanic Garden" would show. A fetch of that
  text for the ~2,000 specimens inside the circle would settle most of the
  review by data. It needs a machine with open egress.
- **The desktop on a real screen.** The species page, the community list's
  switch and the community page were rendered offscreen and read back. The
  window was not used by hand.
- **The review page inside claude.ai.** Its saving, clearing, filtering,
  read-only and offline states were driven in headless Chromium against a
  stand-in database. Against the real one, a session wrote, listed and deleted
  one row. The page's own calls in the real viewer have not run until the owner
  opens it.
- **`tests.test_undo_redo`**, as every container run: excluded from the full
  run for the WebEngine teardown abort CLAUDE.md describes.

## Tests

`tests/test_local_flora.py` (36): the rule on synthetic caches, the VASCAN gate,
rulings and their checks, a rename carrying a ruling, `--merge` (land, replace,
remove, one bad row writes nothing, the page's rows read back, the shipped files
untouched), the review page's builder, the shipped list against a fresh
derivation, known answers, and the words. `tests/test_edmonton_option.py` (14):
every door a plant comes through, in the app and on the website.
`tests/test_edmonton_communities.py` (8): the seven communities and the notes.

The full run (4,655 tests, `test_undo_redo` excluded) found one failure. Two
skill docs quoted schema 92, and both are fixed. `test_filter_widgets.
test_down_and_space_open_the_list` fails in some module orders on an untouched
V3.11 checkout too. It did not fail in this run, and it is not this increment's.

## Handing this over

- **The owner's rulings are the open item.** Until they come back, the list is
  the 193 documented species, and the 108 on the page are out of it, each saying
  why. Folding them in is the README's three steps. It needs no schema bump: the
  list is read from file, not seeded.
- **Answers carry no reasons, and no page prints one.** The app and the website say
  "Native, confirmed on review." or "Not native here, on review.", then the evidence.
- **Another place** is a `PLACES` entry, its own review and its own reads. The
  derivation, the rulings and the read side are keyed by place. The website facet,
  the app's quality and the page builder name Edmonton, and each would take a
  second value without a new mechanism.
