# V2.74 — a plant that was never from here

Remove *Rudbeckia hirta* (Black-eyed Susan) from the catalogue, and therefore
from grownativeplants.ca, because it is introduced in Alberta and the app has
been calling it native since the first seed file.

---

## The ask

> "I need to remove Rudbeckia hirta from the app but particularly the website
> as it does not appear to be native to AB"

Correct, and the "particularly the website" is the right emphasis. Inside the
app a wrong row is one entry in a browser among 432. On the public site it is
a **species page at a stable URL** on a reference work called
GrowNativePlants.ca, reached from every hub its facets put it in — running
`site_facets.index_row` over the record it shipped as gives `colour: yellow`,
`bloom: 7, 8, 9`, `role: bird_food, pollinator, wildlife_habitat` and `type:
wildflower`, so at least eight landing pages listed it. The claim those pages
make is not "here is a plant"; it is **"this plant is native here"**, and that
claim was false on all of them.

## The evidence

**VASCAN** (Database of Vascular Plants of Canada) records *Rudbeckia hirta*
as **introduced** in AB, SK, MB and BC, and native from Ontario eastward.
**Moss, *Flora of Alberta*, agrees**: introduced. It is a garden and
wildflower-seed-mix escape on the prairies, not a member of any Alberta plant
community.

**This session could not machine-verify that.** The container's egress policy
403s `data.canadensys.net`, so the VASCAN API was not queried here and the
above is cited, not fetched. If a later session wants the primary record,
`https://data.canadensys.net/vascan/api/0.1/search.json?q=Rudbeckia+hirta`
returns it in one request from a machine with open egress.

**Where the app's own data disagreed, and why that is not a defence.** The
`plant_ecoregions` entry removed with the row carried **215 georeferenced GBIF
records across eight prairie ecoregions**, 102 of them in Aspen Parkland at
`confidence: high`. That number is exactly what a widely-planted, freely
self-seeding ornamental looks like, and reading it as a range is the mistake
`src/establishment.py` was written to prevent: **occurrence is not nativity**.
V2.61 made the same distinction for the fauna gate — "a starling has tens of
thousands of Alberta records" — and then never applied it to the flora, because
measuring first had found *"the flora was already AB/SK"*.

**How it got in, and why nothing caught it.** The seed row asserted
`native_to_alberta: 1` and `native_region: "Western Canada"`, and its note read
*"Classic prairie wildflower."* Every downstream gate reads that flag rather
than an authority: `tag_prairie_provenance.py` derived `native_provinces:
"AB,SK"` from it plus the ecoregion tags, and `static_site.is_publishable`
publishes anything carrying either field. So the original editorial claim
propagated into three more fields that each looked like independent
corroboration. There was no step anywhere that could have disagreed.

## What came out

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `data/plants_master.json` | 432 | **431** |
| `data/plant_fauna_master.json` documented edges | 7,740 | **7,590** |
| `data/plant_ecoregions.json` species | 423 | **422** |
| `data/fauna_master.json` | 1,156 | **1,150** |
| `data/lepidoptera_attributes_master.json` | 347 | **345** |
| published species pages | 432 | **431** |
| published wildlife pages | 1,138 | **1,132** |
| published edges | 7,574 | **7,424** |

Every count above after the first three is *printed by the build*, not written
down — `build-site` and `build_model().stats` were run and read.

**The 150 edges were all real, and that is the interesting part.** Every one is
GloBI-sourced — 124 from iNaturalist, the rest from Ecdysis, Bumble Bee Watch, a
USGS study and *Robertson 1929, Flowers and insects*, which is an **Illinois**
survey, i.e. the plant's actual native range. 147 nectar, 2 pollen, 1 seed.
Nothing here is fabricated: a real *Bombus ternarius* really did visit a real
black-eyed Susan. But an aggregated flower-visitation record says a bee met a
plant somewhere, not that the plant belongs where the bee lives, and a
catalogue of *what to plant in Alberta* is answering the second question. The
edge is true and the catalogue entry is still wrong; those are separate
statements.

### Six animals came out too, and that was not the first plan

**Six animals held exactly one documented edge, and it was to this plant** —
*Pseudopanurgus albitarsis*, *Neoneides muticus*, *Herpetogramma pertextalis*,
*Nephrotoma ferruginea*, *Chlorochlamys chloroleucaria*, *Agalliopsis
ancistra*. The plan here said they would simply stop getting wildlife pages,
which is true (`_wildlife_pages`: *"An animal with no edges gets no page: '0
plants support this species' would be published as a fact about the animal when
it is a fact about our coverage"*) and was **not enough**.
`test_derived_edges.test_cuckoo_bees_are_no_longer_orphans` failed at **8
orphans against a ceiling of 5**: they were left in `fauna` connected to
nothing at all — no documented edge, no derived edge, no `fauna_fauna` link.

Raising the ceiling would have been the wrong fix, and V2.64 says so in its own
words: its first tail apply *"wrote 20 animals connected to nothing"* and the
answer was to stop writing them, not to widen the guard. So the six came out of
`fauna_master.json` (1,156 → **1,150**) and two of them out of
`lepidoptera_attributes_master.json` (347 → **345**). Orphans are back to the
pre-existing **2** — a kestrel and a bat, whose trophic level the model
genuinely cannot express.

The confirmation that this is the right call is that the project's own gate
already agreed: `curate_new_fauna.writable()`, which asks the real ingest gates
rather than guessing, now names all six in its orphan list beside nine others
that were **never written for exactly this reason**. They were in the registry
because of an edge to a plant that should not have been in the catalogue.

## What replaced it, and where

The plant was not only a row; four places placed it as a *design element*, and
each needed a substitute rather than a hole. **Prairie Coneflower** (*Ratibida
columnifera*) is the answer in three of them — the native yellow-rayed,
dark-coned composite that black-eyed Susan was standing in for, at 0.5 m
against 0.7 m and blooming June–August against July–September.

| Where | Was | Now | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| `onboarding.py` worked example | fallback after Blanketflower | Prairie Coneflower | Same slot, same reason: a yellow composite behind the milkweed |
| `polycultures.py` "Boulevard Pollinator Strip" | member at (−1.0, 0.6) | Prairie Coneflower | The strip is explicitly *"kept short for sightlines"* — 0.5 m is a better fit than the 0.7 m it replaces |
| `polycultures.py` "Tall Prairie Meadow" | member at (−0.7, 0.6) | **Meadow Blazingstar** (*Liatris ligulistylis*) | Prairie Coneflower is wrong here: the variation's whole argument is height. Blazingstar is 1.2 m and August–September, which is what *"a dramatic mid-to-late summer display"* was promising |
| `sprite_gallery` `combo_clump_rays` | exemplar | Common Sunflower | Regenerated, not chosen — `_combo_specs` picks the exemplar itself |

Both polyculture **descriptions** were edited too, not just their member lists.
A community whose prose names a plant its member list no longer contains is the
kind of drift this repo keeps finding, and it would have been invisible.

## The guard, and why this one earns its place

`data/excluded_taxa.json` + `data_quality.validate_excluded_taxa`, wired into
`validate_all` so CI, `check_plant_data.py` and `cli.py` all run it.

A deletion is a one-time edit; a catalogue is edited for years. The re-entry
route here is not hypothetical: **`data/fetched/fauna_edges_candidates.json`
still holds 389 GloBI records naming *Rudbeckia hirta***, and re-adding the
plant row is the only thing standing between them and 150 restored edges,
because `ingest_fauna_edges.py`'s only defence is `drop("plant is not in this
catalogue")`. Somebody re-adding a familiar prairie-looking wildflower in two
years would trip nothing and would have no way to learn that the question had
already been asked and answered.

Three decisions inside it:

**It matches on both names.** The plant files key on `scientific_name`; every
edge in `plant_fauna_master.json` names its plant by `common_name`. A guard that
knew only the binomial would have let all 150 edges back with nothing said —
which is precisely the shape of the bug it exists to prevent.

**It is not a nativity rule.** It is a list of specific calls with specific
evidence. A species absent from the catalogue *and* absent from the list has
simply never been considered, and the file says so. Writing a general
"is it native" gate would mean encoding an authority the repo does not ship.

**An entry with no `authority` is an error.** This file removes species from a
public reference work. The failing message quotes the authority and the release,
so the next person meets the reasoning rather than a prohibition — a bare "not
allowed" sends them to re-derive the call from scratch, which is how the row got
in the first time.

## Left alone, deliberately

- **`floral_host_genera: "…,Rudbeckia,…"` on three bees** (*Bombus
  griseocollis*, *B. huntii*, *Melissodes* spp.). These are **literature records
  about the bee**, not catalogue entries, and each carries a citation. Editing a
  genus out of a published host list to tidy a grep would falsify the source.
  `derived_edges.py` expands them against the catalogue, so with no *Rudbeckia*
  in it the genus now matches nothing and contributes nothing. Harmless, and
  honest.
- **`data/fetched/*.json`.** These are raw fetch output — what the GloBI API
  returned on the day it was asked. Editing them would make a cache disagree
  with its source. The guard above covers what they could otherwise do.
- **The v67 note in `plants.py` and `ingest_fauna_edges.refit`'s docstring**,
  which both cite *"a goldfinch on Black-eyed Susan"* as the V2.60 `fruit_food`
  bug. That is an accurate account of something that happened. This repo
  corrects its changelog in place (see v69→v70) rather than rewriting it, and
  a historical note describing a past release is not a stale reference.
- **`tests/test_flora_read.py`'s FNA fixture**, which parses an invented
  description headed *Rudbeckia hirta*. `flora_read` is the bench's lookup tool
  and is not catalogue-scoped — it can be pointed at any species — so a fixture
  naming a non-catalogue plant is representative rather than stale. Renaming it
  would have meant attaching invented measurements to a *different* real
  species, which is worse.

## Known, not fixed here

**A saved `.perma.geojson` that placed a black-eyed Susan now points at a
different plant.** Plant features store `plant_id`, ids shift on every reseed,
and `persistence.py` resolves through `m._plant_info(p["plant_id"])` by id. This
hazard predates the change and fires on **every** schema bump; removing a row
merely makes it visible. Not fixed here because the fix is a project-load
re-resolution by name and that is its own increment, not a rider on a data
removal. Called out in the hand-back so it can be checked by eye.

The reseed's own machinery does cover the case it owns:
`_remap_user_polyculture_plants` drops user community members whose plant no
longer ships, with a log line rather than silently. That branch has existed
since schema v46 and **this is the first release to exercise it** — no species
had ever been removed before.

## What was actually run

Not "the tests pass" — what was executed, and what it showed.

- **`python -m unittest discover -s tests -t .`** (minus `test_undo_redo`, which
  segfaults in this container per CLAUDE.md), with **PyQt6, PyQt6-WebEngine,
  shapely and libEGL installed first** so the ~200 widget tests actually ran
  instead of skipping. That is how the orphan failure was found; a bare
  container would have skipped past it.
- **`python -m src.cli build-site --no-photos`** twice — before and after the
  fauna removal. `grep -ril "rudbeckia\|black-eyed"` over the **1,679 published
  files** returns nothing.
- **`python scripts/make_gallery_scene.py`** — the sprite gallery is generated,
  so it was regenerated rather than hand-edited, and `species_rudbeckia_hirta`
  went with it.
- **A forced reseed** (`_set_schema_version(conn, 1)` then `init_db()`), then
  queried directly: 0 Rudbeckia rows, fauna 1,150, orphans back to 2, and both
  edited communities carrying their substitutes with full member lists.
- **`api.search_plants(query='Black-eyed')` and `query='Rudbeckia'`** both
  return `[]`; `'Prairie Coneflower'` returns the substitute.
- **The guard was proved to fire**, not merely to pass: re-adding the row to a
  copied `plants_master.json` and an edge to a copied `plant_fauna_master.json`
  produces two errors naming VASCAN and V2.74. That is a test, not a one-off.

**Not verified here:** VASCAN itself (egress 403), and anything needing a real
desktop — the six removed animals had no wildlife page to look at either way,
and the two polyculture edits were checked in the database rather than by
opening the community builder.
