# V2.77 — the cap that hid the herbaria

Draw the layer the printed floras draw, and say what preparing it turned up
about the harvest behind every published range.

---

## Context

V2.75 came out of an outside botanical review of grownativeplants.ca. One of its
questions was the one the site could not answer at all: *listing the number of
observations in an ecoregion doesn't explain where they are in the ecoregion.*
The answer is a dot map, and V2.76 assembled the parts for one — the raw points
cached, the derivation corrected to containment, and
`scripts/fetch_dataset_licences.py` asking GBIF per dataset what may be redrawn.

That last one came back better than expected: **224 datasets, 90.1% of specimen
records CC0 or CC-BY**. So the question became which layer to draw, and the
author's call was specimens first, look at it, then decide about the CC-BY-NC
observations.

Specimens are the right first layer for two reasons that are not about
convenience. iNaturalist obscures coordinates for rare, threatened and
collectible taxa; a pressed sheet in a cabinet with a locality on its label does
not carry that problem, which is precisely why the regional floras plot
specimens. And for a herbarium the *dataset* licence is the record's licence,
because a collection is published under one — whereas iNaturalist's is the
publisher's default and each observer chooses their own.

Then preparing the rendering turned up why it would have been a bad map.

## The cap is not neutral, and nothing could see it

`MAX_RECORDS_PER_SPECIES = 6000` bounds the harvest per species. It was written
as politeness after the first run earned an HTTP 429, and as politeness it
works. What nobody checked is **what falls outside the window**, because GBIF's
default result order is newest-first.

| | records | specimens | year range |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 16 species at the cap | 89,964 | **31** | **2021–2026** |
| the other 418 | 401,877 | 54,238 | full historical record |

*Amelanchier alnifolia* holds 6,000 records, **two** of them specimens, none
dated before 2024. Saskatoon Berry has been collected in Alberta for a century.
The harvest never reached any of it, and a specimens-only contact sheet would
have opened on sixteen blank maps of the catalogue's best-known plants —
blank for our reason rather than the herbaria's. That is the exact failure
`plot_occurrences.py`'s own docstring names: *an empty map of a species with 400
records is the failure that looks most like a finding.*

**The deeper fault is that a truncated harvest and a complete one looked
identical once cached.** This data has now been the subject of two increments of
close work and the cap went unremarked in both, because nothing recorded the
difference between *GBIF ran out* and *we stopped asking*.

## What shipped

**`--specimen-pass` (`scripts/seed_ecoregion_ranges.py`).** Asks GBIF for
`PRESERVED_SPECIMEN` records as their own query and merges them into the point
cache. Filtering the general harvest cannot reach what the cap cut; only a
separate query can. It runs over the whole catalogue rather than over a guessed
list of truncated species, because GBIF returns the whole first page when there
are fewer than `PAGE_SIZE` of them — most species cost one request, so there is
no threshold to get wrong.

The merge de-duplicates on `(lat, lng, year, dataset_key)`. Not the whole
tuple: `uncertainty_m` gets re-stated between exports for what is one sheet in
one cabinet, and counting it twice inflates the number a confidence band is
computed from. Not `(lat, lng)` alone either: two collectors at the same
trailhead in different decades are two records, and silently collapsing them
would erase the very thing a dot map is drawn to show.

**`fetch_occurrences(truncated=...)`.** A list the fetcher appends to when the
loop hit its bound instead of `endOfRecords`. One `while/else`, and the
distinction that was invisible is now recorded.

**`--from-cache`.** Re-derives with no network at all. The cache was built in
V2.75 so that no later question about this derivation would need egress, and
until now nothing read it that way — a changed threshold or a corrected polygon
still meant a fresh harvest on the author's laptop. It is also what let the
specimen pass's effect be verified in a cloud session.

**`--specimens` and `--publishable` (`scripts/plot_occurrences.py`).** The two
filters, composable, reusing `fetch_dataset_licences.PUBLISHABLE` rather than
restating it. A `dataset_key` absent from the licence table is **dropped, not
defaulted** — absent is not permissive, the same rule the photo pipeline runs
on. A missing licence table refuses loudly and names the command that writes
it, rather than drawing an empty map.

**The shading and the dots are two statements.** `species_svg(..., dots=)`
draws a subset while the shading stays derived from *every* point, because the
published range is a claim about all the evidence and filtering the picture
must not quietly filter the claim. That is only honest with a sentence beside
it, so `caption()` prints one on every figure: *"721 of 1,251 records drawn;
492 not a specimen; 38 licence does not permit redrawing. The shading below is
derived from all 1,251, not from the dots."*

The contact sheet ranks by **drawable** records rather than by records, which
is the other half of not opening on sixteen blank maps.

## What the layer actually looks like

Across the cache, **52,924 of 555,477 records (9.5%) are drawable** — 496,732
dropped as not specimens, 5,821 for licence. The 9.5% is the wrong number to
lead with: what matters is per species, and the drawn maps carry **300 to 700
dots each**. Ten of 434 species have nothing drawable, and four of those ten are
capped species the specimen pass is expected to rescue.

The 52,924 matches `fetch_dataset_licences.py`'s independently computed figure
exactly, which is worth stating because the two arrive by different routes.

## The claim that had gone false

`docs/DATA_SOURCES.md` rested the GBIF position on storing *"only the derived
counts, never GBIF's records themselves … not a dataset we redistribute"*.
V2.76 committed 555,477 GBIF records to a public repository, which falsified
that sentence in the same increment that wrote a page about honesty.

The licence lookup shows the position is defensible rather than merely
survivable: 61 datasets CC0, 82 CC-BY, 81 CC-BY-NC. All three permit
redistribution; two require attribution, which the cache carries per record as
`dataset_key`; one restricts commercial use, which this repository's PolyForm
Noncommercial licence already does. The row now says that, including the
increment it was wrong for.

## Deliberately not here

**Publishing the dots.** Nothing this draws reaches the website. That is a
decision to make from the pictures, which now exist.

**The CC-BY-NC observation layer.** Favoured by the author and still one line in
`PUBLISHABLE` plus a `/method/` disclosure and a `DATA_SOURCES.md` row saying
why the occurrence rule differs from the photograph rule. Held so it is decided
beside the specimen map rather than instead of it.

**A schema bump.** `--from-cache` was run over the whole catalogue and
reproduces the shipped derivation **exactly** — 420 species with rows on both
sides, none present in one and not the other, and **zero species with a
differing row**. So nothing in the catalogue moved here, and the committed
points and the committed ranges are now known to be consistent rather than
assumed to be. The bump belongs to the increment that runs the specimen pass
and re-derives, if the numbers move then.

That check took about forty minutes of CPU, which is worth stating: `--from-cache`
is free of the network, not free.
