# V2.46 — Life size, and a net in his hand

## Context

V2.45 gave the animals real wingbeats. Watching them fly produced five reports,
all of them specific, and one of them a scale error large enough that it had
been distorting everything else:

1. **The butterfly beat is *"too fast and unnatural. A butterfly will
   flap-flap-glide"*** — and *"as it is the flapping is visually distracting.
   It is supposed to be visually appealing and improve the experience which it
   does not."*
2. **The creatures are the wrong size.** *"The relative size of the bees,
   butterflies, birds to the person that walks the scene is all wrong. The
   calligrapher bee seems to be the only one rendered smaller/more correctly."*
3. **The two 3D windows still do different things.** *"I want the 3D view and
   the tour a reference ecosystem to have the same functionality. I want to be
   able to plant a plant or remove one, to catch a bug in a net the guy holds,
   to make it more like a game."*
4. **Creatures fly through solids.** *"The guy doesn't go through trees or
   buildings now but the creatures still do."*
5. **The sprites are generic.** *"A generic butterfly does nothing… I am
   willing to enter this myself if you create a similar feature that we did
   with the plant morphology sprites… I remember something about bees and the
   7 bands of colour."*

Stated priorities, verbatim: **education, excitement, realism.**

Item 5 is answered in this document rather than in code — the tool the author
is describing already exists (see *"The bench you already have"* below), and
the part of it that does not exist is a drawing pipeline that deserves its own
increment rather than being squeezed into this one.

## What I found

### The creatures were between six and thirty-nine times too big

Not "a bit off". Measured against the shipped morphology:

| | drawn | real | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| leafcutter bee | 0.54 m | 0.011 m | **27×** |
| butterfly | 0.83 m | 0.079 m | **8×** |
| songbird | 0.74 m | 0.225 m | **6×** |
| hoverfly | 0.47 m | 0.015 m | **39×** |

A bee the size of a cat, standing beside a 1.75 m walker. Every builder in
`07-wildlife.js` ends with a hand-chosen constant — `g.scale.setScalar(0.9 *
(app.size || 1))` — and every one of them was picked by eye in V2.12, *before
there was any morphology in the database to check them against*.

There is morphology now: bee `body_length_mm` (69/69), lep wingspan (31/31),
and since V2.45's schema v63, bird `wingspan_mm` (24/24). So the size can come
from the animal.

The author's aside is the tell that this reading is right: the **calligrapher
fly** was singled out as *"the only one rendered smaller/more correctly"* — and
it is 15 mm, one of the smallest things in the scene, so it was the one whose
wrongness happened to land closest to plausible.

### A "gliding" butterfly was trembling, not gliding

Two separate causes, and the second is the one nobody would guess:

**The bout was a crow's.** `_LEP_STYLE` mapped every non-hovering butterfly
onto `FLAP_GLIDE`, which is shared with corvids and larger birds: 3–8 beats and
a 0.25–1.10 s pause. At a cabbage white's 10 Hz that is a near-continuous
flutter with a token gap.

**And `beatGain` never actually stopped the wings.** It bottomed out at `0.22`
for gliding styles — meaning the wings kept beating at 22% of full sweep **at
full rate**. The comment said *"gliders hold a V"*, and holding a V is exactly
what it was not doing: it was scaling the amplitude of a continuing
oscillation.

Run the arithmetic and the size of it is clear: **what V2.45 called a glide was
a 27.7° sweep, at the full wingbeat rate.** That is the *"visually
distracting"* in the report, and no amount of bout tuning would have fixed it,
because the pause was already there and was full of tremble.

### The obstacle list existed and nothing consulted it

`walkObstacles` has been in `08-modes.js` since V2.12. `animateWildlife` never
looked at it. It is also built **only while walking**, which is wrong for the
animals twice over — they fly in the orbit view too, and that is where you
watch them.

Two things a flying animal needs that a walker does not:

- **A height**, or a bird legitimately over the roof bounces off it.
- **An exemption for its own destination.** A chickadee's entire route is
  *perch in that tree*. Blanket collision would push it out of every tree it
  was sent to and leave it circling the trunk forever — a worse bug than the
  one being fixed.

### The 3D preview was read-only for no reason left

`html/scene3d/16-editing.js` reports a point in scene metres and **does not
know which window it is in**. The reference sandbox got a trowel in V2.43 and
the design preview did not — but nothing in the viewer was ever the obstacle.
What was missing was a Python side, and the difference from the sandbox is
real enough to justify a second module rather than a second caller:

| | reference sandbox | the design |
|---|---|---|
| state | a throwaway sandbox file | the user's real project |
| undo | none | MainWindow's undo stack |
| other views | just the 3D window | map, plant panel, planning panel, score |
| saving | `save_sandbox` per edit | the project's own dirty flag |

### The net was a cursor effect

`animateCatch` flew a hoop in from the camera. Nobody was holding anything.

## What ships

### 1. Life size, measured rather than tabulated

`src/scene_wildlife.py` sends `size: {m, axis}` per creature — the real
measurement in metres, and **which axis of the model carries it**, because the
models are not built alike (a bee's length runs down Z, a butterfly's wingspan
across X). Fallbacks per kind for a species with no morphology, deliberately
small and honest: a hoverfly *is* 12 mm.

The viewer then **measures the object it just built** —
`Box3.setFromObject` along the named axis — and scales so that extent equals
the wanted metres. That single path is correct for the baked GLBs *and* the
procedural fallbacks, needs no table of per-kind fudge factors, and stays
correct if a model is ever re-exported at a different size.

The contact shadow and the name-label offset follow the same number. A
half-metre shadow smudge under an 11 mm bee was doing much of the work of
making the insects look enormous.

**The honest consequence, named:** a life-size bee is a few pixels across from
three metres, which is exactly what a bee is. That is the realism the report
asked for, and at any distance it is at odds with "excitement" and "education",
which were named in the same breath.

So there is a **Creatures:** control — *Life size · ×3 · ×6 · ×12* — in both
windows, driving `window.permaSetCreatureScale`. Three things about it are
deliberate: **life size is the default**, the exaggeration **has a number on
it** rather than being a vague "bigger", and it is **floored at 1×** so it can
never reproduce the bug it replaces. A magnification is a stated
exaggeration; a constant chosen by eye is a lie the app tells without saying so.

(This started the increment as an unwired hook, and `tests/test_bridge_contract`
was right to reject it: a `permaSet*` the app never drives is dead code. Wiring
it to a real control was the correct fix, not an exemption.)

### 2. Flap-flap-glide, both halves

**Butterflies get their own bouts.** Two new styles in `flight_model.py`,
because a crow's bout and a butterfly's are not the same shape:

| style | beats | glide | who |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FLUTTER_GLIDE` | 2–5 | 0.35–1.10 s | whites, sulphurs, blues, fritillaries |
| `SAIL` | 2–4 | 0.90–2.60 s | monarchs, swallowtails, tortoiseshells |

`FLAP_GLIDE` stays where it was, for corvids — and for **skippers**, which
genuinely do buzz between perches; that buzziness is how you tell a skipper
from a butterfly in the field, so `darting` keeps the busiest bout on purpose.

A monarch now beats three times and then sails for 1.75 seconds. Measured out
of the model rather than asserted: 29% of the cycle flapping, 71% gliding.

**And the wings actually stop.** `flapWings` gained a `hold` pose: `gain` now
*interpolates the wing between the beat and a held angle* instead of shrinking
the beat. Leps hold a shallow V a little above horizontal (`hold: 0.18`); a
bounding bird still folds to `base`, so nothing about birds changed. `hold`
defaults to `base`, which means **at gain 1 the output is identical to before**
and a perched bird is untouched.

Checked headlessly rather than asserted, by re-running both formulas over five
seconds of clock: at gain 1 the new function deviates from the V2.45 one by
1.1e-16 (float noise); at gain 0 with a lep the angle is constant at 0.1800 rad
across every sample — genuinely still, not slowly moving; and a perched bird
still holds exactly `base`. That is the property that matters, because "the
glide is now a hold" is only true if the number stops changing.

### 3. Creatures collide with trees and buildings

A parallel `critterObstacles` list, rebuilt on **every** scene push rather than
only while walking, carrying `top` (a height) alongside `{x, z, r}`. Buildings
block their whole footprint below the roof; trees block at the **trunk**, not
the canopy — a bird lands in foliage, it does not bounce off it, which is the
same call `buildWalkObstacles` already makes.

Resolved on the animal's **path** (`c.pos`), not on its drawn position: pushing
the drawn object out every frame while the path kept walking into the wall
would be a jitter rather than a detour.

### 4. The same three verbs in the 3D preview

New `src/scene3d_edit_flow.py` owns the whole feature for the design window —
the buttons, the mode, the bridge slots and the handlers — so `Scene3DWindow`
gains one layout call and one accessor.

Every verb goes through **the path a map click takes**: undo recorded before
the write, `ProjectStore` for the write, then `render_project_to_map` +
`_mark_modified` + `_sync_planning_panel`. A plant put in from the 3D view is
undoable, appears on the map, and counts toward the Habitat Value Score,
because it is not a different kind of plant.

**A trap worth writing down**, because it looks correct and is not: undo has
*two* mechanisms. Planting pushes `{"action": "place_plant", …}`, which
`_do_undo` dispatches through a registered handler pair. **Removal has no such
handler** — it reverses through the generic before/after snapshot that the
`@undoable` decorator takes. A first cut pushed `{"action": "remove plant"}`,
which would have put an entry on the stack, lit up the Undo menu item, and then
undone *nothing*, because the dispatch table has no key by that name.
`test_a_removal_reverses_through_a_snapshot_not_a_made_up_action` reads every
action string in the module and checks it against the real handler table.

It uses **the Plants panel's existing selection** rather than carrying its own
species combo. Picking a plant in one place and planting it in another is one
decision; two pickers that can disagree is how somebody plants a species they
did not choose.

### 5. The net, held — and it has a reach

`makeWalker` now builds a real aerial net — a 38 cm hoop on a metre of handle,
with a gauze bag — and hangs it off the **right arm pivot**, so the existing
walk cycle carries it with no extra code and the catch is one more rotation on
the same joint. Shown only while the Net verb is on.

The hoop size is a number that finally means something: with creatures drawn
life size, an 8 cm swallowtail is genuinely a fifth of the hoop it goes into.

**And a held net has a reach.** 2.4 m. A creature across the yard is not caught
by clicking it — you walk over there. That is the difference between a net and
a cursor, it is the "more like a game" the report asked for, and it is P11 (the
body knows things the screen does not) in the smallest possible form. A miss
comes back on a new `bridge.out_of_reach` signal and says so, because a click
that silently does nothing reads as broken.

Outside walk mode the old flying hoop stays: in the orbit view you are a
god's-eye observer with no hands.

## The bench you already have (item 5)

> *"I am willing to enter this myself if you create a similar feature that we
> did with the plant morphology sprites where I can edit it like a character
> editor. I think we did some of this work before (I remember something about
> bees and the 7 bands of colour that identify them)."*

The memory is right and the tool exists. **`scripts/tune_fauna.py`** is the
fauna catalogue bench — the twin of `scripts/tune_morphology.py` for plants:

```bash
python scripts/tune_fauna.py        # → http://127.0.0.1:8757
```

It already carries, per species:

- **bees** — build, abdomen shape, band count *and* band colours, fuzz,
  metallic sheen, body length in mm;
- **lepidoptera** — wingspan min/max, forewing / hindwing / margin colours,
  wing shape, wing pattern, resting posture, flight style;
- a **drawn vocabulary** beside every dropdown (31 zoological terms as inline
  SVG in `html/botany/fauna.js`), because a word you cannot picture is not a
  control;
- provenance that travels with the values, and out-of-vocabulary refusal at the
  save endpoint.

It is documented in `docs/3D_SPRITES.md`. **Nothing new was built for it this
increment**, and the right next step is for the author to open it and tell me
where it falls short rather than for me to guess.

**What genuinely does not exist is the painting.** *"With lepidoptera I would
even be willing to digitally draw/paint the wings."* That is a different
feature from the bench: a per-species wing texture, an import path for it
(reusing `src/photo_import.py`'s downscale + unconditional EXIF strip), a slot
in the schema, and a material change in `html/scene3d/09-models.js` so the lep
GLB samples a texture instead of flat colours — the GLBs currently ship with
**no UVs and no textures by design**, which is a contract in `01b-surface.js`,
so this is a real change and not a small one. Scoped, not started, and it wants
its own increment and its own conversation about what a wing template looks
like.

## Verification

```bash
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen QTWEBENGINE_CHROMIUM_FLAGS=--no-sandbox \
  python -m unittest discover -s tests -t .
```

`tests/test_creature_scale.py` (26 tests) covers all five items. Three of them
are worth naming because they check the *property*, not the implementation:

- **`test_nothing_is_drawn_at_a_wildly_wrong_size`** — every creature is
  between 5 mm and 1.5 m. This is what would have caught the original bug.
- **`test_the_glide_is_longer_than_the_burst`** — "flap-flap-glide" is a claim
  about a *ratio*, so the ratio is what is asserted, for every butterfly style.
- **`test_the_travel_branches_actually_call_it`** — the collision bug was never
  a missing function; `walkObstacles` existed the whole time and nothing
  consulted it. Counting the call sites is the test that would have failed.

**What a test could not do, and says so.** `test_the_models_still_have_the_shape
_the_axis_was_chosen_from` is a *characterisation* test, deliberately.
`_SIZE_AXIS` is a judgment read off the models, and no assertion can re-derive
it from geometry — the skipper build is longer along Z than its own wingspan
and would fail a naive "the named axis is the longest" rule while still being
correct. So the test pins the measured extents instead and fails the moment the
evidence moves. A first cut of it *did* try the naive rule, and additionally
got the measurement wrong: it unioned raw accessor min/max and reported a bird
0.38 m across, because a wing's geometry is small and its span lives in the
node's translation. It would have "confirmed" the wrong axis.

### The split blanked the viewer, and the guard was pointed the wrong way

Shipped, then reported: **the 3D window came up empty** — a uniform sky-coloured
rectangle with a completely correct toolbar and a completely correct HUD
(`Year 2 · 14 plants · terrain`) sitting on top of it.

The HUD is what makes the diagnosis. It is written at the *end* of
`permaSetScene`, so the page loaded, every chunk loaded, the scene was built and
14 plants were in it. Nothing about the scene had failed. The **render loop had
stopped**, and it had stopped on frame one:

```js
// three.js WebGLAnimation
function onAnimationFrame(time, frame) {
  animationLoop(time, frame);
  context.requestAnimationFrame(onAnimationFrame);   // ← AFTER the call
}
```

The re-schedule is *after* the callback, so a throw does not skip a frame — it
ends the loop permanently. One cleared frame, then nothing, forever.

The throw was mine and it came straight out of the `19-roster.js` split.
`stepSpotlight` moved from a chunk that loads **before** `08-modes.js` to one
that loads **after** it, and the loop's call was unguarded — the two neighbours
on either side of that line, `stepThreads` and `stepTweens`, both carry a
`typeof` guard with a comment explaining this exact hazard. I read those
comments while writing the split and did not apply them to the line between
them.

**What the guard was actually checking.** `test_architecture_guard` enforces
line ceilings and told me to split; `tests/test_creature_scale` checked that the
new file's contents were right. Neither had any notion of *load order*, which is
the one property a split can break, and the viewer has no other mechanical
defence — `html/scene3d/*.js` are classic scripts sharing one global scope
precisely so order carries meaning.

Two rules now, both in `TestTheRenderLoopSurvivesItself`:

1. **The specific one.** Every function the animation loop calls must live in a
   chunk that loads before `08-modes.js`, or be reached through a `typeof`
   guard. The test parses the load order out of `scene3d.html`, finds where each
   callee is declared, and compares. Verified by reverting the fix: it names
   `stepSpotlight` and the file it moved to.
2. **The general one.** The loop body is wrapped, with `renderer.render`
   deliberately **outside** the `catch` — so a broken step function leaves a
   scene that still draws, frozen and inspectable, rather than a black window.
   `window.permaLoopFaults()` answers "is it actually running?".

The wrapper is the part worth keeping. The load-order rule prevents this
mistake; the wrapper caps the *cost* of every mistake in that loop, and the cost
here — a whole release that looked like a total failure to build a scene — was
wildly out of proportion to a missing `typeof`.

**And then the ceiling fired again**, at 625/600, because the fix and its
commentary added 65 lines to `08-modes.js`. The seam this time was chosen for
**load order as much as for size**: `20-walker.js` takes the walker's body and
his net — `makeWalker`, `makeNet`, `setWalkerNet`, `walkerCanReach`, `swingNet`
— and **nothing in it is called from the animation loop**. `walkStep` is the
obvious thing to move and is exactly the wrong thing to move, because it is a
loop callee; moving it would have re-created the bug in the same increment that
fixed it. 08 is now how walk mode *behaves*; 20 is what the walker and his kit
are *made of*.

Two tests failed on that split — `test_the_walker_carries_one` and
`test_it_has_a_reach_and_the_miss_is_reported` — and **neither had found a
defect**. Both grepped `08-modes.js` by name for a property that is about the
*viewer*, not about a file. They now read `_all_code()` across every chunk, so
the next split does not produce two more false alarms to triage.

**What I could not verify here:** every visual claim. No display, no GPU. The
scale change, the bout, the held wing pose, the net in his hand and the
collision detours all need a real desktop — the click paths are in the
hand-back. `tests/test_undo_redo.py` segfaults at teardown in this container
(pre-existing; reproduces on a stashed tree), so the run excludes it per
CLAUDE.md.
