# V3.06 — A north arrow and a scale on the map

*The owner, the morning after V3.05: "a quick addition. Specifically the ability
to include a N arrow and a scale (in kilometers but able to be changed to meters
if needed)." Numbered F205, the next free ID, and shipped with it.*

## What was missing

The 2D map had neither. Leaflet ships a scale control and the app never added
it; there was no north arrow at all. So a screenshot of a design, and the map
page of **Export PDF**, which is a grab of the map as it is shown
(`app._on_export_pdf` calls `map_widget.grab()`), could be read for neither
direction nor distance. The take-it-outside document's **planting plan** already
had both, since V2.31: it is a scale drawing with its own scale bar and north
arrow (`src/planting_map.py`), and it is unchanged here.

## The change

- **View → North Arrow** and **View → Scale Bar** switch each on and off, and
  **View → Scale Units** picks **Kilometres** (the default, as asked) or
  **Metres**. A click on the bar itself switches units too, and the menu follows.
  All three are remembered between sessions (`src/map_furniture_flow.py`). Both
  are on until switched off, so the next Export PDF carries them.
- **Drawn on the map page** (`html/map/11-map-furniture.js`, the eleventh
  script), so what is on screen is what an export carries.
  - **The arrow** sits under the zoom buttons, in their style. The top edge is
    the placement bar's while placing, and the top right is the plant page's and
    the selection badge's. The map is Web Mercator and never rotates, so north
    is always up and the arrow never turns. It takes no clicks.
  - **The bar** sits at the bottom centre, clear of the Legend and its button at
    the bottom left and the relationship web's key at the bottom right. It is
    the longest round length (1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten) that fits in
    120 px, measured across the middle of the map as Leaflet's own control
    measures it, and redrawn as the map moves, because Mercator's metres per
    pixel change with latitude. It is a `<button>` outside the map's container,
    so a click or Enter on it never reaches the map as a click to place.

**Kilometres at yard scale read small**: at zoom 18 over Edmonton the bar says
"0.02 km", where metres say "20 m". Kilometres are what was asked for, and metres
are one click away.

A switch flipped before the map's page has loaded is saved and sent when the
page says it is ready: `run_js` does not queue a call, so sending it early would
only log an error.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **The planting plan's own scale bar** (metres) and north arrow: it is a scale
  drawing to lay out with a tape measure, and already had both.
- **The 3D preview**: it can be turned, so a fixed north arrow would lie there;
  a compass that turns with the camera is its own piece of work.

## How it is checked

- `tests/test_map_furniture.py`: the page's own `scaleBarFor` run in node (the
  round lengths, both units, a yard and a quarter section, nothing drawn for a
  zero or a NaN); the builders; the View menu's switches against a stand-in
  settings store: both on in kilometres until changed, each switch remembered,
  metres from the menu or the bar with the menu following, a setting saved as
  text still read.
- `tests/test_map_js.py` pins `setNorthArrow` and `setScaleBar` as names the page
  defines; `tests/test_bridge_contract.py` finds `onScaleUnitChanged` on the
  bridge; the architecture guard gained the new script's ceiling.
- **Live**, the real window under a virtual display at 1366 × 768: the map
  reported "0.02 km" with the arrow drawn, "20 m" with it switched off, and a
  click on the bar went back to kilometres with the menu and the setting
  following. The page in headless Chromium, for the picture: the arrow under the
  zoom buttons, the bar at the bottom centre, nothing overlapping.

**The suite**, in this container, as CI runs it: 4,422 tests (V3.05: 4,413), 0
failed, 15 skipped, `RESULT: OK`. The wait-for-the-page guard came after that run
and was checked by its own module and the map and guard tests; CI runs the whole
suite on the pushed commit.

## What could not be verified here

- **The picture in a real Export PDF.** `QWebEngineView.grab()` returns a blank
  surface under this container's virtual display, so the PDF's map page could
  not be looked at here. It is the same grab the PDF has always used, and the
  arrow and bar are ordinary page content.
- **Over real map tiles**: this container cannot load them, so the white boxes
  were seen over Leaflet's grey. They are drawn to be read over satellite
  imagery and street tiles alike.
