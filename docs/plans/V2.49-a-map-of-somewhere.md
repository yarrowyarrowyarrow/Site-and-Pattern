# V2.49 — A map of somewhere, and nine filter corrections

## Context

Feedback on the V2.48 website, in two parts. The first was blunt and correct:

> *"The map is atrocious and needs to actually look like the ecoregion map
> which I also include, including the political/provincial boundaries."*

The second was nine specific things wrong with the filters, two of which are
outright bugs and the rest of which are judgement calls the first cut got wrong.

---

## 1. The map

### What was wrong

`data/ecoregions_canada.geojson` held **ten five-vertex rectangles**, drawn by
hand in V2.14 as a placeholder. V2.48 started drawing them, which is what made
the problem visible: a grid of boxes is not a map of anywhere, and with no
provincial borders a reader cannot even tell which part of the country they are
looking at.

### What it is now

`scripts/draw_ecoregions.py` writes both files and is the record of how the
shapes were derived:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Ecoregion polygons | 10 | 6 |
| Vertices | 50 | 115 |
| Provincial borders | none | 4 |
| Cities | none | 8 |

The outlines follow real geography: the **continental divide** up the
Alberta/BC border sampled every half degree, the **parkland as a crescent**
from Calgary through Red Deer and Edmonton then east past North Battleford and
north of Saskatoon, the **grassland triangle** in the south split into dry and
moist, and the **boreal** filling the north with the foothills wedge running
west above Grande Prairie.

Alberta and Saskatchewan are unusually easy to draw honestly: three of Alberta's
four borders and all four of Saskatchewan's are meridians and parallels, and the
fourth is the divide. So the *borders* are accurate even though the ecoregions
are not, and they are what makes the drawing legible.

**The caveat still ships with every map.** These are still hand-authored, and
the real CEC Level III polygons are still a download that has never run in a
session with open egress. What changed is that an approximate outline of the
right thing beats an exact rectangle around the wrong thing.

### The part that made this risky

These polygons are **not decoration**. `ecoregion.lookup_ecoregions` reads them
to decide which ecoregion a user's property is in, which decides which plants
get recommended. Redrawing them silently changes recommendations for real sites.

So the script **self-checks before it writes**: 15 city lookups the suite pins
(Edmonton is parkland, Banff is montane, Saskatoon is moist mixed grassland,
Calgary is foothills), 3 points that must fall outside everything, 2 that must
match exactly one region, and a **coverage sweep** over every sampled point
inside Alberta or Saskatchewan.

That sweep earned its keep immediately. The first cut traced adjacent regions
separately and left **a band east of Calgary, 0.8 degrees tall, belonging to no
region at all**: anyone in Strathmore would have been told the catalogue does
not reach their site. Adjacent regions are now cut from *shared boundary lines*,
so a gap is structurally impossible, and the sweep catches it if one appears
anyway.

### Three tests moved, and why that is not cheating

- `test_the_foothills_overlap_reports_both` used `(53.0, -114.8)`, which was an
  overlap of the *rectangles* rather than of anything on the ground. The
  redrawn polygons put that coordinate squarely in the parkland alone. The
  behaviour under test (a boundary site reports both regions) is unchanged and
  still asserted; the coordinate moved to `(52.0, -115.3)`, Nordegg, where the
  foothills genuinely do run into the parkland.
- `test_duplicate_entries_merge_under_one_key` read the shipped parkland's two
  rectangles. Parkland is now one arc, so the test was measuring the drawing
  rather than the merge. Rewritten against a synthetic two-polygon file.

Regions still **overlap on purpose** at their shared edges, by 0.18 degrees. A
property near a boundary genuinely belongs to both, and the second answer is
exactly the species list that used to go missing (P9).

## 2. The filters

Nine reports. Two were bugs.

### The toxicity count went the wrong way

> *"When I check off 'no known toxicity to pets' it lists 388, then when I
> additionally click 'no known toxicity to humans', it now lists 404. The
> number should decrease imo as I imagine someone clicking these buttons wants
> a plant that is toxic to neither dogs nor kids."*

Exactly right, and the cause was a single rule applied too broadly: within a
facet, ticked values were ORed. That is correct for colour (yellow OR blue) and
wrong for safety, where the union of two safety claims is larger than either.

A facet now declares `combine="any"` or `combine="all"`. Safety is `all`.
**Roles are `all` too**, which was not reported but was the same bug: the
desktop's `search_plants` has ANDed use tags since V1.85, so the two surfaces
disagreed about the same question.

Now: pet-safe 385, pet-safe plus child-safe 385, all three 370. The middle
number does not fall because every pet-safe plant here is also child-safe (more
species are recorded toxic to pets than to people), which is the honest answer
rather than a bug.

### Sedges were "showy"

> *"Showy flower seems incorrect as it includes sedges and other plants I'm
> assuming don't have showy flowers."*

**81 grasses, sedges and rushes** were filed as showy. Same root as the colour
bug: they carry a hex and a `flower_form` of `plume`, which the derivation read
as "has a flower, therefore showy". A wind-pollinated plant does not advertise;
the plume is a seed head. The wind-pollinated families are now never showy,
which is the rule `flower_colour` already applied. Showy 309, not showy 125.

### The rest

| Report | What changed |
|---|---|
| Photograph needs a "no photo" option, and should be nearer the top | Both values, and it is now the **first** facet. 323 have one, **111 do not**, and that list is the worklist |
| Too much scrolling | Facets tile in **two columns**, groups tightened. 21 facets fit a laptop screen collapsed |
| Leaf shape and flower shape can be removed | Removed |
| The count disappears when you scroll the filter list | The search box, the count and the active-filter chips are a **sticky head** inside the sidebar; only the facet list scrolls under them |
| Big-box and garden centre are too generous; any native should also be under native nursery | `availability_class` records the *easiest* place to find a species, which is a reasonable thing to record and the wrong thing to filter on: saying "big-box" implied "not at the native nursery", which is false. A native now carries `native_specialist` **in addition to** its tier. Big-box still returns 17 |
| No non-natives | The 4 garden cultivars and the duplicate Bee Balm row are dropped. **434 published, down from 439** |

### The non-native filter is by file, not by flag

Worth recording because the obvious implementation is wrong twice over.
Filtering on `native_to_alberta` would have thrown out **Stiff Goldenrod**
(*Oligoneuron rigidum*), which is flagged non-Alberta and is a genuine
Saskatchewan and Manitoba prairie native, and would have **kept** the Bee Balm
row, which is flagged native and is a duplicate of Wild Bergamot that arrived
from the garden file. Matching common names against `data/garden_plants.json`
asks the question that was actually meant.

## What this is deliberately not

- **Not real CEC polygons.** Still a download, still described in the V2.38
  runbook, and the caveat still says so on every drawing.
- **Not a fix to `availability_class` itself.** The column still records one
  tier per species; the filter now reads it correctly. Recording several tiers
  per species would be a schema change and a sourcing job.
- **Not a de-duplication of the catalogue.** Two duplicate pairs remain in
  `plants_master.json` itself (*Geum triflorum*, *Valeriana sitchensis*, each
  under two common names). Flagged rather than merged: which name is the
  accepted one is the author's call.

## What I could not verify

- **How the map looks.** No display in this container. It is verified
  structurally: every polygon projects inside the viewport, all 20 pinned
  lookups pass, coverage has no holes, and no external reference is emitted.
  Whether it *reads* as the prairies is a question only a human eye answers,
  which is why it went back for a look before the rest of the work.
- **The filter interactions in a browser.** The AND/OR logic is verified by
  simulating the client filter against the emitted index in Python, not by
  clicking. The two-column layout and the sticky count are CSS nobody has seen
  rendered.
