# V2.59 — the edges were real all along

**F125 — plant↔animal relationship sourcing.** Branch `V2.59`, cut from `V2.58`.
Schema **v66** (reseed only, no DDL). No new IDs; next free stays **F127**.

## Why

V2.58 ended by making a gap *visible*: a user placed sixteen native plants,
was shown no animals, and reasonably concluded the app had broken. It had not.
`plant_fauna` held 361 edges covering **99 of 437 species** — a thin catalogue,
honestly reported for the first time. The user's reply named the real fix:

> *"Let's get the real sourcing started… I do want more plants-animal
> relationships represented."*

Honesty about a gap is not a substitute for closing it. This increment closes
most of it.

| Species with any documented edge | 99 of 437 | **271 of 437** |

## The shape: fetch outside, review inside

The container's egress policy denies GloBI, GBIF and iNaturalist, so this could
not be one script. It is two, and the split turned out to be the right design
independent of the network:

- **`scripts/fetch_fauna_edges.py`** — stdlib only, runs on the author's
  machine, writes a candidate file. Never touches the seed data.
- **`scripts/ingest_fauna_edges.py`** — runs in the repo, offline, over that
  file. **Reports by default; writes only under `--apply`.**

Every gate reports a count, so a run says what was thrown away and why rather
than only what survived. That report is what caught two of the three mistakes
below.

### Why GloBI and not GBIF

GBIF holds *occurrences* — where a species was seen. It does not hold
interactions. GloBI (Global Biotic Interactions) is the aggregator that does,
and it is the union of hundreds of underlying datasets rather than a work in
its own right.

That distinction is recorded rather than smoothed over. The bibliography entry
says so in its own `record_note`: *an AGGREGATOR, not a primary work, and
weaker evidence than a field guide.* The source key on all 2,439 edges is
`globi`, not a study — because that is the claim the data can carry.

## Three things that were wrong, and what caught each

**1. The query, and then the error handling that hid it.** Every one of the
first 55 plants returned HTTP 500. Two faults stacked: a malformed query (a
`fields=` parameter GloBI does not take, pipe-joined interaction types) and a
handler that swallowed the response body, so the run reported "failed" 55 times
and never once said why. Fixed by adding `--probe`, which reports reachability,
which query form works, what columns come back, and **what percentage of rows
actually populate each one**. The author's single probe run settled three API
facts guessing had got wrong.

**2. The citation column does not exist.** The first `--apply` rejected all
14,111 candidates for "no reporting study". GloBI returns `study_title`, not
`study_citation` — and only populates it when asked for observations rather
than distinct interactions.

The instructive part is why the test suite did not catch this: **the fixture
encoded the same wrong guess as the code**, so `test_the_reporting_study_travels_with_the_edge`
passed while both were wrong. A fixture invented from the same assumption as
the code under test proves nothing. Only the live probe settled it.

The provenance bar was then deliberately lowered, on the author's call, from
"a named reporting study" to "a registered, checkable source". That is a real
weakening and it is recorded as one — in the gate's comment, in the
bibliography record, and in the test that now asserts the weaker standard by
name. An edge with *no* source is still refused; that would be an assertion.

**3. Twenty-three monarch caterpillars on goldenrod.** The fetcher defaulted
"life stage unrecorded → `larval_host`", on the reasoning that most *eats*
records on a plant are herbivory. Applied to butterflies that reasoning
inverts: adult nectaring is what GloBI mostly holds, and the default promoted
those visits into host claims — Monarch on goldenrod, aster, blazingstar,
sunflower, yarrow. Monarch caterpillars are obligate *Asclepias* specialists.

**The repo's own `test_fauna.test_monarch_only_hosts_on_milkweed` caught it**,
on a test written years before this pipeline existed. The seed file was
restored from backup and `larval_host` now requires GloBI's explicit `hostOf`
verb. Of the 120 candidate host edges whose animal was already in the
catalogue, that gate drops **107** and keeps 13.

It is a heavy cut and it is the right one. Telling somebody to plant goldenrod
to host monarch caterpillars is worse than telling them nothing.

## The other gates

Beyond the three above, `review()` refuses: an incomplete record; a
relationship or taxon outside the schema vocabulary (restated in the ingester
as a gate, so a candidate file predating a schema change fails loudly instead
of writing a value the CHECK constraint rejects at seed time); a plant not in
this catalogue; a genus-level animal; an already-seeded pair; a duplicate
within the file; and **nectar claimed on a wind-pollinated plant**.

That last one reuses `flower_colour.WIND_POLLINATED_TYPES`, so this gate and
the colour classifier cannot disagree. GloBI recording a hoverfly on a bulrush
is a true observation of a visit and a false statement about a reward — the
insect was after pollen or shelter. `pollen` is deliberately *not* dropped on
grasses: bees genuinely collect grass and sedge pollen, and refusing that would
trade one wrong claim for a missing true one.

Query region is bounded to North America (`_BBOX`), because a bumblebee
recorded on *Achillea* in a New Zealand garden is not evidence about an Alberta
yard.

## A nectaring bird does not close a food chain

Applying the edges broke `test_milkweed_only_food_web_broken_no_birds`, and the
test was right.

GloBI has a documented Ruby-throated Hummingbird nectar record on Showy
Milkweed. `habitat_score`'s food-web check counted **any** bird edge, so a
milkweed-only design promptly reported a complete Tallamy chain. Milkweed is
the worst case it could have picked: monarchs are the textbook aposematic
caterpillar that birds learn *not* to eat.

The looseness pre-dated this increment — 21 seeded bird `nectar` edges were
already in the file — and only became visible when the new data happened to
land on the species a test watched. Every line the food-web status drives talks
about berries, seeds and the protein nestlings need, so the count behind it now
excludes flower visits (`n_birds_forage`). `n_birds` still reports every bird
supported: the hummingbird is not deleted, it is just not evidence of a food
chain, and `design_critic` now says so in a line of its own.

**Relaxing the test to match the new data would have been the easy move and the
wrong one** — it was the guard telling the truth.

## What was left out, and why

**10,937 further edges are ready and blocked on 2,872 new animals.** A `fauna`
row needs a common name, a taxon and a nativity call; an interaction record
supplies none of those, and all three show up in the UI. The author's decision:

> *"I am okay adding more species but I want to ensure they are native to AB
> and the prairies. Perhaps we leave them out at the moment but consider it
> for future work. A curated list should include butterflies/moths, bees,
> beetles, flies, birds and mammals."*

Recorded as **F127** rather than done here, because the curation is the work —
the edges are the easy half.

**Per-study citations and life stages.** A re-fetch with
`includeObservations=true` would carry both, upgrading every `globi` edge in
place *and* recovering the real larval-host records among the 107 the gate
drops. Recorded as **F128**.

**Mammal browse is dropped entirely**, not mapped. The seven relationships have
no slot for it: a deer stripping willow is not `fruit_food`, and `cover` means
the plant *shelters* the animal — a different claim. Forcing it would be the
exact failure V2.58 was spent undoing.

### One test changed shape, not threshold

`test_coverage_actually_improved` pinned documented coverage *below* 30%
("reaches ~23% of the catalogue"). At 62% it failed — the feature working, read
as a regression. An absolute ceiling on how much evidence the catalogue is
allowed to hold was the wrong shape: real sourcing is *supposed* to shrink the
gap the derived layer fills, and one day should close it. It now asserts the
ordering (derived reaches species documented misses), which is the property
that actually matters.

## What could not be verified here

- **The fetch itself.** Egress policy denies GloBI; the author ran it. What is
  tested here is the mapping (against a fixture whose column names the live
  probe confirmed) and every gate.
- **Whether each of the 2,439 edges is true.** They are checkable — anyone can
  query GloBI for the same pair — and that checkability is the whole basis for
  filing them as `documented`. No sample was hand-verified against a flora.
- **The 2,872 held animals' nativity.** Untouched by design.
