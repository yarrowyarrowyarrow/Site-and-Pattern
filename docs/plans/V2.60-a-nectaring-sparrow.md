# V2.60 — a nectaring sparrow

**F124** (the vegetation-layer map) **+ F127a** (the 67 birds, curated).
Branch `V2.60`, cut from `V2.59`. Schema **v67** (reseed only, no DDL).
Next free ID stays **F129**.

## Two jobs, one theme

Both are the app claiming something its own data does not support, and both
were found by measuring rather than by reading.

---

## F124 — 311 of 437 species occupied no vegetation layer

`habitat_score.PLANT_TYPE_TO_LAYER` knew six `plant_type` values. The catalogue
holds eleven. The five it missed:

| type | species | |
|---|---|---|
| `wildflower` | **210** | the largest group in the catalogue, by a factor of four |
| `grass` | 53 | |
| `sedge` | 20 | |
| `aquatic` | 19 | |
| `rush` | 8 | |
| `fern` | 1 | |

So the vegetation-layer component counted 311 species as nothing, and a
prairie meadow of wildflowers, grasses and sedges scored **0 of 15** — this
app's central use case scoring zero on a component it plainly satisfies. Found
while building F91 in V2.57 and held for the author, because the fix **raises**
the score of every affected design and the headline-stability rule reserves
that.

### The number I quoted was wrong, and the fix is smaller than it sounded

Asking for the decision, I said a prairie meadow would go from 0/15 to 9–12/15.
That was not measured, and it is not right. If all five missing types map to
`herbaceous`, the meadow gains **one** layer — 3 of 15.

Every plant in the catalogue has a `mature_height_meters`, and the herbaceous
group is not one stratum: `wildflower` spans **0.05 m to 3.00 m**. So the map
is height-aware for that group, and the boundary is not invented — it is read
off the catalogue's own `groundcover` type, where 26 of 30 species come in
under 0.30 m and the tallest is 0.60.

Measured after the change:

| design | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 10 tall forbs and grasses | 0 of 15 | **3 of 15** |
| the same plus two short forbs | 0 of 15 | **6 of 15** |

Better, correct, and half what I said. The value of the fix moved; its
correctness did not.

### What was deliberately left alone

**The types that already had a layer.** Eleven of the 56 shrubs are under a
metre and a creeping juniper is arguably groundcover — but re-filing them would
*lower* scores, which is the opposite of the change that was approved.

**The five-layer model itself**, which is the residual finding and belongs to
the author. `CANONICAL_LAYERS` is overstory / shrub / herbaceous / groundcover /
vine: the permaculture **forest-garden** stack. A prairie genuinely has two of
those, so even with F124 fixed, a grassland design is structurally capped at
6 of 15 — the component is measuring *how much like a woodland edge is this*
and calling the answer habitat value. In an app for prairie conversion, whose
own reference communities are grasslands, that is worth deciding about rather
than inheriting. Raised as **F129**, not taken.

## F127a — the 67 birds, and three bugs behind them

F125 left 10,937 edges blocked on 2,872 animals. Birds were the tractable
slice: 67 species, all with common names, and 81 of their 115 edges filed as
`fruit_food` — the bird-forage relationship V2.59's food-web chain now depends
on.

`scripts/curate_birds.py` decides each one, three ways: **include** (regular in
Alberta or the prairie provinces), **hold** (real but marginal — regular in
Manitoba, a vagrant here), **reject** (out of range, or introduced).

Every verdict is **an unsourced call from knowledge** — the same footing as the
142 fauna rows already shipped, none of which carries a `source` field either.
That is exactly why it is a diffable table with the reason on every row instead
of 67 silent decisions: an Albertan reading the list can disagree with a
specific line.

Result: **37 include, 5 hold, 24 reject** — and then 25 actually written.

### Bug 1: `eatenBy` is not `fruit_food`

The fetcher mapped GloBI's `eatenBy` to `fruit_food` for any bird. Of the 33
plants that ended up carrying such an edge, **ten bear no fruit at all**:
Lodgepole Pine, White Spruce, Tamarack, Paper Birch, Trembling Aspen, Balsam
Poplar, Canada Goldenrod, Cut-leaved Anemone, Philadelphia Fleabane, Showy
Milkweed.

A White-winged Crossbill on a tamarack is eating **seeds**. A sapsucker on a
birch is drinking **sap**, which the schema cannot express at all. A hummingbird
eats no fruit in any season.

And in the other direction, GloBI filed three `flowersVisitedBy` records for a
**White-crowned Sparrow** — on sagebrush, goldenrod and rabbitbrush. Sparrows do
not nectar. The bird was at the flower gleaning insects.

This is the Monarch bug from V2.59 again: **an unspecific verb read as a
specific claim.** Same answer — refuse what the record cannot support. Each
curated bird carries a `feeds` guild, and `route_bird` uses it:

* `eatenBy` + eats fruit + **the plant has fruit data** → `fruit_food`
* `eatenBy` + eats seeds → `seed_food` *(this recovers the crossbill)*
* `nectar` + is a nectarivore → kept
* anything else → **dropped**, as mammal browse was

An empty `feeds` set is a decision, not a gap: a Red-tailed Hawk takes nothing
from a plant, so every plant record for one is a food-web artifact. That reports
separately from a bird nobody has assessed.

### Bug 2: the gate was not total, and quietly ate 62 good edges

The first run judged only the 38 newly curated birds, so the **24 the catalogue
already held** fell through as "uncurated" and 62 of their existing edges were
binned. A routing gate that silently drops what it does not recognise is worse
than no gate. `SEEDED_FEEDS` covers those 24 — diet only; their nativity was
decided when the rows were authored.

### Bug 3: a second Downy Woodpecker

`Picoides pubescens` is the Downy Woodpecker under its old genus, and the
catalogue already holds it as `Dryobates pubescens`. Adding it would have
created a duplicate species keyed on a name nothing else in the app uses, with
its edges pointing at the wrong row. `SYNONYMS` resolves it before any other
gate looks at the animal.

### `--refit`, because a gate added late does not clean up after itself

The bird routing arrived one increment after the apply it should have governed.
V2.59 had already written 20 `fruit_food` edges on fruitless plants and 14
nectar edges on birds that do not nectar. `ingest_fauna_edges.py --refit`
re-runs the current gates over rows a previous run wrote, touching only
`source == globi` — hand-authored edges are somebody's reading of a book and
are not this script's to revise. **Applied data has to stay re-derivable**, or
every future gate leaves sediment behind it.

Net: 20 re-routed to `seed_food`, 34 removed (14 impossible nectar claims, 20
collapsing into a `seed_food` edge that already existed).

### Orphans, refused

12 of the 37 included birds are **not written**. A sapsucker's every record is
a sap record; a Ruby-crowned Kinglet's, an Alder Flycatcher's and a Magnolia
Warbler's are all `flowersVisitedBy` from insect-gleaning. Writing those rows
would put animals in the catalogue connected to nothing, and this repo has
fought orphan fauna before — V2.42 took them 56 → 3, and
`test_derived_edges.test_cuckoo_bees_are_no_longer_orphans` holds the line. It
caught this, at 14.

### The numbers

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Birds in `fauna` | 24 | **49** |
| Bird edges | 143 | **177** |
| `fruit_food` on a plant with no fruit | 20 | **0** |
| Bird `nectar` edges by non-hummingbirds | 14 | **0** |
| Total `plant_fauna` edges | 2,800 | **2,817** |

The edge count barely moves and that is the honest result: 115 candidate bird
edges became 34 real ones. The value is in the 34 being right and the 34 wrong
ones being gone.

### Two test bounds moved, for stated reasons

`test_masses_and_spans_are_plausible` capped bird mass at **2,500 g**, which
was every bird in the catalogue when it held 24 passerines and raptors. A Snow
Goose is 2.7 kg and a Sandhill Crane is 4.5 kg. Raised to 6 kg rather than
removed: a ×10 decimal slip on any bird under 600 g — 47 of the 49 rows — still
trips it.

## What could not be verified here

- **Every nativity verdict.** Unsourced by construction, and the reason the
  table is shaped to be argued with. The marginal calls are the five `hold`
  rows plus House Finch, which is native to western North America and a recent
  colonist of prairie cities — established, not historically present.
- **Whether the 34 surviving bird edges are individually true.** They are
  checkable against GloBI for the same pair; none was checked against a flora.
- **The 3D wingbeat for the 25 new birds.** The morphology feeds
  `flight_model`, which needs the 3D window and a GPU. The numbers are
  plausibility-guarded; how a Sandhill Crane actually looks flying over a yard
  is not something this container can show.
