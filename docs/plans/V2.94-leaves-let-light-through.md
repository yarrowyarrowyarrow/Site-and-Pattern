# V2.94 — Leaves let light through

*One item: **F186**, found by V2.92: seen from a person's height, tree crowns
rendered near-black. The owner said "Continue" after V2.93, and this was offered
as the next increment, with F185 (water plants in ponds) as the alternative.*

## What was wrong

Every tree in the catalogue (20 species), rendered alone through the real viewer:
`build_scene` → `scene3d.html`, Balanced, the baked models loaded. Each is mature,
in July, under the scene's own default sun (21 June, 13:00 at Calgary: 60° up,
from the south-south-west). There are five cameras: four at a standing person's
eye (1.6 m), one on each side, and one from above at the angle the V2.87 audit
used.

Each frame is drawn a second time with only the plant in it, unlit white on
black. That pass is the mask: brightness is measured on the plant's own pixels,
not the shadow beside it. Brightness is sRGB luma, from 0 (black) to 1 (white).
"Near-black" means under 0.12.

| | Eye level | From above | Near-black at eye level |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2.93, all 20 trees | 0.164 | 0.228 | 57% |

From above, a crown is its sunlit tops, which is how the audit saw them. From a
path, more than half of every crown was near-black: the V2.92 plan's "black
cut-outs against the sky".

**The first measurement was wrong, and so was every number drawn from it.** The
mask's white stand-in material drew front faces only, so every leaf card with its
back to the camera dropped out of the mask. From below, that is most of them.
Balsamroot gave it away: its basal leaves were missing from the mask entirely. The
mask is now two-sided, the wind is stopped so the two passes are the same
geometry, and every number here comes from the corrected pass. The early numbers
overstated V2.93's eye-level brightness, because they left out the darkest faces.

## Why

Three causes. Each was measured by switching it alone.

1. **An opaque leaf seen from behind.** A leaf card faces the sky, as a leaf does,
   so from a path you see mostly undersides. In the viewer an underside took no
   sun at all, and only the dim ground half of the sky light. A real leaf seen
   with the sun behind it glows: in green light a thin broad leaf lets through
   nearly as much as it reflects.
2. **The bake darkened the sun.** Each baked model carries a grey shade per
   vertex (`COLOR_0`). It is ambient occlusion: how much sky a point can see. The
   viewer multiplied it into the leaf's colour, so it dimmed the sun as well as
   the sky. The shadow map already shadows the sun, so a sunlit leaf inside the
   crown was shaded twice.
3. **The bake put the ground at the crown's lowest leaf.** Tree foliage is baked
   on its own, without its trunk. `bake_ao` put its ground plane at the lowest
   vertex it was given, which for a crown is its lowest leaf. Its docstring said
   z = 0. Every ray reaching down past that leaf counted as blocked. With rays
   reaching 60% of the tree's height, nearly every interior leaf was buried: the
   trees' crowns averaged 0.30, with 63% of their vertices below 0.3.

| All 20 trees | Eye level | From above | Near-black at eye level |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2.93 | 0.164 | 0.228 | 57% |
| V2.93's models, the new leaf light | 0.196 | 0.233 | 49% |
| V2.93's light, the bake switched off | 0.219 | 0.387 | 47% |
| **V2.94**: the new leaf light and rebaked crowns | **0.237** | **0.315** | **38%** |

The new light changes the view from a path and barely the view from above, which
is what it is for: it adds light only where you see a leaf's unlit side. The bake
darkened both views. V2.94's view from above stays below the no-bake figure
because the bake still shades, as it should.

## The change

### Leaves let light through (`html/scene3d/01c-leaves.js`, new)

A leaf material now takes the light falling on its far side, the sun's and the
sky's, times the share it lets through. A leaf in another's shadow stays in it,
because the sun's colour at that point already carries the shadow map.

| Preset | Lets through | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `crown` (broad-leaved trees) | 0.55 | A share of what the leaf would reflect in the same light. A thin broad leaf transmits nearly what it reflects, so 0.55 is on the low side |
| `shrubLeaf` | 0.55 | The same leaves, on shrubs |
| `herbLeaf` | 0.45 | A drawing choice, set a little below the trees' and not tuned further: a herb is seen mostly from above, where it changes little |
| `needle` | 0.3 | A needle is thick and lets little through. The 0.3 stands for light passing between the needles of a tuft: a drawing choice |

Not given it: bark; grass blades (V1.92 already tips their normals toward the
sky); the succulents' bodies, which are thick and have presets of their own;
flowers and fruit.

### The bake shades the sky, mostly (`01c-leaves.js`)

The grey is taken out of the leaf's colour and applied to the light instead: all
of it to the sky light, and a share (`BAKE_ON_SUN`, 0.3) to the sun. The share
stands for the shading the viewer's 1,024-texel shadow map is too coarse to see
at leaf scale; the value is a drawing choice.
A tinted gradient keeps its hue: the Stylised and procedural crowns' warm top
passes on only its brightness as shade.

Only the vertex colour's own brightness moves. The per-plant colour (the season,
a plant's health) stays in the leaf.

### The crown bake (`ao_bake.py`, `build_all.py`)

- `bake_ao` takes `floor_z`, where the ground is. `None`, the default, is the
  lowest vertex, which is right for anything baked whole. A crown baked on its own
  passes 0.
- The trees' crowns bake with `CROWN_AO`: strength 0.65, rays reaching 15% of
  the tree, the ground at 0, and the vertical gradient from 0.8 at the bottom to
  1 at the top.
- The bark bake and everything else are unchanged.

Three settings were tried, all with the ground at 0:

| | Strength | Ray reach | Gradient floor | Crowns' mean shade | Below 0.3 | Eye level (trees) | Near-black |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2.93 (ground at the lowest leaf) | 0.85 | 0.6 | 0.55 | 0.30 | 63% | 0.196 | 49% |
| A | 0.85 | 0.6 | 0.55 | 0.37 | 45% | 0.209 | 45% |
| B | 0.75 | 0.25 | 0.7 | 0.48 | 28% | 0.225 | 41% |
| **C, shipped** | 0.65 | 0.15 | 0.8 | 0.58 | 4% | 0.237 | 38% |

(Eye-level figures are all under the new leaf light, so the rows differ only by
the bake.)

A alone, the ground put back where it is, is the bug fix, and it is not enough.
C is a drawing choice. It shortens the rays to a leaf's near neighbours, because
the rest of the job belongs to other parts: the shadow map shades the crown in
the sun, and the split above keeps the bake on the sky. It leaves the inside of a
crown darker than its rim (the tenth-percentile vertex is 0.29 to 0.51).
Occlusion also treats every blocking leaf as black, when a leaf passes on some of
the light it catches. A shorter reach is a rough allowance for that, not a model
of it.

**Only the 25 tree models change.** A full build was run and compared file by
file with the shipped models. Each rebuilt tree differs from its shipped file only
in its foliage's `COLOR_0`: positions, normals, indices and the bark's shade are
identical, accessor by accessor. Nothing else was copied from the build. Every
other file differed in its exporter version string (`v4.2.83` here against
`v4.2.57`), and four had drifted from the catalogue (below).

## Which plants it reaches

- **Trees**: all 25 models, and the procedural trees Stylised draws.
- **Shrubs**: the translucency only. The shrub layer draws its foliage with vertex
  colours off (`04-quality.js`, `buildShrubLayer` passes no `vc` to
  `surfaceMaterial`), so no shrub has ever shown its baked shade or its
  procedural gradient. That is a separate question, **F188**. So the shrubs are
  not rebaked: the files would change and nothing on screen would.
  - Chokecherry at eye level: 0.16 → 0.22, near-black 62% → 40%.
  - Beaked hazelnut: 0.16 → 0.25, near-black 64% → 27%.
- **Herbs, groundcovers and a fern**: 13 of them measured, from Wild Bergamot and
  Blanketflower to Bearberry, Ostrich Fern and Creeping Juniper.
  - **None washed out.** The brightest, Prairie Sage, went 0.52 → 0.53.
  - The groundcovers moved by 0.01 at most: from 1.6 m you look down on their
    tops, which the sun already lit.
  - The dark ones came up: harebell from the north 42% near-black → 6%, fireweed
    14% → 6%, showy milkweed 26% → 18%.
- **Stylised**: the closed faceted crowns take it too. A solid's shaded side now
  gets the light through it, which could flatten the facets. It does not: they
  still read as facets. Near-black at eye level for Stylised trees: 45% → 29%.
- **A whole yard**: the app's worked example, with four trees and four
  structures. From the street, the bur oak's crown turns from black leaf
  silhouettes to green leaves, and the spruce reads blue-green where it was
  near-black.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **The ground bounce (F187, new).** A surface facing down is lit only by the
  hemisphere light's ground colour, `0x5d6e51` at 0.9: 0.13 in luminance. Under
  the viewer's own noon sun and sky, the ground it draws (`#87a56c`) would reflect
  about 0.9 onto that surface. So the bounce is about a seventh of what it should
  be, and that is why tier undersides, trunks and walls stay dark on their shaded
  side.
  - A brighter ground colour was tried at two strengths (`0xa8b890` and
    `0xc4d9ab`). It lifted the conifers only 0.02-0.03 from a path.
  - It would change every surface in every scene: trunks, walls, the shed,
    animals' bellies.
  - That is a look for the whole viewer, so it is the owner's call and not this
    increment's.
- **The evergreen conifers are still darker than the broad-leaved trees from their
  shaded side.** Near-black at eye level is 45-74%, down from 55-80%. Part of
  that is real:
  - needles are thick and let little through;
  - a spruce's tiers shade the tiers below;
  - balsam fir, Rocky Mountain juniper and Douglas-fir have the darkest foliage
    colours in the catalogue (luma 0.30-0.33, against 0.42-0.61 for the
    broad-leaved trees).

  With the new light and the bake switched off entirely, those three still
  render 72-80% near-black from the north, the side away from the sun. The
  published multi-bounce correction for occlusion (Jimenez et al. 2016) was
  worked through too: for a leaf as dark as a spruce needle it lifts a 0.30 to
  0.33, almost nothing. Whether they look too dark beside a photograph was not
  checked, and a colour change needs a source (F172).
- **The shrubs' bake (F188, new)**, above.
- **Four shipped models carry six variants no species uses any more.** These are
  `herb.clump`, `herb.erect`, `layer.groundcover` and `shrub.mound`, left by
  V2.93's catalogue changes. No species is missing its own variant; the extras
  are unused bytes. A rebuild would renumber the variants of four large files for
  nothing visible.
- **Exposure and tone mapping** are untouched.
- **Creeping juniper drawn as a star of blades** showed up in the control renders.
  The audit's groundcover row already has it.

## How it is checked

`tests/test_leaf_light.py` (new; no browser):
- the chunk loads before any material is built (before `02-plants.js`);
- the four leaf presets let light through and nothing else does, with needles
  the least;
- a material with translucency cannot share a compiled program with one without;
- **each shader chunk `applyLeafLight` hooks into exists in the vendored
  three.js**. `String.replace` on a renamed chunk is a silent no-op, and a
  three.js upgrade would otherwise quietly bring back the opaque leaves;
- the crown bake puts the ground at 0, the bark and shrub bakes are unchanged,
  and `bake_ao`'s default is still the lowest vertex;
- **every shipped tree's crown averages at least 0.45**, with no more than a
  fifth of its vertices under 0.3. All of V2.93's fail: 24 of 25 averaged under
  0.45, and every one had at least a quarter of its vertices under 0.3.

`tests/test_accuracy_render.py` (2 new, headless Chromium). Trembling Aspen and
Paper Birch are photographed at 1.6 m from the side away from the sun and from
the side facing it, and measured in the page with the same two-pass mask. The
probe case carries the scene's own sun, and bounds wide enough for the shadow map
to cover a 20 m tree; the probe's default ±5 m would leave most of the crown
outside it.
- Neither crown is a silhouette from its shaded side: under 15% near-black and
  averaging over 0.25. The probe reads aspen 2% and 0.31, paper birch 8% and 0.30.
- The side facing the sun is still the brighter one (0.37 and 0.36), and under
  0.6, so a leaf that lets light through has not been made to glow.

## What could not be verified here

- **Against photographs.** The judgments of look are the author's, from renders.
  The translucency figures come from what leaves are known to do, not from a
  measurement of these species.
- **On a real desktop GPU.** Every render here is headless Chromium on the
  SwiftShader software renderer, running the same viewer files the app loads.
- **Other light.** Only July at midday, at one latitude, was measured. Night,
  October and January were looked at, not measured:
  - at night nothing glows; the trees stay moonlit silhouettes;
  - in October the aspen's and the oak's autumn colours show from a path, where
    V2.93 drew them black;
  - January is unchanged.

  A low sun was not looked at.
- **Frame rate.** The leaf light adds a few shader instructions to every leaf
  pixel. Not measured on a slow machine.
