## The stock lists

**What came in**: five lists, transcribed from the owner's screenshots into
`tools/stock_review/lists/` (one text file each, kept so a test can read them
back and anyone can check a transcription against its source): Arnica
Wildflowers on 3 May, 7 June and 30 August 2026 (24, 48 and 50 species matched)
and the Edmonton Native Plant Society's sales of 16 August and 12 September
2026 (38 and 15). `scripts/merge_stock.py --list` folds each in after checking
every name against the catalogue and the supplier against the pack's nurseries.

**The levels changed with the evidence** (`src/local_stock.py`). V3.25 built
Common / Sometimes / Seed only / Hard to find / Not checked on the assumption
that several nurseries would publish lists. The owner says there are so few
that "Common" cannot be earned, and that a plant on Arnica's list is simply
*available*. So the levels are now:

| Level | When | Edmonton natives |
|---|---|---|
| Available | on a nursery's or a sale's plant list | 70 |
| Seed only | listed only as seed | 0 |
| Hard to find | a restoration plant (the catalogue's `rare`, with its reason) no list carries | 22 |
| Not on a local list | nothing on file covers it | 158 |

The words on the plant page name the source and the months: *"Available:
Arnica Wildflowers (on its June and August 2026 lists)."* A plant no list
covers says *"Not on any Edmonton native-plant nursery list on file. It may
still be sold or grown to order: ask."* That is the honest line (P9): the
Society's sales are occasional and Arnica's lists are three snapshots, so
absence is not evidence. A restoration plant on a list is Available, because
the list is the evidence the owner asked for; one on no list is Hard to find.
The old tiers in a stock file read as the nearest level (common and sometimes as
available). The *Easy to buy* toggle keeps Available plants and, where no list
covers a plant, the ones not known to be seed only or rare, as before.

**The Society's name was wrong.** The pack, the nursery list, the plan and a
seed note said "Edmonton Native Plant Group"; the screenshots say Edmonton Native
Plant Society. Renamed everywhere; schema **v96** reseeds the nursery table.

**Names that needed a rule.** Nurseries label by binomial and by common name,
and the two disagree: Arnica's "Artemisia ludoviciana - Prairie Sagewort" is
*A. ludoviciana*, while the catalogue's Prairie Sagewort is *A. frigida*. So
`stock_lists.match` now lets a binomial win over a common name on the same line,
skips comment lines and reads bracketed common names. New aliases cover the
labels on these lists (Pasture Sage for *Artemisia frigida*, Low Milkweed for
*Asclepias ovalifolia*, Large Northern Aster for *Canadanthus modestus*,
*Helianthus subrhomboideus* for the catalogue's *H. pauciflorus*, and others).
They are my readings, not VASCAN lookups (no session reaches VASCAN), listed in
`stock_lists.ALIASES` so they can be checked.

**What could not be matched, left for the owner**: "pink windflower" on an ENPS
list (perhaps Cut-leaved Anemone, *Pulsatilla multifida*; not guessed); three
species the catalogue does not hold (*Erigeron strigosus*, Prairie Fleabane;
*Boechera grahamii*, Graham's Rockcress; *Stellaria longipes*, Longstalk
Chickweed), which would need rows through `plants_pending_flora.json` and
VASCAN; and three Arnica sells that are not on the Edmonton list (*Potentilla
pensylvanica*, *Capnoides sempervirens*, *Viola pedatifida*), kept in the file
and never ranked, on the owner's V3.25 rule. Canada Violet was read as *Viola
rugulosa*, noted in the file.

The review page (link in `tools/stock_review/README.md`) was rebuilt for the new
levels and republished.

## Plants that lead (PR-1 to PR-4, PR-6, PR-9, ED-10)

**PR-1, `src/plant_priority.py`.** Impact, 0 to 11, from the records only
(decision L1: feasibility is words, not points): caterpillars in steps with a
keystone floor (a woody keystone genus 4, a herbaceous one 3, Tallamy's list),
a specialist 1, birds 0 to 2 (fruit or seed and shelter count; a hummingbird
nectar record counts once), bees at 5 / 15 / 40 species, and early or late
flowering for an insect-pollinated plant. Bands: **Top pick** at 5 or more,
**Good pick** at 3, **Little recorded** below (never "low": a grass has few
records because few people record grasses), **For a pond** for a plant that
needs standing water, **Not native here** for a plant off the region's list,
which goes last. A restoration plant no list carries is capped at Good pick.
Order: band, Impact, then availability (Available 2, seed 1), then name.

On the 250 Edmonton natives: **38 Top picks, 47 Good, 146 Little recorded, 19
for a pond**. The first twelve: Chokecherry, Joe-Pye Weed, Pussy Willow,
Sandbar Willow, Bebb's Willow, Fireweed, Paper Birch, Swamp Aster, Smooth Aster,
Stiff Goldenrod, Late Goldenrod, Many-flowered Aster. Chokecherry leads with its
cautions (L4). The reasons are sentences ("Caterpillar food for 15 kinds of
moth and butterfly", "Willows feed more kinds of caterpillar than almost any
other plant") and the cautions only what is recorded: nothing about spreading
while `spread_habit` is blank (L5), and "too big for a yard this size" only
against a measured outline (L3).

**PR-2, PR-9, ED-10.** *Best picks first* is the default order of the plant
list and of the community library (a community with a member off the local list
after every one without), and the pack's defaults are on: the local list ticked
in the simple app, its first-run goals preselected in Plan My Yard.

**PR-6.** The generator's offline pool, its palette and the review's repairs
rank by the same key (band first when a region applies, then Impact, then
availability), so what it plants first is what the list shows first. An
Edmonton pin's first fourteen are all on the Edmonton list and none needs
standing water (a test).

**PR-3, the rows.** In the simple app the zone and province badges give way to
the band's chip; its tooltip and the screen reader's text carry the reasons.
The first twenty rows of Best picks first are drawn four flowers, three shrubs
or trees, two grasses or sedges and one groundcover or vine per ten, each layer
in rank order, so the first screen looks like a garden: Joe-Pye Weed,
Chokecherry, Fireweed, Creeping Spikerush, Pussy Willow… A pond plant or one off
the list is never pulled forward. **Deliberately not built: the reason drawn
under each name.** Part 05 asked for two-line rows and flagged that they halve
the rows on a 1366 × 768 screen, "check on screen with the owner"; the rows stay
one line until that check, and the reason is one hover or one screen-reader
line away. Creeping Spikerush at row 4 is the doc's own questioned case (a marsh
plant winning the grass slot on one caterpillar record); it stays under L6.

**PR-4, the plant page in two modes** (`src/plant_page_sections.py`, Qt-free).
The simple page is the band, *Why this plant*, *Good to know* (recorded hazards,
and "Not checked for poison" when nothing is recorded), *Growing it* (sun and
water in words, size, the one spacing), the season bar, *Buying it* (the price
and the stock sentence) and *Native around Edmonton.* Chokecherry is 113 words,
Wild Bergamot 77; over the 250 natives 34 to 114, median 63. *Everything about
this plant ▸* opens the long page below it; with Advanced tools on the long
page is all there is. The long page is today's less the Notes, which no screen
shows (L8, P12: Wild Bergamot's read "Traditional cold/flu remedy"), under the
ID line. The word helpers moved from `species_page.py` into the new module, which
kept the page under its 720-line ceiling.

**WD-3 and WD-8, the words.** The page: "Choose a plant to read about it.",
"No photo yet.", "No animals recorded on it yet. That means nobody has written
it down, not that nothing uses it.", specialists "depending on it alone". The
filters: *Native to Alberta*, *Native around Edmonton*, *Easy to buy*, *Not
known to be poisonous to pets* / *to people* (they were *Pet safe* and *Child
safe*, which an unassessed plant passed), *Leaves out known spreaders*, *Food
for a fussy eater*, and Role is *What it does*. The vocabulary ratchet went down
by six. The ID line ("These photos help you choose plants. They are not a guide
to identifying plants in the wild.") is on the long page and in Help › About.
