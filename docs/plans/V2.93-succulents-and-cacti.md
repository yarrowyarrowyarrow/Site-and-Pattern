# V2.93 — Succulents and cacti

*One item: **F177**, the next family on the V2.87 audit's smallest-first list
after horsetails (V2.89) and the pond (V2.90). The owner said "Continue" after
V2.92, and this was offered as the next increment, with F186 (the dark crowns)
and F185 (water plants in ponds) named as the alternatives.*

## What was wrong

Six catalogue species are succulents. Rendered alone through the real viewer in
July (`build_scene` → `scene3d.html`, Balanced, the baked models loaded), all six
are drawn as something else:

| Species | Routed to | What it looked like |
|---|---|---|
| Ball Cactus (*Escobaria vivipara*) | herb layer, `succulent` → `mat` | a scatter of 5 mm scale leaves: from 4 m, nothing but its flowers |
| Brittle Prickly-pear (*Opuntia fragilis*) | groundcover mat | a star of narrow blades, because `scale` is in `_NARROW_SHAPES`; its flowers floating at 0.215 m over a 0.15 m plant (1.43x) |
| Plains Prickly Pear (*Opuntia polyacantha*) | groundcover mat | the same star, capped at the mat's 18 cm with its flowers at 24 cm |
| Roseroot (*Rhodiola integrifolia*) | herb layer, `succulent` → `mat` | a few specks: a 25 cm upright plant drawn as a mat of 3 cm leaves |
| Soapweed Yucca (*Yucca glauca*) | shrub layer, `branching: rosette` | a leafy bush in a dogwood's green, its flowers scattered inside it, no flower stalk |
| Lance-leaved Stonecrop (*Sedum lanceolatum*) | groundcover mat | the same star of narrow blades, its 2 cm leaves drawn about 4x too long |

The audit's F177 row says "five species", and **so did the first draft of this
plan**: it passed the stonecrop as "the groundcover mat draws exactly that". The
first before-and-after was unfair (a far-off tree camera for V2.92, a close one
for V2.93). Rendered again with V2.92 served from a scratch worktree through the
same camera, the stonecrop was plainly the prickly pears' star, since
`lanceolate` is a narrow outline too. So it is six.

## The rule

Same shape as the pond (V2.90): **decided once, in Python**, in a new
`src/succulent_habit.py`, as a `drawn` block that the viewer draws
(`html/scene3d/24-succulents.js`, new) and `scene_wildlife` perches on. Four
bodies:

| Body | Chosen by | Species |
|---|---|---|
| `pads` | `growth_form: pads` | both prickly pears |
| `ball` | `growth_form: globose` | Ball Cactus |
| `swords` | a shrub recorded `branching: rosette` with narrow leaves | Soapweed Yucca |
| `fleshy` | `growth_form: succulent`; on a groundcover, over a mat | Roseroot, Lance-leaved Stonecrop |

**Two new growth forms, schema v91.** `pads` (jointed flat stems) and
`globose` (a spiny ball) are in:
- `data_quality.GROWTH_FORMS`;
- the morphology seeder's genus table (the seeder rewrote exactly three lines of
  `plants_master.json`, checked by a dry run first);
- the two alias tables, `03-herbs.js _FORM_ALIAS` and
  `conventions.HERB_FORM_ALIAS`, both to `mat`, so the baked variant the
  generator resolves for these rows is unchanged.

They are keyed on the record, not on the genus name, as `jointed` was in V2.89:
the record is the thing a future species gets right or wrong.

**The yucca gets no growth form.** `growth_form` is the herbaceous habit and
`branching` the woody one, "one or the other, not both" (`data_quality`), and a
yucca is catalogued as a shrub. Its `branching: rosette` with linear basal
leaves already says what it is, and it is the only shrub recorded that way.

## What each body is drawn from

Each line is either **recorded** (read from the row) or a **drawing choice**
(said here so it can be argued with). Sizes are as measured from the built
meshes.

**Pads.** Flattened obovate joints in chains: a first joint leaning low, one or
two standing on its rim, fewer again on theirs.
- Height: recorded, to the top of the flowers (V2.88's rule).
- Joint length: a drawing choice. It is the height over 2.5, or less if the
  tallest chain needs less to put its flowers at the recorded height. That gives
  Brittle Prickly-pear 4.4 cm (floras: joints 2–5 cm) and the plains species
  10.4 cm (5–15 cm).
- Thickness: a drawing choice. Small joints are plumper (thickness over length
  0.45 against 0.19), the one visible difference the two records can carry;
  *O. fragilis*'s joints are nearly round in section.
- How many: enough to cover about a third of the spread, at most 80 small joints
  or 50 pads (80 and 37 here).
- Chains lean away from the middle more often than not. Leaning all one way made
  a wreath round a bare hole.
- Spines: one pale spine per areole, 28 areoles a joint. Not recorded per
  species; pale straw, as the bark default is a generic brown.
- Flowers: on the rims of joints with nothing growing from them, never on one
  lying on the ground.

**Ball.** Globes of tubercles on a phyllotaxis spiral, each tipped with a star
of nine pale radial spines and one dark central.
- Height: recorded. The tallest globe is 85% of it (12.8 cm), and the flowers
  reach the rest.
- Count: a drawing choice, from growth. One at planting, five at maturity
  (8.3–12.8 cm); floras give "solitary or clustered".
- Flowers: in a ring round the crown of each globe, held clear of the spines.

**Swords.** Rosettes of stiff, straight, channelled leaves radiating from the
ground, flat (the oldest) to upright (the youngest).
- Leaves: at the recorded length (29–52 cm drawn against 50 cm recorded), 75 a
  rosette at that length.
- Rosettes and stalks: one per recorded flowering stem (four), each stalk rising
  to the recorded height (0.90 m).
- Stalk season: from the first bloom month to the last fruit month (June to
  September). Green in bloom, straw after it, none in January.
- Colour: blue-grey. It is the species' name and its recorded `glaucous`
  surface. As the shrub default it had been a dogwood's green, so `yucca` joins
  the scene's per-genus foliage table beside spruce and willow.
- Flowers: the recorded panicle ends at the top of each stalk.

**Fleshy.** Upright stems crowded with fleshy leaves, the flowers on top.
- The leaf is the pad's lens made small, broadest above the middle, and lit as
  a solid. A flat leaf card read as a dark blade from every side facing away from
  the sun: roseroot came out as bamboo.
- Roseroot: four stems (recorded) from one crown, 24 leaves each (1.7–3.3 cm
  drawn, 3 cm recorded), the corymb at each top. Herbaceous, so it takes the herb
  layer's winter colour.
- Stonecrop: eight flowering stems (recorded) standing over a mat of 24 short
  sprawling shoots.
  - The flowering stems die after seeding, so they stand only in the stalk season
    (June and July: no fruit period is recorded). The mat stays all year.
  - Out of season the stems are still generated, into a sink, so the mat does
    not move when the month does.

**The flowers sit on the body.** The floret, billboard and fruit layers already
asked `vineAnchorsFor(p)` where a vine's blooms are (V2.89). They now also ask
`succulentAnchorsFor(p)`. A succulent's anchors carry `top: true`, meaning the
anchor is where the bloom's top is, not the leaf it rises from:
- a solitary flower sits exactly there;
- a cluster ends there;
- in Stylised, a billboard card held upright hangs below it and one lying
  face-up sits on it (the drop scales with the sine of the card's tilt). A flat
  drop sank the prickly pears' daisies into the ground.

Without the flag a 6 cm prickly-pear flower sits 6.6 cm above its pad, because a
vine's solitary bloom is held on a short stalk above its leaf.

**The animals.** `scene_wildlife._drawn_height` capped every groundcover at the
mat's 18 cm. A drawn block's height now wins, as it already did in the viewer's
`bodyHeightOf`. Without that, a bee on the 30 cm plains prickly pear perched
inside it.

## A vine beside a yucca (found on the way)

The yucca is filed as a shrub, so V2.89's rule let a vine climb it. The shrub
layer used to tell the vines where a shrub's crown was, and the yucca is no
longer drawn by it. A clematis beside one fell back to climbing a dome the size
of the yucca, and arched over it like a cage.

That was worse than a drawing problem. F181's generator seats generated vines at
the foot of trees and shrubs, so a design could advise planting a clematis to
climb a 50 cm rosette of stiff, sharp leaves.

The owner's words were *"Vines should climb adjacent plant but only if it is a
tree or shrub"*. "Only if" is a limit, not a promise that every shrub holds a
vine. So `vine_habit.holds_vines` now answers "a tree or shrub with a crown":
- one test, asked by the scene (`can_hold`) and by both of the generator's
  seating callers (`llm_design`, `open_ground`);
- it refuses a shrub recorded `branching: rosette`, which in this catalogue is
  the yucca and nothing else (a test says so);
- a vine beside a yucca now lies on the ground, V2.89's "nothing to climb" body.

**The owner can reverse this**, by emptying `vine_habit.NOT_A_CROWN`.

## What it costs

The bodies are procedural and per plant, like the pond's and the vines', not
baked archetypes shared across plants. The first build drew them far too
finely. The baked budgets are 1,200 triangles for a herb, 1,600 for a
groundcover and 3,600 for a shrub; a climbing vine reaches about 15,000.

| Plant | First build | Shipped |
|---|---|---|
| Lance-leaved Stonecrop | 97,000 | 4,800 |
| Brittle Prickly-pear | 65,000 | 6,100 |
| Roseroot | 31,000 | 2,400 |
| Plains Prickly Pear | 28,000 | 4,000 |
| Soapweed Yucca | 10,000 | 6,000 |
| Ball Cactus | 9,200 | 4,800 |

The cuts were:
- a coarser lens, and a coarser still one for small joints and fleshy leaves;
- one spine per areole;
- fewer joints, leaves and shoots.

Every body was re-rendered after the cuts. The brittle prickly-pear went sparse
at 50 joints and was given 80 coarser ones, which cost almost nothing.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **Fruit.** None of the six records a fruit colour, and nothing recorded draws
  nothing (P9). The fruit layer asks for the anchors anyway, so a red
  prickly-pear fruit recorded later lands on the pad rims.
- **Winter shrivel.** Prickly-pear pads wrinkle and go purplish over winter.
  True, but not recorded; they take the evergreen seasonal colour.
- **Roseroot's colour.** Its hex (`#8e6fc4`) reads lavender, and the species'
  flowers are a dark red-purple. The flora's word is only "purple", so the bucket
  is right and the shade is a guess. Added to F172, which needs a source for it.
- **The yucca's bells.** They hang. The floret layer holds a panicle's florets
  facing out and up, as it does for every panicle: a question of flower
  attitude, which belongs to F180, not of the body.
- **How many flowers.** `flowering_stems: 4` gives each cactus four blooms; a
  prickly pear in full bloom carries a dozen. Also F180.
- **Other short plants with big flowers.** Of the 69 other solitary- or
  head-flowered species, only Prairie Crocus can put a flower above 1.15x its
  height (1.28x at most). The prickly pears were the worst case, and they now
  carry their flowers on their rims.
- **Glochids**, the fine barbed bristles: invisible at yard scale.
- **Spread offspring (F35).** Not drawn for these bodies; none of the six
  records a spread rate.

## How it is checked

`tests/test_succulent_habit.py` (23 tests, no browser):
- every succulent row gets its body from the real catalogue, and no other row
  gets one;
- the counts, heights and stalk season the block carries;
- the scene carries each body, and the yucca is blue-grey;
- the yucca is the one tree or shrub that holds no vine;
- the drawn height reaching the animals;
- the viewer and Python agreeing on the body names, the ball's width, the chunk
  loading and the anchor hook;
- the data, the vocabulary, the seeder and the schema bump.

`tests/test_accuracy_render.py` (7 new, headless Chromium):
- the prickly pears are pads with spines, their flowers on them and not above
  1.15x the plant;
- the ball cactus is its recorded number of globes, flowers at the crown;
- the yucca is swords at their recorded length with stalks at the recorded
  height, its flowers above the leaves;
- the yucca has no stalk in January;
- roseroot is leafy stems with the corymb on top;
- the stonecrop has a mat under its flowering stems.

`tests/test_vine_habit.py` (1 new): a rosette shrub has no crown to climb.

## Results

- **Against V2.92**, run from a scratch worktree with this branch's Python and
  tests:
  - the seven new render checks all fail ("no spiny globes", "drawn without its
    pads and spines", ...), and the 18 older ones still pass;
  - the no-browser module reports six failures and two errors (the chunk,
    anchor hook and alias checks).
- **Here**: the render probe passes 25 of 25; the succulent, vine, seating,
  open-ground, scene-contract, wildlife, data-quality, model-asset, sprite and
  guard modules all pass; `validate-data` is clean.
- **Full suite**: *see below, filled in after the run.*

  Stylised) is in the session, not the repo. No plan has committed an image,

## What could not be verified here

- **Against photographs.** The shapes were judged against floras' descriptions
  and memory of the plants, not side by side with pictures.
- **On a real desktop GPU.** Every render here is headless Chromium on the
  SwiftShader software renderer, the same viewer files the app loads.
- **A design full of cacti.** Frame rate with many of them in one scene was not
  measured; the budgets above are per plant.
