# V2.82 — the harebells were not swapped

*A report that two common names had been exchanged, which turned out to be one
outdated binomial; and the audit that followed — eight more binomials corrected,
five of them publishing the same plant twice, and eight common names that were
simply wrong.*

(Carried on the `V2.80` branch, as V2.81 was.)

## The report

Two cards, side by side in the species list:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Alaska Harebell** | *Campanula lasiocarpa* |
| **Harebell** | *Campanula alaskana* |

> "The common name for the harebells have been misapplied."

Read as a reader reads it, that is obviously right: the plant whose epithet is
*alaskana* is the one **not** called Alaska Harebell. But the names are not
swapped, and swapping them — the fix the cards invite — would have made both
wrong.

*Campanula lasiocarpa* really is Alaska harebell. The error is on the other row:
**`Campanula alaskana` is not a name the flora accepts.** The catalogue's own
VASCAN extract says so and has since V2.80:

```json
"Campanula alaskana": {
  "accepted_name": "Campanula rotundifolia Linnaeus",
  "taxon_rank": "species",
  "native_provinces": "AB,SK", "verdict": "confirm"
}
```

*Campanula rotundifolia* is harebell — the one the row's own notes call *"one of
Alberta's most iconic and widespread wildflowers"*. Filed under a synonym, it
sat beside a genuine *Campanula* from Alaska and read as a mix-up. So the fix is
one rename, not two renames, and the pair then reads correctly with both common
names left exactly as they were.

**The generalisable part: a wrong scientific name is invisible until it is next
to a similar one.** Nobody reads *Campanula alaskana* and objects. Put it beside
*Campanula lasiocarpa* and it produces a bug report about something else
entirely. The site is a browse-by-name catalogue, so every synonym in it is one
page away from looking like this.

## So the audit asked the same question of all 422 rows

The archive was already on disk with an `accepted_name` for every species, and
nothing had ever compared it to the name the row is filed under. Doing that
takes six lines and finds **26 rows whose binomial is not VASCAN's accepted
name**. They are not one problem:

| What it is | Count | Done here |
|---|---|---|
| A synonym or infrataxon of a name **already in the catalogue** — two pages, one plant | 5 | merged |
| A rank VASCAN does not carry, with nothing to collide with | 2 | renamed |
| Filed under a synonym, no clash | 1 (*Campanula*) | renamed |
| A recent recombination the regional floras have not adopted | 18 | **left alone**, allowlisted |

The middle row is the uncomfortable one. **Four of V2.80's eight renames landed
on names the same archive does not accept**, because `--suggest` printed the
accepted taxon *with its authority* and the infraspecific epithet was read off
the wrong end of it:

| V2.80 renamed | to | the archive's accepted name | rank matched |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Stachys palustris* | *Stachys pilosa* var. *pilosa* | *Stachys pilosa* | species |
| *Urtica dioica* | *Urtica gracilis* subsp. *gracilis* | *Urtica gracilis* | species |
| *Oenothera caespitosa* | *Oenothera cespitosa* subsp. *cespitosa* | *Oenothera cespitosa* | species |
| *Deschampsia caespitosa* | *Deschampsia cespitosa* subsp. *cespitosa* | *Deschampsia cespitosa* | species |

`taxon_rank: species` on all four is the tell: the lookup did not match a
subspecies and fall back, there is no such taxon in the checklist to match. And
two of the four landed on a species **the catalogue already carried**, which is
how V2.80 shipped *Urtica gracilis* twice — once as Slender Nettle and once, one
day later, as Stinging Nettle.

## The five duplicate pairs

Each of these published two species pages, two ranges and two sets of
relationships for one plant.

| Retired row | Survivor | Surviving common name |
|---|---|---|
| *Stachys pilosa* var. *pilosa* — Marsh Hedge Nettle (Marsh Woundwort) | *Stachys pilosa* | Marsh Hedge Nettle |
| *Urtica gracilis* subsp. *gracilis* — Stinging Nettle | *Urtica gracilis* | Stinging Nettle |
| *Spiraea alba* var. *latifolia* — Pink Spirea (Rose Meadowsweet) | *Spiraea alba* | Meadowsweet |
| *Dodecatheon pulchellum* — Shooting Star | *Primula pauciflora* | Saline Shooting Star |
| *Hedysarum alpinum* — Sweet Broom (Alpine Sweetvetch) | *Hedysarum americanum* | Alpine Hedysarum (Bear Root) |

Three of the five had **already been diagnosed by hand, in the notes field of
the row itself**, and left as a warning rather than a fix:

> *"NOTE: 'Marsh Hedge Nettle' and 'Marsh Woundwort' are two common names for
> the same species…"*
> *"NOTE: 'Pink Spirea' is ambiguous…"*
> *"NOTE: 'Sweet Broom' is ambiguous — Carmichaelia is a New Zealand genus…"*

And *Spiraea alba* var. *latifolia* had got as far as
`seed_flower_colour.KEEP`, which allowlists its colour audit trip with the
reason *"common name says pink, epithet says white, and the plant is white to
palest pink"*. A row whose common name has to be excused in one file and
apologised for in another is not a row with a colour problem.

**Which name survives is a call, not a lookup** (the rule `merge_duplicate_species.py`
already states). The rule used here: **the surviving row keeps the common name
already published at a URL somebody may have linked, unless that name is the
wrong one.** *Shooting Star* is the exception — it is retired in favour of
*Saline Shooting Star* because the bare name is the ambiguous one and the
catalogue already distinguishes *Desert Shooting Star* (*Primula conjugens*)
beside it.

*Dodecatheon pulchellum* and *Primula pauciflora* carried **identical** derived
data — 116 range cells and 11 ecoregions each — which is what one species
indexed under two names looks like from GBIF, and is the strongest single piece
of evidence in this audit.

## The seven common names that were actually wrong

The report was about a common name, so the audit went looking for those too,
by the three signals available offline: a name that collides with another row's,
a name that contradicts its own row's data, and a name that is misspelled.

| Row | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Oxytropis borealis* | Boreale Oxytropis | **Boreal Oxytropis** | typo |
| *Penstemon lyallii* | Lyalls Penstemon | **Lyall's Penstemon** | missing apostrophe; every other possessive in the catalogue has one |
| *Lithospermum ruderale* | Yellow Pucoon | **Yellow Puccoon** | misspelling |
| *Antennaria microphylla* | Rosy Pussytoes (Littleleaf Pussytoes) | **Littleleaf Pussytoes** | "Rosy Pussytoes" is *A. rosea*, which this catalogue carries as Pink Pussytoes — and `sprite_gallery.py` had already bound the name to *A. rosea*, independently |
| *Physostegia ledinghamii* | False Dragonhead | **Ledingham's False Dragonhead** | same lead name as *P. parviflora* |
| *Drymocallis arguta* | Prairie Cinquefoil (Tall Cinquefoil) | **Tall Cinquefoil** | "Prairie Cinquefoil" is *Potentilla pensylvanica*, two rows down |
| *Anticlea elegans* | White Camas | **Mountain Death Camas (White Camas)** | see below |

**The death camas is the one that matters.** Its own notes read *"TOXIC — ALL
PARTS … it closely resembles edible blue camas"*, and its `safety_source` names
zygacine as *"often fatal"*. The name on the card said **White Camas**. The
entire historical hazard with this plant is being mistaken for an edible camas,
and a catalogue that leads with the resemblance and buries the poison in a field
the website withholds by default is doing the confusing itself. The accepted
English name says it in two words, so the card says it in two words.

*Antennaria microphylla*'s note — *"taxonomically close to Pink Pussytoes
(Antennaria rosea) — some sources treat them as synonyms"* — is the same
pattern as the nettles and the spireas: the confusion was known, written down
beside the data, and not resolved in it.

## Tooling, and the papercut it closed

Three scripts, because all three operations were being done by hand or not at
all:

* **`scripts/rename_common_name.py` (new).** A common name is a foreign key in
  this repository — `plant_fauna_master.json` joins on it, and the seeded
  communities in `src/db/polycultures.py` name their members as literal strings
  — so a rename is never one string. Reports by default, re-points the edges on
  `--apply`, prints the URL that moves, and prints the Python references as a
  checklist rather than rewriting source it does not understand (the rule
  `merge_duplicate_species.py` set after a merge broke three communities).

* **`scripts/remove_taxon.py --merge-into` now merges.** It used to delete the
  row and re-point the edges, which loses every field the retired row knew and
  the other did not: the nettle pair alone disagreed on 21 columns, and the
  retired row held the only photograph on two of the five pairs. It now
  field-merges through `merge_duplicate_species.merge_rows` (unioned tag
  columns, joined notes, conflicts printed), keeps the **larger** derived entry
  under the surviving name, **unions the raw point cache** so the next
  re-derivation gets both record sets, and de-duplicates the re-pointed edges.
  `removed_in` also stopped being the hardcoded string `"V2.80"`.

* **`src/taxon_names.py` (new) owns the comparison**, because both the rename
  tool and the gate below need the same reading of a name, and a gate importing
  its definition of correctness from a dev script has the dependency backwards.
  An authority string is not a grammar and cannot be parsed as one, but it has
  one reliable signal: a capital letter or an open bracket after the epithet
  starts the authority. That is the whole module, and it is the thing four
  renames got wrong by eye.

* **`scripts/rename_taxon.py` no longer throws away a sourced nativity.** It
  cleared `native_provinces_source` on purpose — the right call when the new
  name is a guess — and V2.80 accepted that eight species would read *unsourced*
  until the author re-ran the archive. But when the new name is **the accepted
  name the archive itself resolved the old one to**, the province list is not a
  transcription: it is that record, whose `accepted_name` field says which taxon
  it describes. So the rename re-keys the record in
  `data/fetched/flora_nativity.json` and keeps the source, and says that it did.
  The clearing behaviour is unchanged for every other rename.

## The audit is now a gate

A one-off audit finds this once. Two checks in `data_quality.validate_all`
find it every time, and the weights differ on purpose:

* **`validate_accepted_names`** — an accepted name that is *another row in this
  catalogue* is an error with no allowlist, because that is one plant on two
  pages. An accepted name that merely differs is an error unless the row is in
  `KNOWN_NOMENCLATURE` **with a reason**, which is where the eighteen live. A
  further warning fires when an allowlist entry names a row that no longer
  exists, on the V2.80 lesson about an exclusion outliving the thing it
  excused.
* **`validate_common_name_collisions`** — two rows leading with the same name,
  compared on the **lead** name, since a trailing parenthetical alias hid all
  three real cases from the exact-match duplicate check that already existed.
  An error rather than a warning because `_unique_slugs` resolves a collision
  by appending the scientific name, so the day a collision appears a published
  page moves.

Neither check is new information — both were computable from files already in
the repository in V2.80. That is the whole argument for them.

## What was deliberately not done

**The eighteen recombinations.** *Galium boreale* → *Trichogalium boreale*,
*Spartina* → *Sporobolus* (three species), *Ledum groenlandicum* →
*Rhododendron groenlandicum*, *Potentilla anserina* → *Argentina anserina*,
*Polygala senega* → *Senega officinalis*, *Escobaria vivipara* → *Pelecyphora
vivipara*, *Matteuccia struthiopteris* → *M. pensylvanica*, and ten more. VASCAN
accepts these; Moss's *Flora of Alberta*, every nursery tag and every reader
searching for them does not. That is a decision about **which nomenclature the
site follows**, which is the author's to make and is worth making once for all
eighteen rather than piecemeal. They are listed with their reasons in
`data_quality.KNOWN_NOMENCLATURE`, which is where the gate reads them from. None of them is misapplied — the common name and the binomial on
those rows agree about which plant it is, which is the difference between this
list and everything fixed above.

**Four names that need a flora, not an archive**, and are recorded here rather
than guessed at:

* *Carex rostrata* "Beaked Sedge" and *Carex utriculata* "Small Bottle Sedge"
  may be **exchanged** — the two names have been swapped between these species
  in different references for a century, and *C. utriculata* is the larger
  plant, which makes "Small Bottle Sedge" doubly suspect. The catalogue's own
  data is no help: the *utriculata* row is the only sedge filed as `aquatic`
  with a white flower colour, so it was authored by a different hand.
* *Eriocoma hymenoides* is published as **"Indigenous Ricegrass"**. No flora
  uses that name; VASCAN's is "Indian ricegrass". Renaming a species to
  *Indigenous* — rather than declining to use a dated name, which is a
  defensible thing to do — reads as attributing the plant to peoples who have
  not been asked about it, which is P12 territory and the author's call.
* *Acorus americanus* is published as **"Rat Root"** (VASCAN: American sweet
  flag), and *Hedysarum americanum* as **"Alpine Hedysarum (Bear Root)"**. Both
  are the names the plants are known by here, and both are names carried into
  English from Indigenous use. Kept as they are, flagged once: P12 says point
  toward that knowledge rather than operationalise it, and a common name on a
  public page is the most operational field there is.
* *Viburnum opulus* "Highbush Cranberry" is filed under the **bare species**,
  which in VASCAN covers both the native subsp. *trilobum* and the introduced
  European guelder-rose. The row publishes a native claim that the name it uses
  does not support; the fix is `var. americanum`, which is a rename with a
  nativity re-run behind it.

**No re-derivation of the occurrence data.** Merging unions the raw cache but
the three derived files keep the larger entry, because re-running
`seed_ecoregion_ranges.py`, `seed_species_ranges.py` and
`seed_occurrence_points.py` would rewrite 4 MB of committed V2.81 output for
five species, and V2.81's boundary margin is the reason that output is what it
is. The next re-derivation picks the union up for free.

## How it went

* Catalogue **422 rows → 417**; the built site **422 → 417 species pages**,
  1,121 wildlife pages, no dead links.
* 26 archive mismatches → **18, every one allowlisted with a reason** and none
  of them a misapplication.
* Edges **7,225 → 7,210**: 15 re-pointed records were already on the surviving
  name and were dropped as exact duplicates. V2.80's yarrow merge shipped 135
  of those before anyone noticed, which is what the de-duplication is for.
* Nativity source **preserved on all three scientific renames** rather than
  cleared, so no
  species went back to reading *Not established* while waiting for an archive
  re-run the author has to be at a machine to do.
* `validate-data`: clean before, clean after — 114 warnings → 113, the one that
  went being *Spiraea alba* var. *latifolia* asserting nativity with no
  occurrence record of any kind behind it. It had no records because it was
  never a taxon.
* **Twelve public addresses stop resolving** and nothing in the build issues a
  redirect: the seven corrected common names, four merged-away pages, and
  `/plants/slender-nettle/`, which is now `/plants/stinging-nettle/` — the one
  slug that changed *rows* rather than disappearing. `/plants/harebell/` and
  `/plants/alaska-harebell/` are both untouched, which is the point: the
  reported bug needed no common name changed at all. Worth knowing before the
  next publish; the site is young and every one of these is the address of a
  name that was wrong.
* 14 new tests in `tests/test_taxon_names.py`, and the two gates run inside
  `validate-data`.
* Four existing tests updated, each because the data legitimately moved and the
  test was holding a snapshot of it: the flower-colour histogram (five rows
  gone, five decrements, every one of them accounted for in the comment), the
  specialist-host list (*Slender Nettle* → *Stinging Nettle*), and
  `test_model_assets`' floor, which was set **two** rows under the count it was
  guarding and so failed on a legitimate removal. It is a floor against the
  lookup going empty, and it now sits where that is the only thing it can
  catch.

### A red test found on the way past

`test_nativity_source_column.test_an_unsourced_species_still_names_its_heuristic`
has been failing with a `KeyError` since V2.80 — **not from this work**. It
pins to a species to prove an unsourced row still shows its heuristic, and the
increment that re-pinned it to *Spiraea douglasii* is the same increment that
**removed *Spiraea douglasii* from the catalogue**. It went unnoticed because
the suite cannot reach its own summary in a container without PyQt6, where 13
tests error on the import.

Fixed by not pinning to the data at all: the row under test is synthetic now,
because what is under test is `provenance`, and an assertion about a fixed
input cannot be invalidated by the catalogue improving. The claim it was
*trying* to make now has its own test, asserted over every row: **no published
species is unsourced any more** — 20 when the column shipped, zero after
V2.80's renames and this increment's.

### One thing to watch

`merge_rows` unions `native_provinces`, which is a **sourced** column. It was
harmless here — every survivor's union matches what the archive says about the
accepted taxon, *Hedysarum americanum*'s AB,SK included — but a future merge
of an unsourced row into a sourced one would widen a sourced claim with
unsourced data and nothing would say so. The gate has no check for that yet.

### Two papercuts found by using the tools

**A second rename was erasing the first.** `renamed_from` is a single string
and `rename_taxon.py` overwrote it, so renaming *Oenothera cespitosa* subsp.
*cespitosa* would have dropped *Oenothera caespitosa* — the misspelling a reader
is most likely to search for, and the only reason the field exists. It appends
now, and the two rows it already cost were repaired by hand.

**Re-keying a sorted file appended.** Every one of the five scientific-name-keyed
files is written in name order, and `pop`-then-assign drops the renamed species
at the bottom: a two-line change rendered as a 208-line diff, and the next
rename of the same row rendering as another one. `_rekey` reads sortedness
*before* it mutates.
