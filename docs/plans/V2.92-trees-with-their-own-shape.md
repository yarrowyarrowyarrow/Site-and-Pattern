# V2.92 — Trees with their own shape

*The owner's choice from the audit's remaining batches: **F178**, "trees with
their own shape". The V2.87 audit listed eleven trees drawn as something they
are not. This increment takes ten of them; the eleventh, Black Spruce, is drawn
like White Spruce on purpose (below).*

## What the audit found, and what a render showed

The viewer picks a tree's baked model from two tables in
`html/scene3d/02-plants.js`: a species table (`TREE_SPECIES_PROFILES`, five
rows since V2.33) and a genus table (`TREE_PROFILES`). A tree in neither gets a
generic default chosen by its proportions. The model is then scaled to the
species' own canopy and height, so a borrowed model is also a **stretched** one.

| Tree | Recorded | Drawn as | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| American Elm | 25 m, decurrent, elliptic 12 cm leaf | generic oval default | — |
| Manitoba Maple | 15 m, decurrent, **compound** 15 cm leaf | generic default, simple leaves | — |
| Plains Cottonwood | 25 m × 15 m, ovate 9 cm | the trembling aspen (round leaf, narrow crown) | **1.6x** sideways |
| Narrowleaf Cottonwood | 15 m × 9 m, **lanceolate** 8 cm | the trembling aspen | **1.6x** |
| Peach-leaved Willow | 12 m × 9 m, lanceolate 10 cm | Bebb's willow (8 m, elliptic) | **1.35x** |
| Water Birch | 8 m, **multi_stem** | its own archetype, single trunk | — |
| Bebb's Willow | 8 m, **multi_stem** | the willow archetype, single trunk | — |
| Rocky Mountain Juniper | 8 m × 4.5 m, scale leaves | the default conifer, a spruce's tiers | **1.7x** |
| Lodgepole Pine | 25 m × 6 m, excurrent | needle tufts evenly up a pole | — |
| Jack Pine | 15 m × 4.5 m, decurrent | the same, shorter | — |

Rendered through the real viewer (July and January, side-on), every deciduous
tree was the same figure: one trunk forking into a Y under a small dark crown,
and in winter a Y with four to eight tips. The two cottonwoods were pixel-alike.
The pines were bristled poles. The juniper was a spruce widened to a juniper's
footprint.

**Why the multi-stemmed trees had one trunk.** The generator's deciduous
builder (`assetlib/flora_trees._decid_skeleton`) grew the whole tree from a
single walk, so no archetype could have two stems. `branching: multi_stem` had
been read since V2.29 but only picked a broad crown *form*.

**Why the pines were bottle brushes.** `_build_pine` spread 11 to 44 needle
tufts evenly from mid-height to the top, one to a height, each on its own short
stub. Seen from a yard that is a cylinder of bristles: no limbs, no gaps, no
crown.

## What was built

**Same mechanism as V2.33: species rows and baked archetypes.** Six new
archetypes, keyed in the viewer's species table, and four rebuilt. Each is
authored at its species' recorded height ÷ canopy (`conventions.CROWN_ASPECT`),
with its recorded leaf outline and length (`DECID_LEAF_SHAPE`, `LEAF_CM`), which
removes the three stretches.

| Archetype | For | Built as |
|---|---|---|
| `elm` (new) | American Elm | a short trunk forking at about a seventh of its height into four or five leaders that keep climbing and diverge, drooping twigs: the vase, widest near the top |
| `boxelder` (new) | Manitoba Maple | two or three long trunks from the ground, leaning apart, under a broad crown of three-leaflet compound leaves |
| `cottonwood` (new) | Plains Cottonwood | a thick trunk dividing into three or four heavy ascending limbs, a broad open crown, ovate leaves |
| `cottonwood_narrow` (new) | Narrowleaf Cottonwood | a slender trunk, three upright leaders, **lanceolate** leaves |
| `willow_peach` (new) | Peach-leaved Willow | one or two leaning trunks, a broad irregular crown with a drooping fringe, lanceolate leaves |
| `juniper` (new) | Rocky Mountain Juniper | a dense, lumpy cone foliated to the ground, no tiers |
| `birch_water` (rebuilt) | Water Birch | four to six slender stems from the base, fanning out |
| `willow` (rebuilt) | Bebb's Willow | three to five stems from the base, ascending |
| `pine` (rebuilt) | Lodgepole Pine | a long straight bole with a few dead stubs, then whorls of near-level limbs each ending in a cluster of tufts, sky between the whorls, a rounded top |
| `pine_jack` (rebuilt) | Jack Pine | a crooked trunk, irregular kinked limbs with clumped foliage, a flattish top, eight dead stubs kept below the crown |

**What is read from the catalogue and what is authored.** The leaf, the
proportions and `multi_stem` are recorded. The *architecture* (the elm's vase,
the cottonwood's heavy limbs, the pine's whorls, the box elder's several trunks)
is authored per archetype from the species' standard description, which is what
V2.33 did for jack pine's scraggly crown and water birch's clump. No seed data
changes, so no schema bump. Box elder's catalogue row says `decurrent`, not
`multi_stem`; its several trunks are the authored part, and its three leaflets
are the bottom of the recorded three-to-seven.

### The generator's new knobs

All in `flora_trees.DECID_GENERA`, all optional, **all off by default**, so the
fifteen other trees rebuild byte-identical (checked: see Verification).

* `stems` (lo, hi) and `stem_lean`: how many trunks leave the ground, each
  leaning out on its own bearing and carrying its share of the trunk's
  cross-section.
* `leaders`: the trunk's first fork, `{"n", "angle", "length", "area"}`, where
  the rest of the tree forks in twos and threes.
* `rise`: how strongly each later branch turns back toward the vertical. The
  walk had no sense of up, so after three forks a branch pointed anywhere and
  every limb ended in a ball; the first elm, built with leaders alone, was
  widest in its lower crown. With `rise` 0.8 its tips sit in the top two fifths
  and it is widest near the top, which is the vase.
* `twig_r`: scales the thinnest limb that still forks. The elm takes more,
  finer ends (0.6); the box elder fewer (1.6), because its bark was a third of
  its triangles.
* `leaflet_pairs` and `leaflet_len`: a compound leaf's leaflet count and size.
  At the outline's default (seven leaflets, each 30% of the leaf) a box elder
  leaf cost eight simple ones and covered a fifth of the ground one does, and the
  crown came out as specks on bare twigs. Three leaflets at half the leaf's
  length is the species, and fills. `mesh_ops.add_compound_leaf` and
  `leaf_extent` take the override; every other caller passes nothing and is
  unchanged.
* `_FORM_OVERRIDES`: an archetype may set its own branch angle, length scaling,
  clear bole, split bias and bole over its form's.

**A skeleton must be about one unit tall when it is built.** Card length and
trunk radius are specified as fractions of the tree's height, but applied
before the tree is scaled to height 1. The first box elder forked at 0.13 and
its natural skeleton was 0.33 tall, so everything the conventions sized came out
three times too big: 1.3 m leaves and a trunk a ninth of the tree's height. Each
new archetype's bole is chosen so its natural height is 0.9 to 1.4. (The older
trees are not all like that; see "Found and left alone".)

### The pines: a new builder

Rebuilt, not retuned, because the fault was structural. `_build_pine` now lays a
trunk polyline, whorls of limbs up the live crown (`PINE_KINDS`: crown base,
whorls, limbs per whorl, tilt, reach profile, crook, cluster, stubs, top tufts,
trunk radius), clusters of tufts along each limb's outer end, and dead stubs on
the bole. Three things it took:

* **A tuft is a mass with a fringe.** A small faceted core (20 triangles) with
  ten needle sprays rooted on its surface, fanned about the limb's bearing and
  close to level, so a whorl reads as a tier. The old tuft was eighteen needles
  from one point, a third of them straight up.
* **Limbs are laid out round a straight axis and carried to the trunk after.**
  The aspect solve narrows everything toward the axis, not toward the trunk, so
  built on a crooked trunk every limb's base was pulled off the wood it grows
  from, and the jack pine's stubs were squeezed with the crown into 15 cm nubs.
  Stubs now stay out of the solve and keep their length (0.6 to 1.4 m on a
  15 m jack pine).
* **Trunks at their real girth**: 0.014 and 0.019 of the height, where the old
  0.036 drew a 25 m lodgepole on a 1.8 m trunk.

### The juniper: its own builder

`_build_juniper`: one leader, 26 to 60 short branches at irregular heights and
bearings (more of them low, where a cone's surface is), spreading at the bottom
and ascending at the top, each carrying two tufts on its outer half. A tuft is
the pine's core with five short scale-leaf sprays. It took four builds:

1. Long sprays and tufts filling the core: **a black column**, every spray
   shading every other.
2. The core emptied, sprays shorter: a lumpy column with a waist, leaning.
3. Many small branches instead of a few long ones, because the triangle budget
   drops whole branches when it thins, and dropping a few big ones had left the
   cone lopsided: **a cone**, widest at the ground.
4. Tufts finer toward the top (the youngest tree's big tufts squared off its
   apex into a column), and never below z = 0, because a core reaching under the
   ground made the unit frame lift the whole tree, and the youngest juniper's
   trunk stood on its own foliage.

### Stylised

Stylised draws trees with the viewer's own generator, on purpose. It learned
the same `stems`, `leaders` and `rise` (`decidCfg`, `generateClump`,
`generateDaVinciTree`, the `climb` in `treeToGeometry`), read from the same
`_PROF` rows, so the elm is a vase and water birch a clump there too. That took
`03-herbs.js` to 758 lines against its 700 ceiling, so the procedural trees
moved to a chunk of their own, `html/scene3d/03b-trees.js` (338 lines, ceiling
450), loaded straight after it; the herbs keep `normalizeUnit` and
`applyFoliageGradient`, which both halves use. The juniper's fallback is a cone of skirts: `tiersAdd` −2 had left an 8 m
juniper two skirts in Stylised (which draws 0.6x the count), one on the ground
and a speck atop a bare pole, and it is +4 now. **The pines in Stylised are
unchanged**: the fallback's pine builder draws both kinds alike, which V2.33
already recorded as the fallback's honest limit.

### Left alone, on purpose

- **Black Spruce stays White Spruce's model.** V2.33 declined to split them
  because the catalogue records the same proportions (3.3), and splitting would
  be a fabricated difference (P9). That still holds: its narrow club-topped
  spire is real but nothing in the catalogue records it. Drawing it apart means
  recording its crown form first, which is the owner's call.
- **Plains Cottonwood's leaf stays `ovate`.** It is deltoid, but the leaf-shape
  vocabulary has no deltoid. A vocabulary change for one species is a data
  decision, for F172.
- **The juniper's colour.** It renders dark green; the species is blue-green to
  silvery. Colour is catalogue data, and F172 already holds the colour list.
- **The other fifteen trees** are byte-identical to what shipped.

## Found and left alone

- **Crowns render dark from eye level: F186.** Seen side-on from a person's
  height, most leaf cards render near-black, on the new trees and the old
  alike, and the conifer tufts darkest. The audit judged its renders from
  above, where the crowns read green. Two causes, measured by rendering the
  same view with the bake switched off (every vertex colour white): the crowns
  come out lighter but still largely dark, so the larger part is how the viewer
  lights a leaf card seen from its shaded side, and the bake adds the rest. Not
  diagnosed further; numbered.
- **The shading bake's ground plane sits at the crown's lowest leaf.** Tree
  foliage is baked on its own, and `ao_bake` puts the floor at the lowest vertex
  of what it is given, so every downward ray inside a crown "hits the ground"
  metres above it. With the floor at z = 0 the elm's mean brightness goes 0.31 →
  0.57, a smaller change on screen than that suggests, and the fix rebuilds all
  twenty-five trees. Recorded in F186.
- **Crowns land narrower than their species.** The aspect solve measures reach
  radially; the manifest's half-width is the axis-aligned box. When a crown's
  widest point is off the axes the two differ, so trees land 5 to 23% narrower
  than recorded and the viewer stretches them back: the aspen 3.14 against 2.7
  since V2.33, and now Bebb's willow 2.21 against 1.8, the elm 1.95 against 1.67.
  Inside the test's 35%; fixing it rebuilds every tree.
- **Older trees are built at their natural size, not unit size.** The oak's
  skeleton is 0.67 tall when its cards and trunk are sized, so both come out
  about 1.5x what its conventions intend. The new archetypes avoid it (above);
  correcting the old ones changes how they look.
- **The elm's summer crown is airier than a real elm's.** It has every tree's
  leaf budget (6,000 triangles at maturity, a third of it wood) spread over a
  crown 15 m across. Coarser twigs (`twig_r` 0.85 against 0.6: 64 branch
  segments instead of 101) were tried; the summer crown did not visibly fill
  and the winter crown lost the fine twigging that reads as elm, so 0.6 stayed.
  A fuller broad crown needs a bigger budget or cheaper leaves, which is a
  decision for every tree, not this one.

## Verification

- **Renders**, headless Chromium through the real viewer, July and January,
  side-on: before (V2.91) and after contact sheets of all twenty trees, and
  Stylised for the ten, in the session scratchpad. The ten read as themselves
  and as different from one another; the fifteen others are unchanged. **One
  slip, caught before commit:** the experiment that switched the bake off was
  made in a scratch copy of the viewer whose `scene3d/` was a symlink to the
  repository, so it edited `09-models.js` itself, and the renders between it
  and the end were drawn without the bake. `git status` showed the file; it was
  reverted and every tree re-rendered. The shapes were judged on silhouettes
  and stand; the colours in the final sheets are the real ones.
- **The architecture guard** tripped on `03-herbs.js` (758/700) in the first
  full run; the split above is its answer, not a bigger number.
- **`tests/test_tree_shapes.py`**, ten tests, measured from the shipped GLBs
  (stdlib only) and from the viewer's generator run in Node. Against the
  V2.91 models and viewer, all fail but the ground-contact guard, which exists
  because of the juniper in build 4 above:
  - each of the ten resolves through the viewer's own tables to an archetype of
    its own, and it is baked;
  - water birch and Bebb's willow leave the ground as three or more separate
    stems at every tier, box elder as two or more (V2.91: one);
  - the elm's wood is at least three limbs at 30% of its height (the default
    it borrowed: one);
  - the lodgepole's crown has at least four storeys of limbs with bare trunk
    between them at every tier (V2.91: 0, 3, 2; now 6, 6, 6);
  - the jack pine has four or more dead stubs below its crown (V2.91: none; now
    eight) and a trunk that wanders at least 0.6% of its height (V2.91: 0);
  - the juniper's foliage starts in its bottom tenth, it is widest in its lower
    four tenths, and its top is under 0.6 of its widest;
  - no tree's wood stands clear of the ground;
  - in Stylised, water birch, Bebb's willow and box elder are clumps, the elm and
    cottonwood fork into leaders, and the aspen is unchanged.
- **The existing asset tests** (aspect, crown fraction, leaf-card scale,
  budgets, foliage to the top, keys) pass on all ten. Aspects against target:
  elm 1.95 / 1.67, box elder 1.26 / 1.25, cottonwood 1.90 / 1.67, narrowleaf
  1.68 / 1.67, peach-leaved willow 1.41 / 1.33, juniper 1.85 / 1.78, water birch
  1.81 / 1.8, Bebb's willow 2.21 / 1.8, lodgepole 4.59 / 4.2, jack pine 3.59 /
  3.3. Every tier is inside its triangle budget.
- **Only the intended models changed.** A build of the base commit against this
  one: the fifteen other trees and every herb, shrub, groundcover and layer
  model identical. Fauna and the snag differ between any two builds of the same
  code (float noise) and were not shipped. The ten GLBs add 5.6 MB to
  `html/assets/models` (30 MB).
- **The full suite**: 3,916 tests, 0 failures, 7 skipped (`scripts/run_tests.py
  --exclude tests.test_undo_redo`, as CLAUDE.md prescribes for a container).

## What could not be verified

- **A real desktop.** Renders are software GL in a headless browser.
- **The shapes against photographs.** They follow the species' standard
  descriptions; no reference images were compared here.
- **Performance** in a real design with many trees. Each tier is inside the
  budget every tree already had. The viewer loads every model in the manifest
  when the 3D preview opens (`09-models.js`), so the extra 5.6 MB is read on
  every open, from the local install rather than a network; not timed here.
