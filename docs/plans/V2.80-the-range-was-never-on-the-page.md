# V2.80 — the range was never on the page

The first of V2.79's three open threads, and the diagnosis in the handover was
wrong in a way worth writing down.

---

## Context

V2.79 replaced the ecoregion range map with a 0.25° occupancy grid
(`src/species_range.py`) and a new renderer (`src/range_map.py`), then sent the
author three palettes to choose between. The verdict was *"all look
identical."*

The handover explained that with arithmetic, and the arithmetic is right:

| species | range cells | marks drawn | marks per cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Gaillardia aristata* | 361 | 4,046 | **11.2** |
| *Opuntia polyacantha* | 183 | 5,401 | **29.5** |
| *Cornus canadensis* | 455 | 4,707 | **10.3** |

The palettes differ almost entirely in the range wash; at 460 px the wash sits
under tens of overlapping marks per square. The recommendation that followed —
shade the cells by record count, draw few or no marks — was the right move.

It was not, however, the reason. **The wash was not buried under the marks. It
was not on the page at all.**

## The bug

`range_svg` draws in this order:

```
land · neighbouring provinces · subject provinces · water
range cells
subject provinces again   ← so an edge stays legible against the wash
marks · cities
```

That second pass is wanted: a range that runs to a border looks like it
dissolves the border unless the line is redrawn over it. It reused the CSS class
of the first pass, `.subj`, and `.subj` carries `fill: #ffffff`.

So every build of this map drew all 361 squares and then painted an opaque white
province over the top of them. Three palettes that differ only in a layer nobody
could see render identically — not approximately, **byte-identically apart from
the hex in the stylesheet**. A fourth palette would have changed nothing either.

The fix is one class: a `.subjline` rule that is `fill:none` and stroke only.

**Why nobody caught it.** `src/range_map.py` shipped with no test file and no
caller — it existed to produce PNGs for the author to look at. Nothing here
raises, nothing parses wrong, and the SVG is well-formed. The failure is
entirely in *paint order*, which is invisible to every check short of looking at
the picture, and looking at the picture is the thing V2.79 recorded that it
could not do.

`tests/test_range_map.py` now asserts about paint order directly: no filled
province polygon may appear after the last range cell, and the palettes must
produce distinct output. Both fail against the old renderer.

## A second layer that was never styled

Found by rendering the SVG on its own rather than inside a page: every lake was
**black** and every river **invisible**.

`water_svg` and `cities_svg` are shared with the ecoregion maps and emit
`ecomap-*` classes, which are styled in `html/site/site.css`. The range map's
inline `<style>` block styles `.ctx`, `.subj`, `.cell`, `.spec` and `.obs` and
nothing else — so a lake polygon fell back to SVG's default fill (black) and a
river, emitted as `<polyline fill="none">` with no stroke rule, drew nothing.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that matters:

- The module docstring calls the output *"one self-contained ``<svg>`` string"*.
  It was not, and the sample renders the author was judging had black lakes in
  them.
- The palettes each declare `water`, `river` and `city`. **None of the three
  keys had ever been applied to anything.** They were prose in a table.

The `ecomap-*` rules now live in the inline block, scoped under `.rangemap` so
they also outrank `site.css` when the SVG is embedded in a page. That is what
makes the palette entries real.

## F145 continued — the cell carries the count

With the wash visible the recommended change is still right, for its original
reason: a species with 4,000 records cannot show them as 4,000 marks.

`species_range.cell_counts` keeps the count that `occupied_cells` was throwing
away — the same pass, one dict instead of a set — and `density_band` puts it in
one of five bands. `occupied_cells` is now a sorted view over it, so presence
and counts cannot disagree about which cells exist.

Breaks at **1 · 2-4 · 5-19 · 20-99 · 100+**, log-ish rather than even, because
record counts per cell are heavy-tailed. Measured over the whole catalogue after
the re-run:

| band | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 28,797 | 51.0% |
| 2-4 | 16,335 | 28.9% |
| 5-19 | 8,181 | 14.5% |
| 20-99 | 2,690 | 4.8% |
| 100+ | 510 | 0.9% |

Even breaks would have put the whole province in the lightest colour and Calgary
in the darkest. Half the cells in the catalogue hold exactly one record, which
is worth knowing on its own: the fine structure in these maps is mostly
singletons.

`marks` now defaults to `"none"`. `"all"` is what the F147 specimen/observation
toggle will switch on over a wash that has already done its job, and `"few"`
draws marks only in single-record cells — the lightest band, which a reader is
likeliest to mistake for nothing at all.

The shipped file goes to version 2, rows `[lat, lng, records]`. 56,513 cells
unchanged, 0.86 → **0.98 MB**. A version 1 row with no count reads as **one**
record, not zero: the file only ever held cells that had at least one, and
reading a missing field as an absence is the mistake this repo made three times
in V2.75, V2.78 and V2.79.

## What the ramp must not be read as

**Recording effort, as much as the plant.** A cell holding a city is dark for
nearly every species in the catalogue, because that is where the people with
cameras are — the same collection bias `ecoregion_ranges` already discloses in
its counts. A five-step wash that a reader decodes as *abundance* asserts more
than the records support, which is the entire subject of the review this line of
work came out of.

So three things carry the disclosure rather than one:

- `density_band`'s docstring, for the next person to touch the code.
- The legend, titled **records per square** — and a test forbids the words
  *abundance*, *abundant*, *common* and *density* anywhere in the rendered SVG.
- `caption`, which appends *"Darker squares hold more records, which follows
  roads and towns as much as it follows the plant"* — but **only when the rows
  actually carry counts**. A caption explaining a ramp the reader cannot see is
  noise, and an unexplained ramp invites the abundance reading.

A key is drawn whenever the ramp is, and dropped below 420 px rather than
rendered unreadably. The first cut of the legend used 15 px swatches and the
labels ran into each other; 26 px is what `100+` needs at 7.5 px.

## What was verified, and how

Rendered to PNG through the container's headless Chromium — the first time
anything in this line of work has been looked at inside a session rather than
emailed out. Three species, three palettes.

- *Gaillardia aristata*: dense through the Calgary–Lethbridge corridor, thinning
  north, a scatter across southern Saskatchewan.
- *Opuntia polyacantha*: tight to the dry southeast, empty north of Red Deer.
- *Cornus canadensis*: foothills and boreal, absent from the dry southeast.

Three maps that read as three different plants, which is what "all look
identical" was asking for.

## Deliberately not done

- **F146 and F147 are untouched.** The species page still shows the ecoregion
  map; this renderer still has no caller. Wiring it in is a page-weight problem
  (846 KB of repeated polygons, 421 MB built site) and belongs with F146.
- **No fourth palette.** The three now differ visibly; which one ships is the
  author's call and it is a data change either way.
- **No subsampling of the marks.** Honest only with a caption saying so, and the
  wash makes it unnecessary for the default view.

## Could not be verified here

- **How it looks on a real display**, at real DPI, in the page. Headless
  Chromium at 680×560 is a good check and is not a monitor.
- **Anything needing the network.** `api.gbif.org` and `data.canadensys.net`
  both answer 403 at this environment's proxy, unchanged from V2.79. The
  re-derivation ran entirely off the V2.75 point cache.
- **The VASCAN archive path.** Still waiting on the author's `--from-archive`
  run; the canary is *Amelanchier alnifolia* landing in `confirm` and the
  `review` bucket collapsing from 182.
