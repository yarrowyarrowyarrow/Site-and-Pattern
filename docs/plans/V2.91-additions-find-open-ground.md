# V2.91 — What the design review adds goes in open ground, and grass is not forage

*Two items from the backlog, both the owner's "Go ahead" on the recommendation:
**F183**, found by V2.90, and **F182**, found by V2.89. For F182 the recommended
default was stated with it and taken: **grasses, sedges and rushes never count as
pollinator forage, even with a recorded bloom period.***

## F183 — Where the review's additions go

**What was wrong.** After the main pass, three follow-up steps can add plants:
the critic's repairs (a keystone, a host plant, bloom-gap fillers, three at most),
the goal top-up (one plant when nothing placed meets a hard goal) and the wildlife
top-up (one plant per chosen animal nothing placed feeds). All three took their
spot from `_one_position_in_boundary`, which returns the boundary's **first 6 m
grid cell**, its north-west corner, every time. Measured with the V2.90
reproduction (LLM path, a 30 m square yard, one yarrow drift):

| Density | Plants | Added by the review | Distinct spots | Nearest other plant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 6 | Basket Willow (keystone), Buckbean (host plant), Maximilian Sunflower (Sep–Oct bloom gap) | **1** | 0.00 m, each |
| balanced | 203 | the same three | **1** | 0.00 m, each |

Offline, on a 12 × 18 m yard with an animal chosen, Basket Willow and Maximilian
Sunflower shared a point the same way. And **Buckbean, a bog plant, was added to a
dry yard** as a larval host.

**Why not the main pass's positioner.** The backlog's suggested fix was to place
the additions as the main pass places a group, which means asking its
`ScoredPositioner` for its best free anchor cell. Measured, what it has left once
the main pass is done (offline, anchor cells free / total):

| Yard | sparse | balanced | full |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 × 18 m | 8 / 35 | 4 / 35 | **0 / 35** |
| 30 × 30 m | 14 / 36 | 5 / 36 | **0 / 36** |
| 60 × 40 m | 50 / 70 | 22 / 70 | 13 / 70 |

Up to three repairs, one goal plant and one plant per animal can follow. On a
yard at full density the positioner has nothing to give, at balanced density
barely enough, and its bookkeeping misses what it never reserved: the vines F181
seats at their hosts' feet. So the additions read the ground itself, the plants
actually placed, instead of a pool that is usually spent.

**The rule** (`src/open_ground.py`, one call per plant, so each addition sees the
ones before it):

- **Where a plant may go at all:** inside the boundary, clear of keep-out
  (existing trees and buildings, drawn hardscape, and now the design's own pond,
  swale or rain garden, which the main pass places after its plants), and inside
  the drawn restoration zones when there are any. The same rules the main pass
  follows.
- **Free ground first.** A spot is free for a plant when every placed plant is at
  least half their two spacings away, the centre-to-centre spacing the main pass
  lays its drifts at (`_plant_spacing_m`).
- **Among free spots, the ground that suits it:** the main pass's own cell score
  (`placement_score.score_cell_for_plant`: shade, moisture, slope, edge) when the
  terrain is known, read at the nearest scored anchor cell.
- **With no free spot, the least crowded one.** Never on top of another plant
  while the boundary has any other ground.
- **A vine at the foot of a tree or shrub** when one has room, by F181's seats
  (sunny side first, capacity per host), rebuilt from the placed trees and shrubs
  and the vines already on them. Otherwise it goes in open ground and the V2.90
  note names it.
- **Deterministic:** ties go to the candidate grid's row-major order.

The candidates are a grid over the boundary at half a metre, coarsened on a large
lot so a search never weighs more than 2,500 spots. With no boundary (a pin-only
design) the grid covers the ground the design already occupies, plus a margin.

## F183 — No water plant added to a dry yard

**What needs standing water.** The catalogue records no wetland indicator, so the
test is what it does record: a plant filed `aquatic`, or whose habit stands in
water (`emergent`, `floating`, `submerged`), or whose only recorded moisture is
`high`. That is **23 species**, the same 23 the V2.90 pond survey counted, one
horsetail and three bulrushes filed as sedges included. The test the offline
ranking uses to push wetland plants down, `"high"` anywhere in `water_needs`,
catches **139**: every willow, Red Osier Dogwood, Bunchberry. Those grow in an
ordinary yard and are right for a ranking's last place, wrong for a ban.
`zoning.needs_standing_water` is the new test, beside `preferred_zone_for_plant`.

**What the steps were reaching for** (Edmonton, 30 × 30 m, site-scoped search):

| Search | Rows | Need standing water |
|---|---|---|
| keystone | 36 | none |
| host plant | 93 | Buckbean, **first** |
| pollinator (bloom gaps) | 243 | Broad-leaved Arrowhead, Buckbean, Water Parsnip (the first three), Water Arum |
| wildlife, one search per animal | 1,116 animals | 39 had a water plant first; **5 have nothing else** |

The five: *Lasioglossum zephyrum* (a sweat bee) and Sandhill Crane, fed only by
Broad-leaved Arrowhead; *Macaria truncataria* (a moth), by Buckbean;
*Parhelophilus rex* (a hoverfly), by Broad-leaved Arrowhead and Yellow Pond-lily;
*Zaira georgiae*, by Water Parsnip.

**Decision: the follow-up steps never add a plant that needs standing water.**
They take the next candidate instead. For an animal fed only by such plants the
design notes say so and name them, so the user can plant one by hand where they
have water. The goal top-up says so the same way if every plant meeting the goals
needs water.

**Why no exception for a wet site.** Three places a "wet enough" answer could come
from, and none can carry it:

- The app has **no reading of site moisture**.
- Zoning's `WET` is **relative**: the lowest quarter of the site's elevation grid
  or a local hollow. Every zoned site has some, including a well-drained upland lot.
- A **pond is keep-out**, and the generator does not plant in ponds. That was
  left as a design rule by V2.90 and is now **F185**, the owner's call. Until then
  a water plant "beside the pond" would still be a water plant on dry ground.

**Left alone:** the main pass. An LLM spec that asks for aquatics still gets them,
placed where the positioner's moisture score likes best, and the offline ranking
still puts wetland plants last rather than nowhere. The user asked for those; the
follow-up steps' additions nobody asked for.

## F182 — Grasses are not forage

**What was wrong, corrected.** V2.89's record said all 78 grasses, sedges and
rushes counted as forage "each with the June to September fallback". They count,
but not by the fallback: **all 78 carry a recorded bloom period** (the two rows
filed as rushes without one are the horsetails), and `_has_flowers` returned true
for any bloom period before it looked at anything else. Its own docstring said
grasses do not count. So a meadow's grasses filled May to September with
real-looking entries.

**Where it reached.** Two forage calendars, two implementations, one bug:

- `forage_calendar.build_forage_calendar`, read by the docent's "Through the
  year" beat (peak month, months covered, the gaps), and so by the presentation
  still made from that beat, whose month is the peak.
- **Planning → Wildlife**, which lists each month's "Pollinator blooms" and
  flags "nectar gaps" from `bloom_period` straight out of the database. Not in
  the V2.89 finding; found in this survey.

**Found while wiring it: the season beat had never played.** Both callers of
the docent, Learn → Present and `permadesign_api.docent_script`, pass placed-plant
records: position, name, and in the app's case the plant type and water needs.
**None carries a bloom period**, so the calendar counted nothing in flower and
`_beat_season` returned None on every design. Measured on a five-species design:
beats `opening, score, food_web, species, brood, close` from both callers, and
`season` appears only when full catalogue rows are passed. The docent test that
used the real database asked for four beats and never asked which. So F182 alone
would have changed nothing anyone could see. `build_docent_script` now fills each
placed record from its catalogue row for the calendar (the unused `get_plant`
parameter it already had, put to work), and the worked example's tour gains
"Through the year" for the first time: *"Bloom peaks around June, with forage
across 6 of 7 growing-season months"*, the gap being October.

**And the first thing it said on a prairie mix was wrong.** With one species in
flower in each month, every bloom month ties, and the tie went to the earliest:
six grasses, a crocus and an aster "peaked around March". A tie now reads *"Flowers
open from March to October"*, and the beat's camera month is the tied month
nearest midsummer. A real peak is still named.

**The rule, in one place:** `forage_calendar.is_pollinator_forage(plant)`. A
grass, sedge or rush (`plant_type`) never counts, with or without a bloom period.
Everything else counts as before. Both calendars and the gap-filling suggestions
use it.

**Left alone:**

- **Five wetland monocots filed `aquatic`.** Cattail, Giant Bur-reed, Great
  Bulrush and Small Bottle Sedge are wind-pollinated too. Rat Root is filed with
  them and its pollination is uncertain. The catalogue records no pollination
  mode, and picking them out by genus, or by the reed body they are drawn with,
  would be a guess the data cannot carry. They reach a design only through a pond
  garden.
- **The bee panel** (`bee_habitat`) reads documented nectar and pollen records
  and each bee's host genera, not bloom alone. A grass appears there only with a
  record behind it, which is right.

## F184 — Found: the habitat score counts grass bloom

The score's bloom continuity (20 of 100 points) reads every `bloom_period`,
graminoids included, and the critic's bloom-gap repairs read the score's gaps.
V2.89's note that the score is unaffected was wrong. Measured, points with and
without graminoid bloom:

| Design | Total | Bloom now | Without graminoids |
|---|---|---|---|
| The worked example | 63 | 17.1 | 17.1 |
| Six offline designs (two yards × three goal sets) | 85–90 | 20.0 | 20.0 |
| A prairie mix: six grasses, Prairie Crocus, Smooth Aster | **52** | **20.0** | **14.3 (total 46)** |

Most designs do not move, because their wildflowers already cover the months the
grasses do. The one that does is the grass-heavy restoration planting, the app's
central use case, where June and July are a real forage gap that the score calls
covered, so the critic never offers a June bloomer. **Not changed here:** a change
to the headline is reserved to the owner (the headline-stability rule in
`habitat_score.py`). Until it is decided the forage calendars and the score
disagree about grasses, on purpose, and the forage calendar's docstring says so.

## Verification plan

- **The probe before and after** (`f183_probe.py`, scratchpad): distinct spots for
  the additions, the nearest other plant to each, and no water plant among them.
- **Tests**, each written to fail on the old code:
  - additions land on distinct spots, clear of every placed plant, inside the
    boundary and keep-out, inside a drawn fill zone;
  - on a full yard, the least crowded spot rather than a stack;
  - a vine addition is seated at a shrub's foot when one has room;
  - `apply_repairs` passes the row it is placing to `position_for`;
  - the repairs and both top-ups never add a plant that needs standing water, and
    the note names the animal and its water plants;
  - `needs_standing_water` counts the 23 and not the willows;
  - a grass with a bloom period is not forage, in both calendars;
  - the Planning → Wildlife calendar lists no grass under "Pollinator blooms".
- **The full suite**, and the docent narration before and after on a grass-heavy
  design.

## Results

**The probe, after** (same designs as the table at the top):

| Design | Added by the review | Distinct spots | Nearest other plant, each |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM, 30 m square, density none | Basket Willow, Foothills Rough Fescue, Maximilian Sunflower | **3** (was 1) | 1.83, 1.20, 1.20 m |
| LLM, 30 m square, balanced (203 plants) | the same three | **3** (was 1) | 1.80, 0.60, 0.68 m |
| Offline, 12 × 18 m, an animal chosen | Basket Willow, Maximilian Sunflower | **2** (was 1) | 1.75, 1.50 m |

Each is at or past the spacing it needs from its nearest neighbour (the willow
needs 1.8 m from a yarrow, the sunflower 0.675 m), which is the rule doing what it
says: free ground, nearest the planting. **Buckbean is gone**: the host-plant repair
now adds Foothills Rough Fescue, the next candidate. The room search was checked
against brute force on the balanced design: 2,500 candidates, no disagreement.

**One V2.90 test had leaned on the old behaviour.** Its "nothing to climb" design
got a keystone willow from the review, which used to land on the far grid cell and
now lands beside the planting, where the Wild Clematis next to it climbs it. The
3D scene and the note agree about that, so the rule held; the test's premise did
not. It now holds the review off to test option (c) alone, and a new test runs the
review and checks the note still names exactly the vines the scene leaves on the
ground.

**Full suite: 3,906 tests, 0 failures, 7 skipped** (`scripts/run_tests.py`,
`tests.test_undo_redo` excluded as CI excludes it; the skips are pypdf missing
and four environment ones). A first run failed one test, and the failure was mine
in a different way: I edited `llm_design.py` while it ran, so
`inspect.getsource(generate_design_offline)` read the shifted file and returned
`_record_budget_note`. The rerun, on the committed tree with nothing touched, is
the result above.

**Tests written to fail first.** Against the V2.90 code: the generator's additions
share a spot (LLM path: 1 spot for 3 plants; offline, on a thinned pool: 1 for 2);
the top-ups add Buckbean and Broad-leaved Arrowhead; the critic adds nothing at all
when `position_for` wants the row; every grass counts as forage; Planning →
Wildlife lists the grasses under "Pollinator blooms"; the docent never plays its
season beat. Each of those fails on the old code and passes on the new.
