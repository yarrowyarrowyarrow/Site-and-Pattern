# V3.34 · The colours, folded in; the photographs, planned

*The owner, sending the review's answers: "I answered all the questions … I
greatly enjoyed the multiple photos i got to see of each plant. It would be
nice to add more photos (with appropriate licensing for sharing in my
app/website). Start with the colours and make a plan for adding more pics to
this experience." And, while this was being written: "I'm happy to run any
script on my pc."*

**Why a new branch, not V3.33.** V3.33 was pushed and its installers built. The
updater compares `(major, minor)` only, so colours and a schema bump pushed onto
V3.33 would never reach a V3.33 install: the V2.81/V2.82 case in `CLAUDE.md`.

### What else changed, and why

- **The data gate defers to a photo check.** `validate_flower_colour` failed 8
  plants whose name names another colour (*Purple* Coneflower answered pink,
  *Wild Red* Currant pink to red). The name was evidence because nobody had
  looked; somebody has now, and `seed_flower_colour.py` already let a checked
  plant win for the same reason. A photo-checked row is skipped by the name
  check, and counts as checkable in the genus-sharing warning.
- **A flagged plant leaves the list once looked at.** `colour_checks.reason`
  put the audit's seven first whatever their source, so they stayed on the list
  after they were answered whenever it was built without the checks file.
- **Three colours read as a sentence**: "Yellow, Orange and pink" became
  "Yellow, orange and pink". The bug was old, but eight plants have three
  colours now.
- **No colour's tooltip counts species.** Orange said "three species" and is
  five.

### Worth a second look (folded in as answered)

These are the owner's answers and stand. Each is listed because it disagrees
with the plant's name or with a sibling answer, which is the pattern a slip
leaves:

- **Yellow-flowered Locoweed** (*Oxytropis campestris*): purple, then cream.
  The name says yellow. If the photographs were mostly purple, either some were
  misidentified (common in *Oxytropis*) or the common name is wrong for what
  grows here.
- **Catkin trees, answered two ways.** Trembling Aspen, American Elm, Water
  Birch and Thinleaf Alder are brown to green; Manitoba Maple green; Balsam
  Poplar red; Peach-leaved Willow yellow. Paper Birch, both cottonwoods, Bur Oak
  and Green Alder are no showy flower. The first group now appears under Brown
  and Green in the colour filter and on the website's colour pages, beside
  Cattail. The sages split the same way: Big Sagebrush and Plains Wormwood
  yellow, Prairie Sage no showy flower.
- **Beaked Hazelnut**: pink. That is the tiny female flower in a close-up;
  in April a person notices the yellow-brown catkins.
- **Harebell**: blue, sometimes cream. White harebells exist but are rare.
- **Wild Vetch**: pink. Usually described as blue-purple to lavender.

**To change an answer**, use the same `colour_review.html` (not a regenerated
one: a checked plant leaves a regenerated page's list), change it, *Save my
answers*, and send the file. A second answer replaces the first.

## Part 2: more photographs (planned for V3.35)

### What there is now, measured

- **One photograph per plant**, on 311 of 424; **113 have none** (54
  wildflowers, 23 grasses, sedges and rushes, 12 shrubs, 8 trees). All 311 are
  CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA from iNaturalist's open-data store. **15 of the top 60**
  (`plant_priority.rank`) have none: Bebb's Willow, Many-flowered Aster,
  Maximilian Sunflower, Elegant, Prairie and Sticky Goldenrod, Stiff Sunflower,
  Balsam Poplar, Narrowleaf and Plains Cottonwood, Peach-leaved and
  Short-capsuled Willow, Western Willow Aster, White Prairie Aster,
  Blanketflower.
- **The machinery for several is built and empty.** `plant_photos` (V2.35, F70)
  holds photos in seven named slots (whole plant, flower, leaf, fruit,
  bark/stem, winter, seedling), keyed by scientific name, `seed` or `user`.
  `data/plant_photos.json` holds 2 rows. The plant page and the website show
  only the first photo they are given.
- **The licence gate never reads `plant_photos.json`.** `validate_plant_images`
  checks the one photo in `plants_master.json`. Its 2 rows are clean; hundreds
  would not be checked.
- **A photo is fetched when it is shown and cached** (`src/image_cache.py`), so
  more photos add nothing to the installer. The website build copies them from
  that cache (`_stage_photos`).

### The licence is the whole question

A plant photograph may ship if it is **CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA**
(`data_quality._PLANT_IMAGE_LICENSES`). NC is out for a photograph, on the
owner's V2.79 reasoning (a photograph is redistributed as a work, a coordinate
is a fact), and this plan does not reopen it. **iNaturalist's default licence
is CC BY-NC.** V2.80 measured 329,267 of 365,092 drawable occurrence records
(90%) as NC observations, and photographs are likely similar, so most of the
photographs the colour review showed probably cannot ship. That is a guess, and
the plan rests on it. So the first step measures it:

**`scripts/count_open_photos.py`** (this increment, run on the owner's PC):

- `--seen` counts the licences of the photographs the colour review already
  showed, from its cache. No network.
- `--probe NAME` checks that iNaturalist's licence filter (`photo_license`)
  does what it says, by printing the licence of every photo it returns.
- With no options, for each of the 424 plants, the number of research-grade
  observations with a photo that may ship, in Alberta and Saskatchewan and
  anywhere, and with any photo at all. About 25 minutes, resumable, writing
  `data/fetched/open_photo_counts.json`.

That file says which plants will have plenty to pick from, which few, and which
none. It decides the order of the picking and how big the gap list in D4 is.

### How it works: the colour review, again

The owner enjoyed the colour review, and it suits the constraint (no session
reaches iNaturalist), so the photographs come in the same way.

1. **Fetch, on the owner's PC** (`scripts/fetch_photo_candidates.py`). For each
   plant, using the taxon ids the colour cache already holds: research-grade
   observations whose photo may ship (`photo_license=cc0,cc-by,cc-by-sa`,
   checked again per photo, never trusted), favourites first, Alberta and
   Saskatchewan first. Four kinds, from iNaturalist's Plant Phenology
   annotation: in flower, in fruit or seed, no flowers (leaves and the whole
   plant), and for trees and shrubs the winter months. Also the taxon's own
   photo set, where the current photos came from. Up to twelve candidates a
   plant, none under 500 px, at most two from one photographer. About 40
   minutes; `--probe` first, because the fruiting and no-flowers values (14,
   21) and the month filter have never been seen from here.
2. **Pick, in the browser** (`photo_review.html`, written by the same script).
   One card per plant: the photo it has now, then the candidates in their four
   groups, each with its credit and licence under it, and every one able to
   ship. **The page pre-picks** the most-favoured candidate in each group (D3).
   Tick up to six, star the one that leads, and change a pick's slot with one
   click. Answers stay in the browser; *Save my picks* writes `photo_picks.json`.
3. **Fold, in a session** (`scripts/fold_photo_picks.py`). Every pick is checked
   before anything is written: a plant in the catalogue, a licence that may
   ship, a credit that is not empty, a URL on the open-data store, at most six,
   one leading. It writes `data/plant_photos.json` (`seed`, ranked in the
   owner's order) and sets each plant's `image_url` to the photo that leads, so
   every surface that shows one photo shows the same one. Schema bump.
   **`validate_plant_images` reads `plant_photos.json` too**, closing the gap
   above.
4. **Show them.**
   - **App**: the plant page shows the photo that leads, and under it a strip
     of the others. A click or Enter on one shows it large with its own credit.
     Each is named for a screen reader ("Photo 3 of 5: in fruit") and meets the
     24 px target floor. It adds no words to the short page.
   - **Website**: the species page shows the same strip, each photo linked to a
     larger copy, each with its own credit. The build copies them as it copies
     the one now, and strips any EXIF on the way with `photo_import`'s
     stripper. iNaturalist's resized copies usually carry none, but the rule
     costs nothing.
   - **Credit links** go to `https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/<id>`, derived
     from the photo's own URL, which shows the photo, its licence and its
     observation. No new column.

### Decisions for the owner

| | Question | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Keep the licence bar at CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA? | **Keep it.** It is the V2.79 rule. A free app is not obviously "non-commercial" once it has a website with analytics or is handed to someone else, and the picking page shows only photos that pass, so nothing picked needs checking again. |
| **D2** | Which photo leads: the flower, or the whole plant? V2.35 put the whole plant first (useful for finding it in your yard in May); the homeowner plan's PR-8 says the first photo shows it in flower, chosen for beauty (P13). | **The flower leads**, by default, with the whole plant second. A whole plant is a green smudge at list-row size, and a flower is why somebody stops scrolling. The owner's star overrides either. |
| **D3** | Pre-picked, or pick every photo yourself? | **Pre-picked; confirm or swap.** From scratch, 424 plants at a minute or two each is seven hours or more. Confirming is perhaps two or three, and it can be done in sittings and sent in parts. |
| **D4** | A plant with no photo that may ship, anywhere? | **Say so** ("No photo yet", as now), and keep a list. Next summer the owner photographs the ones that grow near Edmonton (their own photos, under a licence they choose; F72's import strips location data). Asking iNaturalist photographers for permission is possible, but each needs a written record; worth it only for a handful of top picks. |

Defaults that need no decision: up to six photos a plant (what the review
showed); picking order is the 113 with none first, then the top 60, then the
rest.

### What it costs

- **The owner**: the count (25 minutes, unattended), the candidate fetch (40
  minutes, unattended), picking (two or three hours with pre-picks, in
  sittings).
- **The installer**: nothing; photos download when a page shows them.
- **The website**: about 2,000 more photos. At iNaturalist's 240 px thumbnail
  (about 20 KB) and 500 px copy (about 70 KB), roughly 200 MB on a site
  of 328 MB, which stays under GitHub Pages' 1 GB. If that ever pinches, the
  larger copy can link to iNaturalist's photo page instead of being copied.

### Could not verify

- **The share of photos that may ship.** It is estimated from occurrence
  records, not measured on photographs; `count_open_photos.py` measures it.
- **The API's filters**: `photo_license`, phenology values 14 and 21, `month`.
  Each script has a `--probe` that prints the raw answer before anything is
  cached, the V2.79 lesson.
- **Photo sizes on the open-data store**: the 240/500 px figures and the byte
  estimates are iNaturalist's usual sizes, not measured here.

### Left alone

- **Animal photos.** Bees keep the stricter CC0/CC BY bar (F37, Group C).
  The same tool can do animals later, with that bar.
- **Wikimedia Commons as a second source**: only if the count shows gaps
  iNaturalist cannot fill.
- **Hotlinking**: the site copies, as it does now.
- **Colour from pixels**: still no, for the reason in V3.33.
