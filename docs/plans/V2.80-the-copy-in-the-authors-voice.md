# V2.80 — the copy in the author's voice, and the link that shared a grey box

**Branch:** `V2.80`
**Ask:** the author read the built site, rewrote the Method and About copy by
hand, and handed back an ORIGINAL/REWRITE document to apply verbatim. Plus one
thing that was not copy: *"the little blurb that shows up when you share the
link on facebook or reddit"* should be appealing, *"perhaps a photo of one of
the showy plants"*.

---

## What the rewrite was actually doing

Two moves run through the whole document and neither is a matter of taste.

**First person singular.** The site was written in an institutional *we* for
what is one person in Edmonton. That *we* is not neutral: it makes a solo
project sound like an organisation, which is both untrue and, on a page whose
entire subject is *how far can you trust this*, quietly corrosive. The rewrite
is `I` throughout, and it adds a **"Who's behind this"** section to the About
page — the one section only the author could write, and the highest-value change
in the document. Nowhere on the previous site did a person appear.

**One caveat, one place.** The originals restated the same warnings across four
pages and sometimes twice in one paragraph. This repo has a standing rule that
a caveat nobody finishes reading protects nobody (V2.80 already deleted two
Method-page tests on that argument, and the note recording it is three
paragraphs above the one this increment adds). The rewrite applies it again.

## The bug the rewrite was written around

The `/method/` nativity section read, on the live site:

> 422 of 422 species carry an answer from it.
>
> **The other 0** read *Not established* and show no province list. VASCAN
> records some of them as introduced here, carries some only under a name this
> catalogue does not use, and does not carry a few at all.

The count was computed and correct. **The prose around it was not.** That
paragraph was written when 20 species were withheld and did not move when
[the renames](V2.80-the-names-were-the-problem.md) took the number to none.

This is the "computed, never written down" rule failing one level up: the site
puts every *number* through the module that owns it, and then wraps those
numbers in sentences that assume a shape the numbers no longer have. So the
sentence shape follows the count too (`_nativity_claim`): no withheld species
and the paragraph about them does not exist. It comes back, correctly worded,
the first time one does.

The author's document listed this first, under *"these aren't style, they're
broken output, and they're the most conclusive evidence to a sharp reader."*

## The second broken output, on all 422 species pages

The same list named the field dumps. Every published species page carried:

> **Buying it**
> price: about $8–$16 per plant (estimate); availability: garden centre; notes:
> Estimate (herb default); AB retail as of 2026

`_extras_section` has a generic branch that renders a dict as `k: v` pairs, and
`sourcing` is a dict. The desktop has rendered the same dict as prose since the
field existed (`plant_directory_window._sourcing_block`); **only the public
surface printed the schema at the reader.**

Fixed by putting the formatting in `sourcing.describe`, next to the numbers,
rather than in either renderer — which is the point: there were already two
surfaces disagreeing about how to say this and a third was about to.

The estimate flag stays, in its own `.src` mark like every other provenance
mark on a species page. `src/sourcing.py` requires a price to travel with its
disclaimer, and *"Estimate (herb default)"* is the honest statement that the
number is a plant-type default and not a quote from a nursery.

One thing the obvious fix gets wrong, and it is worth writing down because the
first cut shipped it: **`availability_class` is *how you get it*, not always a
kind of shop**, so a single sentence template does not fit all five values.
Dropping the token into *"usually from a …"* produces *"usually from a **seed
or plug**"* on 82 species and *"usually from a **big box**"* on 15, which is
a different unreadable output replacing the first one. `_AVAILABILITY_CLAUSE`
gives each class a whole clause (*"usually sold as seed or plugs"*, *"hard to
find in the trade"*), and an unrecognised class falls back to the bare token,
which is what the desktop has always shown.

## Five disclosures the rewrite removes

Applied as written, because they are the author's pages. Recorded here so the
removals read as decisions rather than as things that fell out in editing:

| Gone | Was on |
|---|---|
| *"Records based on a living specimen, a material sample or a fossil are excluded: a plant in a botanical garden is a place somebody put it."* | Method, what a record is |
| *"Recorded is not native. A garden escape can accumulate hundreds of records."* | Method, the map |
| *"Records closer together than 0.01 degrees are drawn once, which is under half a pixel."* | Method, licences |
| *"Coverage stops at the Saskatchewan border. Manitoba shares several of these ecoregions and is not included."* | Method, known limits |
| *"35 of 422 species have a colour checkable against the plant's own name"* (the count) | Method, known limits |

The third one is the only one I would put back, and it is the smallest: it is
the difference between *two records in one spot* and *one record*, and nothing
else on a public page says it. The dedup rule is still disclosed in the shipped
`plant_occurrence_points.json` comment, which is public data but is not a page.

The second and fourth have arguments for going. **Nativity is sourced now** —
F137/F144 landed in this same release, a species VASCAN cannot settle prints no
province list at all, and the section directly below the map says so. The
"recorded is not native" warning existed because the site could not tell a
garden escape from a native; it can. And the Saskatchewan border is stated on
the About page and in the footer of every page on the site.

The flower-colour count is replaced by *"I have verified some of the flower
colours but not all"*, which is weaker and is also the only line in the rewrite
that asks the reader for something: *"if you notice gross errors please send me
feedback."* That link now goes to `/feedback/`, which V2.80 built.

`test_it_says_recorded_is_not_native` was deleted with its reasoning written
into the file, following the precedent set immediately above it.

## The share card (F150)

Not copy. Every page on the site carried a `<title>` and a
`<meta name="description">` and **nothing else**, so a link pasted into
Facebook, Reddit, Slack, Discord or a text message unfolded into a grey box with
a URL under it.

That is not cosmetic. The whole argument of this catalogue is that the plants
are worth looking at, and the one surface where a stranger meets it first was
the one surface with no picture on it. It is P13 (*a native planting has to be
loved to survive*) at the point of first contact.

**The trap is that a broken share card is invisible from the page it is on.**
The HTML validates, the site renders, nothing errors, and the only way to find
out is for somebody else to paste the link somewhere. Three things follow from
that:

* **`og:image` must be absolute.** The scraper fetches the image from its own
  servers, with no page to resolve a relative path against, so
  `assets/photos/x.jpg` silently produces no card at all. This is the whole
  reason `base_url` is now load-bearing for a third distinct job (sitemap,
  CNAME, and now this). A build with no `base_url` emits **no image tag rather
  than a broken one**, and says so in the build output.
* **Two vocabularies.** Facebook, Reddit, Slack, Discord, LinkedIn and iMessage
  read `og:*`; Twitter/X reads `twitter:*`. One set of values, both spellings.
* **The credit has to ride in the tag.** These are open-licensed photographs and
  the attribution is the licence condition. Inside somebody else's app the page
  carrying the credit is reduced to a link, so the credit goes into
  `og:image:alt` — nominally alt text, and the only place it can travel.

Per page where a page has its own photograph (sharing a species page shows *that*
species), and a site default otherwise. The default is **picked from the built
model**, not pinned to a file, so it can never show a photograph the catalogue
has stopped publishing; `DEFAULT_SPECIES` is an ordered preference and the first
name with a credited photo wins. Prairie Crocus leads: it is the first thing to
flower on ground most people here have written off, which is the catalogue's
pitch in one photograph.

`src/site_share.py` is a new module rather than thirty lines in the renderer,
on the V2.73 precedent — `static_site_render.py` was at 744 of 800 and this is
not rendering, it is *what this build tells other people's machines*. It returns
the tags as `(attribute, name, content)` tuples so `_esc` stays the site's only
escaper; a second escaper in a second module is how `&amp;amp;` reached every
page title once already.

## What was not done

* **No image is generated.** A composed 1200×630 card with the site name on it
  would look more deliberate than a raw photograph, and it would put the site
  build behind Pillow, which this project deliberately treats as optional
  (`photo_import` strips EXIF with stdlib for exactly that reason). A
  photograph cropped by the scraper is the cheaper honest answer.
* **No `og:url`.** It would need every page's own path threaded through
  `_page`'s call sites in six modules, and every scraper falls back to the URL
  it fetched. Worth doing if a canonical-URL problem ever appears; not before.
* **`citations.disclaimer()` is untouched.** The About page now says the same
  two things in the author's first person; every other surface still prints the
  shared wording. The test moved to guarding the substance rather than the
  sentence.

## How it was verified

Full site build (422 species, 1,121 wildlife, 1,662 pages) against
`https://grownativeplants.ca/`, then read back: the nativity paragraph reads
*"At the moment all 422 species carry an answer from it"*, the Buying-it block
reads *"About $8–$16 per plant (estimate), usually stocked by garden centres"*, and
the home page's `og:image` is the Prairie Crocus photograph with Syd Cannings'
credit in `og:image:alt`.

Note the photographs came back **hotlinked**, not staged, because this
container's image cache is cold — `_stage_photos` falls back to the recorded
iNaturalist URL, which is absolute and works as an `og:image` either way. On a
machine with a warm cache the card points at the site's own copy, which is what
`test_it_prefers_the_staged_copy_over_the_hotlink` pins.

What could not be checked here: **no scraper was actually run against the built
site**, because it is not published from this session and Facebook's debugger
cannot reach a local directory. The tags are asserted against the specifications
and against every rendered page; the first real proof is the author pasting a
link somewhere.
